Senate Bill 357 By: Senators Harrell of the 40th and Watson of the 11th
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED AN ACT
To amend Chapter 2 of Title 21 of the Official Code of Georgia Annotated, relating to elections and primaries generally, so as to provide for challenging the qualifications of a person applying to register to vote or any person whose name appears on the list of electors; to provide for grounds; to provide for required documentation relating to such challenges; to provide for related matters; to repeal conflicting laws; and for other purposes.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY OF GEORGIA:
SECTION 1. Chapter 2 of Title 21 of the Official Code of Georgia Annotated, relating to elections and primaries generally, is amended by revising subsection (a) of Code Section 21-2-229, relating to challenge of applicant for registration by other electors, notice and hearing, right of appeal, and sanctions for board's noncompliance, as follows: "(a) Any elector of a county or municipality may challenge the qualifications of any person applying to register to vote in the county or municipality and may challenge the qualifications of any elector of the county or municipality whose name appears on the list of electors. Such challenges shall be in writing conform to the requirements of Code
Section 21-2-230.1 and shall distinctly specify the grounds of the challenge. There shall not be a limit on the number of persons whose qualifications such elector may challenge."
SECTION 2. Said chapter is further amended by revising subsection (a) of Code Section 21-2-230, relating to challenge of persons on list of electors by other electors, procedure, hearing, and right of appeal, as follows: "(a) Any elector of the county or municipality may challenge the right of any other elector of the county or municipality, whose name appears on the list of electors, to vote in an election. Such challenge shall be in writing conform to the requirements of Code Section 21-2-230.1 and specify distinctly shall distinctly specify the grounds of such challenge. Such challenge may be made at any time prior to the elector whose right to vote is being challenged voting at the elector's polling place or, if such elector cast an absentee ballot, prior to 5:00 P.M. on the day before the absentee ballots are to begin to be scanned and tabulated; provided, however, that challenges to persons voting by absentee ballot in person at the office of the registrars or the absentee ballot clerk shall be made prior to such person's voting. There shall not be a limit on the number of persons whose qualifications such elector may challenge."
SECTION 3. Said chapter is further amended by adding a new Code section to read as follows: "21-2-230.1. (a) A challenge filed pursuant to Code Section 21-2-229 or 21-2-230 shall be in writing, filed at least 45 days prior to the first day of advance voting, and shall allege one or more of the following grounds for such challenge: (1) The challenged person or elector will not be eighteen years of age by the next primary, election, or runoff;
(2) The challenged person or elector is not a citizen of the United States; (3) The challenged person or elector has been convicted of a felony and the voter's civil rights have not been restored; (4) The challenged person or elector has been judicially declared ineligible to vote due to mental incompetency; or (5) The challenged person or elector does not live at the residential address provided, in which case the challenger shall either: (A) Provide the challenged person or elector’s actual residence on the challenge form provided in subsection (e) of this Code section; or (B) Submit evidence that he or she exercised due diligence in verifying that the challenged person or elector does not reside at the residential address provided and in attempting to contact the challenged person or elector to learn the challenged person or elector's actual residential address, including that the challenger personally undertook at least two of the following steps: (i) Sent a letter with return service requested to the challenged person or elector's residential address provided and to the challenged person or elector's mailing address, if provided; (ii) Searched county property records to determine whether the challenged person or elector owns any property in the county; and (iii) Searched the state-wide voter registration data base to determine if the person or elector is registered at any other address in this state. (b) The challenger shall file a signed affidavit swearing that, to his or her personal knowledge and belief, the challenged person or elector is neither qualified to vote nor does the challenged person or elector reside at the address given on his or her voter registration record. (c) A challenge filed pursuant to paragraph (5) of subsection (a) of this Code section shall only be entertained if it is alleged that the person or elector does not live at the residential
address provided and that the actual residence of such person or elector is outside the precinct in which such residential address provided is located. (d) All documents obtained pertaining to the challenge shall be considered public records pursuant to Article 4 of Chapter 18 of Title 50. (e) The Secretary of State shall prepare the challenge form provided for in this Code section and shall make such form available on the website of the office of the Secretary of State. The Secretary of State shall provide copies of such form to each county election superintendent and registrar."
SECTION 4. All laws and parts of laws in conflict with this Act are repealed.
