                                      Georgia Power Company
                           Certified Demand-Side Management Programs
                                   Q2 2026 Programs Status Report

In accordance with the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (“Commission”) Final Order of Docket No. 56003,
signed July 8, 2025, Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) provides this second
quarterly report for activity through June 30, 2026.


Residential Programs

Behavioral Program:
Georgia Power’s Residential Behavioral program is designed to encourage customer engagement with home
energy management and energy efficiency to reduce energy consumption. The program utilizes Advanced
Metering Infrastructure (AMI) data to provide customer-specific information that allows the customer to
compare their energy use for the month, and previous months when available, to the consumption of a peer
group of homes with similar characteristics including year built, square footage, and heating type, and homes
that are considered energy-efficient. The reports also include seasonal and household-appropriate energy
savings tips, as well as information on energy efficiency programs.

Demand Response Program:
The Residential Demand Response (“Res DR”) program encourages customers to shift electricity use during
periods of peak demand; hot summer days, cold winter days, or other times when system capacity is
constrained. During demand response events, Georgia Power (or its representative) may manage participants’
home electric load by automatically adjusting eligible smart thermostats. Demand response programs help
reduce reliance on higher-cost peaking plants during high-load periods. Res DR provides education, awareness
messaging, and financial incentives for customers with qualifying existing smart thermostats who agree to
reduce energy use during these events.


                                                      1

Home Energy Improvement Program:
Georgia Power’s Home Energy Improvement Program (HEIP) provides financial incentives to support energy
efficiency improvements across residential building types. Single-family customers may participate through
multiple single-measure options, including bundled home comfort measures that offer increased rebates.
Multifamily properties may participate by meeting the required electric kWh reduction thresholds or through
select single-measure options, with new construction multifamily properties also eligible for limited measures.
The program also includes customer education, outreach, and contractor training to support effective
participation.

Energy Assistance for Savings and Efficiency:
Georgia Power’s EASE program promotes energy efficiency improvements in existing income-qualified and
moderate-income single-family homes, manufactured homes, as well as multifamily properties. The program
targets and provides direct energy efficient improvements to income-qualified and moderate-income
households that are historically under-represented in energy efficiency program participation. The program
also provides members of the community and Corporations the opportunity to donate funds and assist
neighbors in need of home energy efficiency upgrades or improvements. These donations will be additive to
the funding provided through Georgia Power as part of the DSM tariff. Where possible, Georgia Power may
work with community partners and local nonprofits to identify, assess, and implement projects within some of
the state’s most energy burdened neighborhoods. In addition to the energy efficiency improvements offered,
the program includes customer education and awareness campaigns, as well as contractor partnerships,
mentoring and training that can increase market capacity overall. New this cycle is the inclusion of moderate-
income customers and measures for manufactured homes to reach more customers.

Energy Assistance for Savings and Efficiency for Seniors:
Georgia Power’s EASE for Seniors program promotes energy efficiency improvements in existing single-family
and manufactured homes that are owned by income-qualified seniors. The program is designed to reach and
provide direct energy improvements to income-qualified senior households that are historically under-
represented in energy efficiency program participation. EASE for Seniors is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that provides
weatherization and energy efficiency services at no cost for income-qualified seniors. EASE for Seniors works
with community partners and select contractors to identify, assess, and complete energy efficiency measure
improvements.

Commercial Programs
Custom Program:
Georgia Power’s Commercial Custom program provides a platform for comprehensive energy efficiency
projects in existing and new facilities that go beyond discrete measures and common, measure-level efficiency
practices. The Custom program provides incentives for efficiency improvements not included in other Georgia
Power commercial program offerings. All program incentives are based on the calculated energy savings
achieved for each project. The program does not define a specific list of eligible measures and bases
participation on verifiable energy savings resulting from measures or system improvements implemented. Due
to the complexity and variety of measures that could potentially be included, Georgia Power’s Commercial
Custom program requires the applicant to submit calculations using industry-accepted methods for
determining energy savings and appropriate baselines. These savings could be derived from capital
improvements in equipment or from other building upgrades such as retro commissioning (“RCx”).


                                                       2

Prescriptive Program:
Georgia Power’s Commercial Prescriptive program promotes the purchase of eligible high-efficiency
equipment installed at qualifying customer facilities. Incentives offered through this program serve to reduce
the incremental cost to upgrade to high-efficiency equipment over standard efficiency options for Georgia
Power’s new and existing commercial customers. The program includes equipment with a combination of
calculated and deemed savings, provides straightforward and easy participation for customers, and allows for
reduced energy engineering and EM&V costs.

Small Business Energy Savings:
Georgia Power’s Small Business Energy Savings (SBES) program promotes upgrades of eligible high-efficiency
equipment at qualifying customer facilities. A primary objective of the program is to ensure that qualifying
small commercial customers served by Georgia Power receive the information and rebates to make
cost-effective energy-efficiency decisions. The program offers free on-site equipment assessments conducted
by the program implementer for eligible commercial customers, resulting in customized recommendations for
energy-efficient measures that a customer may choose to pursue. If a customer elects to participate, the
selected efficiency measures are coordinated for installation by a qualified contractor chosen by the customer,
with program incentives applied to help reduce the overall project cost.

Georgia Power Company
Certified Demand-Side Management Programs
Q2 2026 Programs Status Report 

In accordance with the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (“Commission”) Final Order of Docket No. 56003, signed July 8, 2025, Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) provides this second quarterly report for activity through June 30, 2026. 


Residential Programs

Behavioral Program: 
Georgia Power’s Residential Behavioral program is designed to encourage customer engagement with home energy management and energy efficiency to reduce energy consumption. The program utilizes Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) data to provide customer-specific information that allows the customer to compare their energy use for the month, and previous months when available, to the consumption of a peer group of homes with similar characteristics including year built, square footage, and heating type, and homes that are considered energy-efficient. The reports also include seasonal and household-appropriate energy savings tips, as well as information on energy efficiency programs. 

Demand Response Program: 
The Residential Demand Response (“Res DR”) program encourages customers to shift electricity use during periods of peak demand; hot summer days, cold winter days, or other times when system capacity is constrained. During demand response events, Georgia Power (or its representative) may manage participants’ home electric load by automatically adjusting eligible smart thermostats. Demand response programs help reduce reliance on higher-cost peaking plants during high-load periods. Res DR provides education, awareness messaging, and financial incentives for customers with qualifying existing smart thermostats who agree to reduce energy use during these events. 


Home Energy Improvement Program: 
Georgia Power’s Home Energy Improvement Program (HEIP) provides financial incentives to support energy efficiency improvements across residential building types. Single-family customers may participate through multiple single-measure options, including bundled home comfort measures that offer increased rebates. Multifamily properties may participate by meeting the required electric kWh reduction thresholds or through select single-measure options, with new construction multifamily properties also eligible for limited measures. The program also includes customer education, outreach, and contractor training to support effective participation. 

Energy Assistance for Savings and Efficiency: 
Georgia Power’s EASE program promotes energy efficiency improvements in existing income-qualified and moderate-income single-family homes, manufactured homes, as well as multifamily properties. The program targets and provides direct energy efficient improvements to income-qualified and moderate-income households that are historically under-represented in energy efficiency program participation. The program also provides members of the community and Corporations the opportunity to donate funds and assist neighbors in need of home energy efficiency upgrades or improvements. These donations will be additive to the funding provided through Georgia Power as part of the DSM tariff. Where possible, Georgia Power may work with community partners and local nonprofits to identify, assess, and implement projects within some of the state’s most energy burdened neighborhoods. In addition to the energy efficiency improvements offered, the program includes customer education and awareness campaigns, as well as contractor partnerships, mentoring and training that can increase market capacity overall. New this cycle is the inclusion of moderate-income customers and measures for manufactured homes to reach more customers.

Energy Assistance for Savings and Efficiency for Seniors:  
Georgia Power’s EASE for Seniors program promotes energy efficiency improvements in existing single-family and manufactured homes that are owned by income-qualified seniors. The program is designed to reach and provide direct energy improvements to income-qualified senior households that are historically under-represented in energy efficiency program participation. EASE for Seniors is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that provides weatherization and energy efficiency services at no cost for income-qualified seniors. EASE for Seniors works with community partners and select contractors to identify, assess, and complete energy efficiency measure improvements.

Commercial Programs
Custom Program: 
Georgia Power’s Commercial Custom program provides a platform for comprehensive energy efficiency projects in existing and new facilities that go beyond discrete measures and common, measure-level efficiency practices. The Custom program provides incentives for efficiency improvements not included in other Georgia Power commercial program offerings. All program incentives are based on the calculated energy savings achieved for each project. The program does not define a specific list of eligible measures and bases participation on verifiable energy savings resulting from measures or system improvements implemented. Due to the complexity and variety of measures that could potentially be included, Georgia Power’s Commercial Custom program requires the applicant to submit calculations using industry-accepted methods for determining energy savings and appropriate baselines. These savings could be derived from capital improvements in equipment or from other building upgrades such as retro commissioning (“RCx”). 


Prescriptive Program: 
Georgia Power’s Commercial Prescriptive program promotes the purchase of eligible high-efficiency equipment installed at qualifying customer facilities. Incentives offered through this program serve to reduce the incremental cost to upgrade to high-efficiency equipment over standard efficiency options for Georgia Power’s new and existing commercial customers. The program includes equipment with a combination of calculated and deemed savings, provides straightforward and easy participation for customers, and allows for reduced energy engineering and EM&V costs. 

Small Business Energy Savings:  
Georgia Power’s Small Business Energy Savings (SBES) program promotes upgrades of eligible highefficiency equipment at qualifying customer facilities. A primary objective of the program is to ensure that qualifying small commercial customers served by Georgia Power receive the information and rebates to make costeffective energyefficiency decisions. The program offers free onsite equipment assessments conducted by the program implementer for eligible commercial customers, resulting in customized recommendations for energyefficient measures that a customer may choose to pursue. If a customer elects to participate, the selected efficiency measures are coordinated for installation by a qualified contractor chosen by the customer, with program incentives applied to help reduce the overall project cost.
