a.Please explain which resources the Company retains all the output as part of the IIC and pool settlement calculations, and explain why it would not sell this generation to the pool at the interchange rate (AIER)?
b.Please explain why this generation is not available for sale to the pool, but is considered to be available to serve RTP customers in the methodology to set the RTP fuel credit.
c.If the Company had excluded the costs and generation from these resources from the RTP fuel credit methodology, would the fuel credit have been higher or lower in each hour?

Response:

Under Section 3.3 of the IIC, hydro and nuclear units are treated as retained resources of the owning company. Because those resources are deemed to serve the owning company’s requirements first, they are not treated as interchange energy sold to the Pool at the AIER.

Conventional hydro and nuclear resources are retained by the owning operating company under the IIC and are deemed to serve that operating company’s retail load obligations before interchange energy transactions are determined. Real time pricing (“RTP”) customers are part of Georgia Power’s retail load obligations and therefore receive the benefit of those retained resources through the RTP fuel credit methodology. 

Excluding the costs and generation associated with these resources would typically increase the resulting RTP fuel credit during hours in which those resources were contributing generation because these resources are typically among the lowest-cost resources included in the methodology. Excluding the resources from the calculation would be inappropriate as these resources contributed to serving all retail customer load obligations including RTP customer load.

===== DKT 56765 STF-JKA-10 PD/STF-JKA-10-7.docx =====
STF-JKA-10-7

Question: 

Refer to the Company’s responses to STF-JKA-7-2 and STF-JKA-8-3.

a.Why are BESS charge and discharge MWhs valued AIER (Interchange Rate) rather than at GPC BOS, TOS, Lambda, or something else?
b.Is BESS charge and discharge costs computed the same way as pumped storge resources for purposes of determining a cost for the fuel balance? If not, please explain.

Response:

Network connected Battery Energy Storage System (“BESS”) value is based on the system marginal cost (AIER) at the time of charge/discharge and is often referred to as “lambda.” Because of the flexibility and uniqueness of the resource, it may be called upon at any time for operational or reliability reasons. Given the primary use of the BESS is to charge during low price hours and discharge during high price hours, the benefit of any BESS activity is shown as a net credit to the overall fuel cost for customers with a corresponding offset to pool purchases (i.e. a portion of the pool purchases are allocated to the charging and discharging of the BESS unit). Therefore, utilizing AIER provides the best measure of the charge/discharge value at the time of operation. It is important to note that the assignment of any value to BESS ultimately does not impact the total fuel costs incurred since any value assigned to charge and discharge, currently at AIER, has to be reflected as an offset to fuel costs elsewhere (i.e. pool purchases) since all  fuel costs generated have already incorporated the benefit of BESS within its dispatch. As a reminder, the BESS units are operated as part of the total system resource mix to minimize total production costs, subject to the constraints of the system. 

Note: “Bottom of stack” (BOS) represents the marginal cost of serving the collective requirements of the operating companies. “Top of stack” (TOS) represents the marginal costs of serving the collective requirements of the operating companies plus any additional opportunity sales. There is no specific “GPC BOS.” AIER is the settlement BOS lambda. 

b.Pumped storage hydro resources are not accounted for using the same methodology as BESS. Pumped storage resources utilize an established weighted-average pond cost methodology, under which energy added to storage is accumulated in the pond balance based on the AIER in the hours of pumping. The generation, when discharged, is then valued based on the existing weighted-average pond cost. Pumped storage resources generally operate on longer charge-discharge cycles, typically pumping during lower-load periods and generating during peak-demand periods.

a.Please explain how off-system sales costs are determined for purposes of deriving a margin that is shared between customers and the Company.

Response:

Off-system sales (also called Opportunity Interchange Energy) are energy transfers used to meet an Operating Company’s opportunity sales obligations. The Opportunity Interchange Energy Rate is based on the variable dispatch cost of the incremental resource(s) supplying the sale (please see Intercompany Interchange Contract (“IIC”) Manual §3.3 found in MFRH-6 Attachment B from the Company’s initial filing in Docket No. 56765).  In calculating this rate, the SCS billing process identifies the incremental generation required when the sale is added (often referred to as “Delta Ps”) to determine which resources contributed. The transaction margin equals the external sale price minus the variable dispatch cost of those resources serving the transaction. Each Operating Company is allocated an applicable share of the sale based on their peak period load ratio and the treatment of any resulting margin is determined by each respective Operating Company.  Georgia Power credits 75% of the energy margin associated with opportunity sales to the fuel clause as a benefit to customers.

Off-system sale costs reflect the variable costs of the incremental resources deemed to supply Opportunity Interchange Energy. Accordingly, as part of incremental dispatch, these costs include marginal replacement fuel, variable O&M, emission allowance replacement, transmission losses, and other energy-related costs that would not otherwise have been incurred. (Collectively, these costs are referred to as “variable dispatch costs.”)  These costs capture the variable cost of serving the opportunity sale, regardless of whether the transaction occurs during peak or off-peak periods. Given that firm transportation (“FT”) costs represent fixed costs, they are not part of the incremental dispatch costs to serve off-system sales. The IIC invoice is produced on the second workday of the month for the prior calendar month. By this time, the vast majority of the input data has been validated and will remain unchanged. After the second workday, SCS continues to validate data with external counterparties for market purchases and sales. There may also be updates to some generation and tie flow data that would impact pool settlement calculations. Finally, certain data from external sources, such as other utility counterparties, is not available by the second workday of the month. Generally, these additional data checkouts are completed around the 20th day of the month. At this point, SCS runs “actual” pool settlement calculations. Any updates to the pool settlement calculations are rolled into a prior month true up that will be part of the following month’s IIC invoice.  “Associated Adjustments” represent adjustments to transactions involving affiliated companies and “Non-Associated Adjustments” represent adjustments to transactions involving non-affiliated companies.
