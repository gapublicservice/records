          JOINT APPLICATION OF GEORGIA INTERFAITH POWER & LIGHT,
               PARK AVENUE BAPTIST CHURCH, TEMPLE SINAI, AND
              UNITARIAN UNIVERSALIST CHURCH OF SAVANNAH FOR
                            LEAVE TO INTERVENE

       Georgia Interfaith Power & Light, and three of its Georgia Power customer members: Park

Avenue Baptist Church, Temple Sinai, and Unitarian Universalist Church of Savannah, through

counsel, apply to the Georgia Public Service Commission for leave to intervene in Docket 57171

pursuant to O.C.G.A. §§ 46-2-59 and 50-13-14 and Commission Rule 515-2-1-.06. In support of

this application, they respectfully show the Commission as follows:

                                               1.
       Per the Commission’s Order in Docket 56765, this docket will review the accounting

methodology by which Georgia Power credits Real Time Pricing (RTP) revenue from large

customers like data centers to fuel costs. Because fuel costs are treated as “pass-through” costs

paid by customers, changes to RTP accounting impacts what other, non-RTP, customers pay on

their power bills.

                                               2.
       This application for leave to intervene is timely filed pursuant to the Commission’s

Procedural and Scheduling Order of July 7, 2026.

                                                3.
       Georgia Interfaith Power & Light is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization headquartered in

Decatur, Georgia. GIPL represents the interests of communities of faith in promoting

environmental justice, energy conservation, energy efficiency, renewable energy, and related

sustainable practices. GIPL engages faith communities in the stewardship of Creation. GIPL is a

growing organization with more than 300 member congregations across the state of Georgia

representing a wide variety of faiths and over 150 congregationally based Green Teams working

to advance GIPL’s objectives. While several of GIPL’s participating congregations are located

throughout the Atlanta metropolitan area, many are located in other areas of the State, including

the Acworth, Adel, Albany, Athens, Augusta, Brunswick, Canton, Carrollton, Clayton, Covington,

Dalton, Darien, Ellijay, Gainesville, Kennesaw, Macon, Newnan, Roswell, Sautee-Nacoochee,

Savannah, Valdosta, Warner Robins, and Waycross areas.

                                                4.
       GIPL has an interest in this proceeding both on its own behalf and on behalf of its members.

Many of GIPL’s participating congregations and members of those congregations purchase

electricity from Georgia Power Company, both for their places of worship and for their individual

residences, including the three member congregations jointly intervening here. Their electricity

consumption and costs for electricity will be affected by the Commission’s actions in this docket

determining who pays for what fuel costs.

                                                5.
       Additionally, GIPL’s core mission is to inspire and equip communities of faith to organize,

implement practical climate solutions, and advocate across Georgia on issues of climate change,

environmental justice, and community resilience. GIPL’s mission is thus directly implicated by

Georgia Power’s accounting here. If RTP revenue is credited to fuel costs in a way that benefits


                                                2

large load customers to the detriment of residential and small business customers, GIPL, along

with its member congregations, will have less to contribute towards achieving their missions. GIPL

intends to address the concerns of both itself and its members regarding how Georgia Power’s fuel

cost and revenue accounting—and Georgia Power and the Commission’s processes—impact both

God’s Creation and the resulting bills that customers, and in particular low-income customers, pay.

GIPL envisions a Georgia where all people live safely in a healthy environment, a stable climate,

and resilient communities. The results of these proceedings will directly impact how much of

GIPL’s member congregations’ limited budgets will be spent on monthly electric bills and how

much may be spent in direct service to their communities. As a result, GIPL and its members have

substantial and vital interests in the outcome of this proceeding and seek to intervene to protect

these interests.

                                                  7.
       Park Avenue Baptist Church is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization headquartered in

Atlanta, Georgia with roots dating back to the 1870s. Park Avenue’s mission is to co-create the

world anew with the God of all possibilities, empowering others to love boldly and serve

generously. For over a decade, Park Avenue has worked closely with GIPL to advance shared

goals regarding caring for God’s creation, environmental stewardship, and environmental justice,

both through GIPL’s Power-Wise program and Green Team membership. As a Green Team

member, Park Avenue creates opportunities for congregational fellowship, working towards

improving the natural habitat of Park Avenue’s land, and engages with a number of other

environmental initiatives within its community.

                                                  8.

       Despite efforts to mitigate its own energy burden, Park Avenue’s Georgia Power bills have

significantly increased in recent years, causing additional financial strain. Park Avenue’s energy


                                                  4

bill remains one of its most significant operational costs, which has required the congregation to

make a number of tradeoffs in its operations. As part of its mission, Park Avenue provides meeting

space and storage, such as an energy-intensive deep freezer, for a number of community groups

that would not otherwise have a place to meet or store food. However, increasing energy bills have

required Park Avenue to reduce offerings in other areas to compensate. Park Avenue has not

directly intervened in any prior proceeding before the Commission, but GIPL has intervened on

its behalf in prior proceedings and adequately represents their interests in more equitable power

bills and cleaner energy solutions before the Commission.

                                                9.

       Temple Sinai is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization headquartered in Sandy Springs,

Georgia with its start dating back to 1968. Temple Sinai is a sacred and welcoming community

that values compassion, kindness, dignity, and respect. For nearly a decade, Temple Sinai has

worked with GIPL as a Green Team member, tying together Temple Sinai’s ethical and religious

obligations to protect the environment as good stewards of the community. For example, Temple

Sinai has made deliberate efforts to transform its property to be more eco-friendly including by

composting and aggressively recycling. Further, Temple Sinai runs computer and technology

recycling drives; teaches children about protecting the environment; and hosts other community

events centered around protecting the environment.

                                               10.

       Despite investing a significant amount of time and money into solar panels and other

energy efficiency measures—many of which GIPL’s Power-Wise program recommended—

Temple Sinai has experienced significantly higher Georgia Power electricity bills in recent years.

In 2019, Temple Sinai replaced all of its lights with energy efficient LEDs. Temple Sinai has also


                                                5

replaced its HVAC units equipment that meets a minimum 17 SEER two-stage standard and

actively monitors and controls the units using computer software. But due to increasing fuel costs

and higher electricity bills, Temple Sinai is forced to reduce money spent on congregation and

community programs that many benefit from. Temple Sinai has not directly intervened in any prior

proceeding before the Commission, but GIPL has intervened on its behalf in prior proceedings and

adequately represents their interests in more equitable power bills and cleaner energy solutions

before the Commission.

                                                11.

       Unitarian Universalist Church of Savannah is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization located

in Savannah, Georgia with roots dating back to the 1820s. UUCS is a welcoming congregation

that centers on love and values interdependence, pluralism, justice, transformation, generosity, and

equity. For almost a decade, UUCS has worked with GIPL to advance shared goals regarding faith,

creation care, and environmental stewardship. As a Green Team member, UUCS has participated

in numerous activities around education and advocacy, including hosting education events focused

on water, climate, and other environmental concerns related to habitat loss and climate change,

Earth Day, and serving as a key coalition member advocating for Savannah’s 2021 100% Clean

Energy Plan.

                                                12.

       Despite efforts to mitigate its own energy burden, UUCS’s Georgia Power bills have

increased, raising UUCS’s operational costs and causing the congregation to dedicate funding to

electrical power bills that it would otherwise use to support its mission. UUCS has not directly

intervened in any prior proceeding before the Commission, but GIPL has intervened on its behalf

in prior proceedings and adequately represents their interests in more equitable power bills and


                                                 6

cleaner energy solutions before the Commission.

                                                 13.

       No other party shares the unique interests of GIPL and its member congregations Park

Avenue, Temple Sinai, and UUCS; and those interests are not adequately represented by other

parties to these proceedings. The applicants’ intervention would not unduly delay the proceedings

or prejudice the rights of other parties, as they intend to jointly participate in the proceedings in a

non-duplicative manner.

                                                 14.

       If this application is granted, GIPL, Park Avenue, Temple Sinai, and UUCS, through

undersigned counsel, intend to participate fully and jointly in this docket, including cross-

examining witnesses, filing direct testimony, and filing proposed orders and/or briefs. GIPL, Park

Avenue, Temple Sinai, and UUCS expect to explore a number of issues in cross-examination and

potential briefing, including but not limited to: what costs the RTP rate covers, how that impacts

other billpayers, and what modifications the Commission may make to ensure RTP revenue is

properly credited to fuel costs.
