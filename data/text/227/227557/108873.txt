ANSWER OF RESPONDENT, VOLTAGRID, LLC

Comes now, VOLTAGRID, LLC, (“Respondent, VOLTAGRID”) Respondent in the above styled docket and pursuant to the provisions of O.C.G.A. § 46-3-8 and Commission Rule 515-2-1-.04 files this Answer to the Petition for Declaratory Judgment and Enforcement Against VoltaGrid, LLC (the “Petition”) brought by SUSTAINABLE NEWTON, SARA and RANDALL VINSON and THEODOSIA and WILLIAM WADE (collectively, PETITIONERS), showing the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”), as follows:
FIRST DEFENSE
PETITIONERS’ Petition fails to state a claim under the Georgia Territorial Electric Service Act., O.C.G.A. § 46-3-1, et seq. (the “Territorial Act”) over which this Commission has jurisdiction with the result that their Petition must be dismissed.
SECOND DEFENSE
Respondent, VOLTAGRID is not an electric supplier as defined in O.C.G.A. § 46-3-3(3), and as a result is not subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction.
THIRD DEFENSE
Respondent, VOLTAGRID has not conducted any retail sale of electricity to any party in Georgia, and as a result has not committed any act that is subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction.
FOURTH DEFENSE
PETITIONERS are not “interested parties” with authority to initiate this Petition under the Territorial Act with the result that their Petition must be dismissed.
FIFTH DEFENSE
PETITIONERS lack standing to pursue the allegations set forth in their Petition with the result that their Petition must be dismissed.
SIXTH DEFENSE
In the event that PETITIONERS are “interested parties,” PETITIONERS’ interests are limited by O.C.G.A. § 46-3-8(c)(1) with the result that their Petition may only assert allegations limited to whether “the service of an electric supplier then serving a premises or exclusively entitled under this part to serve such premises is not adequate or dependable or that such electric supplier’s rates, charges, service rules and regulations, or the application thereof unreasonably discriminate in favor of or against the consumer utilizing such premises, or that an electric supplier is in violation of subsection (b) of Code Section 46-3-11.”  Inasmuch as PETITIONERS do not raise any of these claims in their Petition, it must be dismissed.
SEVENTH DEFENSE
The provisions of the Territorial Act do not apply to nor restrict the ability of any “person” to develop facilities under The Georgia Cogeneration and Distributed Generation Act of 2001, O.C.G.A. § 46-3-50, et seq. (the “CoGen Act”).
