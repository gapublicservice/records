STF-ASR-11-5

Question: 

Refer to the Company’s response to STF ASR 9-10. The Company states that “Actual inventories are not static, and it is common for actual inventory levels to fluctuate above and below targets.” Please provide the Company’s assessment of whether any such variances created reliability risks and identify all mitigation actions implemented in response, including procurement adjustments, rail scheduling changes, and supplemental purchases. Reference the specific months in which such risks were identified.

Response:

There were no instances of reliability risk in 2025. The Company’s coal inventory management strategy is governed by Southern Company Services’ (“SCS”) Fossil Fuel Policy and is designed to ensure system reliability while optimizing supply chain performance by balancing onsite inventories and planned deliveries. Coal stockpiles are maintained on-site to protect against unanticipated demand changes and supply disruptions caused by factors such as weather, transportation issues, or production variability. Each plant operates under defined target, critical, and physical inventory limits that are reviewed annually by Georgia Power and SCS. SCS emphasizes steady, ratable deliveries while allowing inventories to draw down during highburn periods and rebuild during lowburn periods to better accommodate changing burn requirements. To manage risk, SCS continuously evaluates coal burn scenarios and projected inventory levels. When critical situations are anticipated, SCS implements mitigation measures such as planned burn, load curtailment, accelerated or deferred deliveries, use of offsite inventory, or—subject to Georgia Power approval—coal buyouts or resales.  
Please see the Company’s response to STF-ASR-11-3 for details on a prudent and proactive mechanism to manage any misalignment between electricity demand and coal delivery while optimizing the stockpile and maintaining safety and reliability at each plant.

STF-ASR-11-4

Question: 

Refer to STF ASR 9-9 Attachment A. Please provide the accounting basis and a narrative explanation for each reported coal inventory adjustment.

Response:

Many of the adjustments in the referenced attachment are related to aerial survey adjustments. As has been the Company practice for several decades, aerial surveys are conducted quarterly to ensure the coal and limestone stockpile inventory is accounted for in an accurate manner and at sufficient levels to support plant operations. Using a prescribed stockpile compaction density, physical inventory weight is computed and compared to book quantities. The adjustments performed in January, April, July, and October were the results of the aerial survey. The adjustment performed in August was the write-off for the remaining unrecoverable coal upon contract expiration for the offsite storage terminal in Calvert City, Kentucky, which was utilized for Plant Bowen.

STF-ASR-11-3

Question: 

Refer to STF ASR 9-9 Attachment A. Please describe the Company’s process for identifying and investigating burn rate variances, including months exhibiting unusually high or unusually low burn levels. Explain how such variances are evaluated for potential impacts on fuel cost recovery, fuel cost projections, and economic dispatch decisions. Identify the specific months during which variances triggered internal review.

Response:

For coal generation, actual burn compared to target burn is reviewed continually and discussed with Fleet Operations, Coal Services, and other key stakeholders on at least a monthly basis as part of the Company's normal operating process. Accordingly, all months reflected in STF-ASR-9-9 Attachment A were subject to review. 

Factors that can drive variances include weather, load, fuel prices, outages, inventory levels, and reliability requirements, as well as other system operational limitations. Unit Commitment and Real-Time Operations staff continuously review and evaluate all of these variables to dispatch the system in the most economic and reliable way possible to minimize total production costs for the system, as well as fuel costs to the extent possible.

Variances are evaluated to determine whether modifications to coal procurement/delivery schedules, short-term inventory levels, operational forecasts, or fuel cost projections are warranted. The Company does not establish separate economic dispatch decisions based solely on burn target variances; rather, dispatch decisions continue to be made based on prevailing system conditions, reliability requirements, fuel prices, and other operational constraints. Variances are also evaluated to determine whether they may impact forecasted fuel costs included in the annual energy budget process. To the extent actual coal generation differs from budgeted coal generation, its impact to fuel cost recovery (FCR) is evaluated monthly through the FCR variance analysis report compiled by the Company on a monthly basis.

STF-ASR-11-3 Attachments A through L include materials that were reviewed with Fleet Operations, Coal Services, and key stakeholders on at least a monthly basis in 2025.
