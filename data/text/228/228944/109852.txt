STF-PIA-7-2

Question: 

Please refer to STF-JKA-3-12 in Docket No. 56765. Explain the theoretical justification for using the RIM test to inform the fixed and marginal costs that should be recovered from a large load customer.

Response:

Please note, the rate impact measure (“RIM”) test in and of itself does not inform the fixed and marginal costs that should be recovered, but rather verifies that the forecasted revenue to be collected is sufficient to recover the cost incurred to serve the load. The RIM test used by the Company for large load customer offers is conducted using the PRICEM model. This is the same analysis and model the Company uses to calculate RIM for load additions and expansions, and demand-side management (“DSM”) programs. PRICEM is a marginal cost model, developed by Southern Company Services, that estimates changes in revenue requirements and other impacts resulting from load and/or revenue changes. Its inputs align with the AURORA production cost model, drawing on: (1) revenue requirement streams from the Standard Analysis Model (SAM); (2) marginal energy costs from AURORA; and (3) technology cost assumptions. 

The RIM test is a net present value calculation of the total benefits and total costs over the life of the project. As with demand-side management (“DSM”) and other analyses, a RIM calculation result above zero is considered passing, meaning that the revenues sufficiently cover costs, and any positive amount greater than zero represents downward pressure on embedded costs.

STF-PIA-7-3

Question: 

Please refer to STF-JKA-3-12 in Docket No. 56765. How does the RIM test estimate long run marginal cost? If no such estimate is made, how is the Company confident that long run marginal costs are being covered by the large load customer?


Response:

The PRICEM model used for the rate impact measure (“RIM”) test includes 30-year projections of the marginal generation, transmission, and distribution costs that would be incurred to serve incremental load (including incremental load from large load customers). The cost components are reviewed and updated at least annually and capture projected cost increases including inflation expectations over the 30-year analysis.
