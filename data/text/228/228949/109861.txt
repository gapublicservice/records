PETITION TO INTERVENE OF GEORGIA EMC

Georgia EMC hereby petitions the Georgia Public Service Commission for leave to intervene in this docket under O.C.G.A. §§ 50-13-14 & 46-2-59 and Commission Rule 515-2-1-.06.
1.
Georgia EMC is a statewide trade association serving the needs of Georgia’s forty-one electric distribution cooperatives that provide retail electric service in Georgia (“EMCs”).  Georgia EMC is a not-for-profit, member-owned organization controlled by a board of directors elected by its members.  The EMCs provide retail electric service to approximately 5 million Georgians.  The assigned electric service territories of these EMCs comprise over 73% of the landmass of the state.
2.
The interests of Georgia EMC and its member EMCs may be substantially affected by any decisions reached by the Commission in this proceeding. The Petition for Expedited Declaratory Judgment filed in this case asserts that the planned sale of power from VoltaGrid to Serverfarm violates the Georgia Territorial Electric Service Act, O.C.G.A. §§ 46-3-1 through 15 (“Territorial Act”), and the Cogeneration and Distributed Generation Act of 2001, O.C.G.A. §§ 46-3-50 through 56 (“Cogeneration Act”). The Notice of Hearing and Scheduling Order entered in this matter identifies the “Matters at Issue” as including both jurisdictional issues and “[w]hether VoltaGrid’s planned construction and operation of the proposed generating facility, and delivery of the resulting energy to the data center Serverfarm, violates” the Territorial Act and/or Cogeneration Act. The EMCs have significant statutory rights and interests arising under the Territorial Act and the Cogeneration Act. The issues raised in this proceeding implicate the rights and interests of Georgia EMC and its member EMCs, including, without limitation, the interpretation, application, and enforcement of the Territorial Act and Cogeneration Act. 
3.
The interests of Georgia EMC and its member EMCs are not adequately represented by any other party to this proceeding. The participation of Georgia EMC will not unduly delay the proceedings or prejudice the rights of other parties.
4.
Georgia EMC does not presently intend to submit direct testimony in this proceeding.
