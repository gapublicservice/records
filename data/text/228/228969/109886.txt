                              ORDER ON RESPONDENT, VOLTAGRID, LLC’S
                                  MOTION TO STAY PROCEEDINGS

                 This matter comes before the Hearing Officer on respondent VoltaGrid, LLC’s
          (“VoltaGrid”) Motion to Stay (the “Motion”), filed on September 16, 2026, seeking to “stay the
          PSO deadlines and merits proceedings; establish a prompt schedule for resolving VoltaGrid’s
          Motion to Dismiss; and enter a revised merits schedule only if necessary, after the Motion to
          Dismiss has been heard and decided.” No other parties of record have made filings pertaining to
          the Motion. Having considered the Motion, the Hearing Officer finds as follows:

                 On July 13, 2026, Sustainable Newton, Sara and Randall Vinson, and Theodosia and
          William Wade (collectively, “Petitioners”) petitioned the Commission for an expedited
          declaratory judgment that VoltaGrid’s contractual arrangement with Serverfarm, Inc.
          (“Serverfarm”) violates O.C.G.A §§ 46-3-1 through 46-3-15 (“Territorial Act”) and O.C.G.A. §§
          46-3-50 through 46-3-56 (the “Cogen Act”), and for an order directing VoltaGrid to cease and

desist. VoltaGrid answered on August 12, 2026. On August 24, 2026, the Public Service
Commission issued an Assignment to Hearing Officer (the “Assignment”). In addition to assigning
the matter to a Hearing Officer, the Assignment “authorizes the Staff to issue discovery pursuant
to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57(a)” and instructed the Hearing Officer to set a “hearing on the issues set
forth in the Petition and on whether Respondent is providing service or offering to provide service
that is subject to the jurisdiction of the Commission and, if so, what if any action the Commission
should take.” Since the issuance of the Assignment, the Scheduling Order has been set, Staff has
filed its first set of Data Requests, and VoltaGrid has responded to said Data Requests.

        Although Respondent argues that a request for a stay is not extraordinary, neither the
Georgia Administrative Procedure Act 1, nor the Commission’s own rules 2, provide a standard for
the Hearing Officer to consider a stay of proceedings. Instead, the APA authorizes the Hearing
Officer to regulate the course of the hearing, which includes the authority to stay proceedings. 3
VoltaGrid argues that a stay is appropriate “when another threshold determination may materially
affect – or eliminate – the proceedings that would otherwise go forward.” To this point, VoltaGrid
argues that the threshold questions raised in its Motion to Dismiss could dispose of the Petition in
its entirety, and the Commission should ensure that such jurisdictional questions are resolved
before VoltaGrid incurs substantial, irreversible costs defending this proceeding.

        However, as recited above in the Assignment, the Hearing Officer in this case is charged
with more than the adjudication of Petitioners requested relief. As stated in paragraph two of the
Assignment, the Commission has ordered its own investigation into “whether Respondent is
providing service or offering to provide service that is subject to the jurisdiction of the Commission
and, if so, what if any action the Commission should take.” VoltaGrid's Motion to Dismiss raises
three threshold questions. Two of them, whether Petitioners are "interested parties" under
O.C.G.A. § 46-3-13 and whether the Commission may enforce the Cogen Act, would not dispose
of the question the Commission separately referred for hearing, even if resolved in VoltaGrid's
favor. The third, whether VoltaGrid's arrangement is subject to the Commission's jurisdiction
under O.C.G.A. § 46-2-21(b)(5), is substantially the question the Commission itself directed the
