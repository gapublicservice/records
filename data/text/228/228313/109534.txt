COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before October 5, 2026.

Refer to the Direct Testimony of Dr. Ross Beppler, Lee Evans, and Brandon Looney (“Panel Testimony”), p. 9, lines 11-14 and Georgia Power’s response to STF-JKA-10-11 Attachment A in Docket No. 56765.
Please identify which Commission order or orders in the 2010, 1994, and 1992 rate cases referenced on p. 10, lines 11-21 approved the Company’s current method for determining the value of capacity used for the reliability component of the real time pricing (“RTP”) rate.
Please identify all relevant testimony and discovery responses that describe the Commission-approved method for determining the value of capacity used for the reliability component of the RTP rate as referenced in your response to part (a).
Please explain how the decisions described in the value of capacity used for the reliability component of the real time pricing (“RTP”) rate for the years 2023 through 2025 are consistent with the material identified in response to parts (a) and (b). In your response, please describe the specific limitations on the Company’s exercise of discretion in determining the value of capacity for purposes of RTP rates.
Please provide all internal documentation of the decisions to change the basis for the capacity values, and any documentation of alternatives to the changes made.
Please identify which customers, or customer representatives, if any, knew about the change in method for determining capacity value.
The Company defines the “peaker theorem,” on p. 7, lines 7-10 and in STF-JKA-1-7, Docket No. 56765. Please explain how the methods used for the years 2023 through 2025 are consistent with the peaker theorem.
Please identify any other regulatory filings in which the Company files a value of capacity that represents the marginal capital cost of peaking generation, and provide the calculated value for the years 2023 through 2025 as filed. If there are any discrepancies between those values and the values used in the RTP rate, please explain.
Documentation of decision in 2022. Did any customers or groups of customers know the Company changed the method for determining the value of capacity used for the reliability component of the RTP rate? if so, which customers and how did they know?


Please refer to Georgia Power response to STF-PIA-2-9 and STF-JKA-2-28 in Docket No. 56765.
Has the Company, in the past decade, had the to remarket firm transportation (“FT”)? If so:
Please explain how and why the FT capacity was re-marketed, including the specific operating conditions under which a re-marketing decision was made, how the re-marketed capacity was priced, and what the results of the re-marketing activity were from 2023-2025. Please provide supporting data in an excel spreadsheet.
How are FT re-marketing revenues recorded for cost allocation purposes (e.g., as an offset to FT fixed costs)? 
Please identify how Georgia Power’s proposed allocation of new FT would work if there is a net cost remaining for the next FCR period, due to any failure to remarket all new FT costs. Would those remaining costs be recovered from FCR customers only, under a Part B under-recovery mechanism?

With respect to the Panel Testimony, p. 16, lines 2-11, please provide a working copy of the most recent Class Cost of Service Study model. The model should be sufficiently detailed to understand the following:
Allocation of capital, fixed, variable and fuel costs to the revenue requirement for customer classes eligible for RTP rates, at a level of detail sufficient to determine, for example, the firm transportation (FT) embedded cost allocation to those customer classes;
Billing determinants used for customer classes eligible for RTP rates; and
Revenue forecast for customer classes eligible for RTP rates, including the specific revenue forecasts for RTP rates.

With respect to the Panel Testimony, p. 16, lines 2-11, please explain how the Company’s COSS demonstrates that the RTP rate is revenue neutral with respect to rates designed directly from class-allocated embedded costs.

With respect to the Panel Testimony, p. 17, lines 6-10:
Please explain why it is reasonable to allocate FT costs using a different class allocation factor than the Company uses for transmission costs.


Please identify all Georgia Power costs in the most recent Class Cost of Service Study that are, or have been, allocated on a 1-CP basis. 

Refer to Georgia Power’s Response to STF-JKA-2-4(f) in Docket No. 57171, [1-CP]
Please provide the costs in each category by month covering historic period FCR-26, FCR_27, and projected period FCR-27.  Identify which categories have increased in cost between FCR-26 and FCR-27, and by how much.
Please describe how procurement decisions are made for each of the cost categories. For example, when and how are decisions made to procure rail car leases or incremental FT?  Does the Company rely on projected load and projected unit dispatch when determining coal take minimums?
For each procurement decision, identify what load forecast was or would be the basis at the time of decision.
Provide each such existing load forecast in spreadsheet format, separating our components of load growth split by customer class, and further split by RTP, organic data center, and large load demand.
To the extent not provided in the answer to the prior question, provide all load forecasts filed with the Commission beginning with the 2022 IRP, with the same components broken out as in the above question.

Refer to pages 63-65 of the September 1, 2026 hearing transcript. 
Please provide the Company’s analysis estimating the amount of forecasted new FT that would be assigned to the new large load customers versus the existing RTP customers.
