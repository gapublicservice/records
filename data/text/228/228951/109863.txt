 1   Summary

 2   Q   PLEASE SUMMARIZE YOUR FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS.

 3   A   My findings and recommendations are as follows:

 4             •   RTP is a pricing mechanism which allows customers who are willing to take
 5                 considerable price risk to blend higher cost electricity purchased under a
 6                 standard tariff with lower cost energy purchased at “market” prices.
 7                 Although RTP energy is not procured through actual market purchases, it
 8                 reflects GPC’s expected cost to generate electricity and is, thus, a “proxy”
 9                 for market pricing.
10             •   RTP prices vary hourly to reflect the marginal costs to serve incremental
11                 load and, therefore, send effective price signals to RTP customers to
12                 minimize consumption during high-load hours and maximize consumption
13                 during low-load hours. Under this construct, all customers benefit from the
14                 more efficient use of GPC’s generation fleet.
15             •   Over the past seven years prices under both RTP-DA and RTP-DA have
16                 increased, and the hourly prices have become significantly more volatile.
17                 The higher volatility demonstrates how RTP customers are taking much
18                 greater price risk than in the past.
19             •   Despite the concerns of PIA Staff and certain intervenors, incremental FT
20                 costs associated with new very large load customers are not necessarily
21                 related to the issue of how RTP revenues are credited to offset FCR costs.
22                 The only overlap between these two issues is that new very large load
23                 customers may elect to have all or a portion of their load served through
24                 RTP.
25             •   No specific portion of RTP revenues should be credited against FT costs
26                 because FT costs are fully fixed costs that affect GPC’s average cost of
27                 service. In contrast, RTP is a marginal cost-based rate.
28             •   However, RTP sales can contribute to the recovery of these costs when
29                 the system lambda charge, an RTP component which reflects the
30                 replacement cost of fuel, exceeds GPC’s average fuel costs. In addition,
31                 other components of RTP contribute to the recovery of fixed production
32                 operation and maintenance (O&M) expenses.
33             •   PIA Staff and certain intervenors also have concerns with the treatment of
34                 non-dispatchable PPAs in the RTP fuel credit calculation. To address
35                 these concerns, GPC has proposed two possible modifications to the
36                 calculation methodology. First, GPC proposes to exclude both the
37                 generation and costs of these PPAs from the calculation. The second
38                 option would include both the generation and costs of these PPAs in the
39                 calculation.

 8   Overview of Real Time Pricing

 9   Q    WHAT IS REAL TIME PRICING?

10   A    RTP is provided on two Commission-approved rate schedules – RTP Day-Ahead

11        (RTP-DA) and RTP Hour-Ahead (RTP-HA). These rates allow customers who are

12        willing to take considerable price risk to blend higher cost electricity purchased under

13        a standard tariff with lower cost energy purchased at “market” prices. Thus, RTP

14        provides an opportunity to purchase more competitively priced electricity. This is an

15        important tool for manufacturers and other energy-intensive customers who face ever-

16        increasing domestic and global competition.


17   Q    HOW LONG HAVE THE RTP RATES BEEN IN EFFECT?

18   A    RTP rates have been in effect since 1992.


19   Q    HOW DOES RTP WORK?

20   A    Customers taking service on either RTP-DA or RTP-HA are subject to two sets of

21        prices. First, each RTP customer has a defined customer baseline load (CBL), which

22        represents the load that the customer has demonstrated an ability to operate during

23        an economic curtailment. The CBL is priced under a standard Power and Light tariff

24        (i.e., PLS, PLM, PLL, PLH). Second, incremental load (that is, load above the CBL)

25        is subject to hourly prices under the RTP tariff. These prices are communicated to

 1       customers either on a day-ahead basis under RTP-DA (for all 24 hours) or on an hour-

 2       ahead basis under RTP-HA.


 3   Q   HOW ARE REAL TIME PRICES DETERMINED?

 4   A   Real time prices change hourly to reflect the marginal costs to serve incremental load.

 5       The hourly marginal costs include:

 6          •   Incremental energy (fuel, variable O&M, and emission) costs (i.e.,
 7              system lambda);
 8          •   Incremental losses;
 9          •   Marginal production costs (i.e., reliability adder) which apply during
10              periods of tight reserves;
11          •   Marginal transmission costs (i.e., transmission adder) which apply
12              when system demand approaches and exceeds the projected annual
13              system peak; and
14          •   A risk adder to compensate for price forecast errors.

15       Together, these components provide real-time price signals that encourage customers

16       to minimize their electric power and energy consumption during high-price hours and

17       to maximize their electricity consumption during low-price hours. The adders paid by

18       RTP customers lower the remaining costs that GPC must recover from non-RTP

19       customers.   As discussed later, RTP revenues reduced GPC’s system revenue

20       requirements by $            in 2025.

21              Importantly, RTP is not an embedded (or average) cost rate, nor is it a

22       discounted rate. All of the components reflect the marginal cost to provide incremental

23       energy. Accordingly, RTP is a fully cost-based rate.


24   Q   HOW     DOES     RTP    BENEFIT      GEORGIA      POWER        COMPANY     AND     ITS

25       CUSTOMERS?

26   A   RTP is a “win-win-win” proposition. GPC benefits from the demand response provided

 1       by RTP customers and the ability to use its generation fleet more efficiently. RTP

 2       customers benefit directly by their ability to use RTP to lower their average cost of

 3       electricity, which is important to remaining cost competitive and, thus, providing more

 4       sustainable contributions to the state and local economy. Non-RTP customers benefit

 5       from lower rates because a portion of RTP revenues is credited to base rates, thereby

 6       offsetting costs to all customer classes. In addition, they also benefit from increased

 7       reliability through price signals sent to RTP customers and by having a more vibrant

 9   Q   IS RTP A DIFFERENT QUALITY OF SERVICE THAN THAT PROVIDED UNDER

10       OTHER TARIFFS?

11   A   Yes. RTP is a more risky type of service for two reasons. First, customers do not

12       have a set hourly price — the prices vary each hour, and customers do not know the

13       hourly prices until either the day before (RTP-DA) or the hour before (RTP-HA).

14       Second, RTP prices can be extremely volatile. For example, during 2025, hourly

15       prices ranged from less than    ¢ to over      ¢ per kilowatt-hour. In contrast, GPC’s

16       standard tariffs have fixed prices that do not change each hour.

17              Thus, customers with RTP experience ever changing and volatile prices that

18       non-RTP customers do not. For these reasons, RTP is a more risky type of service

19       than the service provided under GPC’s standard tariffs.

 4   Q   WHAT HAS BEEN THE IMPACT OF THE HIGH REAL TIME PRICES?

 5   A   Higher real time prices have resulted in much higher contributions to fixed costs by

 6       RTP customers. The fixed cost contributions consist of:

 7          •   A reliability adder that reflects the marginal cost of generation capacity;
 8          •   A transmission adder that reflects the marginal cost of transmission
 9              capacity; and
10          •   Risk adders ranging from $2 to $3 per megawatt-hour for Day Ahead
11              (DA) and Hour Ahead (HA) pricing.

12       The Reliability and Transmission adders apply when demand approaches and

13       exceeds the projected Southern Company system peak, and the Risk adder applies

14       to all hours. As a result of the higher prices, RTP customers have paid $            in

15       fixed cost contributions from 2019 through 2025.

 2   Q    HAVE PARTIES IDENTIFIED POTENTIAL ISSUES WITH GEORGIA POWER

 3        COMPANY’S ACCOUNTING OF RTP REVENUES?

 4   A    Yes. As previously stated, PIA Staff and certain intervenors have asserted that GPC

 5        is understating the amount of RTP revenues that are credited to GPC’s fuel balance.

 6        Specifically, PIA Staff is particularly concerned that no RTP revenues are being

 7        credited to offset FT costs that are recoverable in the FCR rate. Additionally, PIA Staff

 8        is concerned that the costs incurred under certain non-dispatchable “must-run” PPAs

 9        are also not reflected in the calculation of RTP fuel credit.


10   Q    IS THE RTP FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY THAT GEORGIA POWER COMPANY

11        IS CURRENTLY USING APPROVED BY THE COMMISSION?

12   A    Yes. The current RTP fuel credit methodology was approved by the Commission in

13        GPC’s 2010 rate case, Docket No. 31958.


14   Q    IS THE TREATMENT OF FIRM TRANSMISSION COSTS RELEVANT TO THIS

15        PROCEEDING?

16   A    No. The discussion surrounding FT costs in this proceeding appears to conflate RTP

17        and the pricing for service applicable to new very large load (i.e., 100 megawatts and

18        larger) customers. However, the only overlap is that these customers may elect to

19        have all or a portion of their load served through RTP. In addition to RTP, new very

20        large load customers will pay access charges that reflect GPC’s incremental costs.

21        Except for the fact that RTP is a component of the overall cost to serve new very large

22        load customers, the allocation and recovery of FT costs are entirely unrelated to RTP.

 1       Q       HAS GEORGIA POWER COMPANY ADDRESSED THE FIRM TRANSPORTATION

 2               COST ISSUE?

 3       A       Yes. GPC is proposing that the costs of the new FT capacity be allocated to new very

 4               large load customers based upon their contribution to the winter coincident peak load

 5               relative to organic load growth.2 This proposal would ensure that incremental FT costs

 6               attributable to new very large load customers are fully recovered from these

 7               customers.


 8       Q       SHOULD ANY SPECIFIC PORTION OF RTP REVENUES BE CREDITED AGAINST

 9               FIRM TRANSPORTATION COSTS?

10       A       No. FT costs represent the costs GPC must pay monthly to reserve capacity on

11               natural gas pipelines to ensure sufficient gas supplies are available when necessary.3

12               They are a fully fixed cost that affects GPC’s average cost of service. Thus, FT is an

13               embedded cost that is most appropriately addressed and allocated through a full cost-

14               of-service study. This is in stark contrast to RTP which, as previously discussed, is a

15               marginal cost-based rate.

17               RECOVER FIRM TRANSPORTATION COSTS?

20               replacement cost of fuel.4 In the hours when system lambda exceeds GPC’s average

Summary
qPlease summarize your findings and recommendations.
AMy findings and recommendations are as follows:
RTP is a pricing mechanism which allows customers who are willing to take considerable price risk to blend higher cost electricity purchased under a standard tariff with lower cost energy purchased at “market” prices.  Although RTP energy is not procured through actual market purchases, it reflects GPC’s expected cost to generate electricity and is, thus, a “proxy” for market pricing.
RTP prices vary hourly to reflect the marginal costs to serve incremental load and, therefore, send effective price signals to RTP customers to minimize consumption during high-load hours and maximize consumption during low-load hours.  Under this construct, all customers benefit from the more efficient use of GPC’s generation fleet.
Over the past seven years prices under both RTP-DA and RTP-DA have increased, and the hourly prices have become significantly more volatile.   The higher volatility demonstrates how RTP customers are taking much greater price risk than in the past.  
Despite the concerns of PIA Staff and certain intervenors, incremental FT costs associated with new very large load customers are not necessarily related to the issue of how RTP revenues are credited to offset FCR costs.  The only overlap between these two issues is that new very large load customers may elect to have all or a portion of their load served through RTP.
No specific portion of RTP revenues should be credited against FT costs because FT costs are fully fixed costs that affect GPC’s average cost of service.  In contrast, RTP is a marginal cost-based rate.
However, RTP sales can contribute to the recovery of these costs when the system lambda charge, an RTP component which reflects the replacement cost of fuel, exceeds GPC’s average fuel costs.  In addition, other components of RTP contribute to the recovery of fixed production operation and maintenance (O&M) expenses.
PIA Staff and certain intervenors also have concerns with the treatment of non-dispatchable PPAs in the RTP fuel credit calculation.  To address these concerns, GPC has proposed two possible modifications to the calculation methodology. First, GPC proposes to exclude both the generation and costs of these PPAs from the calculation.  The second option would include both the generation and costs of these PPAs in the calculation.
The revenues derived from RTP sales place downward price pressure on non-RTP rates.  Thus, any increase in the amount of RTP revenues credited to GPC’s fuel account will necessarily reduce the RTP revenues currently used to offset GPC’s base revenue requirements.
Should the Commission find that GPC’s RTP fuel credit calculation methodology should be modified, either of GPC’s proposed changes would adequately address PIA Staff and intervenors’ concerns.
 Overview of Real Time Pricing
qWhat is real time pricing?
ARTP is provided on two Commission-approved rate schedules – RTP Day-Ahead (RTP-DA) and RTP Hour-Ahead (RTP-HA).  These rates allow customers who are willing to take considerable price risk to blend higher cost electricity purchased under a standard tariff with lower cost energy purchased at “market” prices.  Thus, RTP provides an opportunity to purchase more competitively priced electricity.  This is an important tool for manufacturers and other energy-intensive customers who face ever-increasing domestic and global competition.  
qhow long have the RTP rates been in effect?
ARTP rates have been in effect since 1992.  
qhow does rtp work? 
ACustomers taking service on either RTP-DA or RTP-HA are subject to two sets of prices.  First, each RTP customer has a defined customer baseline load (CBL), which represents the load that the customer has demonstrated an ability to operate during an economic curtailment.  The CBL is priced under a standard Power and Light tariff (i.e., PLS, PLM, PLL, PLH).  Second, incremental load (that is, load above the CBL) is subject to hourly prices under the RTP tariff.  These prices are communicated to
customers either on a day-ahead basis under RTP-DA (for all 24 hours) or on an hour-ahead basis under RTP-HA.  
qhow are real time prices determined?
AReal time prices change hourly to reflect the marginal costs to serve incremental load.  The hourly marginal costs include:
Incremental energy (fuel, variable O&M, and emission) costs (i.e., system lambda); 
Incremental losses;
Marginal production costs (i.e., reliability adder) which apply during periods of tight reserves; 
Marginal transmission costs (i.e., transmission adder) which apply when system demand approaches and exceeds the projected annual system peak; and
A risk adder to compensate for price forecast errors.
Together, these components provide real-time price signals that encourage customers to minimize their electric power and energy consumption during high-price hours and to maximize their electricity consumption during low-price hours.  The adders paid by RTP customers lower the remaining costs that GPC must recover from non-RTP customers.  As discussed later, RTP revenues reduced GPC’s system revenue requirements by $________ in 2025.  
Importantly, RTP is not an embedded (or average) cost rate, nor is it a discounted rate.   All of the components reflect the marginal cost to provide incremental energy.  Accordingly, RTP is a fully cost-based rate.  
qhow does rtp benefit georgia power company and its customers?
ARTP is a “win-win-win” proposition.  GPC benefits from the demand response provided
by RTP customers and the ability to use its generation fleet more efficiently.  RTP customers benefit directly by their ability to use RTP to lower their average cost of electricity, which is important to remaining cost competitive and, thus, providing more sustainable contributions to the state and local economy.  Non-RTP customers benefit from lower rates because a portion of RTP revenues is credited to base rates, thereby offsetting costs to all customer classes.  In addition, they also benefit from increased reliability through price signals sent to RTP customers and by having a more vibrant and sustainable economy.  
qIs RTP a different quality of service than that provided under other tariffs?
AYes.  RTP is a more risky type of service for two reasons.  First, customers do not have a set hourly price — the prices vary each hour, and customers do not know the hourly prices until either the day before (RTP-DA) or the hour before (RTP-HA).  Second, RTP prices can be extremely volatile.  For example, during 2025, hourly prices ranged from less than __¢ to over ___¢ per kilowatt-hour.  In contrast, GPC’s standard tariffs have fixed prices that do not change each hour.  
Thus, customers with RTP experience ever changing and volatile prices that  non-RTP customers do not.  For these reasons, RTP is a more risky type of service than the service provided under GPC’s standard tariffs.

qHave parties identified potential issues with Georgia power COmpany’s accounting of RTP revenues?
AYes.  As previously stated, PIA Staff and certain intervenors have asserted that GPC is understating the amount of RTP revenues that are credited to GPC’s fuel balance.  Specifically, PIA Staff is particularly concerned that no RTP revenues are being credited to offset FT costs that are recoverable in the FCR rate.  Additionally, PIA Staff is concerned that the costs incurred under certain non-dispatchable “must-run” PPAs are also not reflected in the calculation of RTP fuel credit.  
qIs the RTP fuel credit methodology that georgia power Company is currently using approved by the commission?
AYes.  The current RTP fuel credit methodology was approved by the Commission in GPC’s 2010 rate case, Docket No. 31958.  
qis the treatment of firm transmission costs relevant to this proceeding?
ANo.  The discussion surrounding FT costs in this proceeding appears to conflate RTP and the pricing for service applicable to new very large load (i.e., 100 megawatts and larger) customers.  However, the only overlap is that these customers may elect to have all or a portion of their load served through RTP.  In addition to RTP, new very large load customers will pay access charges that reflect GPC’s incremental costs.  Except for the fact that RTP is a component of the overall cost to serve new very large load customers, the allocation and recovery of FT costs are entirely unrelated to RTP.
qhas Georgia Power COmpany addressed the firm transportation cost issue?
AYes.  GPC is proposing that the costs of the new FT capacity be allocated to new very large load customers based upon their contribution to the winter coincident peak load relative to organic load growth.  This proposal would ensure that incremental FT costs attributable to new very large load customers are fully recovered from these customers.  
qshould any specific portion of RTP revenues be credited against firm transportation costs?
ANo.  FT costs represent the costs GPC must pay monthly to reserve capacity on natural gas pipelines to ensure sufficient gas supplies are available when necessary.  They are a fully fixed cost that affects GPC’s average cost of service.  Thus, FT is an embedded cost that is most appropriately addressed and allocated through a full cost-of-service study.  This is in stark contrast to RTP which, as previously discussed, is a marginal cost-based rate.  
qdoes this mean that RTP sales contribute no revenues to recover firm transportation costs?
ANo.  As previously discussed, and as stated in GPC’s direct testimony, among the components of the hourly real time price is a system lambda charge which reflects the replacement cost of fuel.  In the hours when system lambda exceeds GPC’s average
fuel costs, RTP sales provide a contribution to the recovery of fixed production O&M expense, which also includes FT.  Thus, although RTP incremental load is not served through a fully embedded rate, it contributes to GPC’s fixed costs.

This amount represents a significant increase from the RTP revenues that reduced non-RTP base revenues in GPC’s 2022 rate case, Docket No. 44280, which totaled $214 million.

AYes.  GPC proposes two options to address this concern.  First, GPC proposes excluding both the generation and costs of these PPAs from the RTP fuel credit calculation.  In the alternative, GPC proposes to include both the generation and costs of these PPAs in the calculation.
