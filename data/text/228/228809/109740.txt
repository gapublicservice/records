RESPONDENT, VOLTAGRID, LLC’S
MOTION TO STAY PROCEEDINGS

Comes now VOLTAGRID, LLC (“VoltaGrid”), Respondent in the above styled docket, and moves the Hearing Officer to stay the deadlines and proceedings established by the Procedural and Scheduling Order (the “PSO”) pending a ruling on VoltaGrid’s Motion to Dismiss, which was filed concurrently with this Motion to Stay. In support of this motion, VoltaGrid shows the Commission as follows:
BACKGROUND
On July 13, 2026, Sustainable Newton, Sara and Randall Vinson, and Theodosia and William Wade (collectively, “Petitioners”) petitioned the Commission for an expedited declaratory judgment that VoltaGrid’s contractual arrangement with Serverfarm, Inc. (“Serverfarm”) violates the Territorial Act and the Cogen Act, and for an order directing VoltaGrid to cease and desist. VoltaGrid answered on August 12, 2026. The docket was assigned to Hearing Officer Chris Collado on August 24, 2026, and the PSO followed, directing that witnesses be produced, testimony pre-filed, and a hearing held — all within a condensed three-month span. 
VoltaGrid has moved to dismiss the Petition because it exceeds the Commission’s jurisdiction and Petitioners lack standing under the Territorial Act to challenge VoltaGrid and Serverfarm’s private contractual arrangement. As explained below, the Petition raises novel questions that test the outer limits of the Commission’s authority. These threshold jurisdictional questions, if resolved in VoltaGrid’s favor, would dispose of the Petition completely, and they should be answered before the parties expend substantial time and resources on hearing preparation. Accordingly, VoltaGrid respectfully requests that all deadlines in the PSO be stayed pending resolution of the Motion to Dismiss.
Argument
A Stay Pending Resolution of the Motion to Dismiss Is an Ordinary and Appropriate Exercise of the Hearing Officer’s Case Management Authority
The requested stay is not extraordinary; it is an ordinary and commonly used case management tool. The Administrative Procedure Act expressly authorizes the agency or hearing officer to regulate the course of a contested-case hearing, fix filing deadlines, and dismiss for “lack of agency jurisdiction over the subject matter or parties,” or “any other ground.” Temporarily suspending merits deadlines until a potentially dispositive motion is resolved is a direct and practical exercise of that authority.
Georgia courts likewise recognize broad authority to stay proceedings in the interest of sound case management, explaining that such stays are a familiar and prudent case management measure when another threshold determination may materially affect — or eliminate — the proceedings that would otherwise go forward. 
 Those considerations strongly favor a stay here. The Motion to Dismiss presents threshold questions that go to the heart of the Commission’s jurisdiction and could dispose of the Petition in its entirety. Meanwhile, the PSO requires VoltaGrid to identify witnesses by October 1, 2026, file written direct testimony by October 9, 2026, file rebuttal testimony by October 30, 2026, and prepare for a hearing — all before the Motion to Dismiss can be fully briefed, much less decided. Requiring the parties and the Commission Staff to undertake those merits-related tasks before the Motion to Dismiss is decided creates a substantial risk their work will prove unnecessary.
More, the requested stay is also limited. Proceedings related to VoltaGrid’s Motion to Dismiss will continue. And if any part of the Petition remains once that motion is resolved, the Hearing Officer may establish a revised schedule providing for the prompt resolution of any remaining claims. This preserves the parties’ ability to litigate any remaining issues, while avoiding potentially unnecessary testimony, discovery, and hearing preparation for a proceeding that will have been “a nullity” if the Petition is dismissed.
The Commission Should Ensure It Has Jurisdiction Before VoltaGrid Incurs Substantial, Irreversible Costs Defending This Proceeding 
Petitioners’ challenge departs from the kinds of matters the Commission ordinarily hears. Neither the Petitioners nor the Respondent is a public utility company or otherwise provides a public service. This is not a dispute between electric suppliers over the right to serve a premise, nor a customer complaint about the adequacy or cost of service. Instead, Petitioners ask the Commission to decide novel legal questions bearing on the scope of its own authority: whether a behind-the-meter facility serving a single on-site customer under a private contract is “furnishing service to the public” and subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction under O.C.G.A. § 46-2-21(b)(5); whether the Commission may enforce the Cogen Act, which it appears never to have enforced in this manner and which Georgia law does not clearly authorize it to enforce; and whether citizens with no stake in an industrial facility or the source of its electric service qualify as “interested parties” under O.C.G.A. § 46-3-13. 
In its Motion to Dismiss, VoltaGrid contends the answer to each of these questions is no and the Petition should be dismissed. However, the expedited deadlines in the PSO make it unlikely the Motion to Dismiss will be decided before most, if not all, hearing preparation must be completed. Absent a stay, VoltaGrid will be compelled to make substantial investments — in attorneys’ fees, employee hours, and potentially expert witness expenses — for a hearing that may never happen or prove unnecessary if dismissal is ultimately granted. On the other hand, a limited stay will guarantee the parties appropriate time to brief, and the Commission staff appropriate time to consider and decide, the jurisdictional arguments VoltaGrid raises. 
Petitioners Will Not Be Prejudiced or Otherwise Harmed by the Requested Stay
As alleged in the Petition, Petitioners brought this challenge to vindicate their interests in protecting the natural environment and avoiding the human health and climate impacts associated with air pollution. Those do not fall within the zone of interests protected by the Territorial Act, but in any event, they will not be harmed by a stay of the proceedings. VoltaGrid has not finished construction of its Conversion Assets and is not legally authorized to do so until an air quality permit is granted by the Georgia Environmental Protection Division. That permit is also required before VoltaGrid can operate any natural gas generators that comprise the Conversion Assets. As such, no air emissions have occurred from the Conversion Assets, and none can occur until the permit issues, construction is completed, and natural gas is connected to the Conversion Assets. Therefore, a stay can be granted and the Motion to Dismiss decided before any of the harms Petitioners complain of would reasonably be expected to materialize — if they ever do. 
Nor would a stay prejudice Petitioners’ legal claims should the Commission determine it does have jurisdiction over VoltaGrid and its private contract with Serverfarm. While Petitioners’ challenge is a preemptive one, before a single kilowatt has ever been generated using the Conversion Assets, the merits of their Territorial Act and Cogen Act claims do not turn on whether or when generation actually commences. Thus, the Commission could continue to hear this case at any time. 
CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons, VoltaGrid respectfully requests that the Hearing Officer stay the PSO deadlines and merits proceedings; establish a prompt schedule for resolving VoltaGrid’s Motion to Dismiss; and enter a revised merits schedule only if necessary, after the Motion to Dismiss has been heard and decided.
