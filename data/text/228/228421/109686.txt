Please describe in detail any basis for VoltaGrid’s dispute, if any, of the assertion that VoltaGrid is an “electric power company”

RESPONSE:VoltaGrid objects to Data Request PIA-1-18 on the grounds that the term “electric power company” is vague and ambiguous with the result that VoltaGrid cannot reasonably formulate a response thereto.  Subject thereto, VoltaGrid shows that the term “electric power company” is not found in the Territorial Act which forms the basis of the Petitioners’ allegations.  The Petition asserts a claim to enforce the Territorial Act which defines and applies to an “electric supplier” as “any electric light and power company subject to regulation by the commission, any electric membership corporation furnishing retail service in this state, and any municipality which furnishes such service within this state.”  (O.C.G.A. § 46-3-3(3)).  The term “electric utility” is defined in O.C.G.A. § 46-1-1(4) as “any retail supplier of electricity whose rates are fixed by the commission.”  VoltaGrid denies that it falls within the definition of “electric supplier” in the Territorial Act.  VoltaGrid instead produces electricity on behalf of its customer, in quantities determined by the customer, to be dispatched as needed by the customer.

In VoltaGrid’s May 20, 2026, letter to Georgia’s Environmental Protection Division, it asserted that its “VoltaGrid ATL-1 facility may provide capacity for other similarly qualified large load facilities.”
