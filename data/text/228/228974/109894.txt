STF-NU1-3-1

Question: 
Regarding proposed uprate ZEC program, please explain whether the Company is proposing a standalone, distinct program specific to nuclear uprates, or if this represents an expansion of the existing Flex-REC program.  Compare and Contrast the proposed program to the Flex-REC program and identify limitations with the Flex-REC program.
Response:
The Nuclear Uprate Subscription program is proposed to be a standalone program and not an expansion of the Flex-REC program, which is a program that offers access to attributes from renewable energy resources. There are several differences between the proposed Nuclear Uprate Subscription program and the Flex-REC program listed below.

The Nuclear Uprate Subscription program allows for a 25-year term, which permits the customer and Georgia Power to lock in long-term fixed prices for the Zero Emissions Credits (ZECs). In contrast, the Flex-REC program only allows for a maximum 3-year term and is based on market prices, which can fluctuate over time.
The Nuclear Uprate Subscription program allows for hourly matching of ZEC production and customer energy consumption since the ZECs are created by baseload nuclear units. Whereas, the Flex-REC program only procures solar RECs produced during daylight hours, which do not necessarily align with the customer’s energy consumption.  The customer interested in participating in the ZEC program is seeking to support resources that produce clean energy that aligns with their energy use.
The revenue from the Nuclear Uprate Subscription program will reduce rates for customers while the Flex-REC program is simply a pass-through for the actual costs to procure the RECs. Additionally, a portion of the revenue from the Nuclear Uprate Subscription program will be realized during the construction period to offset some of the cost of the uprates, preceding the delivery of ZECs. The Flex REC program bills monthly as RECs are delivered.

AVAILABILITY:
Available throughout Georgia Power Company’s service area.
APPLICABILITY:
Applicable to Commercial and Industrial customers, as those terms are defined in the Georgia Power Company Rules, Regulations and Rate Schedules for Electric Service, that are adding new electric load of 100 megawatts (MW) or greater before the commercial operation date (“COD”) of the applicable uprate project at a single premises and subject to the contracted minimum bill pursuant to General Rule A.1. The Company will accept subscriptions from eligible customers on a first-come, first-served basis until the capacity being made available for subscription by the nuclear uprate projects is fully subscribed.
NU-1 will be offered periodically, as determined by the Company, to eligible customers.
DESCRIPTION:
The NU-1 tariff enables customers to subscribe to the carbon free attributes of nuclear uprates at existing, certified nuclear facilities (Nuclear Uprate Portfolio) within the Georgia Power fleet. Each participating customer will pay a Program Enrollment Contribution (PEC) and a NU-1 tariff Monthly Subscription Payment that will be separate and in addition to the cost of electric service under the customer’s then-existing retail electric service tariff. In exchange for the Monthly Subscription Payment, Georgia Power will retire on behalf of each participating customer zero emission credits (ZECs) in an amount based on the customer’s subscription level and the corresponding output of the Nuclear Uprate Portfolio, measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh). The PEC amount and price of ZECs will be defined in the customer agreement. The energy produced by the Nuclear Uprate Portfolio will be transmitted onto Georgia Power’s electric system and will not be distributed directly to participating customers. Customers subscribing to the NU-1 tariff will continue to be served on a network basis from all energy resources generating into or otherwise supplying the Company’s electric system.
SUBSCRIPTION LEVEL:
