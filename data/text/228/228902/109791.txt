STF-JKA-4-4

Question: 

Refer to the Company’s Direct Testimony of Dr. Ross Beppler, Lee Evans, and Brandon Looney, p. 17, lines 6-10.

Would the Company’s proposal using a winter 1-CP allocation of FT costs be used to allocate all FT costs for every month that FT costs are incurred during the year? Please explain.
How would the Company allocate FT costs between the base and fuel revenues? Would the allocation be done on a historic basis with non-RTP customers responsible for the monthly FT cost until the winter peak month occurs? Please explain.
Would the Company differentiate between winter peaking generating capacity with fuel oil back-up versus generating capacity that relies on natural gas FT during the winter season? Please explain.
How would the Company differentiate between organic growth and large load demand growth for allocation purposes? What metrics, time periods, or rate schedules would be contemplated? Please explain.
Would the Company assign the “large load demand growth” FT costs to a specific rate schedule (e.g. RTP-HA, RTP-DA, TOU-SC-MAC)? Please explain.

Response:

Yes, the Company proposes to allocate the total annual firm transportation (“FT”) costs on a winter peak demand basis, as the winter peak drives the need to procure FT to support reliable operation of the unit. The payment mechanism (monthly fixed payment) is not a reflection of the cost driver. 

Please see the Company’s response to hearing request STF-1-1. The Company proposes to determine the fixed amount of annual revenues on a monthly basis that need to be moved from base to fuel based on the identification of new FT costs and the allocation of those costs to large load based on the forecast filed in the 2029-2031 All-Source Certification in Docket No. 56298. The winter peak demand allocator will be used for new FT costs incurred throughout the year. The ultimate amounts to be moved from base to fuel will be determined by the Commission in this proceeding. 

Yes, the Company would differentiate between resources for which FT is procured and those for which new FT costs are not incurred. The fuel assurance costs associated with peaking generation capacity with fuel oil back-up are captured in the cost and capacity assumptions of those resources. Since FT costs are not incurred to support those resources, there is no misalignment in revenue recording. 

Please see the response to Hearing Request STF-1-1. The Company will base the allocation on the share of load growth that drove the resource additions. For FT costs associated with new generation resources identified in the FCR-27 Budget, the Budget 2026 forecast, which supported the 2029-2031 All Source Resource Certification filing, serves as the basis for the allocation. 

The revenues that are moved from base to fuel to align the costs and revenues associated with large load demand would not be assigned to a specific rate, as the FCR rate is applied consistently across all applicable base rates.

STF-JKA-4-3

Question: 

Regarding the Company’s Direct Testimony related to new FT costs

The Company’s Direct testimony at p. 14 l. 10 states, “The Company is distinguishing new FT from existing FT in recognition that the growth of large load customers is a primary driver of new FT costs and they may need to be treated differently. As with the non- dispatchable PPAs, existing FT costs are not driven by large load growth.” (emphasis added) It is true that existing FT costs are not driven by new large load growth, but does the Company believe existing FT costs should be exclusively assigned to non-RTP customers, despite collecting revenues for fixed FT from RTP customers? Please explain.
The Company’s Direct Testimony at p. 16, lines 20-22 states, “The Company proposes that the costs of new FT attributable to large load customers be fully recovered from those customers and not borne by non-large load customers in the FCR clause.” Why didn’t the Company propose that the costs of existing FT attributable to existing RTP customers be fully recovered from those customers and not borne by non-RTP customers?

Response:

Existing firm transportation (“FT”) costs are not exclusively assigned to non-real time pricing (“RTP”) customers. RTP customers pay the embedded fuel rate for the customer baseline load (“CBL”) portion of their load. Additionally, as the Company has discussed, these costs are collected from RTP customers in total. RTP incremental load also contributes to fixed costs. Given the 16-year history with the existing RTP fuel credit methodology, which has worked for the 2,500 existing RTP customers, the lack of significant FT acquisition over that historical period, and that standard RTP incremental load was not a driver of existing FT costs, the Company believes the existing framework is appropriate. 

The costs of existing FT are not marginal costs and thus should not be recovered from a marginal cost-based rate such as RTP. RTP incremental load makes a contribution to fixed costs but does not pay a fully embedded rate.

STF-JKA-4-5

Question: 

Refer to the Company’s Direct Testimony of Dr. Ross Beppler, Lee Evans, and Brandon Looney, p. 18, lines 11-13.

Please confirm that the RTP fuel credit under this second option would still be performed hourly and still rely on marginal fuel price (e.g. spot commodity gas pricing) for dispatchable technologies (e.g. natural gas combined cycles)
Please confirm that the RTP fuel credit under this second option would still be based on an average of GPC generating units and purchases.
Please confirm that the RTP fuel credit under this second option would fluctuate with market pricing dynamics. For example, please confirm that RTP fuel credit would rise when system lambda rises, and RTP fuel credit would fall when system lambda falls. If not, explain why not.

Response:

Yes, the calculation would still be performed hourly. Under the second option, the marginal replacement fuel cost for dispatchable units would continue to be included consistent with the current methodology. 

Yes, the second option would perform an hourly production weighted average cost of all Georgia Power units, including the fixed cost associated with non-dispatchable PPAs, and Georgia Power’s share of pool sales or purchases. 

Yes, the RTP fuel credit would continue to fluctuate hour-to-hour based on changes in the output of units running to serve load and the marginal replacement costs for those units. Under the second option, the RTP fuel credit would be less correlated with changes in lambda than with the first option because fixed costs from non-dispatchable PPAs would be included in the calculation.
