Q.WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF YOUR TESTIMONY?
A.In this testimony, we present Staff’s analysis and recommendations concerning Georgia Power Company’s (“Georgia Power” or “the Company”) Real Time Pricing (“RTP”) Fuel Credit Methodology.
Q.WHY IS THE DEVELOPMENT OF A REAL TIME PRICING FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY NECESSARY?
A.Non-RTP customers, like residential and small commercial customers, are subject to separate rates to collect base revenues and fuel revenues. RTP sales are based on a single rate. RTP revenues must be allocated between base revenues and fuel revenues. The RTP Fuel Credit Methodology is used by the Company to calculate how much RTP revenue to allocate to fuel revenues. The remainder of RTP revenues are allocated to base revenues. 
Q.WHAT GUIDELINES DID STAFF USE IN EVALUATING GEORGIA POWER’S RTP FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY?
A.Staff had three guidelines. The first guideline is that fuel related costs should correspondingly be matched with fuel revenues to cover those costs. The second is that costs recovered through the fuel clause should be recovered from all customers, and the third is that costs recovered from fuel revenues should be allocated based on energy usage (MWhs). 
Q.IS GEORGIA POWER’S RTP FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY CONSISTENT WITH THE THREE GUIDELINES IDENTIFIED ABOVE?
A.No. The Company’s treatment of certain costs in its RTP Fuel Credit Methodology is not consistent with the three guidelines.
Q.WHAT IS STAFF’S RECOMMENDED RATEMAKING TREATMENT OF THESE CERTAIN COSTS?
A.If these costs are to be recovered from fuel revenues, then the ratemaking treatment should be consistent with the three guidelines listed above. Otherwise, these costs should be recovered from base revenues and not fuel revenues.
Q.WHICH COSTS ARE INCONSISTENT WITH STAFF’S THREE GUIDELINES?
A.The Company does not include the following costs in the RTP Fuel Credit Methodology calculation. 
•Natural gas firm transportation
•Natural gas storage
•Natural gas hedging gains/losses
•Natural gas gains/losses on resales
•Coal quality penalties and premiums
•Coal rail car leases
•Coal rail car shortfall/liquidated damages costs
•Start-up costs for Purchased Power Agreements (PPAs)
•Energy costs associated with non-dispatchable PPAs (Solar, Wind, Biomass, and Qualifying Facilities)
•FCR over/under-balance carrying costs
•Income Qualified Discount (IQD) 
•Non-subscription renewables program expenses
•Corporate overhead and labor 
Omitting these costs decreases the RTP revenues allocated to fuel revenues and results in non-RTP customers absorbing all these costs. This is inconsistent with Staff’s guideline that costs recovered through the fuel clause should be recovered from all customers.
Q.WOULD IT BE REASONABLE TO INCLUDE ALL OF THESE COSTS IN THE  COMPANY’S CALCULATION OF RTP REVENUES CREDITED TO FUEL REVENUES?
A.Yes; however, Staff does not necessarily expect that all these costs be included in the RTP Fuel Credit Methodology calculation as there are policy and cost causation arguments that suggest some of these costs could be omitted when calculating the RTP revenues credited to the fuel revenues.

Q.PROVIDE A BRIEF OVERVIEW OF THE COMPANY’S RTP TARIFF AND REVENUES.
A.RTP customers may be charged based on a two-part rate structure. Each RTP customer may have a Customer Baseline Load (“CBL”), for which the customer is charged based on standard tariffs. For energy usage above the CBL, the customers pay an hourly RTP price that varies based on market conditions.  If the customer’s actual usage falls below its CBL, the customer receives a credit based on the RTP price for the amount of usage below the CBL. 
Q.HOW ARE RTP REVENUES ASSIGNED TO FUEL REVENUES? 
A.The Company’s allocation of RTP revenues to fuel revenues is referred to as the RTP Fuel credit.  In the 2010 rate case, the Company proposed a change to the RTP fuel credit from the prior methodology that had been in use at the time:
[T]he Company is also proposing to change its accounting methodology for crediting the Fuel Cost Recovery (“FCR”) account from RTP usage.  At present RTP credits the fuel account at current FCR rates.  The Company is proposing to use an average hourly marginal replacement fuel cost to credit the fuel account. This average hourly marginal fuel cost will be based on all Georgia Power generating units running in each hour plus power pool purchases or deliveries in each hour.
Despite the 2010 testimony stating that the accounting methodology for crediting the FCR account from RTP usage will be based on all Georgia Power generating units running in each hour plus power pool purchases or deliveries in each hour, the Company’s RTP fuel crediting methodology does not account for all Company generating unit and purchase fuel costs that are charged to the fuel cost recovery account.

Q.HOW IS THE REMAINDER OF YOUR TESTIMONY ORGANIZED?

Take or Pay Contracts
Q.EXPLAIN STAFF’S CONCERN REGARDING NON-DISPATCHABLE PPA CONTRACTS? 
A.Staff is concerned the current RTP Fuel Credit Methodology does not include the costs of non-dispatchable PPA contracts, also referred to as Take or Pay contracts, in the calculation of RTP revenues credited to the fuel revenues. This results in less RTP revenue being credited to fuel revenues. The Company recognized this is a problem in this proceeding, and in testimony recommended either excluding both the energy and the costs of non-dispatchable PPAs from the RTP Fuel Credit calculation (Option 1) or including both the energy and the costs associated with non-dispatchable PPAs in the calculation (Option 2). 
Q.WHAT IS STAFF’S RECOMMENDATION? 
A.Staff recommends Option 2, which includes both the energy (MWhs) and costs (dollars) associated with all non-dispatchable PPAs in the Fuel Credit Methodology. This is consistent with the 2010 rate case proposal, which assumed that all Georgia power unit and purchase costs would be included in the calculation. 
Option 1, on the other hand, would exclude the costs and energy of all non-dispatchable PPAs, which would not be consistent with the 2010 rate case protocol. Option 2 treats RTP sales in a consistent manner with non-RTP sales and is the most appropriate way to allocate RTP revenues to fuel revenues considering the hourly cost of all generation and purchases required to serve that load. 
Q.WHY DOES STAFF OPPOSE THE COMPANY’S OPTION 1?
A.Option 1 is inconsistent with Staff’s guidelines. All costs should be included when calculating the amount of RTP revenue to allocate to fuel revenues. Option 1 omits non-dispatchable costs from the calculation when the energy from these non-dispatchable resources serve all customers.
Q.WHY DID THE COMPANY FAVOR OPTION 1?
A.The Company testified that Option 1 was in line with the Commission’s original intent for marginal fuel cost recovery. The Company also stated this in its direct testimony stating:
The first option is to exclude both the costs and the output from non-dispatchable PPAs in the RTP fuel credit calculation. This is consistent with an approach that the RTP fuel credit should be derived from marginal replacement fuel costs.

In the 2010 rate case, the Commission approved Georgia Power’s proposal to record RTP fuel revenues based on the average marginal replacement fuel costs (rather than the embedded FCR rate) on an hourly basis. This method appropriately records more RTP revenues to fuel when marginal fuel prices are high and less when they are low, which mirrors the concepts behind the RTP rates and better tracks the RTP revenues collected with the marginal fuel costs being incurred. 
Q.DOES THE COMPANY’S CURRENT RTP FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY USE AN ACCURATE MARGINAL PRICE TO CALCULATE FUEL CREDIT?
A.No. Had the Company wanted to use an accurate “marginal” cost RTP Fuel Cost Credit methodology, the Company should have proposed the associated interchange energy rate (“AIER”) for use as the RTP fuel credit methodology. The AIER is widely used by the Company to determine marginal cost in many other circumstances. The Company uses the AIER rate to price energy transactions between affiliates, to price the value of battery charge and discharge on the system, and the Company has proposed use of the AIER rate to price certain customer programs, such as the Clean and Renewable Energy Subscription Customer Identified Resources (“CARES CIR”) program.  Given the wide application of AIER by the Company it would be reasonable to use AIER to calculate the amount of RTP revenue to allocate to fuel revenues.

A.Yes. The Company provided settlement data for 2025, which showed that approximately 9.8 million MWh of non-dispatchable generation was included in the RTP fuel credit calculation, while the Company reported that $486 million in associated costs were excluded from the RTP fuel credit calculation. By including MWhs and omitting dollars from take-or-pay contracts in RTP Fuel Credit calculation the Company unreasonably suppressed the amount RTP revenues credited to fuel revenues.

Natural Gas Firm Transportation and Storage
Q.WHAT IS STAFF’S CONCERN REGARDING NATURAL GAS FT COSTS AND NATURAL GAS STORAGE COSTS IN THE RTP FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY?
A.Natural gas FT and storage costs are fixed costs the Company pays to ensure natural gas is deliverable via various pipelines and from storage facilities to ensure generating unit reliability.  These costs are labeled “fixed” because they generally do not change with the volume of natural gas consumed to generate energy. Staff’s investigation noted that RTP customer load occurs in both high and low load hours, making it reasonable that RTP revenues be allocated to the fuel revenues to address the natural gas FT and storage costs. 
Q.HAS THE COMPANY ACKNOWLEDGED STAFF’S CONCERNS?
A.Yes, partially. GPC acknowledged Staff’s concerns and recommended a change. The Company proposed that only new FT costs be shared and allocated on a winter 1-CP basis. The Company’s proposal does not address the allocation of RTP revenues to fuel revenues to cover existing FT cost. Therefore, the Company’s proposal to allocate new FT costs is only a partial solution and adds undue complexity to the RTP fuel credit methodology.
Q.ARE THERE FUNDAMENTAL ISSUES ASSOCIATED WITH THE COMPANY’S PROPOSAL? 
A.Yes. The Company has asserted that a portion of the revenue paid by RTP customers that is currently credited to base revenues is intended to cover some FT costs. This raises the possibility of double recovery because all FT costs are indisputably charged to and recovered from fuel revenues. Absent a modification to the RTP Fuel Methodology, there is a risk that the Company may recover a portion of its FT costs twice.  
To eliminate this risk, Staff recommends that the RTP Fuel Credit Methodology be modified so that all FT costs, both existing and new, are allocated across all customers on the basis of monthly energy consumption. Because the FCR tariff rate is an energy-based rate, allocating FT costs on an energy basis is consistent with the manner in which fuel costs are recovered.  This is also consistent with how FT costs have been recovered from non-RTP customers over the past 15 years. Staff’s proposal ensures that all FT costs and all associated FT revenue recovery occurs entirely within the same accounting mechanism, the FCR, thereby eliminating the potential for double recovery.  
Staff further recommends existing and new FT costs not be assigned separately and that different allocation methodologies not be applied to existing and new customers. Maintaining separate methodologies would perpetuate the current situation in which certain FT costs and their associated revenue recovery are divided between base and fuel revenue mechanisms, creating unnecessary complexity and increasing the risk of cost misallocation and double recovery.  
Q.DO YOU HAVE CONCERNS ABOUT THE COMPANY’S 1-CP PROPOSAL? 
A.Yes.  The Company noted in testimony that in Docket No. 56765, PIA Staff presented results of a calculation that used a 1-CP allocation of FT costs, which the Company agreed with. As a preliminary matter, in FCR-27 Staff relied on the summer peak 1-CP allocation simply for illustration purposes to demonstrate the magnitude of the FT issue that needed further consideration. First, 1-CP is inconsistent with how FT and storage cost have been allocated to non-RTP customers for the past 15 years. The Company currently allocates fixed FT cost entirely to non-RTP customers based on energy consumption. This same approach should be used to allocate all (existing and new) FT to all customers (non-RTP and RTP). It was not Staff’s intention at the time, nor does Staff recommend now, use of a 1-CP allocation approach for RTP cost allocation. 
Furthermore, the Company also argues that capital substitution collections occur in nearly every hour to help cover fixed costs, and the Company charges certain cost components to RTP customers such as reliability capacity costs over approximately 50 hours a year, and transmission costs over approximately 300 hours a year.  It is clear that fixed cost recovery and reliability are not issues limited to a single 1-CP winter hour. FT is necessary for all hours across each month in order to reliably serve customers.

Q.PLEASE SUMMARIZE STAFF’S RECOMMENDATION?
A.Staff recommends the Company’s RTP fuel credit methodology allocate all natural gas FT and storage costs on a monthly basis, without distinguishing between existing and new fixed FT costs. Staff recommends the allocation be performed monthly and all FT costs be allocated on an energy basis to all customers (both RTP and non-RTP), consistent with the current treatment of existing FT costs for non-RTP customers. Staff’s methodology appropriately assigns responsibility for fixed FT across all customers in all months and avoids the additional complexity of establishing a threshold for “new FT” or requiring an ongoing determination of the allocation between organic and new large load.  Both existing and new fixed FT are procured to ensure that generating plants serving all customers always have sufficient fuel to operate and produce power. 
If the Commission prefers a demand allocator, or separate treatment of existing and new FT for allocation purposes, Staff recommends that all FT costs no longer be recovered through the FCR mechanism. Instead, these costs should be recovered through base rates to provide full transparency and ensure that costs and revenues are matched within the same recovery mechanism.

Marginal Coal Pricing and Coal Inventory Management
Q.HAS STAFF IDENTIFIED ISSUES CONCERNING MARGINAL COAL PRICING AND COAL INVENTORY MANAGEMENT? 
A.Yes. In FCR-27, Staff investigated marginal fuel costs, referred to as Marginal Replacement Fuel Costs, that the Company uses in the RTP Fuel Credit calculation. If Marginal Replacement Fuel Costs are understated this could result in a shortfall of RTP revenues credited to fuel revenues. Staff submitted numerous discovery requests in both FCR-27 and this proceeding to investigate this issue. Staff considers this to be an important matter that warrants continued monitoring in future fuel proceedings. 
Also, in FCR-27, Staff identified instances of the Company adjusting the cost of coal or coal transportation in dispatching coal units to encourage fuel consumption and burn down excess coal inventory. These adjusted costs were used by the Company in the RTP Fuel Credit calculation. This could result in an under-allocation of RTP revenues to fuel revenues. 
Q.WHAT IS STAFF’S RECOMMENDATION REGARDING MARGINAL COAL PRICING AND COAL INVENTORY MANAGEMENT?
A.At this time, Staff recommends that additional review of these issues be performed in future FCR proceedings. To facilitate that review, Staff recommends that the following additional minimum filing requirements be adopted for future fuel proceedings:
1) Marginal Coal Pricing - Staff recommends the Company provide support for its marginal coal pricing in a format consistent with what was provided in response to STF-JKA-2-5 Attachment B for each month of the historic period (Docket No. 57171, RTP investigation). 

2) Coal Inventory Management – Staff recommends the Company provide information consistent with what was provided in response to STF-JKA-7-1 for each month of the historic period (Docket No. 56765, FCR-27). In that response, the Company provided presentations and other information from the Company’s monthly coal inventory management meetings showing the monthly schedule of projected burns for Plants Bowen and Scherer. Also, to the extent that the Company makes dispatch pricing adjustments such as lowering the assumed cost of coal to burn down excess coal inventory, Staff recommends the Company provide workpapers showing those adjustments for any month in which that occurred during the historical period. 
Rate Case Considerations
Q.DID THE COMPANY ASSERT THAT CHANGING THE RTP FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY COULD HAVE AN IMPACT ON BASE RATES?
A.Yes. However, since the fuel costs are completely the responsibility of ratepayers and the Company has no financial exposure, it is critical to get the RTP fuel credit methodology correct. Any impact to base rates would be a natural consequence of revising the methodology to accurately match costs and cost recovery in the same mechanism. The direction and magnitude of any future impact would need to be determined in a base rate case. Staff reviewed the 2023 – 2025 Annual Surveillance Review filings and concluded the Company would have had the financial capability to absorb additional RTP revenues credited to fuel revenues while maintaining its targeted Return on Equity (“ROE”).
