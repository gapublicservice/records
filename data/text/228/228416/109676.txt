GIPL-1-1

Question:

Identify which RTP price components are included in the 2021 cost of service study (used in the 2022 rate case) and which line number(s) correspond to each component.

Response:

Exhibit GIPL-2, Schedule 2.20, includes expense items from the 2021 Cost of Service Study reflecting marginal costs assignments of the respective RTP price components. Lines 3 and 4 indicate expenses removed from non-marginal rate groups and assigned to marginal rate groups, which reflects the capital substitution that is derived from the lambda component. Lines 11 and 12 indicate expenses removed from non-marginal rate groups and assigned to marginal rate groups, which reflects the reliability marginal cost. Lines 56 and 57 indicate expenses removed from non-marginal rate groups and assigned to marginal rate groups, which reflects the transmission marginal cost.

GIPL-1-2

Question:

Provide the Company’s analysis of the impact of the two alternative approaches the Company identified to address non-dispatchable PPAs (GPC Direct Testimony, Pg. 18).

Response:

Please see GIPL-1-2 Attachment A for the Company’s analysis of the impact of Option 1, excluding both the costs and output (MWh) associated with non-dispatchable PPAs from the RTP fuel credit calculation. Revenues shown reflect the Company’s estimate of additional revenue that would be booked to fuel revenues if Option 1 was adopted. 

Please see GIPL-1-2 Attachment B for the Company’s analysis of the impact of Option 2, including both the fixed costs and output (MWh) associated with non-dispatchable PPAs within the RTP fuel credit calculation. Revenues shown reflect the Company’s estimate of additional revenue that would be booked to fuel revenues if Option 2 was adopted.

===== STF-1-1.docx =====
STF-1-1

Question:

Provide a forecasted allocation of new FT through 2031.

Response:

Please see STF-1-1 Attachment for the forecasted allocation of new FT from 2027 through 2031, consistent with the amounts included in the FCR-27 Budget. 

The percentage allocated to Large Load is based on the share of winter peak demand growth from the Budget 2026 Load Forecast, which supported the 2029-2031 All Source Resource Certification filing. This allocator and supporting load forecast information has been provided previously in the Large Load Cost models, including the most recent OpenAI Effingham model, in the ‘Summary Data – Rev Req’ tab.
