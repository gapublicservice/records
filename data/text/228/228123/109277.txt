The forecast used in Aurora is described in detail in Technical Appendix Vol.1, Section 2 of the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan, filed in Docket No. 56002. In summary, monthly energy sales (MWh) forecasts are produced at the customer class level. The commercial, industrial, and MARTA class energy sales forecasts, which are inclusive of RTP loads, are then spread to estimated hourly shapes by class. For budgeting purposes, the monthly incremental kWh and an average of RTP prices are used as a simplifying assumption; however, the Company recognizes that load will not be equal in every hour. 

For revenue forecasting used in budget projections, the Company calculates the monthly average of the hourly RTP fuel credits. The Company also calculates the monthly average of total RTP prices. The difference between the average monthly total RTP price and the average monthly RTP fuel credit is the base, or non-fuel RTP price. Each resulting monthly average price components, base and fuel credit, are applied to the monthly RTP kWh projections to determine the base RTP revenue and the RTP fuel credit revenue forecasts.

Refer to the Main Panel rebuttal on p. 31 in Docket 56765, which states, “The RTP incremental load is assessed a marginal cost-based rate. a.Does the Company contend that the RTP fuel credit rate is a marginal based rate and that “most of the kilowatt hours sold are generated by plants with a lower running cost”. Please explain.
b.What evaluation has the Company performed to assess the underlying energy costs to serve the RTP customers compared to the RTP rate charged to customers? Please provide an explanation and copy of the analysis performed.

Response:

The real time pricing (“RTP”) fuel credit rate is a calculation used to determine the amount of revenue that is booked to the fuel clause; it is not a component of the price that the RTP customer is assessed. The RTP fuel credit rate is derived based on an average of the marginal replacement fuel costs for all Georgia Power units, plus the Company’s share of pool sales or purchases. The lambda component of RTP prices is set by the marginal unit on the system and thus will be higher than the average marginal fuel cost for all units in most hours. 

The Company has provided hourly data for the historical period  showing the RTP fuel credit in the Company’s response to STF-JKA-2-2 Attachment B, which reflects the energy costs to serve incremental RTP load. The hourly RTP prices for the historical period are provided in the Company’s response to STF-JKA-3-5 in Docket No. 56765, which represents the RTP rate charged to customers.
