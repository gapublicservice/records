GEORGIA POWER COMPANY’S
APPLICATION FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE

Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”), pursuant to O.C.G.A. §§ 50-13-14 and 46-2-59 and Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) Rule 51521.06, hereby files this Application for Leave to Intervene (“Application”) in the above-styled docket. In support thereof, Georgia Power respectfully shows as follows:

On July 13, 2026, Sustainable Newton, Sara and Randall (“Randy”) Vinson, and Theodosia and William (“Billy”) Wade (collectively, the “Petitioners”) filed a Petition for Expedited Declaratory Judgment and Enforcement Against VoltaGrid, LLC (“Respondent”). On August 24, 2026, the Commission issued its Notice of Hearing and Scheduling Order applicable to the above-referenced Docket. Georgia Power hereby files this Application to participate as a party in the above-referenced Docket.

Georgia Power is a vertically integrated electric utility regulated by the Commission that serves approximately 2.6 million retail customers across more than 57,000 square miles of assigned electric service territory in Georgia. Georgia Power is subject to both the Georgia Territorial Service Act, O.C.G.A. §§ 46-3-1 et seq. (the “Territorial Act”) and the Georgia Cogeneration and Distributed Generation Act of 2001, O.C.G.A. §§ 46-3-50 et seq. (the “Cogen Act”) and serves its customers in compliance with those laws. Specifically, Georgia Power is an electric supplier as defined in the Territorial Act, see O.C.G.A. § 46-3-3(3), and an electric utility as defined in the Cogen Act, see O.C.G.A. § 46-3-52(8).

Georgia Power has a direct, substantial, and legally protected interest in these proceedings that warrants intervention as of right. Respondent seeks to provide bridge power to Serverfarm—an entity slated to become a Georgia Power customer—at premises situated squarely within Georgia Power’s assigned service territory as designated under the Territorial Act. The outcome of these proceedings will therefore necessarily affect Georgia Power’s service within its assigned territory. These are not abstract or speculative concerns: Georgia Power holds defined rights and bears enforceable obligations under both the Territorial Act and the Cogen Act, and the hearing officer’s determinations regarding Respondent’s activities will directly govern the scope and application of those rights and obligations. 

For these reasons, Georgia Power respectfully requests that the Commission grant the Company leave to intervene in these proceedings and to participate fully as a party. The Company does not presently intend to submit direct testimony in this proceeding, but Georgia Power may present rebuttal testimony after considering the direct testimony filed by other Parties. Georgia Power’s interest and those of its customers are not adequately represented by the other parties to these proceedings. Georgia Power’s intervention and participation in this matter will not unduly delay the proceedings or prejudice the rights of any other party.

Georgia Power requests that the following persons receive all notices, correspondence, and copies of any and all pleadings, orders, and other materials in this Docket:
