 2   Q.   YOU NOTED PREVIOUSLY THAT THE STAFF FOCUSED ITS ANALYSES ON

 3        THE BASE TARIFF PROJECTIONS THAT RELIED ON STATISTICAL

 4        AVERAGE RATE CURVE MODELING.                         WOULD YOU DISCUSS THE

 5        COMPANY’S AVERAGE RATE CURVE MODELING?

 6   A.   The Company derived its average rate curve models utilizing the EViews statistical

 7        software. Each statistical regression model followed the same general specification;

 8        average cost per kWh for the rate class or, in the case of the commercial and industrial

 9        classes, by sub-class was the dependent variable and the independent variables consisted

10        of various formulations of average kWh usage per customer and indicator variables (binary

11        variables). To produce the monthly revenue forecast, the projected average rate per kWh

12        is multiplied by the kWh energy sales forecast for the rate class (or sub-class) using the

13        B22 energy forecast presented in the recent IRP case.

14


15        For certain classes (commercial incremental RTP, industrial incremental RTP, industrial

16        NonRTPFPA, Government Lighting, MARTA), the revenue forecasts are derived using

17        more direct approaches. The forecasts are made up of numerous subparts that are summed

18        together to derive the class forecast. The subparts, or components, have specific short-

19        hand, and generally include an average rate curve component, an “FPA” component which

20        describes the Fixed Pricing Alternative rate design, or a Real Time Pricing (“RTP”)

21        incremental rate and an RTP Customer Baseline Load (CBL) component. The total forecast
