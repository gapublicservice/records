II.SUMMARY AND RECOMMENDATIONS

Q.WHAT ARE THE SPECIFIC ISSUES THAT YOU ADDRESS IN YOUR TESTIMONY?
A.The issues we address are associated with Georgia Power’s projected retail revenues, at present rates, for the test year in this case (12 months ending July 31, 2023) and each of the three Plan Years: 2023, 2024 and 2025.  For each of these four periods, the Company has developed a detailed revenue forecast that estimates the revenues that the Company will receive at present Commission approved rates.  In order to develop the revenue deficiency in this case, projected revenues at present rates are compared to the revenue requirement for each of the four periods.  The difference between these two amounts (revenues at present rates and revenue requirements) produces the amount of the revenue deficiency and rate increase that is necessary to recover the revenue requirement.  

The purpose of this testimony is to present our findings and recommend appropriate adjustments to the Company’s revenue projections based on the Staff’s independent development of alternative revenue forecasts for a number of rate classes.

III.REVIEW OF REVENUE FORECAST METHODOLOGY
ENERGY FORECAST
Q.WHAT WAS THE STARTING POINT FOR DEVELOPING THE REVENUE FORECASTS FOR EACH OF THE FOUR PERIODS?
A.Each of the four revenue forecasts (test year and three Plan Years) were developed using the identical methodology.  Separate forecasts were developed for each of the five rate classes (residential, commercial, industrial, governmental lighting and MARTA).  The forecasts are driven by the kWh energy sales forecasts presented by the Company in the 2022 IRP case and is designated as the B22 Forecast.  This rate class energy forecast was previously evaluated by the Staff in the IRP case.  The Staff did not recommend any adjustments to the energy sales forecast in the IRP case and the Company’s B22 sale forecast was approved by the Commission in its Order in that case.  The Company did not make any adjustments to the approved B22 sales forecast in its revenue forecast in this case.
REVENUE FORECAST
Q.HOW DID THE COMPANY DEVELOP ITS REVENUE FORECAST?

A. For the residential and parts of the commercial and industrial rate classes not associated with a real-time pricing (“RTP”) rate, the revenue forecast is based on the product of the B22 energy forecast each month and a projection of the average rate/kWh for the class (e.g., residential) or subclass within a class (e.g., commercial FPA, commercial Non-RTPFPA, commercial RTP CBL, industrial FPA, industrial RTP CBL, where “FPA” stands for fixed pricing alternative).  For the residential class and five commercial and industrial subclasses, the Company developed statistical regression models to forecast the average rate/kWh as a function of monthly average kWh usage per customer for the class or subclass.  For commercial and industrial RTP subclasses, and lighting and MARTA revenues, revenues were projected using a number of different methodologies, which will be discussed later in our testimony.   The methodology that the Company employed is much more complex than what was presented in its minimum filing requirements. 

In addition to the development of base tariff revenue forecasts described above, the Company also developed separate projections of rider revenues associated with Nuclear Construction Cost Recovery (NCCR), Demand Side Management (DSM), Environmental Compliance Cost Recovery (ECCR), and Municipal Franchise Fee (MFF) tariffs.

FORECAST COMPONENT REVIEW
Q. YOU NOTED PREVIOUSLY THAT THE STAFF FOCUSED ITS ANALYSES ON THE BASE TARIFF PROJECTIONS THAT RELIED ON STATISTICAL AVERAGE RATE CURVE MODELING.  WOULD YOU DISCUSS THE COMPANY’S AVERAGE RATE CURVE MODELING?
A. The Company derived its average rate curve models utilizing the EViews statistical software.  Each statistical regression model followed the same general specification; average cost per kWh for the rate class or, in the case of the commercial and industrial classes, by sub-class was the dependent variable and the independent variables consisted of various formulations of average kWh usage per customer and indicator variables (binary variables).  To produce the monthly revenue forecast, the projected average rate per kWh is multiplied by the kWh energy sales forecast for the rate class (or sub-class) using the B22 energy forecast presented in the recent IRP case.  

For certain classes (commercial incremental RTP, industrial incremental RTP, industrial NonRTPFPA, Government Lighting, MARTA), the revenue forecasts are derived using more direct approaches.  The forecasts are made up of numerous subparts that are summed together to derive the class forecast.  The subparts, or components, have specific short-hand, and generally include an average rate curve component, an “FPA” component which describes the Fixed Pricing Alternative rate design, or a Real Time Pricing (“RTP”) incremental rate and an RTP Customer Baseline Load (CBL) component. The total forecast can be distilled down into approximately 12 individual forecast components, as summarized in Table 6.  The Company’s forecasting approach breaks the commercial, industrial and MARTA rate classes into these separate groups (components) that reflect similar rate designs.  For example, customers taking service under the Commercial RTP rates pay separate charges for their CBL usage and their incremental usage.  Since the underlying rates for these two components (CBL, incremental usage) are priced differently, it is reasonable to separately develop revenue forecasts for each component.  These are then combined to develop the total Commercial RTP revenue projection.  The table describes the components of each forecasted revenue class as well as a note on Staff’s review and any proposed adjustment.

COMMERCIAL
Q. HOW DID THE COMPANY ESTIMATE COMMERCIAL CLASS REVENUES?
A. The Company developed its commercial class revenue forecast based on three commercial sub-classes: FPA, NonRTPFPA and CBL RTP.  The FPA sub-class includes legacy customers who were on the rate beginning in 2011 and customers who have been on an RTP rate for three consecutive years.  The RTP revenue forecast is comprised of an RTP incremental component and a CBL component.  The CBL component is priced based on a standard tariff.  The “Com Incr RTP” component represents the non-fuel portion of the commercial class RTP rate (the fuel portion is included in overall retail fuel revenues).  This incremental non-fuel RTP revenue forecast is developed using spreadsheet analysis.  These revenues include both non-fuel RTP revenue produced by day ahead and hour ahead tariffs.
 
For the commercial FPA, non-RTPFPA and CBL forecasts, the Company developed average rate curve models as it did for the residential class.  The Staff’s evaluation determined that an alternative average rate curve model would be appropriate for the non-RTPFPA average rate curve model, which resulted in an adjustment to forecasted revenues.  The Staff’s evaluation of the average rate curve models is discussed subsequently in our testimony.

AVERAGE RATE CURVE AND STATISTICAL ANALYSES

Q. WOULD YOU DISCUSS THE COMPANY’S AVERAGE RATE CURVE STATISTICAL MODELING?
A. As shown in Table 6, the Company develops statistical regression models to forecast the average rate per kWh for six rate classes and sub-classes.  These models are all based on a general relationship between a rate schedule’s average rate per kWh and the average kWh usage per customer for the rate.  The estimated regression models are then used to forecast the average rate per kWh for the class using the Company’s B22 energy forecast (kWh per customer) that was presented in the 2022 IRP case.  The product of the forecasted average rate per kWh and the B22 forecasted kWh sales for the rate class or sub-class produces the base tariff revenue forecast.  The focus of the Staff’s review and analyses was on the various average rate curve statistical regression models.  The kWh energy sales forecast (“B22”) was already addressed by the Staff in the IRP case.  

Q.HOW DID THE COMPANY DEVELOP ITS MODELS?
A.Georgia Power Company uses a statistical analysis software package called “EViews” to develop its average rate curve models.  The Staff obtained EViews and was able to fully evaluate each of the Company’s models.  

Q.WHAT WERE THE RESULTS OF THE STAFF’S EVALUATION OF THE COMPANY’S AVERAGE RATE CURVE MODELS?
A.The Staff found that for three of the Company’s models, an alternative specification produced more reasonable statistical results.  The Company’s models used monthly binary variables for most months that were formed by taking the product of the average kWh usage in the month and a “1” if the observation was for the same month and a “0” if the month was different.  For example, if the observation was for January, the January binary variable would be equal to the average kWh use in the month times “1.”  If the observation was for February, the January binary variable would be equal to “0” (average kWh use in February times “0” equals “0”).  The effect of this modeling specification is that, in general, each month has its own estimated coefficient that relates average usage and the average rate per kWh.  While the Staff does not have concerns with this approach, it is somewhat similar to estimating separate average rate curve models for each month.  The Staff’s alternative models are based on modified specifications and estimation periods that the Staff believes are more appropriate. Q.HOW DO THE STATISTICAL RESULTS PRODUCED BY THE STAFF’S ALTERNATIVE MODELS COMPARE TO THE COMPANY’S MODELS?

IV.RECOMMENDED REVENUE FORECAST DOCUMENTATION

Q. DOES STAFF HAVE ANY RECOMMENDATIONS TO ESTABLISH A REQUIREMENT FOR FILING DETAILED REVENUE FORECAST TESTIMONY AND WORKPAPERS IN FUTURE RATE CASES?
A. Yes.  Notwithstanding the Staff’s recommended test year and Plan Year revenue forecast adjustments, the Staff did not find specific problems with the Company’s overall revenue forecast process in this case.  However, in order to make this assessment, the Staff was required to issue multiple data requests for Excel workbooks, narrative explanations, and follow-on data requests in order to fully evaluate and understand the basis for the Company’s test year and three Plan Year projections.  The Company did not present any testimony on this important issue, nor did the Company file any supporting workpapers with its filing that would facilitate and independent evaluation by the Staff.  In the end, following the Staff’s formal and informal data requests, the Company provide numerous Excel workbooks containing about 50 individual Excel models that provide the support for its revenue forecasts.  The Company also provide a number of narrative descriptions supporting the development of the retail revenue projections for the four test periods (Test Year and Plan Years 2023, 2024 and 2025).

In future Georgia Power Company base rate cases, the Company should be required to file a complete set of workpapers, consistent with all of the Excel workbooks and spreadsheets ultimately provided to the Staff in this case.  This should be included in the minimum filing requirements.  In addition, the Company should file witness testimony supporting the revenue forecast for each rate class.  There should also be a complete set of Excel files that reconcile the various supporting workpapers and resulting rate class revenue forecasts to the retail revenues included in the Company’s revenue deficiency analysis.
