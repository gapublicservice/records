Question:

IIJA’s National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) Program

Is the Company coordinating with the Georgia Department of Transportation (GDOT) to support the Alternative Fuel Corridors funded by the NEVI program? If so, please provide details on how that support and coordination is taking place.
Is the Company expecting to receive IIJA NEVI funds from GDOT to build out the NEVI network?
What role is the Company expecting to play in the buildout of the NEVI network?  
Is the Company planning on building the T&D?  Is the Company planning on installing and owning or operating the chargers themselves?

Is the Company requesting funds in the rate case to support the buildout of the NEVI network?  If so, what funds is the Company requesting for what purposes?
Has the Company calculated how many of the Alternative Fuel Corridors that do not yet comply with the NEVI program requirements are in the Company’s service territory? Has the Company estimated how much it would cost to provide charging infrastructure in those territories? If so, is that amount included in this Rate Case?
Please refer to the Company’s response to STF-PIA-3-3 (e). Will the $81 million the Company plans to spend in T&D work for “EV Expansion” during 2023-2025 cover the T&D needed to meet the full scale of the 5-year NEVI program? If not, will the amount requested cover the T&D needed to meet the first three years of the NEVI program?

What other actions is the Company taking to build out, or support the buildout, of the NEVI network?

Is the Company requesting approval of funds to build charging infrastructure in highways beyond the NEVI program? If so, please specify how much and explain whether such a request is adequate to build the T&D necessary to provide high-speed charging for the number of EVs that are being predicted in 2030.


Response:

a. Yes, the Company is one of the stakeholders engaged in the process to support GDOT as it leads the strategy and administration of the NEVI funds. At this time, the Company is one of several utility stakeholders working with GDOT to ensure GDOT understands and can account for the electric service requirements for the new electric vehicle (“EV”) charging service sites provided through the NEVI program funding.
The Company is not expecting to receive IIJA NEVI funds from GDOT to build out the NEVI network at this time. 
The Company expects to support Georgia Power customers who want to apply for funding through the NEVI program by providing retail electric service and rate options to fit their needs as an EV charging service provider through the NEVI program.
Consistent with providing retail electric service to customers, the Company will follow policies and processes to evaluate and respond to the potential need for any infrastructure upgrades to support customers that are selected to receive NEVI funding from GDOT. The Company is not currently planning to own or operate chargers provided through NEVI funding.

b. No, the Company is not requesting funds in the rate case to support the buildout of the NEVI network.
No. The Company has not calculated how many of the Alternative Fuel Corridors that do not yet comply with the NEVI program requirements are in the Company’s service territory. Based on the Company’s current understanding from the Georgia Electric Vehicle Infrastructure Deployment Plan released by GDOT in August 2022, the exact locations of these sites are not yet determined and will be subject to a future competitive solicitation process that will be managed by GDOT or its designee. 
Please see part (b)(i) of this response. The $81 million requested in this rate case filing is to support the continuation of Georgia Power’s Make Ready program only, which provides the infrastructure up to the charger to support commercial and industrial customers’ electric transportation needs. The Company’s T&D New Business budget will track potential distribution upgrades to its T&D infrastructure as a result of projects created by the NEVI program. These upgrades are not captured in the Make Ready program budget.

c. The Company will support both GDOT and any Company-served customers who apply for NEVI program funding. The Company will support GDOT’s requests as GDOT evaluates sites and finalizes the process for prospective private entities to request funding through the NEVI program. The Company cannot specify further actions until GDOT defines this process and its requirements.

d. Yes, the Company has requested $9 million in this rate case filing during 2023 through 2025 to support the continuation of the Community Charging program through which the Company installs public direct current (“DC”) fast chargers in a limited number of locations throughout the state along travel corridors. This program focuses on travel corridors and communities that are not receiving other investments in public EV DC fast charging. Therefore, new locations established by the $9 million funding request will not overlap with any sites that are funded through NEVI or by private entities providing public EV charging services as described in the Company’s response to STF-LA-6-16 part (e).

c. No. The Company’s request does not include funding for any other EV charging programs besides its Make Ready Program, Community Charging program, and rebates.

d. The Community Charging and Make Ready programs are independent programs, and each program is implemented in a way to best meet customer needs. The decision on whether the Community Charging program or the Make Ready program is implemented is driven by the customer’s interests and through conversations with each customer. The Company works closely with each customer to identify the customer’s interests and find the best fit for the customer while ensuring alignment with program design.  

For instance, if a qualifying customer is not interested in owning and operating EV chargers and their location fits the criteria of the Community Charging program per the Company’s response to STF-LA-6-16 part (a), the Community Charging program is potentially a good fit. However, if the customer would prefer to own and operate EV chargers or if the design of the customer’s preferred project scope does not align with that of the Community Charging program, the customer’s project may be a good candidate for the Make Ready program if it fits within the program criteria as described in the Company’s response to STF-LA-6-20.

For the 2022 IRP, Georgia Power adopted the EPRI Medium forecast as this growth scenario (almost 16% CAGR over the next 20 years) aligns better with historical trends and current market expectations of a stronger EV adoption and represents a balanced view of uncertainties around future EV adoption paths. Future T&D investments needed to support EV expansion will be identified through the Company’s current planning processes. For distribution planning, the process entails an annual review of peak loading for all circuits on the Company’s distribution system to ensure that capacity is available and adequate for the accommodation of new loads. Should the need arise to accommodate new loads, such as EV expansion, the Company will identify and initiate projects to upgrade the capacity of the system. If the need arises to accommodate large EV charging solutions, such as last-mile charging centers, the Company will work directly with the customer on the front-end of installation to ensure that the system is built to support their EV charging needs. For transmission planning, load forecasts are updated and modeled every year based on trends identified by distribution and area planners, as well as the Integrated Transmission System (“ITS”) participant load serving members. Transmission upgrades needed to meet future EV load will be identified through the planning studies performed by the Company and the ITS participants, as detailed in Technical Appendix Volume 3 in the 2022 IRP in Docket No. 44160.

STF-PIA-12-6

Question:

How is the Company planning on addressing demand charges at public DCFC chargers and at electrified fleet locations?
Response:

The Company has several rate options available to customers interested in installing electric vehicle chargers for public direct current fast charging or electrified fleet locations. In addition to the options that exist today, the Company has proposed the Charge It rider (CIEV-1) that pairs with Power and Light base tariffs. The CIEV-1 has a billing demand adjustment factor that reduces the billing demand on a Power and Light rate by 80% in the first year, 60% in the second year, 40% in the third year, and 20% in the fourth year. This temporary reduction to billing demands for new EV charging loads addresses the impact of demand charges as EV sales, and the additional charging loads that follow, increase in Georgia over the next few years. If approved, CIEV-1 would be exclusively available to new electric vehicle charging loads and will add to the Company’s existing portfolio of options to meet customers’ needs.
