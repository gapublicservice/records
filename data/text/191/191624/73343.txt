STF-LA-4- 1

Question:

Refer to Table C of the Company’s 2022 Selective Meter Test Report.  Please explain how the Company determines the number of meters to keep in stock for each meter strata.

Response:

Table C in the Selective Meter Test Report refers to manufacturer and model as meter strata. This data is a good indicator of what manufacturer and model of meters are in the field, and it is very useful for tracking the accuracy trends across meter populations as is done in the Selective Meter Test Report. The data used to determine stocking levels includes more detailed information referred to as meter forms. Meter form is an American National Standards Institute (ANSI) standard. The form number provides information on what meter configurations should be set on certain service types. For instance, a form 2s meter is utilized on a 3 wire 120/240V service. 
The number of meters to keep in stock is mainly dependent on the projected number of new customers, their service type, and system maintenance needs.

STF-LA-4-18

Question:

According to the Company’s 2022 Selective Meter Test Report, with the exception of the AMI meter strata identified as “SEICNA1”, all of the other AMI meter strata identified in Table C of the 2022 Selective Meter Test Report are currently being installed.  Please provide the following information:  

The Company’s plans for time-varying rates; and indicate whether any such offering would be the default tariff for customers with smart meters;
The Company’s proposals for any customer “opt-out” provisions;
The Company’s analysis of how the AMI plan promotes demand response, energy efficiency, and conservation.

Response:

The Company already offers a variety of time-varying rate options for residential, commercial, and industrial customers. Georgia Power’s customers have the option to choose any rate for which they qualify. However, the Commission authorized the Company to utilize the Smart Usage rate (TOU-RD) as the default for residential customers taking service at new premises created after January 1, 2021, if the customer does not choose to enroll on a different rate plan.

The Company does offer an AMI Opt-Out tariff that allows customers to pay an extra charge per month and utilize a meter without radio frequency.

The Company has not performed an analysis of how its AMI system specifically supports demand response, energy efficiency, or conservation. The business case for AMI implementation was designed around automated meter readings and the associated cost savings. However, the Company does utilize the AMI system to provide customers with hourly usage data through the My Power Usage platform, which can help customers use energy efficiently or conserve.

Question:

For the test year and each rate plan year (2023-2025), please provide the estimated cost of removal and disposal of existing meters that were replaced with new AMI meters, along with the average age, average remaining life and remaining uncovered balance of the replaced meters.
Response:
 
The Company develops removal projections at the functional level and not the asset level. Therefore, there are no retirements of specific AMI meters that would provide the average age, average remaining life, and remaining uncovered balance. The Company does not project its removal costs based on the age of the assets being retired, nor does it estimate its retirements based on the average remaining life. However, it can be reasonably inferred that the average age of a meter upon retirement is 10 years based on actual retirement of meters in service.
