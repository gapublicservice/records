69            THE MARTA PUBLIC TRANSIT SYSTEM AND ITS OPERATION

70   Q.   PLEASE DESCRIBE THE GENERAL AUTHORITY OF MARTA

71   A.   MARTA is a public authority established by an Act of the Georgia General Assembly in

72        1965, and approved by subsequent voter referenda, for the purpose of acquiring, planning,

73        designing, building, maintaining, and operating a public rapid transit system in the City of

74        Atlanta, and in Fulton, DeKalb, and Clayton Counties. In 1972 MARTA acquired the

75        Atlanta Transit System, Inc., and took over the operation of bus service in Fulton and

76        DeKalb Counties and the City of Atlanta. In June 1979 MARTA initiated rapid rail service

77        powered by electricity provided by Georgia Power Company.

78

79   Q.   DESCRIBE THE GENERAL FEATURES OF MARTA’S RAPID RAIL SYSTEM

80   A.   MARTA’s rapid rail system consists of over one hundred twenty (120) miles of electrified

81        rail in a dedicated right of way, providing, providing forty-eight (48) miles of total rail

82        service via thirty-eight (38) rail stations. The Authority owns a fleet of three hundred


                                                   4

 83        thirty-eight (338) rail cars powered by alternating current (AC) electrical power. All rail

 84        cars are equipped with regenerative braking systems, so power produced during braking

 85        (when propulsion motors operate as generators) is available for consumption by adjacent

 86        trains fed from the third rail system.

 87   Q.   HOW DOES MARTA COMPARE TO OTHER HEAVY RAIL TRANSIT

 88        PROVIDERS IN THE UNITED STATES?

 89   A.   MARTA serves 2.2 million residents of Metro Atlanta living within our service area and

 90        is among the largest transit system in North America (by unlinked passenger trips)

 91        providing bus, rail, and mobility services. According to analysis by the National Transit

 92        Database, MARTA has one of the lowest costs per-revenue mile for heavy rail service of

 93        any public transit system in the United States.

102        Transit Link Authority. This connection service provides access to employment, services

103        and opportunities for the transit-dependent, mobility for the disabled, convenience for

104        visitors and special events patrons, and a reduction in traffic congestion and pollution for

105        all residents of the region.

161   Q.   DESCRIBE IN GENERAL MARTA’S RELIANCE ON GEORGIA POWER

162        COMPANY IN MARTA’S OPERATIONS OF THE RAPID TRANSIT SYSTEM.

163   A.   The light rail (streetcar) and rapid rail systems are powered by electricity provided by

164        Georgia Power Company. During the design and construction of the heavy rail system

165        the Authority contracted with Georgia Power for the construction of electrical

166        distribution facilities to serve the heavy rail system. During the construction of the heavy

167        rail system, MARTA funded the construction of over 100 substations for traction and

168        station power, cabling, and electrical duct banks. This system delivers electrical power

169        from Georgia Power directly to MARTA for use by rail vehicles and for essential station

170        loads such as lighting, faregates, ticketing machines, elevators, escalators, and station

171        audio-visual information systems. MARTA has placed its first six zero-emission electric

172        buses in service in May 2022. MARTA was awarded a $3.85 million Senate earmark

173        and a $19.3 million Federal Transit Administration Low and No Emission Bus grant to


                                                     8

174        further advance the fleet transition to electric. Further, all future Bus Rapid Transit

175        projects are expected to utilize electric buses.

176   Q.   HOW DOES GEORGIA POWER COMPANY PARTICIPATE IN THE

177        CONSTRUCTION, MAINTENANCE, AND IMPROVEMENT OF THE

178        ELECTRICAL TRANSMISSION SYSTEM FOR MARTA’S RAPID RAIL

179        SYSTEM?

180   A.   Georgia Power Company is a contractor to MARTA for work required for the

181        construction, maintenance, and improvement of the electrical transmission system for the

182        rapid rail system. Contracts are negotiated between the Company and MARTA for the

183        performance of specified maintenance or improvements to the electrical transmission

184        system service. Georgia Power is compensated under those contracts through MARTA’s

185        operating or capital budgets, as applicable, for each project, in addition to all sums paid to

186        Georgia Power for electrical consumption. Since the inception of the MARTA rail

187        system, MARTA has invested well over $500 million in capital funds for the

188        construction, maintenance, and improvement of the electrical transmission system by

189        which Georgia Power serves MARTA. The financial and operational responsibilities for

190        the maintenance and continued improvement of the electrical transmission system are

191        borne by MARTA. Georgia Power’s other ratepayers do not subsidize MARTA. We

192        currently have plans to spend nearly $200 million in modernizing our 40-year- old

193        electrical system. This does not include numerous transit- oriented development (TOD)

194        projects or major expansion projects outlined in the Atlanta More MARTA Plan and

195        Clayton County Plan, which will bring new high capacity transit, new maintenance


                                                      9

196        depots, new stations and additional vehicles to the Atlanta region and increasing the

197        MARTA’S electrical load.

198   Q.   PLEASE DISCUSS MARTA’ BASIC FINANCIAL STRUCTURE. HOW IS

199        MARTA’S OPERATING BUDGET FUNDED?

200   A.   As a public authority, the funding sources for operation of the MARTA system consist

201        of: (1) the proceeds of a one percent sales and use tax levied in Fulton County, DeKalb

202        County, Clayton County and 1.5 percent from the City of Atlanta, (2) fare box revenues

203        collected from passenger fares, (3) leases of property, including long term ground leases

204        for TOD, (4) federal formula funds for preventive maintenance, (5) automobile ad

205        valorem taxes from Fulton, DeKalb and Clayton counties and (6) other revenues derived

206        from activities such as advertising on buses, rail cars, rail stations and bus shelters, and

207        short term leases of surplus property.

208   Q.   HOW IS MARTA’S CAPITAL BUDGET FUNDED?

209   A.   The funding sources for capital improvements and expansion of the MARTA system

210        consist of: (1) the proceeds of the one percent sales and use tax from Fulton, DeKalb and

211        Clayton counties and the 1.5 percent sales tax from the City of Atlanta, and (2) federal

212        and state grant funds.

213   Q.   AS A PUBLIC AUTHORITY, DOES MARTA RECEIVE ANY BUDGET

214        BENEFITS FROM ITS PAYMENTS TO GEORGIA POWER?

215   A.   No. As a public authority, MARTA does not pay taxes and does not return a profit.

216        Therefore, MARTA’s payments to Georgia Power for the provision of electrical service

217        are borne completely by the operating budget, without any offset for tax deductions.

218        When electrical power costs increase, a corresponding increase must occur in operating


                                                     10

219        revenue to provide for payment of electrical costs, or a corresponding decrease in costs

220        must occur to continue offering the same level of service. Since the sales tax is fixed by

221        state law, the only mechanisms for dealing with increased electrical costs are to increase

222        passenger fares or reduce operating expenses.

224   Q.   PRIOR TO THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC WHAT DID MARTA DO TO REDUCE

225        ITS COSTS AND IMPROVE ITS FINANCES?

226   A.   Prior to the COVID-19 Pandemic, MARTA implemented measures to prepare for the

227        demands of the future while maintaining and improving quality services. MARTA began

228        implementing prudent and aggressive cost-cutting measures, efficiency innovations and

229        new revenue generation opportunities to improve service delivery to its customers and

230        avoid a fare increase. MARTA’s pre-pandemic cost reductions were made to improve

231        existing transit services while positioning itself for future investment and expansion. One

232        of MARTA’s major strategic priorities was to achieve financial viability and stability. To

233        this end we have engaged in cost reduction measures, including staff reductions,

234        outsourcing, and asking all business partners to assist MARTA by holding down costs. In

235        August, Fitch upgraded MARTA’s credit rating to AA based on MARTA's having

236        demonstrated a sustained maintenance of liquidity and financial resilience.

237   Q.   WHAT ACTION HAS MARTA TAKEN OVER THE YEARS TO REDUCE ITS

238        ELECTRICAL COSTS, OTHER THAN REDUCING SERVICE?

239   A.   After the 2007 Rate Case, a small part of MARTA’s electrical power usage began to be

240        purchased under marginal or real-time pricing (RTP) to realize cost savings where

241        possible. At the conclusion of the 2013 rate case MARTA moved 40% of its rail


                                                   11

242           propulsion load to marginal pricing. However, as the scale of operations changed this

243           needed to be adjusted. For obvious reasons, the rail system must provide service, and

244           therefore has a greater demand for electrical power during the weekday morning and

245           afternoon rush hours when there is the greatest passenger demand for service. Generally,

246           those needs are difficult if not impossible to delay or shift to off-peak times. However,

247           MARTA developed new system operations to reduce electrical use in peak times through

248           sophisticated speed controls and other methods. We have successfully demonstrated this

249           to Georgia Power’s satisfaction and had been able to lower the customer base load (CBL)

250           for propulsion service under the ET tariff. Other changes including renovation of

251           MARTA’s HVAC system at its headquarters and replacement of station lighting with

252           more energy efficient LED fixtures.

253

254   Q.      HAS MARTA DEVELOPED OTHER INITIATIVES FOR ALTERNATIVE

255           ENERGY CONSUMPTION?

256   A.      Yes. MARTA received a federal grant to design, construct and maintain a solar power

257           canopy array at the MARTA Laredo Bus Facility in DeKalb County which is one of four

258           bus facilities operated by MARTA in its service area. The solar project has been

259           operating since the fall of 2011 and generates enough electricity to offset a significant

260           portion of the Laredo Facility’s electricity consumption. Also, MARTA has upgraded

261           lighting fixtures at all rail stations, maintenance and office facilities to LED and replaced

262           HVAC and chillers, resulting in 14% energy use reduction since 2012. MARTA is

263           currently evaluating additional solar investments throughout the system.

264        There are several things we are doing or planning that will assist the Georgia Power system:


                                                       12

265   •   We ordered $680 million of new and more energy efficient rail cars which will begin

266       arriving in November 2024.

267   •   We are replacing natural gas-driven compressors with large electric motors for refueling

268       our natural gas-powered buses. These new motors will operate at night when the buses

269       return to the refueling stations. This increases the utility load factor during off-peak

270       hours making the system more efficient.

271   •   MARTA like other transit systems, is studying the use of “super capacitors” on our rail

272       cars. These devices capture braking energy and use the stored energy to restart rail car

273       motion. This technology not only reduces energy use but helps stabilize the utility

274       system.

275   •   We have increasingly sophisticated centralized controls for our trains and will be able to

276       reduce speeds to reduce power use during critical periods.

277   •   MARTA was awarded a federal grant in 2019 for six (6) electric buses and two fast

278       charging stations to begin the transition from diesel and compressed natural gas-powered

279       buses to all electric buses. The buses are in service between North Avenue and

280       Edgewood/Candler Park. With additional federal funding from a Senate earmark and an

281       additional Federal Transit Administration grant, MARTA has plans to add thirty-one

282       (31) more electric buses by 2024. Future Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) lines including

283       Summerhill, Clayton Southlake and Campbellton will be served by electric buses.

284   •   MARTA has been and continues to work with Georgia Power on deployment of Electric

285       Vehicle (EV) charging facilities at stations and parking facilities. Over 60 chargers have

286       been installed at nine stations as part of Phase One of the EV program. MARTA has EV

287       charging spaces at 9 locations with 11 more in the implementation stage.

288             THE IMPACT OF THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC UPON MARTA’S
289                 OPERATIONS AND FINANCES SINCE MARCH 2020
290
291   Q.   WHAT IMPACT HAS THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC HAD ON MARTA’S

292        CURRENT FINANCIAL OUTLOOK?

293   A.   The Covid-19 Pandemic has had a dramatic impact on MARTA’s ridership and budget.

294        Passenger revenues decreased dramatically after the declaration of the pandemic and

295        implementation of shelter in place orders in March 2020. April 2020 revenues were

296        approximately $2 million, down from $10 million in February 2020. MARTA’s actual

297        passenger revenues for 2020 were down $30 million and $79 million in 2021 due to the

298        pandemic. While passenger revenues are starting to recover, they are still 60% of pre-

299        pandemic levels and not projected to return to pre-pandemic levels until 2024 or 2025.

300        MARTA has not included a fare increase in its current budget to provide an additional

301        incentive to its riders to use public transportation again as more people return to work,

302        school, and social events and activities. Additionally, all 110 bus routes were restored to

303        full service by April 24, 2021.

304   Q.   HAS THE FEDERAL PANDEMIC ASSISTANCE COMPLETELY OFFSET THE

305        SALES TAX AND PASSENGER REVENUE LOSES?

306   A.   While the Federal financial assistance has been critical to MARTA during these difficult

307        times it has not completely offset or replaced all MARTA’s lost revenues from lower

308        sales taxes and passenger revenues. The Federal assistance has filled the short-term

309        revenue gap in FY2020 and FY2021. Furthermore, the Federal assistance buys MARTA

310        the time necessary, due to the uncertainty in timing and levels of fare revenue recovery,

311        to reset MARTA’s revenue and expense baselines. MARTA will make budget

312        adjustments over the next few years to maintain balanced budgets into the future without

                                                    14

313        stimulus fund use. While MARTA anticipates growth in ridership over the next few

314        years, passenger revenues still hover around 60% below pre-pandemic levels, and ar not

315        projected to return to pre-pandemic levels until 2025. Of course, those projections

316        depend on how long the pandemic lingers and how inflation impacts the economy.
