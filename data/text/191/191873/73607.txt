 4           III.    CREDIT RATING METHODOLOGY
 5
 6   Q.      HOW ARE CREDIT RATINGS USED IN THE CAPITAL MARKETS?
 7   A.      Credit ratings reflect the rating agency’s opinion of a utility’s financial strength and
 8           ability to service its debt under different market conditions over time. Fixed income
 9           (bond) investors include credit ratings along with many other factors in their
10           assessment of whether to buy, continue to hold or sell bonds issued by utilities. As
11           such, depending on the credit rating determined appropriate by the credit rating
12           agencies, bond investors may require higher or lower rates of interest to compensate
13           for the level of risk implied by a given credit rating all else equal.
14
15   Q.      PLEASE EXPLAIN THE IMPLICATION OF A CHANGE IN THE CREDIT
16           RATING OF A PUBLIC UTILITY.
17   A.      Companies are downgraded and upgraded by the credit agencies as the financial
18           condition of a company changes and to take into account economic and financial
19           markets changes as well. Being downgraded does not imply a company cannot
20           access the capital markets. A downgrade implies that the bond rating agency has
21           determined the risk of owning debt securities of the affected company has risen.
22           All else equal, investors would require a higher return for purchasing a company’s
23           debt. Conversely, being upgraded implies that the affected company’s financial
24           condition has improved and therefore investors would require a lower rate of
25           interest on a company’s debt securities.
26

 7                   D.      Financial Metrics

 8
 9   Q.      PLEASE        DESCRIBE        HOW      MOODY’S        ASSESSES        FINANCIAL
10           STRENGTH IN ITS CREDIT RATING METHODOLOGY.
11   A.      Financial strength represents 40% of the Moody’s credit rating analysis and is
12           defined as the ability of a utility to make its debt interest and principle payments
13           consistently in a timely manner and to provide a return for shareholders sufficient
14           to attract capital for investment purposes. The Moody’s analysis is not only a
15           historical analysis but is also a forecasted analysis of various financial metrics
16           primarily focused on cash flow generated by operations relative to annual interest
17           expense and amount of debt. The specific Moody’s metrics are listed below:
18

19               •   Cash flow interest coverage, which is defined as the sum of cash flow from
20                   operations pre-working capital plus interest divided by interest;

21               •   Cash flow pre-working capital divided by debt; and
22               •   The sum of cash flow from operations pre-working capital less dividends
23                   divided by outstanding debt.
