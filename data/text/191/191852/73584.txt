 1         Q.       IN SETTING THE REVENUE REQUIREMENT, RETURN ON EQUITY

 2                  (“ROE”), ALLOCATION, AND RATE DESIGN CHANGES FOR THE

 3                  COMPANY, SHOULD THE COMMISSION CONSIDER THE IMPACT OF

 4                  THE PROPOSED RATE INCREASE ON BUSINESS CUSTOMERS?

 5         A.       Yes. Electricity is a significant operating cost for retailers such as those in the

 6                  Commercial Group. When electric rates increase, the increased cost to retailers can put

 7                  pressure on consumer prices and on the other expenses required by a business to

 8                  operate. The Commission should thoroughly and carefully consider the impact on

 9                  customers when examining the requested revenue requirement and ROE, in addition to

10                  all other facets of this case, to ensure that any increase in the Company’s rates is the

11                  minimum necessary to provide safe, adequate, and reliable service, while also

12                  providing GPC the opportunity to recover its reasonable and prudent costs, earn a

13                  reasonable return on its investment, and meet large commercial customer expectations

14                  for customer service.

19                  and approximately 66,000 associates in Georgia, spent $25.8 billion with Georgia-

20                  based suppliers, and supported over 277,000 supplier jobs in the state.1

 2        Q.   DOES      THE     COMMERCIAL             GROUP         HAVE    ANY      ADDITIONAL

 3             COMMENTS REGARDING THE PROPOSED ARP?

 4        A.   Yes.    The Commercial Group is concerned about the near-term impacts of the

 5             Company’s levelization proposal on Georgia Power’s customers, particularly in light

 6             of customer bill impacts from recent increases in fuel rates, and for RTP customers,

 7             marginal prices. While the overall impact through 2025, on a net present value basis,

 9             consider these impacts as it determines whether to levelize, and if so, how much

10             revenue requirement should be levelized.

11                     Additionally, in its consideration on the appropriate ROE for the Company, the

12             Commission should recognize that the proposed ARP shifts risk from the Company’s

13             shareholders to customers by removing the effects of regulatory lag from 2023 through

14             2025.

 5      Q.     GENERALLY, WHAT IS THE COMMERCIAL GROUP'S POSITION ON

 6             SETTING RATES BASED ON THE UTILITY'S COST OF SERVICE?

 7      A.     The Commercial Group advocates that rates be set based on the utility's cost of service

 8             for each rate class. This produces equitable rates that reflect cost causation, send proper

 9             price signals, and minimize price distortions.

10      Q.     DOES THE COMMERCIAL GROUP TAKE A POSITION ON THE

11             COMPANY’S PROPOSED COST OF SERVICE MODEL AT THIS TIME?

12      A.     No. However, to the extent that alternative cost of service models or modifications to

13             the Company’s model are proposed by other parties, the Commercial Group reserves

14             the right to address any such proposals.

15      Q.     HOW DOES THE COMPANY REPRESENT WHETHER RATES FOR A

16             CUSTOMER CLASS ACCURATELY REFLECT THE UNDERLYING COST

17             CAUSATION?

18      A.     The Company reflects this relationship in their cost of service results through the use

19             of class-specific and rate schedule-specific rates of return. These rates of return can be

20             converted into a relative rate of return (“RROR”), which is an indexed measure of the

21             relationship of the rate of return for an individual customer class to the total system rate

22             of return. An RROR greater than 1.0 means that the customer class is paying rates in

23             excess of costs incurred to serve that class, and an RROR less than 1.0 means that the


                                      Direct Testimony of Steve W. Chriss
                                      On Behalf of the Commercial Group
                                               Docket No. 44280
                                                    Page 15

 1        customer class is paying rates less than the costs incurred to serve that class. As such,

 2        those rate classes with an RROR greater than 1.0 are subsidizing the classes with an

 3        RROR less than 1.0. A class with an RROR equal to 1.0 is said to be at parity.
