Q.ON WHOSE BEHALF ARE YOU TESTIFYING?
A.I am testifying on behalf of the Georgia Public Service Commission Public Interest Advocacy Staff (“PIA Staff” or “Staff”). 
Q.WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF YOUR TESTIMONY?
A.The purpose of my testimony is to review and make recommendations with respect to the Georgia Power Company’s (GPC or Company) 2022 Rate Case’s Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Expenses for the test year ending July 31, 2023 and for the three Alternate Rate Plan (ARP) Years 2023, 2024 and 2025 with respect to just and reasonableness of the expenses and whether any of the expenses have been overestimated.  My testimony addresses the Company’s historical O&M Expenses that were used as a basis to set the test year O&M Expenses for the twelve months ending July 31, 2023. III.CASE OVERVIEW AND STRUCTURE OF TESTIMONY- PIA STAFF RECOMMENDATIONS 
Q.PLEASE PROVIDE A BRIEF OVERVIEW OF GPC’S RATE CASE FILING?
A.On June 24, 2022, GPC filed a base rate case proceeding with the Georgia Public Service Commission requesting recovery of a Levelized Revenue Deficiency applicable to traditional base rate tariffs of $738.661 million each year in 2023, 2024 and 2025. The Company is also requesting increases in Environmental Compliance Cost Recovery (ECCR), Demand Side Management (DSM) and Municipal Franchise Fee (MFF) tariffs’ revenue requirements to be recovered from customers.   The Company is proposing to recover an additional $312.245 million during the test year period ending July 31, 2023 and $426.415 million is proposed to be recovered during the 2023-2025 Levelized Adjustment periods ending December 31, 2023, December 31, 2024 and December 31, 2025 (Company Exhibit APA/SPA/ADH/MBR-1 Schedule 2 Traditional Base).   
Q.DID THE COMPANY UPDATE ITS FILING SUBSEQUENT TO THE JUNE 24, 2022 FILING?
A.Yes. On August 22, 2023, the Company updated its filing which contained revenue requirement impacts from various items included in the Final Order approved by the Commission in the 2022 Integrated Resource Plan (IRP) and DSM Certification proceedings in Docket Nos. 44160 and 44161, respectively (August 22, 2022 petition).

Q.WHAT APPROACH DID YOU USE IN DETERMINING YOUR ADJUSTMENTS TO THE COMPANY’S O&M EXPENSES?
A.In developing my adjustments to certain of the Company’s O&M Expenses, I determined whether the expenses were reasonable, prudent, known and measurable and realistic, and whether the Company should recover these expenses from ratepayers, and whether they were a necessary part of the Company’s operations.   I reviewed the Company’s historical expense data, as well as the Company’s forecasted data to determine my recommended O&M expense levels related to the Company’s category of expenses for the test year and for each of the rate year periods.  The more reliance on forecasted data the more subjectivity was used to determine the estimates proposed in the Company’s rate year periods.  My review and recommendation of the Company’s O&M Expense levels reflects cost that are recurring over time. My approach incorporates whether expenses should be normalized or averaged out based upon prior historical data and/or whether expenses should be recovered consistently over the rate year periods.  The ultimate goal in setting rates for service is to provide the ratepayers with safe and reliable service at reasonable rates with the Company being provided the opportunity to earn a reasonable rate of return on its funded plant investments, and to recovery all known and measurable expenses.  
Q.WHAT DID THE COMPANY STATE REGARDING O&M EXPENSES?
A.Mr. Womack stated that despite inflation and increased labor and healthcare costs over the last three years, the Company has worked hard to hold O&M cost escalation below the rate of historical inflation, budgeting about $1.7 billion in 2023 compared to about $2.0 billion of O&M expenses in current rates (Womack testimony at 12).  Included in the revenue requirement proposal are incremental inflation adjustments totaling $94 million and adjustments to its non-fuel O&M expenses that reflects a reduction of $163 million that are attributable to projected decreases in costs for employee benefits and effective cost management and reductions for outages and maintenance work on existing and retired coal units (Abramovitz, Adams, Houston and Robinson testimony (hereinafter as AAHR) at 18).   The Company stated that storm damage expenses have been reduced from $213 million per year to $65 million per year which reflects the average annual storm damage costs incurred over the past ten years (AAHR testimony at 19). 
Q.DO ANY OF THE COMPANY’S O&M EXPENSES INCLUDE COSTS RELATED TO SOUTHERN COMPANY AND SOUTHERN NUCLEAR OPERATING COMPANY?
A.Yes.  Southern Company services (SCS) expenses include various engineering costs, system costs, terminal processing charges, miscellaneous computer charges and other general charges to Georgia Power Company (STF-LA-2-6 page 18). With respect to Southern Nuclear Operating Company (SNOC) the Company is allocated costs related to Incentive Compensation and restricted stock awards (STF-LA-2-78 TRADE SECRET and STF-LA-2-81).

Q.PLEASE EXPLAIN YOUR OVERALL REASONING RELATED TO THE COMPANY’S PROPOSED INFLATION ADJUSTMENTS AS IT PERTAINS TO THE SETTING OF RATES.
A.It is my opinion that the use of inflation rates and factors to set rates for service in ratemaking are not a known and measurable cost, in that these types of cost increases do not reflect the actual costs the Company will incur, but rather estimates with no foundational reasoning.  These types of costs are essentially broad economic adjustments that do not particularly address any one individual expense category.  Inflation adjustments are overall blanket type adjustments that are applied to all goods and services that may or may not be related to costs incurred by the Company, especially if the GDP is used, which is a broad basket of consumer goods and services such as housing, clothing, food, etc.   While such inflation costs adjustments are typically used to develop economic data, they should not be used for ratemaking purposes in most cases.  In this case, under the Company’s ARP, inflation may not stay elevated during the term of the 3-year rate plan.  I believe that the Company’s inflation adjustment forecast is too high, and it may result in rates being set too high for utility service. Given this situation, I am recommending lowering the Company’s proposed Inflation Adjustments in the Rate Years as follows:

Q.PLEASE EXPLAIN YOUR REASONING FOR A VACANCY RATE RATIO ADJUSTMENT.

Given that the  Company experiences vacancies throughout the year, it is appropriate to take these vacancies into consideration when setting labor costs. These vacancies are due to a number of factors -- employee stability, voluntary and involuntary leave by employees, increased / decreased work load, shifting of employees from one affiliated entity to another (business unit staffing), difficulty to fill vacancies due to the challenge of attracting and retaining employees and overall business strategy.   Using the Company’s projected Contract Labor of $428,467,945 minus my recommended reduction related to Performance Pay Incentive Compensation booked in the Test Year, my net Contract Labor for purposes of calculating my vacancy rate ratio is $322,291,871.
