Question:

How much make ready equipment is on the customer side of the meter (as a percentage)?

Response:

Please see STF-HR-1-3 Attachment, which provides a visual representation of the types of equipment and costs that can be part of the Make Ready program. The equipment labeled “ET Charging Infrastructure” represents equipment on a customer’s side of the meter, and the equipment labeled “Electric Distribution Service” represents equipment on the Company’s side of the meter.

To date, nearly all of the total Make Ready program spend (over 99%) is for the funding of equipment on the customer side of the meter. These costs include expenses already incurred and those currently estimated and in progress from projects approved for Make Ready program funding from the 2019 Rate Case Order.

STF-HR-1-4

Question:

Please provide any EV make ready RIM studies GPC has performed.

Response:

Please see STF-HR-1-4 Attachment. The RIM analyses performed for the Make Ready program includes many different scenarios of electric vehicle end use of the chargers as shown in the attachment. These scenarios include technologies customers have already adopted and those that they will likely adopt in the next several years. The outcome of this analysis varies between each of the scenarios and assumed customer tariff between PL, TOU, and RTP tariffs. As an overall program, the average RIM value taking into account all scenarios and tariffs is positive, which creates downward pressure on rates for all customers.

Question:

Where does the revenue GPC receives from customers thru special charges associated with dedicated facilities for those customers show up? Is that as credit or offset in calculation of the revenue requirement? Are the Company’s special facilities charges (EFC charges) in the “other operating revenue” line item?

Response:

The revenues associated with excess facilities charges received from customers are recorded to “Other Operating Revenues” in FERC account 451. The revenues are included in the Company’s retail cost of service and result in a reduction to the retail revenue requirement.

STF-HR-1-8

Question:

To the extent there are materials available, please explain how the Smart Usage notifications and alerts work. Will they signal when a customer uses more than one appliance at one time or when the bill will be higher?

Response:

Customers on Smart Usage can enroll in alerts for monthly and daily usage thresholds within My Power Usage. This is true for all customers who utilize the My Power Usage system to view their usage data. Customers are also able to enroll in a Daily Usage Summary report that is sent via email each day to inform customers of their prior day’s as well as recent usage information. There is not an option to enroll in dollar-based alerts for customers on the Smart Usage rate.  

My Power Usage does not provide real-time data to customers – such as signaling when more than one appliance is being used. My Power Usage relies upon the AMI network, which provides usage readings used for billing purposes, but the readings are not passed between the meter and the AMI network in real-time. However, customers who do wish to monitor their usage in real-time can purchase devices that can be installed in the home to provide highly granular usage insights – including the ability to determine when multiple appliances are being used.
