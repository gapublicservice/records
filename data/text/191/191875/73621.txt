 3   Q.   PLEASE PROVIDE SOME BACKGROUND ON THE COMPANY’S MFF

 4        TARIFF.

 5   A.   In the 2007 Georgia Power Rate Case Order, the Commission ordered the establishment

 6        of the MFF tariff in accordance with the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 21112. The

 7        MFF tariff initially reflected 50 percent of MFF revenues being collected from customers

 8        located within municipal areas covered by a franchise agreement and 50 percent being

 9        collected from all customers, regardless of location. However, over the years, the

10        Company has experienced a shift with more of its total revenue coming from customers

11        within municipalities. Because the MFF tariff is a percentage rate and is calculated based

12        on each customer’s total bill before sales taxes are applied, the percentage rate does not

13        have to be changed even if revenues change.

14

16   A.   As described on pages 30-31 of the Abramovitz/Adams//Houston/Robinson Direct

17        Testimony, the primary components of the MFF tariff that could change from year to

18        year are the amount of revenues collected relative to whether or not customers are

19        receiving service within a municipality’s boundaries. As customer demographics change,

20        so would the respective MFF tariff.

4    Q.      HOW IS THE OBLIGATION FOR CCR COMPLIANCE RECORDED FOR

5            ACCOUNTING PURPOSES AND RECOVERED FROM CUSTOMERS IN

6            RATES?

7    A.      For accounting purposes, the Company's obligation for compliance with CCR regulations

8            is recorded as an Asset Retirement Obligation. A related liability is also recorded to

 9           recognize the legal obligation to incur such costs. Historically, costs related to the

10           remediation of ash ponds and landfills that have been used to store coal ash at plants that

11           were still operational would have generally been included in the cost of removal/negative

12           net salvage component of depreciation rates and would have been recovered over the

13           estimated remaining life of the coal-fired generating plants. CCR compliance costs for

14           coal-fired generating plants that have already been retired or are being retired before their

2    Q.   WHAT IS STAFF’S RECOMMENDATION REGARDING ELECTRIC

3         TRANSPORTATION MAKE READY?

4    A.   The Make Ready program proposed by Georgia Power Company in this docket should be

5         denied and the associated costs be removed for ratemaking purposes. Investments

6         needed to provide an unregulated service to private groups should be paid for by the

7         business or organization wanting to provide that unregulated service. This is true whether

8         or not the unregulated charging service is private or available to the public. As with

9         investments associated with regulated public utilities, the costs should be recovered from

10        cost-causers. The general body of ratepayers should not be subjected to paying for private

11        assets that primarily benefit Make Ready participants. The program, if continued for the

12        next decade, will increase the revenue requirement by close to half a billion dollars.

13

14   Q.   WHY IS THE MAKE READY PROGRAM NOT IN THE PUBLIC INTEREST?

15   A.   The Make Ready program obligates captive electric service customers to subsidize the

16        provision an unregulated service, electric vehicle charging. The vast majority of retail

17        electric customers do not own electric vehicles and will not own EVs this decade.

18        Additionally, Make Ready equipment is only needed in order to provide unregulated

19        charging services to a small set of Georgia Power’s customers. The equipment is not used

20        and useful in providing regulated retail electric service to Georgia Power’s customers.

21        As noted in the response to STF-HR-1-2, “To date, nearly all of the total Make Ready

 4        and useful in the provision of retail electric service, but also on unregulated assets fully

 5        funded by customers who do not have a choice of providers.

3    Q.      HOW IS AN ALTERNATIVE RATE PLAN DIFFERENT FROM A

4            TRADITIONAL RATE CASE ORDER IN TERMS OF EARNINGS ABOVE THE

5            ROE BAND?

6    A.      Under a traditional rate case order, the Company could earn in excess of an authorized

7            ROE without the ratepayers receiving any rate reductions or any benefit from the

8            application of earnings above the top of the rate band. While the Commission would have

 9           the authority to bring the Company in under a Rule Nisi proceeding to reduce its rates,

10           such a proceeding would take a significant amount of time and any order reducing rates

11           would be prospective in nature.

12

13   Q.      HOW IS THE ALTERNATIVE RATE PLAN DIFFERENT FROM A

14           TRADITIONAL RATE CASE ORDER IN TERMS OF EARNINGS BELOW THE

15           AUTHORIZED ROE OR LOWER ROE BAND?

16   A.      Under a traditional rate case order, the Company could earn less than the authorized ROE

17           and would not have any recourse except to file a rate case. Any relief would be

18           prospective in nature.

19

21           YEAR RATE PLAN IN TERMS OF WHEN THE COMPANY FILES RATE

22           CASES?

23   A.      Under a traditional rate case order, the Company would be able to file another rate case

24           whenever it deemed appropriate, and the Commission would have the ability to bring
