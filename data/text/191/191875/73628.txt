STF-LA-1-44

Question:
Plant Held for Future Use.
   a. Identify the amount of PHFFU by item as of 12/31/2021.
   b. For each item of PHFFU, indicate whether it represents rights of way.
   c. For each item of PHFFU, indicate whether it represents land owned by the Company.
   d. For each item of PHFFU, identify and explain the intended use and when the item is
      anticipated to become utility plant in service.
   e. For each item of PHFFU, identify when the Company originally recorded the amount in
      the PHFFU account.
   f. Identify where each item of PHFFU is addressed in the Company's 2022 IRP. If an item
      of PHFFU is not addressed in the Company's 2022 IRP, explain why.


Response:
Please see STF-LA-1-44 Attachment. The Company is complying with the agreed upon
timeframes and forums for review of Plant Held for Future Use (“PHFFU”) as provided in
Paragraph 16 of the Stipulation approved by the Commission in the Company’s 2019 Rate Case
in Docket No. 42516:
With the exception of easements and right of ways, Generation and Transmission property           in
PHFFU that has been held in PHFFU account for 15 consecutive years will undergo review during
the first IRP proceeding following the 15th year that such property has been held in PHFFU.
Distribution property in PHFFU that has been held in PHFFU account for 15 consecutive years
will undergo review during the first base rate proceeding following the 15th year that such property
has been held in PHFFU. In the respective proceeding, the Company will be required to present
the specific plan for the property(s) that has exceeded 15 consecutive years in PHFFU, the
Commission will decide the matter in that proceeding.

        As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2022 Rate Case filed in Docket No. 44280 (“2022 Rate
Case”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public
Service Commission its response to STF-LA-2-111 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has
provided information related to cost allocation methodologies of the Company (the “Information”).
All such Information constitutes trade secret information of the Southern Company, Georgia Power,
and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-
.11.

         The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being
readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its
disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information could be used by competitors of the Company to
gain confidential details regarding the Company’s internal cost allocation methodologies. These
methodologies are proprietary and have been developed over time and through the expenditure of
significant resources. Access to such methodologies would bestow on the Company’s competitors an
unfair advantage to the extent that such competitors would not similarly be required to expend such
resources to develop similar methodologies. In addition, access to the Information would provide
insight into the Company’s costs, which could also undermine the Company’s position. Competitors
are not required to disclose similar customer information and to require Georgia Power to do so
would put it at an economic disadvantage.

       The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select
Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information.
Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who
have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality
agreements with respect to the Information.

Pursuant to the Commission Order in Docket No. 42516 (the “Order”), the Company is fully
amortizing the unprotected excess federal and state ADITs and the tax expense savings related to
reduction in state income taxes for 2019 that were recorded to regulatory liabilities over the 3-year
period ending December 2022 for the benefit of customers. The regulatory liability balances and
the amortization of these items for the forecasted test year ending July 31, 2023 and forecasted
calendar years 2022 through 2025 are provided in the Company’s response to STF-LA-2-16.

Customers will continue to receive the benefit of the savings through the amortization of protected
excess ADITs in 2022 through 2025. For federal protected excess ADITs, the amortization is
required to follow the average rate assumption method under the Internal Revenue Service
normalization rules. The amortization is approximately $66 million for test year and 2023, and $67
million and $69 million for 2024 and 2025, respectively, as provided in further detail in the
Company’s response to STF-LA-2-116. The protected excess ADIT amortization is reflected as a
reduction to Federal and State Income Taxes in Exhibit___(APA-SPA-ADH-MBR-1, Schedule 1
Total Company) line 38, page 4 for the forecasted test year, and line 35, pages 6, 8, and 10 for
each forecasted year 2023-2025, respectively. The protected excess ADIT balances are identified
in the Company’s response to STF-LA-2-101 and included in Exhibit___( APA-SPA-ADH-MBR-
1, Schedule 1 Total Company) line items 45 and 46 under “Accumulated Deferred Income Taxes,”
page 3 for the forecasted test year, and line items 44 and 45, pages 5, 7, and 9 for each forecasted
year 2023-2025, respectively.

In addition, the Company’s revenue requirements for the test year and forecasted years 2022
through 2025 continue to reflect the lower federal and state income tax rates in the calculation of
income tax expenses, resulting in savings through lower income tax requirements for customers.

STF-LA-5-14

Question:

Will the Company be eligible to obtain any tax credits, or tax savings in 2022, 2023, 2024 or 2025
from the Inflation Reduction Act that was recently passed, such as, but not limited to, using
renewable energy, reduced employee and retiree health care and prescription costs, or other
provisions?

a. If not, explain fully why not.

b. If so, identify, quantify and explain all expected tax credits and tax savings benefits that the
   Company would be eligible for.

c. If different than the response to part b, identify, quantify and explain all benefits under the Act
   which the Company expects to claim for each year 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025.


Response:

a. The Company is continuing to evaluate the impacts of the Inflation Reduction Act (“IRA”).
   Based on the analysis completed thus far, the Company expects the IRA will provide tax
   benefits that, over time, can help reduce costs for customers. The Company will work with the
   Commission to determine which benefits best serve customers.

b. At this time, the Company has identified approximately $33 million of total potential benefits
   for 2022 through 2025 for the Commission’s consideration related to projected, qualified
   renewable and electric vehicle investments. Listed below are the capital investments, along
   with estimated tax savings by year for 2022 through –2025, of the incremental benefits the
   Company has identified from the IRA, with related assumptions.

     Capital Investment (in millions)                2022       2023       2024      2025
     Energy Storage                                     $ -     $ 105.6      $ -        $ -
     Solar Qualifying for Production Tax
     Credits                                             $-         $-         $-   $ 32.7

     Potential Tax Credits (in millions)            2022       2023      2024       2025

     EV Charger Credit Amortization                 $ 0.1     $ 0.2      $ 0.2     $ 0.3


   Energy Storage Credits: The IRA provides Investment Tax Credits (“ITC”) for standalone
    energy storage systems, no longer requiring it to be paired with and charged by solar
    generation. The Company believes the 65 MW Mossy Branch battery storage project, which
    is projected to be on-line in 2023, will qualify for a 30% ITC. The IRA also gives an “opt-out”
    from the existing ITC normalization, which permits the Company to recognize the full ITC
    benefit in the year the project goes into service, thereby providing a benefit to customers by
    lowering the levelized cost of electricity over the life of the project as compared to the cost
    under the existing ITC normalization option. In order to take advantage of this “opt out” option
    offered in the IRA, the Commission must specifically approve the “opt-out” option. The
    Company’s calculation above assumes that the full $31.7 million of estimated credit resulting
    from the normalization opt-out option would be recognized in 2025.

   Solar Credits: The IRA provides a PTC option, which may be utilized instead of the existing
    normalized ITC for solar investments. The Company estimates the PTC option will provide an
    approximate $0.4 million of additional benefits for customers in 2025 on projected self-build
    ash pond solar investments compared to the normalized ITC option.

   EV Charger Credits: The IRA extends and modifies the credit for alternative fuel refueling
    property. This 30% ITC applies to property placed in service through 2032 and increases the
    credit limitation to $100,000 per EV charger. The Company is still required to normalize these
    ITCs over the life of the assets and expects an additional $0.8 million of total amortization in
    2022-2025.

c. The Company is actively reviewing the wide range of flexible tax credits and other benefits
   from the IRA and, as such, the Company is still working on the analysis to be able to quantify
   further potential customer savings. For example, the Company’s evaluation of the IRA impacts
   on employee and retiree healthcare and prescription costs is in early stages and cannot be
   quantified at this time. Additionally, the Company is evaluating potential benefits for the
   Company’s electric vehicle purchases as well as the Hydro Modernization and Integrated
   Hydrogen Microgrid projects approved by the Commission in the 2022 IRP. Guidance is still
   needed to ensure proper implementations for all benefits, and the Company will continue to
   work with the Commission for customer impacts as it completes the analysis on these and any
   further identified benefits.
