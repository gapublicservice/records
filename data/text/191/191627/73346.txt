STF-LA-5-11

Question:

During 2020, 2021, and to-date in 2022, did any of GPC's coal suppliers with whom GPC had coal supply contracts, declare force majeure events that excused having to make coal deliveries?  If so, please identify and explain each such instance. Also describe how that affected GPC 's access to coal supply and the delivery of coal to each of GPC 's generating plants, and whether and how it impacted GPC 's coal inventory and coal procurement decision making.

Response:

On August 14, 2021, REDACTED declared force majeure on its REDACTED (“REDACTED”) due to elevated carbon monoxide readings. The REDACTED consists of two separate underground longwall mines (M-Class and Viking). Both mines were under force majeure until February 9, 2022, at which time the Viking mine was able to resume production. Since that time, the entire REDACTED has been limited to 50% coal production and continues to be limited with no estimation as to when the M-Class mine will be back in production. Due to the limitation of production at the REDACTED, Plants REDACTED and REDACTED have experienced reduced coal deliveries since August 2021. As a result, the Company has had to procure additional coal during this period in order to supplement the unfilled large coal commitment under contract with REDACTED. The additional purchases were needed to help provide reliability of unit operations and reach desired coal inventory levels at Plants REDACTED and REDACTD.

STF-LA-5-12

Question:

During 2020, 2021, and to-date in 2022, has GPC experienced disruptions in getting contracted coal supplies delivered to any of its generating plants?  If so, please identify and explain each such instance and also describe how that affected GPC 's coal inventory levels and GPC 's planning for and management of coal inventory at each of its plants.

Response:

During 2020 and through the first part of 2021, the Company experienced minimal disruptions in getting contracted coal supplies delivered to any of its generating plants. During that period, domestic coal demand was very weak and the Company’s plants maintained healthy inventories. The impact of COVID 19 and the accelerated emergence of wind and solar energy continued to apply downward pressure on coal markets and coal burns. Coal producers and transportation providers continued to downsize their organizations as they looked to a future with reduced coal demand. As gas prices started to increase toward the end of 2021, coal burns also started to increase. With an already tight coal supply, it became apparent that coal producers as well as transportation providers would be unable to meet the unanticipated rise in coal demand. As the fall of 2021 approached inventories at the Company’s plants began to decline as transportation providers continued to struggle to provide adequate service to maintain desired inventory levels. As a result, the Company began curtailing coal generation to conserve coal inventories at its plants in preparation for winter 2022 demand. As 2022 began, coal production remained constrained as labor and transportation issues for coal producers and transportation providers continued. Curtailment practices continued throughout the year to prepare for summer demand.  REDACTED.

Energy Storage Credits:  The IRA provides Investment Tax Credits (“ITC”) for standalone energy storage systems, no longer requiring it to be paired with and charged by solar generation. The Company believes the 65 MW Mossy Branch battery storage project, which is projected to be on-line in 2023, will qualify for a 30% ITC. The IRA also gives an “opt-out” from the existing ITC normalization, which permits the Company to recognize the full ITC benefit in the year the project goes into service, thereby providing a benefit to customers by lowering the levelized cost of electricity over the life of the project as compared to the cost under the existing ITC normalization option. In order to take advantage of this “opt out” option offered in the IRA, the Commission must specifically approve the “opt-out” option. The Company’s calculation above assumes that the full $31.7 million of estimated credit resulting from the normalization opt-out option would be recognized in 2025.

Solar Credits:  The IRA provides a PTC option, which may be utilized instead of the existing normalized ITC for solar investments. The Company estimates the PTC option will provide an approximate $0.4 million of additional benefits for customers in 2025 on projected self-build ash pond solar investments compared to the normalized ITC option.     

EV Charger Credits: The IRA extends and modifies the credit for alternative fuel refueling property. This 30% ITC applies to property placed in service through 2032 and increases the credit limitation to $100,000 per EV charger. The Company is still required to normalize these ITCs over the life of the assets and expects an additional $0.8 million of total amortization in 2022-2025.

The Company is actively reviewing the wide range of flexible tax credits and other benefits from the IRA and, as such, the Company is still working on the analysis to be able to quantify further potential customer savings. For example, the Company’s evaluation of the IRA impacts on employee and retiree healthcare and prescription costs is in early stages and cannot be quantified at this time. Additionally, the Company is evaluating potential benefits for the Company’s electric vehicle purchases as well as the Hydro Modernization and Integrated Hydrogen Microgrid projects approved by the Commission in the 2022 IRP. Guidance is still needed to ensure proper implementations for all benefits, and the Company will continue to work with the Commission for customer impacts as it completes the analysis on these and any further identified benefits.
