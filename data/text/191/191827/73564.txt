Q.Please describe your educational background.  
A.I received a Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering from Virginia Tech in 1982, a Master of Science in Electrical and Computer Engineering from North Carolina State University in 1990 and an Executive Master of Business Administration from the University of North Carolina – Chapel Hill in 2000. 
Q.Please describe your professional background prior to founding UMS. 
A.From June of 1982 through December of 1997, I worked in a variety of customer service, engineering and management roles at Carolina Power & Light Company (“CP&L”).  In my first position at CP&L, I was responsible for providing customer service, rate analysis, rate consulting and contract administration services to industrial, governmental and larger commercial customers in several counties in northern North Carolina.  
By the end of my career at CP&L, I managed a workforce of 240 employees and 240 contractors.  These individuals were responsible for providing customer service to 330,000 customers in 19 counties in eastern North Carolina as well as designing, building, operating and maintaining the distribution system throughout that 10,000 square mile territory.  These individuals were also engaged in providing rate consulting services to our customers. 

Q.Do you have any professional designations?
A.Yes.  I am a registered Professional Engineer (Certificate # 13728) in North Carolina.  I am also certified as a Certified Energy Manager (# 12712), a Certified Energy Auditor (# 1767), a Certified Demand Side Management Professional (# 1180) and a Certified Energy Procurement Professional (# 1166) by the Association of Energy Engineers.  A copy of my current CV is attached as Exhibit 1. 

Q. What services does UMS provide?
A.UMS is an electric bill auditing and rate consulting company.  We enter into agreements with our clients which establish UMS as the customer’s agent.  As their agent, we work to reduce our clients’ electric expenses by identifying billing errors, overcharges and other savings opportunities.  
We also advocate on behalf of our customers to increase the fairness of electric rates and/or service regulations. This typically includes advocating for more pricing options and flexibility through time-of-use rates, dynamic pricing rates, real-time-pricing rates, curtailable/interruptible, critical peak pricing rates and more.   

We are in our 25th year in business.  We are one of the largest businesses of our type.  We have worked with approximately 10,000 business customers with approximately 500,000 electric service accounts.  This includes working with customers in the Georgia Power service territory as well as working with customers in more than 40 states.    

Q.Have you testified before state regulatory commissions in the past?
A.Yes.  I have testified in front of other state regulatory commissions in VA, NC and SC on numerous occasions. 

Q.Who are you representing in this rate case?
A.RTC Investments, LLC (RTC).  
Q.What is RTC Investments, LLC?
A.RTC is a company that owns and operates four restaurants in Georgia.  Along with their sister companies, they own and operate a total of 13 restaurants.  Their restaurant brands include Captain D’s, Arby’s and Huddle House. 
Q.Please describe the electrical loads used by RTC electrical accounts.
A. The loads include refrigerators, freezers, ice machines, fountain drink machines, air conditioning, heating, ventilation, lighting, cooking equipment and the other types of loads that would typically be associated with running a restaurant.  

Q.Do restaurants have somewhat unique or unusual operating characteristics?
A.Yes.  Restaurants have high load factors and an unusually large amount of off-peak energy usage.  Restaurants are typically open seven days per week.  They typically serve three meals per day.  Therefore, they have very long running hours.  In addition, since the largest loads in restaurants tend to be refrigerators, freezers, ice machines and other refrigeration equipment, their largest loads continue to operate even when the restaurant is closed.  The operating characteristics described above tend to make restaurants have high load factors and a lot of off-peak energy usage.

Q.Compared to other industry types, is the restaurant industry unusually energy intensive?
A.Yes.  According to the U.S. Energy Information Administration and the Association of Energy Engineers, the restaurant industry uses more energy per square foot than any other industry type.  

Q.What relief is RTC requesting in this rate case proceeding?
A.RTC is requesting that the Time-of-Use – Food and Drink (TOU-FD) rate be modified so that new qualifying customers can continue to enroll in the rate.  The wording change in the rate that is being requested is to eliminate the sentence  “Qualifying accounts will be accepted on TOU-FD on a first come, first allowed basis until the number of accounts equals 6,000 or until December 31, 2022, whichever comes first.” at the end of the second paragraph under the Applicability section of the rate. 

RTC has plans to open additional restaurants.  They wish to be able to take advantage of the TOU-FD rate for those restaurants.   

Q.What is the history of the TOU-FD rate?
A.The TOU-FD rate was formerly known as the Time-of-Use – Multiple Business (TOU-MB) rate.  Until January 1, 2011, the TOU-MB rate was available to groups of restaurants, hotels or other businesses that had multiple locations throughout the GA Power service territory that were operating under the same ownership or brand name.  To qualify for the rate, the KW demands at the multiple restaurants or hotels had to collectively add up to 3,000 KW.  For example, if all the McDonald’s restaurants or Holiday Inn hotels in the territory added up to more than 3,000 KW, each of the McDonald’s restaurants or Holiday Inn hotels would qualify for the rate regardless of who owned the particular franchise.  

On January 1, 2011, the TOU-MB rate was modified such that only “Food Services and Drinking Places” qualified to receive service under the rate. The 3,000 KW aggregate load requirement still applied.  Therefore, large chains like McDonald’s, Burger King, etc. qualified for the rate but smaller chains and mom and pop restaurants did not qualify.  

On January 1, 2020, the TOU-MB rate was replaced with the TOU-FD rate.  The TOU-FD rate grandfathered any existing customer on the TOU-MB rate to be able to continue to receive service under the TOU-FD rate.  The 3,000 KW aggregate load requirement was eliminated.  This important revision made many thousands of additional restaurants eligible to receive service under the new TOU-FD rate.  This includes many small chains of restaurants as well as many individual mom and pop restaurants that would not have met the 3,000 KW aggregate load requirement under the TOU-MB rate.

Q.Is more time needed to analyze the TOU-FD rate before it becomes available on an uncapped basis?
A.No.  The rate has existed for more than 12 years.  It has been serving approximately 3,000 customers for many years.  There has been ample time to test and analyze the rate to make sure that it does not create any surprises or produce unfair subsidization of the restaurant industry.    

Q.Why hasn’t the number of restaurants on the TOU-FD rate not grown more quickly over the last two years?
A.COVID.  Two months after the new TOU-FD rate became available to small restaurant chains and mom and pop restaurants, COVID drastically changed the restaurant industry.  Restaurants were closing throughout Georgia. According to the below Washington Post article, a copy of which is attached hereto as Exhibit 2, the National Restaurant Association estimates that 90,000 restaurants in the U.S. closed permanently just during the first year of COVID.  
Here’s how many restaurants closed during the pandemic - The Washington Post

The article shown at the below link, and attached hereto as Exhibit 3, lists 51 restaurants just in Atlanta that closed permanently just in the first year of COVID.  
The Atlanta Restaurants and Bars That Have Permanently Closed During the COVID-19 Pandemic - Eater Atlanta

Given the many restaurants that permanently closed in the GA Power service territory during COVID, some of which were receiving service under TOU-FD, the fact that the overall number of customers on the TOU-FD rate has grown during COVID is strong evidence that restaurants are interested in this rate.

Q.Have restaurant owners and managers faced many challenges during COVID?
A.Yes.  Customers stopped going to restaurants for inside dining.  Restaurants were having to adapt and adjust to curbside service.  Restaurants were having trouble maintaining staffing levels. Restaurants were struggling with supply chain issues.  

As COVID slowly fades away, restaurants in GA are continuing to have trouble finding and retaining qualified employees to run their operations.  Restaurant managers and owners were filling in as dishwashers, hostesses, servers, bus persons, bartenders and other functions that they were previously able to hire others to perform.  

With these large challenges and issues to address, many restaurant owners and managers have been busy just keeping their doors open.  Many have not had the time to devote to analyzing their electric rate options and making sure they were on the best available rate.  

If the TOU-FD rate is allowed to continue to be available to new customers, many more restaurants will enroll in TOU-FD as COVID fades away and our economy returns to a more normal situation.  
Q.Is the TOU-FD rate providing a fair rate of return for GA Power?
A.Yes.  The Average $/kWh revenue data by rate class shown in the tables below was copied from Georgia Power’s document M.F.R. Item – F-4 provided by GA Power witness Larry T. Legg in this rate case proceeding:

The revenue received through the TOU-FD rate is 3.33% higher 
((.1024-.0991)/.0991)) than the revenue received from All Rates & Groups combined.   

The revenue received through the TOU-FD rate is lower than the revenue received per kWh from the overall Total Medium Business group to which the TOU-FD rate belongs.  This is to be expected.  The restaurants and hotels receiving service under the TOU-FD rate have much higher overall load factors, and more off-peak usage, than the typical other customers within the Total Medium Business group.  This high load factor and high amount of off-peak usage creates a lower overall cost/kWh for restaurants and hotels.  If the customers on the TOU-FD rate were transferred to one of the other rates in the Total Medium Business group (PLM or TOU-GSD), the former TOU-FD customers would continue to have an average cost/kWh that is lower than the overall Total Medium Business group due to their higher than class average load factors.  

As the above table shows, GA Power has proposed a percentage increase of 5.76% for customers on the TOU-FD rate.  This is higher than the overall increase for All Rates & Groups of 5.55%.  It is lower than the overall increase of 5.94% proposed for the Total Medium Business group.  If GA Power had felt that the TOU-FD rate was not providing a fair return to their shareholders, they could have proposed correcting that problem in this rate case proceeding.

Q.Does the TOU-FD rate result in restaurants being subsidized by other ratepayers?
A.No.  See above question and answer for details.  

Q.Does closing the TOU-FD rate to new participants create an unfair advantage to the 3,027 customers who are already receiving service under the rate?

A.Yes.  These 3,027 customers will be grandfathered under a rate that is not available to new customers.  This provides the existing customers an unfair competitive advantage over new restaurants.  

Q.If the restaurant industry needs a time-of-use based rate, can’t they just enroll for service under the TOU-GSD rate?
A.No.  In most cases, the TOU-GSD rate is not very attractive or practical for restaurants.  The TOU-GSD rate has a very high on-Peak kW charge of $16.94/kW.  This is during the hours of 2 PM to 7 PM during the summer months.  Restaurants are typically serving dinner between 6 PM to 7 PM and often hit their highest peak demands during those hours.  

Q.If, for any reason, the PSC is not comfortable with completely eliminating the enrollment cap on this rate, is there a middle ground?

A.Yes.  If the PSC is not comfortable with fully eliminating the enrollment cap on the rate, they could eliminate the words “or until December 31,2022, whichever comes first” from the last sentence of the second paragraph in the Applicability section.  This would eliminate the December 31, 2022 enrollment deadline while still have a participation cap of 6,000 customers on the rate.  

Q.        Will keeping TOU-FD open permanently provide an economic benefit to the State of Georgia and Its Residents?  
A.        Yes, according to the Georgia Department of Labor, the Accommodation and Food Service Industry is the third largest source of jobs in Georgia.  According to GA DOL data, this industry segment had 442,849 employees in the 4th quarter of 2019.  In the first quarter of 2022 (most recent data available), this industry group was down to 406,967 employees.   That is a net drop of 35,882 jobs in Georgia. As the below graph clearly shows, this industry group had the largest overall impact on employment from COVID.  Employment in this vital industry has not rebounded to pre-covid levels.  

Continuing to offer the TOU-FD rate to this important industry segment will help the industry continue to recover from the impacts of COVID and continue to provide jobs for the people of Georgia.  

Q.Does this conclude your testimony?
A.Yes.  Thank you for your consideration.
