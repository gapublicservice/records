Question:

Refer to Minimum Filing Requirement F-1, “6.20_RTP-DA-7 Tracked.doc” and pricing methodology described as 

“Hourly prices are determined each day based on projections of the hourly running cost of incremental generation (including approved environmental costs), provisions for losses, projections of hourly transmission costs and reliability capacity costs for each day (when applicable), and a three (3) mill/kWh recovery factor.  The amount of fuel charges from hourly incremental kWh usage are applied to the recovery of fuel cost at the hourly average marginal fuel cost for the applicable hour.”

Please confirm that the Company forecasts its incremental RTP price on a monthly basis to derive the retail revenue forecast for the test year.  Please explain.
Does the Company forecast the various revenue components of its RTP rates including incremental generation costs, transmission, capacity, and recovery factor?  Please explain.
Please confirm that the forecasts provided in “STF-JKA Informal Request - RTP Attachment TRADE SECRET.xlsb” tab “Inputs” column V is inclusive of the 3 mill/kWh recovery factor.  If not, please explain why not.

Response:

Real Time Pricing - Day Ahead (“RTP-DA”) prices are forecasted on an hourly basis. The hourly forecasted RTP-DA prices are used to develop average monthly forecasted RTP-DA prices and are multiplied by the forecasted monthly RTP-DA energy, by class, to determine the forecasted RTP-DA monthly retail revenues by class.

Yes, the Company forecasts all components of RTP-DA prices, which are included in the RTP-DA price forecast.

Yes, the forecasts provided in the Company’s response to “STF-JKA Informal Request – RTP Attachment TRADE SECRET.xlsb” tab “Inputs” column V include the 3 mill/kWh recovery factor associated with RTP-DA prices.

STF-JKA-5-3

Question:

Refer to “STF-JKA-1-2 Attachment R Update 2022-25 TRADE SECRET.xlsx” and the tab: “FlatBill.”
Please provide a narrative explanation of the derivation of the old residential and commercial premiums.  
Please provide a narrative explanation of the derivation of the new residential and commercial premiums.  
Please confirm that flat bill premiums are included in the class average rate curves derived in EViews. 
Please confirm that the flat bill premiums included in the class average rate curves needed to be removed and replaced with updated premiums. 
Please provide the derivation of the flat bill premiums in this tab, please provide the source workpapers with all formulas intact.
Please explain what changes in flat bill tariff offerings are included in the forecast.  Has the Company assumed additional subscriptions and customers taking advantage of this program?  Please describe commercial trends separate from residential trends.
Please provide the percent premiums collected each month/year since the flat bill tariff option was approved.  Please explain if the premiums are consistent across each rate class in a given month/year and when premiums are changed.

Response:

Forecast of flat bill premiums are developed in each annual budget cycle. The forecasts of residential and general services flat bill premiums are calculated in Excel rows 21 and 41, respectively, in the “Monthly Revenues” tab in STF-JKA-5-3 Attachment. These values are derived for each customer class from monthly estimates of the average bill multiplied by the 5% risk premium multiplied by the forecasted projections of customers. The monthly estimates of the average bill for each customer class are calculated by multiplying usage and fuel rates by estimated weather-normal monthly energy use. 

The “Old Flat Bill Premiums” in columns D and E in tab “FlatBill” of STF-JKA-1-2 Attachment R Update 2022-25 TRADE SECRET.xlsx,” are the prior budget flat bill risk premiums. During each annual budget cycle, calculations for the development of the new revenue budget include an adjustment for changes in the forecast of flat bill premiums. This is performed for residential and commercial classes by removing the prior budget flat bill risk premiums and adding the updated premiums for the current budget cycle. 

The “New Flat Bill Premiums” in columns F and G, in tab “FlatBill” of STF-JKA-1-2 Attachment R Update 2022-25 TRADE SECRET.xlsx”, are the current budget flat bill risk premiums. As described in the response to question (a) above, in each annual budget cycle, calculations for the development of the new revenue budget include taking the prior budget flat bill risk premium, backing it out of the base tariff Residential and Commercial classes, then adding on the base rate increase, the recovery rider tariffs, and lastly the new flat bill risk premium for the new budget year. 

Yes, flat bill premiums are embedded in the actual history of the class average rate curves.

Yes, flat bill premiums included in the class average rate curves need to be removed and replaced with updated premiums. Since the actual flat bill premiums are embedded in actual history of the class average rate curves of the base tariff revenues, they need to be removed before applying the base rate increase (when applicable), the recovery rider tariffs, and the new flat bill risk premium for the new budget year.

Please see STF-JKA-5-3 Attachment.

Please see Excel row 7 in the “Monthly Revenues” tab in STF-JKA-5-3 Attachment for forecasted projections of residential customers and Excel row 30 for general service. The forecasted customers were based on estimated future campaigns, historical take rates of campaigns, and historical churn.

The risk premium for the Flat Bill forecast is 5% across all rate classes and in every month. This has remained unchanged since the Flat Bill tariff was approved.

STF-JKA-5-9

Question:

Refer to “STF-JKA-1-2 Attachment M TRADE SECRET.xlsx” tab “Historical” row 348.  Please explain this significantly higher sales and revenue for October 2018.  Please provide any available memos, workpapers, and descriptions explaining this behavior. 

Response:

Fluctuations in historical load and revenues are normal and to be expected. Therefore, the Company does not ordinarily track or keep records for monthly variances. On a limited basis, and when determined appropriate, an observable variance may be investigated. In the case of the higher sales and revenue for October 2018, the Company did perform such an investigation and determined that the higher Governmental Lighting sales and revenue reported in October 2018 resulted from a billing delay with respect to a large customer from June through September 2018. The customer’s October bill included current and accumulated energy and revenue amounts for the months in which there was a billing delay, which resulted in higher sales and revenue numbers for the class in October 2018.  

Please note that historical sales and revenues have no impact on the Governmental Lighting revenues used in the rate case for the test year or the years 2023-2025. A narrative of the method used to produce the Governmental Lighting energy and revenue forecasts has been provided in the Company’s response to STF-JKA-1-2 Attachment L.
