Question:

Supplemental Executive Retirement Plan (SERP).

Please provide the level of SERP expense, by account, included in the Company's cost of service for the test year ending July 31, 2023. Include with your response all charges to GPC from SCS and/or SNOC for SERP expense.

Please provide the level of SERP expense, by account, included in the Company's cost of service for each plan year 2023, 2024 and 2025. Include with your response all charges to GPC from SCS and/or SNOC for SERP expense in each rate plan year.

Please provide comparable SERP expense recorded by GPC, by account, for calendar years 2020, 2021 and 2022 to date.

Provide the two most recent actuarial reports for SERP.

Identify and provide all actuarial studies, reports and estimates used for SERP for the test year ending July 31, 2023, as well as for calendar years 2023, 2024 and 2025.

Identify by job title and company all current GPC and SCS and SNOC affiliated employees and executives that are included in the SERP.

Identify by former highest job title and pre-retirement company affiliation for all retired GPC and SCS and SNOC affiliated company employees and executives that are included in the SERP.

Are any of the SERP amounts related to employee contracts with current or former executives? If so, explain fully and provide a copy of the employment agreements.

Response:

The Company’s non-qualified pension plans include both the Supplemental Benefit Plan (“SBP”) and SERP. For the purposes of this response, the Company is also including SBP information in its reference to SERP.

a-c. Please see the Company’s response to STF-LA-2-71 Attachment A Excel rows 9 and 12 for Georgia Power’s SERP expense by account included in its cost of service for the requested periods. Please note, the SERP service cost in Excel row 9 is subject to capitalization, and the capitalized portion is included in the total capitalized pension costs in Excel row 13. 

Please see STF-LA-7-2 Attachment A for SERP costs allocated to Georgia Power from SNC in 2020 and 2021, and projected for the test year ending July 31, 2023, and years 2023 through 2025.  Starting with calendar year 2022, the actual SERP costs allocated to Georgia Power from SNC are embedded in the total allocated pension costs, which include both qualified and non-qualified pension costs.  Therefore, it is not feasible to identify the specific SERP costs allocated from SNC for 2022. 

SCS SERP costs are embedded in the total pension costs allocated to Georgia Power from SCS. The pension costs are a part of a broader benefits cost center, which are allocated to Georgia Power based on direct labor charges billed to Georgia Power from SCS. Therefore, it is not feasible to identify the specific SERP costs allocated from SCS.

d. Please see STF-LA-7-2 Attachments B and C. Please note, the Company removed from the attachments information that is not specific to Georgia Power. 

e. Please see the Company’s response to STF-LA-2-71 Attachment B.

f-g. Please see STF-LA-7-2 Attachment D. There are a total of 150 active employees for Georgia Power that receive nonqualified pension plans (SBP and SERP). Due to the number of employees, the Company is providing employee count by pay grade levels and general description of positions for each grade level rather than providing individual job titles of all the employees. The Company does not have this information for SCS and SNC, nor does it keep track of the highest job titles held by retirees who qualify for these pension plans. It can be reasonably assumed that the SCS and SNC employees as well as Georgia Power retirees who receive these pension plans hold or have held positions within these pay grade levels.

h. The Company has two employees with deferred compensation agreements under the SERP.  Please see STF-LA-7-2 Attachments E and F for copies of these agreements.
