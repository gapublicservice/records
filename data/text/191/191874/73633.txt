 1        CATEGORY. IS THIS A REASONABLE APPROACH TO JUSTIFY THE

 2        REPLACEMENT OF AGING TRANSMISSION ASSETS?

 3   A.   No. Using the Company-defined expected useful life is not a reasonable and cost-effective

 4        approach to determining the replacement of the transmission infrastructure. The

 5        Company’s primary focus is on the age of the assets to develop the transmission

 6        replacement plan and uses a Companywide grouping method to identify the highest

 7        priority replacement based on age. The Company is employing a practice of proactively

 8        rounding up (grouping or clustering) the transmission assets for replacement based on age,

 9        not whether such assets are operating reliably and safely, and simply because they have

10        exceeded their Company-defined expected useful life. The useful life can be used as a

11        starting point for screening. Still, it must not be used solely in decision-making to target a

12        transmission infrastructure for replacement. Replacement of aging infrastructure can

13        reduce operations and maintenance expenses slightly, but prematurely replacing

14        infrastructure before it shows signs of reduced performance or failure results in

15        accelerated and unnecessary capital spending, which is not cost-effective. Identifying the

16        highest priority replacements must be based on monitoring, inspections, and objective test

17        data and analytics, not just because such facilities meet or exceed the Company-defined

18        useful life criterion.

 1        IV. CONCLUSIONS - SUMMARY AND RECOMMENDATIONS


 2   Q.     PLEASE SUMMARIZE YOUR TESTIMONY

 3   A.     As part of the Company's GIP, the Company will replace transmission lines prematurely.

 4          The practice of replacing equipment solely based on its age is not cost-justified and

 5          inefficient. This is because the GIP plan will:


 6             •     Replace transmission assets that are healthy and functioning safely and reliably
 7                   and have some life left in them. This practice will increase the replacement cost
 8                   unnecessarily,
 9             •     Not deliver customer reliability improvements sufficient to outweigh
10                   corresponding rate increases,
11             •     Not reduce O&M expenditures significantly,
12             •     Divert investment dollars away from funds needed in other important areas.
13
14          Instead, the replacement of transmission lines must continue based on the objective testing

15          and inspection practices the Company is already using. These preventive, corrective, and

16          condition-based maintenance practices allow the Company to determine the appropriate

17          times for transmission line replacement. The Company’s ongoing Capital Maintenance

18          program and associated corrective maintenance for transmission replacement are simple to

19          implement and highly predictable, which will result in transmission assets at risk receiving

20          the full attention and priority they merit. Furthermore, this approach will make the most

21          efficient use of the transmission investment dollars while ensuring the system operation is

22          safe and reliable and will prevent resources from being diverted away from more critical

23          areas.
