Q.WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF YOUR TESTIMONY?
A.The purpose of my testimony is to respond to Georgia Power Company’s (Company or Georgia Power) 2022 Rate Case filing as it relates to the Company’s proposed transmission lines investment as part of the Grid Investment Plan (GIP). The other aspects of the GIP relating to the distribution lines and the transmission and distribution (T&D) station equipment are addressed in the testimony of Staff Witnesses Messrs. Paul J. Alvarez and Dennis Stephen. Many of the themes in their testimony relevant to transmission are repeated in my testimony and vice versa.
Q.WHAT IS YOUR PRIMARY CONCLUSION?
A.As explained in the rest of my Testimony, the Company is proposing to invest about $3.6 billion for transmission lines and associated station equipment during the three year period 2023-2025 on behalf of the customers. This represents a significant increase of 360% of over the annual transmission expenditure for the 2009-2021 period.  The Company proposes to invest about $1.2 billion per year in transmission under the GIP over 2023 – 2025 as part of these massive transmission investments. Under the Grid Investment Plan (GIP) for 2023-2025, the Company is proposing to use the Company-defined age criteria solely to replace transmission lines and associated station equipment prematurely irrespective of their actual operating performance. Furthermore, the assets slated for replacement have passed periodic, objective tests and inspections and performing reliably. The industry standard and most economical way to replace transmission lines and the associated equipment is based on the approaches that the Company is successfully applying now under the Capital Maintenance programs. These approaches involve using performance metrics that are developed based on the results of the objective tests, monitoring, and data analysis to identify equipment for replacement. The GIP will not improve reliability for customers and will only reduce O&M costs only slightly. Instead, the GIP will unnecessarily impact the ratepayers significantly as the rates will increase.  I will explain why premature equipment replacement is inappropriate, more costly, and unnecessary and recommend all proposed investments under GIP be rejected.

OVERVIEW OF THE TRANSMISSION SYSTEM
Q.PLEASE PROVIDE AN OVERVIEW OF THE COMPANY’S TRANSMISSION SYSTEM.
A.As of December 31, 2020, Georgia Power’s transmission system consisted of 46kV (2,756 miles), 69kV (105 miles), 115kV (5,831 miles), 230kV (2,482 miles), and 500kV (1,154 miles) lines totaling approximately 12,328 miles. In addition, the Company also owns approximately 1,776 transmission and distribution substations. This transmission system and other Integrated Transmission System (ITS) transmission facilities connected approximately 14,413 MW of the Company’s installed generating capacity. The total Company residential, commercial, and industrial peak demand served in 2021 was approximately 16,214 MW. 
Q.PLEASE SUMMARISE THE TRANSMISSION LINES-RELATED REQUEST FOR WHICH THE COMPANY IS SEEKING APPROVAL IN THIS PROCEEDING.
A.The Company’s primary focus is replacing its aging transmission lines and the corresponding station equipment. In this regard, the Company is seeking to recover its transmission capital investments since 2019 and those planned for replacement in the next three years (2023 – 2025) as part of the GIP and proposing to add a few new transmission lines. The Company is also seeking to recover the corresponding depreciation expense. As part of the proposal, the Company is requesting a total of about $1.2 billion per year or $3.6 billion for transmission capital expenditures for the next three years.
TRANSMISSION CAPITAL PLAN AND GRID INVESTMENT PLAN
Q.PLEASE DESCRIBE THE COMPONENTS OF THE COMPANY’S GRID CAPITAL PLAN.
A.The Grid Capital Plan includes a request of about $2.3 billion for transmission and distribution per year over the next three years (2023-2025), of which transmission is about $1.2 billion and distribution is about $1.1 billion per year. The Company has defined six types of investments as part of this proposed transmission grid expenditure plan. These categories (types) include:
- New Business: Facilities required to provide service to new customers and new load.
- Growth/Load Flow: facilities required to address customer load growth and changing generation impacts. This includes new distribution feeders, new transmission lines and substations, and land for future use.
- Commitments: Facilities required for projects such as Department of Transportation relocations.
- Reliability: Facility upgrades that include substation relay modifications, replacement of obsolete equipment, insulation and grounding improvements, and grid modernization projects.
- Capital Maintenance: Includes replacement of broken poles, replacement of deteriorating conductor with an equivalent conductor, and replacing damaged or failed transformers and other equipment. This also includes cycle-based asset replacement programs that replace decaying poles, transformers, reclosers, and substation breakers.
- Grid Investment Plan: Includes a multi-year plan to strategically increase expenditures primarily in the Capital Maintenance category to mitigate the risk of outages associated with assets and infrastructure that will be past their expected useful lives by 2031.
The Company has been replacing aging infrastructure and obsolete equipment as part of the Reliability and Capital maintenance categories even before the introduction of the Grid Investment Plan in 2019 and continuing now.

A.As shown in Figure 1, the Company proposes a massive program to invest $6.8 billion in T&D or about $2.3 billion per year during the period January 2023 through December 2025, which is an increase of 44 percent from the three-year accounting order period (2020 – 2022) of $1.6 billion per year and a whopping increase of 164 percent from prior accounting periods (2013 – 2019) of about $870 million per year. The T&D portion is about 47.4 % of the total revenue requirement or 5.64% of the 11.9 % proposed increase, or $7.72 of the proposed rate increase of $16.29 per month.

The Company’s proposed increases for New Business and Growth / Load Flow should be more than sufficient to address growth in Georgia. The GIP is not necessary to address growth in Georgia.

Q.THE COMPANY HAS ALSO CLAIMED THAT AGING TRANSMISSION INFRASTRUCTURE IN GEORGIA PARTICULARLY IN METRO ATLANTA AREA IS ANOTHER REASON THE GIP INVESTMENT IS NECESSARY. DOES THE DATA IN TABLE 2 SUPPORT THAT ASSERTION?

The Company’s proposed increases for Reliability and Capital Maintenance should be more than sufficient to address any service issues in Georgia. The GIP is not necessary to address service issues in Georgia.

Q.WHAT IS THE BREAKDOWN OF THE 2023-2025 PERIOD REQUEST FOR TRANSMISSION LINES?
A.The Company plans to replace/rebuild a total of 137 miles, 74  miles, and 25 miles of lines in 2023, 2024, and 2025, respectively, and some transmission-related station equipment. The proposed capital expenditures are about $333 million per year for the 2023-2025 period under the GIP category. 
Q.THE COMPANY HAS FOCUSED ON THE AGE OF INFRASTRUCTURE AS A DETERMINING FACTOR TO JUSTIFY THE REPLACEMENT OF TRANSMISSION LINES AND THE ASSOCIATED EQUIPMENT THAT HAVE EXCEEDED THEIR COMPANY-DEFINED USEFUL LIFE UNDER THE GIP CATEGORY. IS THIS A REASONABLE APPROACH TO JUSTIFY THE REPLACEMENT OF AGING TRANSMISSION ASSETS?
A.No. Using the Company-defined expected useful life is not a reasonable and cost-effective approach to determining the replacement of the transmission infrastructure. The Company’s primary focus is on the age of the assets to develop the transmission replacement plan and uses a Companywide grouping method to identify the highest priority replacement based on age.  The Company is employing a practice of proactively rounding up (grouping or clustering) the transmission assets for replacement based on age, not whether such assets are operating reliably and safely, and simply because they have exceeded their Company-defined expected useful life. The useful life can be used as a starting point for screening. Still, it must not be used solely in decision-making to target a transmission infrastructure for replacement. Replacement of aging infrastructure can reduce operations and maintenance expenses slightly, but prematurely replacing infrastructure before it shows signs of reduced performance or failure results in accelerated and unnecessary capital spending, which is not cost-effective. Identifying the highest priority replacements must be based on monitoring, inspections, and objective test data and analytics, not just because such facilities meet or exceed the Company-defined useful life criterion. 
Q.WHAT CRITERIA DOES THE COMPANY USE TO JUSTIFY FACILITY REPLACEMENT UNDER THE GIP?
A.The criteria used for the transmission asset replacement by the Company are shown in Table 3. The age of the transmission line (conductor or structure or both) is compared to the Company’s thresholds for expected useful life to determine when a transmission line has exceeded the Company’s criteria for expected useful life. As noted above expected useful life is a reasonable screen to determine which transmission lines may be best candidates for replacement but actual performance metrics should be the deciding factor. The expected useful life for conductors and structures of transmission lines is developed by Company’s internal subject matter experts based on operational experience. The transmission line is a replacement candidate if the age of the transmission line under evaluation exceeds the Company’s criteria for expected useful life. However, the cut-off percentages used in these criteria are also developed by Company’s internal subject matter experts based on operational experience not supported by formal analysis. As part of the $10 billion grid reliability and resiliency initiative, the Company has installed new technologies, such as modern sensors, communications devices, and other hardware that allow transmission assets to be tracked and controlled remotely and collect health data remotely. New technologies also include analytical tools for processing, interpreting, and responding to data from transmission assets that use real-time performance data to guide asset replacement decisions rather than relying solely on the Company-defined useful life and aged-based asset replacement criteria without considering actual operating performance.

Q.IS THE COMPANY TAKING AN AGGRESSIVE APPROACH TOWARD TRANSMISSION INFRASTRUCTURE REPLACEMENT UNDER GIP?
A.Yes, the Company is taking an extremely aggressive approach to replacing transmission infrastructure under the GIP, which is unnecessary and results in steep rate increases for customers. The Company already has a replacement program under the Capital Maintenance and Reliability categories which is increasing further in 2023 - 2025.  Under these categories, the Company identifies and replaces assets requiring immediate replacement/repair based on objective inspections, tests, and other information.  The Company has relied on it in the past. It continues by using the industry-wide accepted, highly effective best practice of testing and inspecting each facility to identify the need for replacement. Such assets were repaired/rebuilt using informed decisions based on each facility's inspections, tests, and monitoring results rather than the age of the assets. As part of the GIP, the Company is clustering or grouping facilities to prioritize replacement by targeting the age of assets that exceeds the expected Company-defined useful life by 2031, irrespective of their actual operating performance.
Q.IS THE COMPANY’S APPROACH HARMFUL TO RATEPAYERS? 
A.Yes. This approach, from a financial perspective, is problematic in two ways : 1) the Company prematurely replacing assets that are performing safely and reliably with new ones that advance expenditures, and 2) unnecessarily resulting in a rate increase for the customers.
Q.HOW SHOULD THE COMPANY’S APPROACH TO GIP BE MODIFIED TO PROTECT RATEPAYERS? 
A.Transmission and distribution assets replacement prioritization must be based on objective testing and inspection results not just the age of the asset. This can be achieved by replacing assets using ongoing corrective maintenance (i.e., replacing/repairing an asset that has been damaged or has failed its tests or inspections or those rarely failed in service) as part of the Capital Maintenance and Reliability categories/plans. 
Q.DOES THE GIP DIVERT INVESTMENTS AWAY FROM WHERE THEY ARE MOST NEEDED?
A.All electric utilities are facing regulatory and public pressure to upgrade existing infrastructure while holding down costs and rates to customers and using the approved dollars wisely. The Company is requesting about $335 million per year for the next three years to proactively replace transmission assets based solely on age. The proposed GIP will dispose of some healthy, functional transmission assets and replace them with new ones, thus diverting investments from where they are most needed. On the other hand, if the Company continues to monitor and maintain its infrastructure transmission assets under the Capital Maintenance and Reliability categories and determines which assets are at the most significant risk of failure based on operating data and testing and where those failures would have the most significant impact, will result in transmission assets at risk receiving the full attention and priority they merit. This approach will prevent resources from being diverted away from more critical areas. It will also make the most efficient use of the Company’s transmission investment dollars while ensuring the system operation is safe and reliable. The Company’s ongoing routine-based Capital Maintenance program for maintenance, repair, and replacement is simple to implement, and highly predictable, which helps with planning spending and investments.
Q.WILL THE COMPANY’S MAINTENANCE COST BE REDUCED SIGNIFICANTLY WITH THE PROPOSED GIP?
A.No. The Company’s maintenance cost will not be reduced significantly with the proposed GIP. In most industries, such as manufacturing, revenue-generating assets are replaced due to increased maintenance costs or because newer assets have dramatic productivity improvements. The situation is different with the transmission asset management in electric utilities. There are no significant improvements in productivity, and maintenance cost is only slightly reduced with the new assets. The company’s primary focus is replacing the  infrastructure, and Operations and Maintenance (O&M) expense reduction is not the primary driver. The O&M expense reduction savings by replacing the transmission assets with newer ones is only expected to be about $6 million annually.

A.No.  In this Rate Case filing and discovery, the Company indicated that a cost/benefit study was not conducted for the proposed transmission investments. Furthermore, the Company did not perform the risk failure analysis using the historical failure data to justify the transmission assets replacements offered under the GIP.

CONCLUSIONS - SUMMARY AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Q.PLEASE SUMMARIZE YOUR TESTIMONY
A.As part of the Company's GIP, the Company will replace transmission lines prematurely. The practice of replacing equipment solely based on its age is not cost-justified and inefficient. This is because the GIP plan will:
Replace transmission assets that are healthy and functioning safely and reliably and have some life left in them. This practice will increase the replacement cost unnecessarily,
Not deliver customer reliability improvements sufficient to outweigh corresponding rate increases,
Not reduce O&M expenditures significantly,
Divert investment dollars away from funds needed in other important areas.

Instead, the replacement of transmission lines must continue based on the objective testing and inspection practices the Company is already using. These preventive, corrective, and condition-based maintenance practices allow the Company to determine the appropriate times for transmission line replacement. The Company’s ongoing Capital Maintenance program and associated corrective maintenance for transmission replacement are simple to implement and highly predictable, which will result in transmission assets at risk receiving the full attention and priority they merit. Furthermore, this approach will make the most efficient use of the transmission investment dollars while ensuring the system operation is safe and reliable and will prevent resources from being diverted away from more critical areas.
Q.WHAT RECOMMENDATIONS DO YOU MAKE? 
A. I recommend that the Commission require the Company to ensure that transmission assets replacement and rebuild projects are selected based on current health assessments and performance using physical inspections and objective tests under the ongoing Capital Maintenance and Reliability improvement plans, and not just based on the Company-defined age criterion. 
I recommend that the Commission enhance the oversight of approved transmission investment to ensure confidence that reasonable project implementation cost estimates are being made, ensure estimates are reasonable, and ensure that transmission builds are appropriately managed and completed within the 2023-2025 timeframe to contain costs consistent with initial cost estimates (i.e., avoid cost overruns during replacements).
Furthermore, I recommend that the Commission consider limiting the transmission costs exceeding estimates by a certain percentage to ensure that the Company's capital expenditures on transmission infrastructure are cost-effective and prevent cost escalation.
