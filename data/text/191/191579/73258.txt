LATE APPLICATION FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE OF
SOUTHERN RENEWABLE ENERGY ASSOCIATION

COMES NOW, the Southern Renewable Energy Association (“SREA”), pursuant to O.C.G.A. §§ 50-13-14 and 46-2-59 and Commission Rule 515-2-1-.06, and hereby files this Late Application for Leave to Intervene (“Application”) in the above-referenced docket. In support hereof, SREA respectfully shows as follows:
1.
On April 7, 2022, the Georgia Public Service Commission (“GPSC”) filed the Procedural and Scheduling Order (“PSO”) regarding Georgia Power Company’s (“GPC”, or “Company”) 2022 Rate Case in Docket Number 44280. In accordance with the PSO, the Company filed its rate case on June 24, 2022. On August 5, 2022, the GPSC filed its second amended PSO, setting the first hearing date as beginning on September 27, 2022.  Pursuant to that filing, SREA files this late application for leave to intervene and participate in this docket.  
SREA is filing this late application as allowed under the final and amended PSOs. This application is late due to several other competing priorities for SREA including the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on transmission planning (Docket RM21-17), concluding the Georgia Power Integrated Resource Plan, and other programmatic and administrative activities. SREA agrees to accept the record as developed to this point and does not seek to slow down or postpone this regulatory proceeding. 
2.
SREA is a regional trade association that works to promote the responsible development and use of utility-scale wind energy, energy storage, solar energy and transmission throughout the South. Georgia is one of eleven states in which SREA works. SREA members have a vital interest in the pending docket inasmuch as its members develop and deploy facilities to generate and produce energy, and those resources affect rates and are affected by rates. The Commission’s actions in this docket will affect development, deployment, or use of the various energy resources in the region and thus will affect the investment decisions made by members of SREA. Moreover, SREA members want to ensure that they can provide low cost energy resources in Georgia to reduce prices for consumers, and economic development for the state.  As a result, SREA has substantial interests in the outcome of this proceeding and desires to intervene in order to protect those interests.
3.
As required in the Application Requirements adopted by the PSC in the Procedural Schedule Order (PSO), SREA has reviewed the other intervening party applications including applications. SREA’s rights and interests cannot be adequately represented by any other party to the above-styled docket. SREA’s specific interests in utility-scale solar energy, wind energy, energy storage, hybrid resources and transmission are not represented in this docket. Its participation in this matter will not unduly delay these proceedings or prejudice any other party. 
4. 
SREA respectfully requests that it be granted leave to intervene and participate fully as a party in these proceedings, including the right to present testimony and exhibits, cross-examine witnesses, and file briefs.  SREA has participated in proceedings in Georgia previously, thus experience would enable efficiency and provide valuable information. Our expertise regarding utility-scale wind energy, energy storage, solar energy, transmission and utility resource planning would be valuable in this proceeding. SREA intends to participate in the docket by conducting cross examination and providing briefs and/or comments. At this point, SREA does not plan to submit witness testimony; however, we would like to preserve the right to do so. 
5.
