HR-1-5 Supplemental 

Information requested by:  Georgia Interfaith Power & Light – Whitfield (Tr. 364)

Question:
QDo you have any examples, Mr. Grubb, when PPAs have been unable to perform for Georgia Power, as they're contractually required to, in a way that has endangered customer load?
A(Witness Grubb) We would have to look at those.  I do know the company was able to perform better with company-owned resources in Winter Storm Elliott than PPAs did.  But, again, we would have to go back and look at what the outages were and those types of things.  But, again, not saying that they don't ever perform, but at the end of the day, there is a different reliability responsibility.
MS. WHITFIELD:  Chairman Shaw, could we make that a hearing request that they provide that PPA information?
CHAIRMAN SHAW:  Yes.
MR. HEWITSON:  Well, can I confirm what the hearing request is?
MS. WHITFIELD:  For evidence that PPAs have been unable to perform as they're contractually required in order to meet customer load.
MR. HEWITSON:  Based on what statement? Mr. Grubb said that there's a difference in reliability.  I don't think he said that they're unable to perform.  So I'm just trying to figure out how we respond to it.  It's an allegation made, asking us to respond.  If there's data that we can provide, I mean, is there --
MS. WHITFIELD:  It's not an allegation.  It's a question.  He said he wasn't sure.  Seems pretty important if this is sort of the legal basis of your request.
MR. HEWITSON:  Yeah.  But I'm asking for what is the data that we would need to refer -- I mean, a hearing request is for data.
MS. WHITFIELD:  Yes.  For the instances, the times when PPAs have been unable to perform, as contractually required and that's endangered customer load, the company's ability to meet customer load.
Response:
See the tables below for unavailable capacity, total capacity, and percentage of total capacity for which an unavailability event occurred during the periods of highest reliability risk during two recent winter storm events: Winter Storm Elliott in December 2022 and Winter Storm Heather in January 2024. This information is provided for Georgia Power’s aggregated capacity power purchase agreement (“PPA”) resources and Georgia Power’s owned capacity resources. 
Please note that an Energy Emergency Alert was declared by the Southern Balancing Authority in each winter storm event.
