As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-JKA-5-10 (“Response”). STF-JKA-5-10

Question:
Regarding real-time operations BESS charging and discharging: 
Does the Company have the logic completed to compute optimal dispatch for BESS projects.  What is the timeline for developing this logic? Please explain. 
Please discuss the various use cases the Company believes the BESS projects are capable of providing (e.g. economic arbitrage, grid services, load following, frequency control, etc.) 
Please explain the expected “State Of Charge” management practices the Company expects to utilize for the BESS projects and how they will determine the optimal usage case for the BESS. 
Please discuss the constraints of the various use cases with respect to “State of Charge” management. 
Please explain how the Company’s Aurora model for generic BESS resources in the Resource Mix Study aligns with the various use cases.  Please explain the Company’s utilization of a demand shaping model in Aurora.     
Please explain how the Company’s Aurora modeling for Moody, Robins, and REDACTED in the economic analysis aligns with the various use cases.  Please explain if the modeling assumes an economic arbitrage model dispatched against avoided cost, and if these resources would be operate in a demand shaping model in actual operations.

 
Response:
Yes. The Automated Generation Control (“AGC”) algorithm has been configured and is ready for operational testing. This algorithm uses data from the unit commitment and real-time processes to provide an optimal operational schedule for the Battery Energy Storage System (“BESS”) facility.
Generic BESS resources may be able to provide multiple use cases subject to the availability of transmission and general system conditions. The proposed BESS facilities can provide energy arbitrage benefits and capacity value benefits. During real time operations, the BESS may also be able to provide operating reserve benefits and similar benefits during periods when the adjacent solar resources are not utilizing the interconnection capacity. The Company’s economic analysis for BESS resources conservatively assumed only energy arbitrage and capacity value benefits. During real-time operations, system operators will evaluate system conditions and make the best decision on BESS operation based on system needs that occur during real-time or near real-time periods. 
Georgia Power does not expect that there will be a vendor specific state of charge (“SOC”) requirement for degradation warranty purposes, but one of the most important ways to ensure battery longevity is to maintain a SOC < 80% (recommendations by vendor can vary slightly). AGC operation can maintain an average SOC within any desired range. This metric can be tracked and, if necessary, manual adjustments can be made to the operational schedule. Additionally, if desired, the AGC algorithm can be placed in regulation mode whereby the SOC is maintained within a certain range and the BESS provides regulation services only. Regarding optimal use case determination, when on AGC, the BESS will automatically deviate from the day-ahead operational schedule in real-time for reliability or system needs based on economic evaluations.  When peak load hits a high threshold, the BESS will be held back and prioritized for system reliability concerns. In that case, the BESS will be maintained near full SOC until the reliability event is over.
If manual adjustments are made to the operational schedule for the sole purpose of SOC management or if the AGC system is placed in a mode where only regulation is utilized, there is the potential that some use cases may not be utilized for a period of time to ensure the desired SOC range is maintained. For example, the BESS may be temporarily limited in the ability for energy arbitrage if it is not desirable to have the BESS at low or high states of charge. However, at this time, it is not expected that this will be a routine occurrence.  
Generic BESS resources, as modeled in the Resource Mix Study, dispatch to mainly provide capacity value benefits. The Aurora model dispatches BESS to minimize the variance of the demand net storage and reduce overall volatility of the demand shape. Additionally, the generic BESS resource is modeled as a quick start resource to provide operating reserve benefits and frequency regulation benefits.
Please see the response to part (b). System operators will monitor system conditions and dispatch the battery to minimize costs and preserve system reliability during real time operations.

Question:
Regarding Fixed and Variable O&M Costs for proposed BESS resources.
Please explain what the VOM costs for BESS resources in the mix studies includes.
Please explain what VOM costs are assumed for the BESS resources in the economic analysis for REDACTED REDACTED.
Please reconcile parts a and b to explain if VOM assumptions are consistent between generic BESS in the mix study and VOM modeling for the economic analysis of REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED.
Please explain what the Fixed O&M costs for BESS resources in the mix studies includes.
Please explain what Fixed O&M costs are assumed for the BESS resources in the economic analysis for REDACTED REDACTED.
Please reconcile parts d and e to explain if Fixed O&M assumptions are consistent between generic BESS in the mix study and Fixed O&M modeling for the economic analysis of REDACTED REDACTED.

Response:
The Variable Operations and Maintenance (“VOM”) costs represent the difference in augmentation costs needed to maintain beginning of life capacity between a system with only calendar year degradation (the passive degradation incurred in the battery cells over time) and the additional degradation incurred by a 365 cycle per year operation schedule.
No VOM costs were assumed for the BESS resources in the economic analysis for Moody, Robins, or REDACTED. Rather, augmentation costs were modeled as maintenance capital costs in the economic analysis.
Both the economic analysis and the Resource Mix Study take BESS augmentation into account to maintain the capacity of the BESS through the end of the resource life.  The Resource Mix Study, or generic BESS, accounts for augmentation as a combination of annual capital maintenance outlays with an additional VOM component to account for BESS usage as mentioned in part (a) above.
The economic analysis accounts for all augmentation spending as periodic maintenance capital investments in the SAM model. The augmentation outlays for the economic analysis align with project specific expectations that can cause them to differ from the generic resources.
Fixed O&M costs in the mix studies include remote troubleshooting expense, degradation guarantees, 24/7 monitoring, insurance premiums, and labor required to manage the BESS system.
Fixed O&M costs in the economic analysis for the BESS resources mentioned include monitoring expenses, insurance, labor required to manage the BESS system, and service costs associated with maintaining the project substation and other power delivery equipment.   
The categorization of Fixed O&M costs assumed for the BESS resources in the various analysis are largely consistent. Differences in estimates for the specific resources mentioned and generic resources are primarily due to cost estimates being updated specifically for the proposed resources.
