STF-GS-2-1

Question:
Please confirm the below information requests. 
 
Please confirm that the Company has requested Energy Resource Interconnection Service (ERIS) and not Network Resource Interconnection Service (NRIS) for the proposed combustion turbine additions at Plant Yates. (If needed, please see IC-1166, IC-1167, and IC-1168 on page 2 here: https://www.oasis.oati.com/woa/docs/SOCO/SOCOdocs/Active-Gen-IC-Requests.pdf) 
Please confirm that ERIS is non-firm transmission service, in that generation using that type of service can be curtailed during periods of transmission system congestion. 
Please confirm that on Georgia Power’s transmission system, congestion often occurs during periods of peak need. 
Response:
The interconnection requests for IC-1166, IC-1167, and 1168 are ERIS.
ERIS is a type of interconnection service. Neither ERIS nor NRIS convey any delivery rights or grant any form of firm or non-firm transmission service. Firm transmission service will be separately obtained through the designation of the resource. 
Please see the Company’s responses to STF-GS-1-6 and STF-DEA-8-1. Transmission constraints occur in all seasons. As such, the Company studies both peak and off-peak and will develop transmission solutions for all constraints identified in analyses (e.g., peak or off-peak). These solutions can range from operating guides to full projects.

Question:
Has the Company assessed how curtailment risk from the use of ERIS will limit the capacity contribution of the proposed Plant Yates CTs? If so, what did the Company find? If not, why not? 


Response:
As noted in the Transmission Screen Analyses Overview in the Technical Appendix of the 2023 IRP Update filing, the Company plans to designate all 1,350 MW of proposed Plant Yates combustion turbines, and the facility will be limited to 600 MW of firm output until all identified transmission improvements are in-service by summer 2028. The requested energy resource interconnection service (“ERIS”) does not contribute to or increase the likelihood of transmission constraints or curtailments. Please refer to the Company’s response to STF-GS-2-1 part (b).

STF-GS-2-3

Question:
Please reconcile the Company’s statement in the Transmission Screening Analyses that “the 750 MW Mississippi Power Company PPA can be accommodated along with the PPA for capacity and energy from the Santa Rosa combined cycle facility located in northwest Florida with no required transmission projects,” with the Company’s conclusions in the Southwest Quadrant Stability report (available at: https://www.oasis.oati.com/woa/docs/SOCO/SOCOdocs/SOCO-Southwest_Quadrant_Stability.pdf). In particular, given the report’s conclusions that southern Mississippi and northwest Florida are in the Southwest Quadrant, which is “a stability-limited region which constrains the total amount of generation that can be simultaneously delivered from the area. The level of constraint is impacted by on-line generation, load, and transmission topology in the area.” and that “Substantial, long lead-time transmission improvements may be required to accommodate additional transmission service sourced in this area,” does the Company believe the Mississippi Power and Santa Rosa PPAs will be deliverable capacity resources that are not at risk of curtailment during periods of peak need? Please provide any analysis the Company has conducted assessing that risk. If the Company has not conducted that analysis, please explain why. 


Response:
The Santa Rosa Energy Facility and any Mississippi Power Company resources that are located in the Southwest Quadrant (“SWQ”) and supporting the 750 MW power purchase agreement (“PPA”) are existing resources that have been included in previous SWQ analysis. As such, their output has already been considered in the calculation of any limits. Additionally, the SWQ constraint occurs during light load and therefore does not pose a risk of curtailment during periods of peak system need.

BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE  
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET 
 
As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-GS-2-4 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided competitively sensitive details specific to the projected generation, capacity factors, fuel consumption, and carbon emissions for resources under contract, proposed to be acquired or developed. All of such information (the “Information”) constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11. 
 
 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information includes competitively sensitive details specific to the projected generation, capacity factors, fuel consumption, and carbon emissions for resources under contract, proposed to be acquired or developed. If the Information were made public, competitors, bidders, and suppliers could use the Information to unfairly manipulate the request for proposals process and competitive market to structure future bids and set an artificial price floor to arbitrarily increase prices to the detriment of the Company and its customers. Public dissemination of the Information would undermine Georgia Power’s ability to negotiate the best price and contract terms and could harm the Company’s ability to secure the best cost bids and resources for the benefit of customers. In addition, the terms of the agreements themselves provides for the confidentiality and protection of the Information. Compromising the confidentiality of such Information could harm Georgia Power in current or future negotiations, as counterparties may use the Information as a bargaining tool in negotiations or fear compelled disclosure of key contractual terms. Lastly, the Company’s competitors are not required to reveal or publish similar information and to require the Company to do so would put it at an economic disadvantage. 

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.

STF-GS-2-6

Question:

Please describe the methodological justification for providing multiple "tranches" for battery energy storage resources in the Aurora planning software, and any key data input assumptions that differentiate the various tranches. 

Response:
As shown in JKA-2-11 Attachment F, average and incremental capacity values for battery resources drop as penetration increases. Because the number of tranches has a considerable impact on the computational time necessary to solve each planning scenario, the decision was made to limit the number of tranches. The tranche sizes and the number of incremental MW represented by each tranche are also detailed in JKA-2-11 Attachment F. The final tranche capacity values are roughly equal to the average 1 GW incremental capacity values that each tranche represents. There are no modeling or key data input assumption differences between the four battery tranches, besides that the reliable capacity of each tranche reflects the resulting effective load carrying capability (“ELCC”) value, as detailed in JKA-2-11 Attachment F.

Question:
Provide the Area Control Error values on a minute basis for the dates from December 21, 2022 to December 28, 2022 and provide a brief narrative explaining the primary drivers regarding any ACE value in excess of 500 MW deviation. 

Response:
During the time frame of December 21, 2022 to December 28, 2022 there were four instances when the Southern Balancing Authority Area (“SBAA”) had Area Control Error (“ACE”) values in excess of 500 MW deviation. Those instances are denoted by red shading in STF-GS-2-9 Attachment, which provides the SBAA ACE values on a minute basis as requested. The days and times associated with those instances are as follows:
12/23/2022 06:26:00 – 06:27:00,
12/23/2022 23:15:00 – 23:20:00,
12/24/2022 00:20:00 – 00:21:00, and
12/24/2022 03:05:00 – 03:08:00.
For each instance, generating units within the SBAA that did not perform in the manner desired can be associated with the ACE deviations.
