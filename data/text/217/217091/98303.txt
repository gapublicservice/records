STF-DEA-6-1

Question:
Attachment A provided in response to Question 1-1 DEA provides a list of customer choice customers with kW associated.  During the technical session on December 6, 2023 Georgia Power Staff noted that a “threshold” had been established for commercial and industrial customers that were considered to not be “organic” growth in the standard load forecasting approaches.  The thresholds noted were “GP set a threshold of 45 MW for Industrial and 150 MW for commercial customers who would be in the customer choice group and be additive to the structured models so these are not organic”.  
Are these the correct thresholds based on our notes from the session?
Please comment on each of the customer addition opportunities listed in this response that are lower than the threshold amount and indicate why they are noted as customer choice?  
If the thresholds were set after 2022, please indicate what statistics are captured in the “historical analysis” noted during the technical session as the approach used to establish the distributions and success factors utilized in the 2023 LF update.
Attachment A to the response to Question 1-1 DEA is entitled Customer Choice projects and Attachment B is titled Economic Development which contains all the projects not locating in GP service territory – what is the difference between the two groupings and how is the data used in the development of the Customer Choice Model?

Response:
No. The thresholds used were 45 MW for the Industrial class and 115 MW for the Commercial class.
The threshold amount used for the external adjustment to the forecast does not define customer choice opportunities. As defined by the Georgia Territorial Electric Service Act, a project is eligible to be considered customer choice if the connected load is above 900 kW, and not located in an exclusively assigned territory, or if the project locates in specific overlapping corridors or territories. 
Since the load growth that is occurring is based on unprecedented economic growth in the state, the Company based its thresholds on the latest forecast rather than on historical data. The methodology used to develop these thresholds is discussed in the Company’s response to STF-DEA-5-1. 
Economic Development refers to potential customers who are considering locating in Georgia. Some projects may initially consider Georgia and elect to locate in another state and never progress to the point at which they can be considered for customer choice. After the potential customer selects Georgia, the project’s location and size determines whether or not the project is eligible to be considered customer choice. 
Refer to Section 1.5 of the Load and Energy Forecast Technical Appendix of the 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update for a discussion on how this data is used in the Load Realization Model.

BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-DEA-6-3 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided confidential data on the business plans of individual customers. All of such information (the “Information”) constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11. 
 
 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains competitively sensitive commercial and strategic information related to Georgia Power’s projected load forecasts and corresponding short- and long-term capacity needs. The Information also identifies the various projects and businesses anticipated to increase the Company’s load requirements. Finally, the Information identifies individual customer load opportunities specific to those projects and businesses.   

Public dissemination of the Information would allow Georgia Power’s competitors, suppliers, and customers access to the Company’s anticipated load strategies and objectives, thereby bestowing insight into the Company’s strategic initiatives and forecasting capabilities. Competitors would obtain an unfair advantage because they are not required to reveal similar information and can structure their offers for competitive choice opportunities based on the Information rather than their own costs, capacity, abilities, strategies, or analysis. Prospective bidders may artificially set their bids and proposal prices to their advantage based on the Information. Any sensitive budgetary Information, if disseminated, would provide insight to competitors regarding the Company’s financial positions, performance, and status. If the identity of the various projects and the details of their specific opportunities were disseminated to the public, competing projects could use the information to leverage less-than-competitive prices for Georgia Power’s services. If the Information were revealed to the public, the Company could lose business to competitors and pay artificially higher prices to bidders, ultimately harming customers as well as Georgia Power. The identified projects may also lose the competitive incentives to locate within Georgia Power’s service territory, to the detriment of those businesses, their workforces, and the State of Georgia.
 
The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.

STF-DEA-6-4

Question:
Please refer to the most recent Load Realization Model submitted by the Company, on the "Correlation" and "Matrix Choices" tabs. 
Explain why the correlation matrix only includes 0 and 1 values, and no correlations between those two values.
Explain why the company assumes a Triangular distribution of Random Variation. Defend this choice with historical data. If data is not available, explain how this decision was made.
Explain why the Triangular Random Variation correlation assumption was "Independent across segments, perfect within." Explain why the other options were not appropriate. 
Given a "perfect within segments" assumption for the Triangular Random Variation, explain how to interpret this assumption. Does this suggest that if Project A in Segment X were to materialize at a certain % of load, then the likelihood that Project B within Segment X would materialize is perfectly correlated with that of Project A? 
Explain why the Bernoulli Random Variation correlation assumption was "Independent". Explain why the other options were not appropriate.
Response:
In the absence of historical data, it is common practice to assume either perfect correlation or no correlation between variables to ensure that the correlation matrix is valid mathematically. The Company has reasonably assumed zero correlation for binary events that determine if a customer will become a Georgia Power customer. For load realization, the Company assumed perfect correlation within the same business segments and zero correlation across segments.
The Triangular distribution is chosen because it provides a simple yet powerful approach for estimating outcomes when dealing with a range of variables. This distribution requires only three points: the lowest, highest, and most likely outcomes. Please refer to Section 1.5.3 on page 21 of the 2023 IRP Update Load and Energy Forecast Technical Appendix for more details.
We assume “Independent across segments, perfect within” because economic or market conditions that affect load realization tend to impact companies within the same industry segment similarly. In contrast, companies in different segments are less likely to be similarly affected by these conditions. It is unlikely that the load realization of two companies will be similar merely because they are within the same Class, which is either Commercial or Industrial and very broad. 
The assumption of ‘perfect within segments’ suggests that if Project A within a given segment reaches the nth percentile of Project A’s load distribution, then Project B within the same segment will also reach the nth percentile of Project B’s load distribution.
The ‘Independent’ assumption for the Bernoulli Random Variation is appropriate because it reflects the individual nature of customer decisions, such as choosing a state, selecting an electric service provider, or deciding whether or not to follow through with a service request for economic or financial conditions. These decisions are customer-specific and should have zero correlation.
