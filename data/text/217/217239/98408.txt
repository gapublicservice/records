STF-DEA-9-2

Question:
Please refer to the December 4 Supplemental Filing (2023 Request For Information For Capacity Resources – Summary Of Results), stating that “There is not enough time for an RFP to be conducted, resources to be constructed following certification, and transmission projects to be identified and completed to allow delivery by the end of calendar years 2025, 2026 or 2027.” 

Table 0-3-2 states that more than 2,700 MW of resources responding to the RFI are expected to be available by the end of 2027. Please provide a detailed explanation of the timeline issues that prevent these resources from being procured and placed in service prior to the end of 2027.

Why did the Company seek information on new capacity resources in the RFI if it had determined that there is insufficient time to procure a new resource? 


Response:
The potential capacity shown in Table 2-3-2 did not include any existing operating assets, and only included new or planned projects. Please also see the Company’s responses to STF-JKA-4-2, STF-JKA-4-10(e), and STF-JKA-6-5.
As described in Section 1.1 (Purpose and Scope) of the RFI Summary filed as part of the Company’s Supplemental Filing on December 4, 2023, the purpose was “to gather market information regarding the availability of existing and planned Capacity Resources for the capacity need identified for years 2026 through 2028, the timeframe being addressed in the Company’s 2023 IRP Update, as well as for years 2029 through 2031, the time period to be addressed in the Company’s 2029-2031 All-Source Request for Proposals (“RFP”). The primary objective of the RFI was to provide Georgia Power with current market information on the size, cost, technology, availability, risk, and operational and technical attributes of any reliable resource that can meet the firmness and dispatchability requirements, as defined in the RFI, including, but not limited to: (i) Standalone Energy Storage System (“ESS”) with grid charging capability; (ii) ESS with a Renewable Resource  with grid charging capability; or (iii) thermal generation (e.g. natural gas, oil fired) (each a “Capacity Resource” and collectively, “Resource Types”).” 
The RFI gathered information from the market that indicated that there were no existing resources available for the need years addressed in this IRP Update and gathered valuable information to help the Company prepare and understand potential market offerings for the forthcoming 2029-2031 All-Source RFP, which will likely include new projects given the amount of capacity this RFP will seek to procure.

STF-DEA-9-3

Question:
Please refer to the December 4 Supplemental Filing (2023 Request For Information For Capacity Resources – Summary Of Results), section 2.3, stating that “the RFI participants were primarily interested in a PPA option for ESS with a Renewable Resource, with grid charging capability.” If the developer interest in PPA is stronger than BOTs, why is the Company pursuing company ownership only? Will this limit the competitiveness of procurements?


Response:
As indicated in the “Commission Approval of Proposed Resources” section of the 2023 IRP Update Main Document filed on October 27, 2023, and in Section 2.3 of the “Request for Information Summary” filed on December 4, 2023, there is not enough time to complete the Request for Proposals (“RFP”) process and construct new generation and transmission resources to meet the Company’s accelerated capacity needs in 2026-2028. Georgia Power is requesting approval of Company-owned battery energy storage systems (“BESS”) as part of a comprehensive portfolio of resources that can meet the capacity needs identified in the 2023 IRP Update.

See the Company’s responses to STF-JKA-2-17, STF-JKA-4-1, STF-JKA-4-2, STF-JKA-4-3, STF-JKA-4-10(e), STF-JKA-4-11(f)-(g), and STF-JKA-6-5 for additional information regarding the amount of time required to complete the RFP process, the continued need for the RFPs approved in the 2022 IRP, and the development of Company-owned BESS.

As stated in the Company’s Direct Testimony filed on December 4, 2023 and at the Direct Hearings on January 16-17, 2024, the Company is fully supportive of the Commission’s RFP process. The proposed Company-owned resources included in the 2023 IRP Update request are separate and apart from the Company’s open and upcoming RFPs and should not impact the competitiveness of power purchase agreement (“PPA”) bids compared to build own transfer (“BOT”) bids in a future Company RFP.

STF-DEA-9-5

Question:
Please refer to the Company’s Direct Testimony, p. 35, lines 22-26, stating that the Robins and Moody BESS projects “can be charged by the Company’s largest owned solar resources located adjacent to each site but may have opportunities to be charged by the grid depending on the availability of firm transmission capacity.” 
Please explain why firm transmission capacity would be needed to charge the battery from the grid. 
Are the Robins and Moody solar projects fully deliverable or is there curtailment? If the projects experience curtailment, please provide data detail curtailed hours and energy volumes. 
Will the battery and solar projects be able to simultaneously discharge to the grid at full capacity, if needed or economic? Or will the discharge be limited to the interconnection size of the solar facility. 
If the battery is unable to charge from the grid, please explain what advantages there are of siting the facility at the Robins and Moody locations, since the battery’s operations will be more limited than a site where grid charging is feasible. 

Response:
Firm transmission capacity is preferred when charging from the grid because it ensures that the battery can charge when needed to meet reliability needs.
The Robins and Moody solar projects are fully deliverable. 
The discharge will be limited to the interconnection size of the solar facility. 
The advantage of siting a battery at Robins and Moody is that these resources have known deliverability, and they are the best options to meet capacity needs in 2026 and 2027. Given the current system assumptions, the BESS and solar combination will receive a 100% Equivalent Load Carrying Capability “ELCC,” by only charging from the solar. The Company will investigate firm transmission deliverability service for charging in the future to expand the operational flexibility of the resource, but the immediate focus is identifying and deploying resources that can meet capacity needs in the 2026 and 2027.

STF-DEA-9-7

Question:
Please refer to Technical Appendix 4 (“Robins and Moody BESS”), stating: “For the proposed Robins and Moody BESS resources, the base assumption is that these projects will meet the PWA requirements and qualify for the 30% ITC benefit.” What is the foundation of this assumption? Provide any documentation regarding the tax credit qualification of the projects. 


Response:
The Company expects to qualify for the Prevailing Wage and Apprenticeship (“PWA”) labor bonus, which would align with an Investment Tax Credit (“ITC”) of 30%. The Company issued a request for proposal (“RFP”) to battery suppliers and Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (“EPC”) companies to identify venders and contractors for BESS resources. The RFP requires contractors providing labor falling within the scope of prevailing wage or apprenticeship requirements under Public Law 117-169, enacted into law on August 16, 2022 (the Inflation Reduction Act), to comply with such requirements and price their proposals accordingly.

BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-DEA-9-9 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided project specific information of a potential acquisition with sensitive pricing and confidential details (the “Information”). All of such information trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contained herein include competitively sensitive details specific to the resource proposed to be acquired. If the Information were made public, competitors, bidders, and suppliers could use the Information to unfairly manipulate the request for proposals process and competitive market to structure future bids and set an artificial price floor to arbitrarily increase prices to the detriment of the Company and its customers. Public dissemination of the Information would undermine Georgia Power’s ability to negotiate the best price and contract terms and could harm the Company’s ability to secure the best cost bids and resources for the benefit of customers.  

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.
