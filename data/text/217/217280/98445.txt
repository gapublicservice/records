                   AFFIDAVIT AND BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT PORTIONS OF THE
                      INFORMATION SUBMITTED ARE PROTECTED TRADE SECRETS

         As part of its 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update (“2023 IRP Update”), filed in Docket No. 55378,
Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service
Commission an Updated Economic Analysis for Capacity Resources, which contains data supporting proposed
projects in the 2023 IRP Update including Company-owned proposals, which include sensitive pricing,
resource operational capability data, and development timelines, and also certain information regarding the
Company’s proprietary planning processes, financial data, technology screening assumptions, generic
technology and resource cost information, specific resource, technology and avoided cost information,
economic analyses, load and energy forecast data assumptions, and other data, including confidential details
regarding current and future transmission projects (the “Information”). Certain portions of the Information
are trade secrets of Georgia Power and Southern Company and their affiliates and is therefore protected from
public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

        The trade secret portions of the Information derive economic value from not being generally
known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain
economic value from their disclosure or use. Specifically, the trade secret portions of the Information
contained herein include competitively sensitive pricing and operational capability data, including project
development timelines where applicable, specific to the resources under contract within each power
purchase agreement or proposed to be developed. If the Information were made public, competitors,
bidders, and suppliers could use the Information to unfairly manipulate the request for proposals process
and competitive market to structure future bids and set an artificial price floor to arbitrarily increase prices
to the detriment of the Company and its customers. Public dissemination of the Information would
undermine Georgia Power’s ability to negotiate the best price and contract terms and could harm the
Company’s ability to secure the best cost bids and resources for the benefit of customers. In addition, the
terms of the power purchase agreements themselves provides for the confidentiality and protection of
the Information. Compromising the confidentiality of such Information could harm Georgia Power in
future PPA negotiations, as counterparties may fear compelled disclosure of key contractual terms. Lastly,
the Company’s competitors are not required to reveal or publish similar information.

         The trade secret portions of the Information also contain competitively sensitive cost information
related to available technology options, processes and data used by Georgia Power in analyzing resource
addition schedules, financial data used in the Company’s resource analyses, and other confidential details
including data supporting resource economic evaluations. Public dissemination of the trade secret
portions of the Information would allow Georgia Power’s competitors and suppliers to have access to such
processes and strategies and thereby gain an unfair competitive advantage in the marketplace.
Competitors would obtain an unfair advantage because they are not required to reveal similar information
and can utilize such trade secret portions of the Information to manipulate pricing and timing of supply
to the disadvantage of Georgia Power. Competitors would also unfairly benefit in having access and insight
into the Company’s planning processes and methodologies. This competitive advantage for the
Company’s suppliers and competitors would mean that Georgia Power will potentially pay higher prices
to suppliers, ultimately harming Georgia Power and its customers.

Executive Summary
This Application for the Certification of Plant Yates Units 8-10 (“Application”) is made pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-3A-4 and Commission Rule 515-3-4-.07(3) and in support of the capacity needs identified in Georgia Power Company’s (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update (“2023 IRP Update”). As explained in the Company’s 2023 IRP Update and this Application, Plant Yates Units 8-10 are necessary to meet the needs of Georgia Power customers in the winter of 2026/2027 and beyond, and approval of this Application is in the public interest. 
With the 2023 IRP Update, Georgia Power is seeking approval of approximately 3,300 megawatts (“MW”) of generation capacity resources to serve its immediate growing customer demand. As a part of the Company’s 3,300 MW request, Plant Yates Units 8-10 represent approximately 1,300 MW of critical and essential capacity resources that will provide an economical and reliable supply of electric power for the Company’s retail customers during this period of extraordinary growth and for years to come.     
As demonstrated by the load forecast in the 2023 IRP Update, Georgia is experiencing extraordinary economic growth that has significantly impacted the magnitude and timing of Georgia Power’s capacity needs. To support this economic growth, the Company must maintain an electric system that can reliably meet the needs of customers. Since the approval of Georgia Power’s 2022 Integrated Resource Plan (“2022 IRP”) in Docket No. 44160, Georgia’s successful economic development efforts have added more than $24 billion in capital investment and more than 38,000 jobs to the state. A substantial portion of this rapid economic expansion is expected to occur over the next five years, and as a result, Georgia is experiencing a historic increase in the state’s energy and capacity needs. Based on the information known and the economic activity evident at the time of the 2022 IRP, Georgia Power anticipated less than 400 MW of load growth between the winter of 2023/2024 and the winter of 2030/2031. As clear evidence of Georgia’s economic development success, in the sixteen months from the approval of the 2022 IRP to the end of 2023, the Company has been chosen by large load customers to serve more than 3,500 MW of new load. Now, with this 2023 IRP Update, the Company’s current projections reflect significant load growth of 6,600 MW through the winter of 2030/2031. Georgia Power expects retail rates to decrease for its customers as increased sales and customer growth should more than offset the cost of the critical resources requested in the 2023 IRP Update, which are needed to serve the incremental load from these new customer projects.

With the 2023 IRP Update, Georgia Power has set forth a plan to help ensure it can continue to reliably serve the state’s growing capacity needs while also lowering rates for all retail customers. Georgia Power’s long-standing ability to meet the energy needs of our growing state is based on prudent and forward-thinking resource planning, combined with constructive regulation by the Commission, that ensures Georgia Power can provide clean, safe, reliable, and affordable energy to its customers. The development of Plant Yates Units 8-10 is one of several critical steps needed to address the extraordinary near and long-term energy needs of this state and our customers. Collectively, these three units will generate more than 1,300 MW of necessary peaking capacity, with the first unit available to serve customers beginning in the winter of 2026/2027. This peaking capacity resource is paramount to the Company’s ability to continue to reliably meet the capacity needs of our customers and our state. 

The certification of Plant Yates Units 8-10 is essential to help ensure Georgia Power can (i) continue to reliably meet the electricity needs of its customers, (ii) provide long-term reliability and economic benefits to all customers, and (iii) support continued economic development in Georgia. The state’s continued economic growth will provide numerous benefits to our state, including job creation and local tax revenues. Critically, as Georgia’s economy grows, the number of customers who are in need of electric service grows, and that customer growth provides the opportunity to spread the cost of electric service over a larger customer base, which, in turn, lowers rates for retail electric customers. 

Plant Yates Units 8-10 are three advanced frame, dual-fuel simple cycle combustion turbines (“CTs”) that will provide a combined total of approximately 1,300 megawatts (“MW”) of capacity and associated energy, with Unit 8 projected to come online in 2026 and Units 9 and 10 in 2027. Georgia Power has selected cost-effective advanced CT technology developed by Mitsubishi for these units. Each Yates CT is a dual-fuel capable unit that consists of a high-efficiency compressor, combustor, and high-efficiency turbine along with Selective Catalytic Reduction (“SCR”) emission controls. When operating on natural gas, the units would provide up to approximately 1,300 MW of capacity. If natural gas is unavailable during any given time, the units will run on ultra-low sulfur diesel (“ULSD”) fuel, providing approximately 1,070 MW of capacity. The Plant Yates units will run on ULSD fuel during peak winter and summer periods if the natural gas pipeline capability is constrained. As developed, Plant Yates Units 8-10 will be the most efficient CTs on the Company’s system and capable of providing cost-effective dispatchable energy to customers for decades. 

The Company entered an Engineering Procurement and Construction (“EPC”) Agreement with a consortium of Mitsubishi Power Americas, Inc., and Black and Veatch Construction, Inc., (the “Consortium”) for Plant Yates Units 8-10. The Consortium has substantial experience building CTs and recently completed such a project for Southern Company. The EPC Agreement provides a mechanism to meet the necessary in-service dates for Plant Yates Units 8-10, while mitigating construction risk for the Company and appropriately incentivizing the Consortium to develop the units on time and on budget. The Company has experienced personnel in place to provide oversight and appropriate execution of the EPC Agreement by the Consortium and is highly confident in its ability to successfully execute the project. 

Plant Yates Units 8-10 will be located at the existing Plant Eugene A. Yates in Coweta County, Georgia. Utilizing the existing plant site allows the Company to avoid issues related to the development of a greenfield site, while taking advantage of its familiarity with the site and existing site infrastructure. Moreover, the environmental conditions, fuel supply, and life cycle operation and maintenance associated with the Plant Yates site are all favorable. Plant Yates is also located in an area of the state that is continuing to see significant electrical growth. Thus, locating these new CTs in this growing area provides additional reliability to the area, which has seen limited growth in generation development. 

In conclusion, development and certification of Plants Yates Units 8-10 are critical components of the 2023 IRP Update as these new units will join our existing generation resource portfolio as necessary peaking capacity that will allow Georgia Power to meet the imminent 2026/2027 capacity need in a cost-effective way that benefits all customers. Considering the magnitude and accelerated timing of the capacity need years, Plant Yates Units 8-10 should be exempted from Request for Proposal (“RFP”) requirements as allowed by Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6) and (7) as requested by the Company in the 2023 IRP Update. As shown in both the Company’s 2022 IRP and the 2023 IRP Update, both CTs and combined cycle units continue to be necessary sources of dispatchable generation. The lack of existing dispatchable resources in the marketplace and the unprecedented capacity needs facing Georgia necessitate the development of Plant Yates Units 8-10 so that the Company can continue to reliably serve its customers. Moreover, the development of these units provides long-term reliability to customers that will support the Company’s transition to cleaner sources of generation while meeting this unprecedented capacity need as early as 2026. 

The Company requests that the Commission consider this Application on an expedited timeline as part of the 2023 IRP Update in Docket No. 55378, considering the capacity offered from this transaction will be used by the Company to meet its accelerated capacity needs described in more detail in the 2023 IRP Update. 

Description of the Proposed Capacity Resources
The Company seeks to certify three advanced frame, dual-fuel simple cycle CTs at Plant Yates that will provide a total of approximately 1,300 MW of capacity and associated energy. The Company has contracted for Plant Yates Unit 8 to reach commercial operation by December 1, 2026, with Unit 9 to achieve commercial operation by May 1, 2027, and Unit 10 to achieve commercial operation by August 1, 2027. Each unit will be comprised of a single advanced frame combustion turbine with both natural gas and fuel oil firing capability. The capacity of each CT is 441 MW when utilizing natural gas and 351 MW when utilizing ULSD fuel. The units will be located at the existing Plant Yates site in Coweta County, Georgia and will contribute to satisfying the Company’s 2026/2027 capacity needs identified in the 2023 IRP Update. 
Once developed, Plant Yates Units 8-10 will serve as dispatchable resources that provide customers with a reliable and economical source of electricity required by the winter of 2026/2027. In addition, they will form a critical component of the Company’s diverse generation portfolio, helping ensure the Company has the mix of technologies necessary to provide reliable and resilient electric service for all customers during all hours.

Georgia Power’s Near-Term Action Plan
The Company’s near-term action plan for supply capacity, as set forth below, reflects the relevant plans approved in the 2022 IRP, specific details relating to actions proposed in the 2023 IRP Update, and potential actions to be addressed in the 2025 IRP.
The Commission approved the following resource actions in the 2022 IRP:
Distributed Generation (“DG”) Renewable RFPs. The 2023 DG RFP was approved on August 15, 2023, and sought to procure energy from 193 MW of renewable resources. The 2024 DG RFP is scheduled to be issued in the fourth quarter 2024 and will target the procurement of energy from 100 MW of renewable resources. The resources procured from these RFPs will provide winter planning capacity of 15 MW by the winter of 2025/2026.
Utility Scale Renewable RFPs. The CARES 2023 Utility Scale RFP was issued in December 2023 and will seek to procure energy from 2,875 MW of renewable resources. The Company anticipates issuing the CARES 2025 Utility Scale RFP in 2025, targeting 475 MW and any remaining unfilled MW from the 2023 RFP. The resources from these RFPs are expected to provide winter planning capacity for solar of approximately 168 MW by the winter of 2028/2029.
Biomass RFP. The Company is currently evaluating bids received in the Biomass RFP, with contract execution expected in the second quarter 2024. Georgia Power projects that 140 MW of biomass resources will be online by the end of 2028.
Energy Storage System (“ESS”) RFP. The Company plans to issue the ESS RFP in 2024 seeking 500 MW of energy storage resources online by the end of 2028.
All Source RFP. For the 2029-2031 capacity needs, Georgia Power will purchase generating capacity from the market as a result of the All-Source RFP, which was launched prior to the end of 2023 with plans to issue to the market by the end of second quarter 2024, with certification of selected resources planned by the end of 2025. Updated Load and Energy Forecast
As outlined in the 2023 IRP Update, Georgia continues to be one of the fastest growing states in the country. Over the last decade, the state’s economy has continued to thrive, and today, Georgia’s economic development pipeline is more robust than ever, with record growth as companies look to locate or expand within the state. This growth has produced tremendous benefits for the state. Since the approval of Georgia Power’s 2022 IRP, Georgia’s successful economic development efforts have added more than $24 billion in capital investment and more than 38,000 jobs to the state. A substantial portion of this rapid economic expansion is expected to occur over the next five years, and as a result, Georgia is experiencing a historic increase in the state’s energy and capacity needs. This robust economic growth has been driven in large part by Georgia’s recognition as one of the best places in the country in which to do business and the state’s continued strong customer growth across residential, commercial, and industrial classes.
Georgia Power’s 2023 IRP Update Load Forecast, completed in September 2023, projects substantial customer load growth stemming from the rapid economic development occurring in Georgia. The demand projected in the Company’s 2023 IRP Update Load Forecast far exceeds the demand projected in the 2022 IRP. Based on the information and economic activity at the time of the 2022 IRP, the Company anticipated less than 400 MW of growth between the winter of 2023/2024 and the winter of 2030/2031. In contrast, current projections reflect load growth of 6,600 MW through the winter of 2030/2031.
The significant increase in the projected demand for energy since the 2022 IRP reflects the extraordinary growth associated with the numerous new large load projects Georgia Power has been selected to serve, as well as a considerable pipeline of potential future projects. Prior to 2021, on average, the Company was selected to serve approximately 100 MW of large load projects per year. In 2021, the Company was selected to serve 336 MW, which is three times this historical average. In 2022, the Company was selected to serve 2,197 MW, or twenty-two times the historical average. During 2023, the Company was selected to serve nearly 1,900 MW of large projects. Moreover, in the sixteen months from the approval of the 2022 IRP to the end of 2023, the Company has been chosen by large load customers to serve more than 3,500 MW of new load. This growth confirms the supply need for resources in 2026 and beyond. A detailed discussion of the territorial energy and demand forecasts is found in the Technical Appendix of the 2023 IRP Update.
Updated Capacity Needs
The increase in the projected demand for energy driven by Georgia’s extraordinary economic growth has significantly impacted the magnitude and timing of the Company’s projected capacity needs. The Company has a capacity need when its existing, committed, and planned capacity is less than its projected seasonal, weather-normal load forecast plus the seasonal planning target reserve margin. Based on the load levels in the 2023 IRP Update, Georgia Power has a projected capacity need beginning in the winter of 2025/2026. In addition, the projected capacity need through the winter of 2030/2031 has increased from approximately 3,700 MW, based on projections in the 2022 IRP, to as much as 8,500 MW, based on the 2023 IRP Update Load Forecast. In the 2023 IRP Update, the Company is requesting approximately 3,300 MW of resources to meet the capacity needs through 2028. For additional information on the Company’s revised capacity needs, please see the Company’s 2023 IRP Update filed with the Commission on October 27, 2023.

Certification Requirements
Commission Rule Exception to the RFP Requirement in Rule 515-3-4-.04
Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6) and (7) provide exceptions to the Commission rule that requires the Company to procure supply-side resources through an RFP process. Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6) provides that the Commission shall expressly consider in each IRP, and make a determination in each IRP, whether to exclude from the RFP Process any new supply-side resources identified in the soliciting entity’s approved IRP. Therefore, the Commission may find it appropriate to approve the construction of Plant Yates Units 8-10 and certify the resources without having been procured through a competitive RFP process. The Company requested the Commission exempt Plant Yates Units 8-10 from its RFP requirement as part of its request in the 2023 IRP Update. 
Further, Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(7) articulates the Commission’s policy that investor-owned utilities under its regulation, such as Georgia Power, shall maintain a minimum percentage of their capacity as self-owned rate-based assets. Pursuant to the 2001 IRP Final Order, the Commission established the minimum ratio of self-owned assets to purchased power at 70/30. The Commission Rule provides that when the utility is nearing or finds that its self-owned capacity would fall below this minimum percentage (here, 70%), the utility shall inform the Commission of this eventuality in advance of the RFP Process and seek guidance from the Commission on how to proceed. The Company provided notice to the Commission that it was close to falling below the minimum ownership percentage as part of its 2022 IRP and 2023 IRP Update filings. 
As demonstrated by the Company’s Request for Information (“RFI”) Summary Report included in the 2023 IRP Update Supplemental Filing on December 4, 2023, there are no existing market resources identified that can meet the Company’s capacity needs in the winter 2025/2026 to winter 2027/2028 timeframe. The RFI results support the Company’s resource plan identified in the 2023 IRP Update. While the Company supports the procurement of market resources through the Commission’s competitive RFP process for the benefit of customers, market participants selected through that process are not obligated to serve Georgia Power’s customers and are not able to address the Company’s capacity needs on the accelerated timeline identified in the 2023 IRP Update. So that it can continue providing its customers with reliable service and meet the timelines of customers’ needs, Georgia Power must develop new resources in accordance with the RFP exceptions in the Commission’s Rules in addition to procuring resources through the typical RFP process. Moreover, to ensure a reliable and efficient generation fleet, it is critical that the Company maintain a diverse resource portfolio that includes market resources, Company-owned resources, and a balance of resource technologies.

Site Selection Analysis
Plant Yates was selected as the location for the proposed dual-fuel CTs primarily due to available space, available transmission, and the fact that site conditions are well understood based on previously performed due diligence. This combination of factors enables the Plant Yates site to support the expedited development of the three dual-fuel CTs.
Work in recent years at Plant Yates included assessments of available site space; engineering and construction factors such as geotechnical conditions, accessibility for heavy equipment, and delivery of oversized equipment; and the ability to tie into existing Plant Yates infrastructure. In addition to these assessments, the Company evaluated the environmental conditions, fuel supply, and life cycle operation and maintenance associated with locating three CTs at Plant Yates, all of which were determined to be favorable. 
The benefits of using the Plant Yates site also includes the reuse of an existing generation site and industrial developed land. Finally, Plant Yates is a favorable site for these units due to continuing electric load growth in north Georgia and the Atlanta metro area, given that local generation additions have not kept pace with the growth in this region. Siting these units at Plant Yates best meets essential capacity needs for Georgia. 
Fuel Use
The three advanced frame CTs proposed at Plant Yates would provide approximately 1,300 MW of capacity when fueled by natural gas, providing higher output and greater efficiency than previous generations of simple cycle CT designs. If natural gas is unavailable as a fuel source, the units are capable of operating on ULSD fuel and providing approximately 1,070 MW of capacity. The units will run on ULSD fuel during peak winter and summer periods if the natural gas pipeline capability is constrained. On-site fuel oil storage capability will allow for 72 hours of continuous operation to provide reliability and resiliency benefits to customers. 
In addition, the Company is proposing to build a new natural gas pipeline lateral from Transco’s interstate pipeline to the plant. The new lateral would be adjacent to an existing pipeline lateral to Plant Yates from Transco. Natural gas may be delivered to the plant site via existing firm gas transportation for Plant Yates Units 6-7. No firm transportation or additional gas capacity is being proposed to support these units, since these units can operate on ULSD fuel year-round.

Special Design Features
The proposed project will utilize three dual-fuel capable Mitsubishi M501JAC CTs. This technology consists of a high-efficiency compressor, combustor, and high-efficiency turbine that operate as described below. First, the inlet air is filtered and then compressed in a multiple stage axial flow compressor. The compressed air and fuel are mixed and combusted in the turbine combustion chamber. Hot exhaust gases from the combustion chamber expand through a multi-stage power turbine that results in energy to drive both the air compressor and electric power generator. Advancements in CT design and exhaust gas temperature management enable the simple cycle units to be fitted with catalyst systems to reduce emissions.

As set forth in the Company’s 2023 IRP Update, Georgia Power’s current supply-side and demand-side plans as revised in the 2023 IRP Update, including the development of Plant Yates Units 8-10, will provide cost-effective and reliable sources of capacity and energy for customers. The request in this Application complies with applicable Commission rules and is in the public interest as the new Plant Yates CTs will help ensure Georgia Power can economically and reliably meet customers’ energy needs beginning in the winter of 2026/2027. Therefore, the Company requests that the Commission approve and certify Plant Yates Units 8-10 and grant the cost recovery as described above. The Company requests that the Commission consider this Application on an expedited timeline as part of its decision on the 2023 IRP Update in Docket No. 55378, considering the capacity offered from this transaction will be used by the Company to meet its accelerated capacity needs described in more detail in the 2023 IRP Update.
