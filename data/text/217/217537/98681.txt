Q.WHAT IS A VIRTUAL POWER PLANT?
A.The fundamental components of a VPP are 1) behind-the-meter (“BTM”) distributed energy resources or “DERs” and 2) software which enables behind-the-meter DERs to be controlled and networked with other similar devices in order to bring aggregated value to the larger energy system. Presently, these DERs fall into three broad categories: 1) renewable energy generators, predominantly rooftop solar; 2) battery energy storage systems; and 3) intelligent, digitalized loads, such as smart thermostats, electric vehicles and their charging equipment, and grid interactive water heaters, among others. Virtual power plants can provide a wide range of grid services, including capacity, energy, frequency response, demand response, and spinning and non-spinning reserves. Virtual power plants can also support load shifting or act as load sinks, absorbing excess renewable energy that would normally be curtailed. 
Q.HOW DO VIRTUAL POWER PLANTS WORK?
A.For many utilities, customer-sited resources are largely unmanaged. Rooftop solar injects energy into the grid when it is produced, at roughly the same time that all other rooftop, community-scale, and utility-scale solar facilities are producing and injecting into the grid. Customers run their air conditioners or heat pumps essentially whenever they want to, trusting that there will always be enough electricity to achieve and maintain their desired temperature.
In some limited cases, such as cases of customers with EVs or customers with rooftop solar paired with storage, rate structures might exist to encourage customers to charge at a certain time or to store their excess generation and consume it during system peaks, but these price signals are static and are not responsive to current conditions. These rate structures also do nothing to coordinate resources, so that—for example—excess solar generation from one household can “pre-cool” the next house over ahead of the afternoon peak.
Virtual power plants take these unmanaged and largely uncontrolled generation systems, storage resources, and loads and orchestrate them to achieve grid harmonization and balance on a dynamic basis, in response to real time conditions. Resources are grouped into thoughtful, purposeful energy networks that can interact as a single firm dispatchable grid asset with the greater distribution system to provide specific “grid services.” All of this is made possible by powerful VPP software that can remotely monitor and control compatible technologies via a simple internet connection.
Q.HOW MUCH CAPACITY CAN VIRTUAL POWER PLANTS PROVIDE?
A.The beauty of virtual power plants is that they are highly scalable and modular. The maximum capacity of a virtual power plant is limited only by the technical, economic, and achievable potential of the distributed energy resources that it dispatches.  
For perspective, our WattSmart virtual power plant in Utah currently includes more than 20 MW of capacity, but our goal is to reach 40 MW by the end of 2024 and to continue to scale from there to 100 MW and beyond in the 2030s. 
Q.HOW QUICKLY COULD A VIRTUAL POWER PLANT BE DEPLOYED?
A.At this point, there are several off-the-shelf software products and control systems that can integrate with a utility’s energy management system in a matter of months. For example, for the WattSmart program, we utilized 100% in house sonnen VPP software, which we were able to integrate into Rocky Mountain Power’s grid management system (OSI’s Monarch System) in less than six months. It took an additional 6 months to add an IEEE2030.5 server to allow our VPP software to control non-sonnen batteries. In other words, even if some degree of customization is needed, a full-scale, brand-agnostic program can be rolled out in roughly a year, and certainly less than two years. More so than the platform integration, the driving factors for the timeline are the regulatory process, which was very supportive in Utah, and the speed with which new resources can be installed, interconnected, and enrolled.
Q.WHERE ARE VIRTUAL POWER PLANTS BEING USED?

A.Virtual power plants are in use around the world, but they are particularly prevalent in Europe, Australia, North America, and Asia. Here in the United States, utilities and independent aggregators are operating virtual power plants in Utah, Vermont, New York, Hawaii, California, Texas, and in the ISO-NE market, just to name a few, and plans are in motion to deploy a VPP in the Carolinas. At a sub-utility scale, there are several examples of apartment buildings or planned communities that are operating as virtual power plants, including projects that sonnen has supported in Arizona, Florida, and Michigan.

Q.PLEASE DESCRIBE THE WATTSMART PROGRAM.
A.The WattSmart Batteries program is a fully operational (i.e. non-pilot) distributed battery grid management program that is directly dispatched by the utility on a daily basis. This program is first-of-its-kind in the USA and offers a wide and growing range of grid services, including non-wires alternatives for local load pocket decongestion and T&D deferral; demand response; capacity planning; energy; frequency response; mitigation of the “duck curve” effect; individual and system-level resiliency; and spinning and non-spinning reserves. In the near future, the utility hopes to explore using the batteries for reverse demand response, using the batteries as load sinks during times of low grid demand and high excess generation.
Under this program, participants receive upfront and ongoing incentives for purchasing batteries that the utility can access to support the grid. The initial commitment is four years, with the opportunity to continue after that in exchange for a larger participation incentive.   
Q.WHAT BATTERIES ARE ELIGIBLE TO PARTICIPATE IN THE WATTSMART PROGRAM?
A.Right now, the WattSmart program is open to six battery models from four manufacturers—sonnen, Solar Edge, Fortress, and Torus. Rocky Mountain Power is actively engaging other battery manufacturers in hopes of expanding this list.
Participation is open to any batteries that meet the following requirements:
Ability to integrate with Rocky Mountain Power’s distributed battery grid management program, including 30 second interval data.
Utility grade batteries with a minimum of 4 kW / 10 kWh.
Minimum of 10-year battery warranty.
Minimum of 7,500 battery cycle life to accommodate daily load cycling.
Ability to charge/discharge multiple times a day.
Full dispatch control by PacifiCorp to meet the needs of the program.
Proper UL or equivalent safety certifications for residential and commercial applications.
Additional requirements must be met in terms of telemetry and communications protocols.

Q.WHAT INCENTIVES DO CUSTOMERS RECEIVE FOR PARTICIPATING IN THE WATTSMART PROGRAM?
A.The Wattsmart Program offers customers an upfront incentive of $400 per kW of storage capacity for a four-year commitment. Larger incentives of $600 per kW are available to households that currently have photovoltaic systems and commercial customers. 
In addition to the upfront incentive, participants receive an ongoing incentive of $15 per kW in years 2, 3, and 4.  This ongoing incentive rises to $50 per kW beginning in year 5. Participants who had batteries prior to the program begin at the $50 per kW incentive.
Q.WHEN THE UTILITY DISPATCHES A CUSTOMER’S BATTERY, IS THAT ENERGY EXPORTED TO THE GRID OR CONSUMED BY THE CUSTOMER?
A.When Rocky Mountain Power dispatches a battery, it serves the customer’s energy needs first before exporting to the grid. This ensures customers receive the full financial benefit of having solar and having a battery in terms of reducing their energy bills.
As noted earlier, Rocky Mountain Power is planning to eventually use customer batteries to absorb excess solar energy that would otherwise be curtailed as a form of reverse demand response. When this occurs, that energy will be exported directly to this grid, since it was not produced by the customer. 
Q.MANY CUSTOMERS USE BATTERY STORAGE FOR ENERGY ARBITRAGE UNDER A TIME OF USE RATE. DOES THE WATTSMART PROGRAM PREVENT CUSTOMERS FROM DOING THIS?
A.The Wattsmart Program includes daily load cycling as a feature. Batteries charge during solar producing hours from connected rooftop solar systems and discharge when energy prices are highest and solar is unavailable. For customers on a time of use rate, this automatic process is functionally and financially equivalent to customer-initiated arbitrage. For customers on a standard residential rate, this is the equivalent of optimizing your battery to maximize self-consumption.
As noted in the prior question, the batteries will eventually be used to absorb excess energy from utility scale solar or wind facilities that would otherwise be curtailed. In that case, customers will not receive the arbitrage benefit for the energy they did not produce. However, this will not prevent customers from realizing significant financial benefits from the battery program through the upfront and ongoing participation incentives, as well as available tax credits and the savings they will receive through self-consumption of the energy they generate.
Q.WHAT PERCENTAGE OF THE BATTERY CAPACITY CAN ROCKY MOUNTAIN POWER USE?

A.The agreement allows for up to 90%. In practice, grid conditions rarely require that level of discharge. 

Q.DOES ROCKY MOUNTAIN POWER DISPATCH THE BATTERY IF WEATHER CONDITIONS ARE EXPECTED TO BE POOR?

A.No. As you would expect, utilities have access to some of the best meteorological data available. If the utility expects severe weather that could cause outages, they do not dispatch customer batteries and reserve the full capacity for the use of the customer. The same is true for planned outages for maintenance.

Q.HOW DOES THE WATTSMART PROGRAM PERFORM DURING THE WINTER?
A.In cold temperatures, all batteries experience some degradation in their performance. The chemical reactions in the battery slow, so batteries’ ability to accept a charge and reach full capacity decrease.  However, these challenges can be mitigated with thoughtful siting. I will note that sonnen batteries, which use a lithium iron phosphate chemistry, are specifically engineered to be safe for indoor use, so it is possible to prevent any temperature-related performance degradation through indoor siting. This is the case at our Soleil Lofts project, but even locating the battery in an unconditioned garage offers some advantages relative to a fully outdoor installation.
Another consideration in winter is that there isn’t as much solar generation overall, which could make it harder to fill the battery from connected solar alone. This has not been a significant problem in Utah, since net metering encouraged customers to size solar arrays to cover their entire annual usage, but even here in Georgia where systems tend to be smaller, a partially full battery can still be useful. As noted earlier, Rocky Mountain Power rarely discharges batteries to the 10% reserve limit. Plus, the batteries can grid charge using excess solar or wind energy from utility scale systems if needed.  
Q.THE WATTSMART PROGRAM IS ONLY FOCUSED ON BATTERIES AT THIS STAGE. DO OTHER SONNEN VPPS IN THE UNITED STATES INCLUDE OTHER RESOURCES?
A.At this stage, our U.S. VPPs are only focused on batteries. Many of our utility partners already had demand response programs for thermostats and other controllable loads when we initiated our battery programs, so there was no need or desire to control those technologies with our VPP product. However, our European VPPs tend to have a much wider array of products than just batteries, so we certainly have the capability to support a multi-technology VPP.

Q.PLEASE TELL US ABOUT THE GEORGIA BRIGHT PROGRAM.

A.Georgia BRIGHT is a solar and storage leasing program that was specifically designed for households earning under $100,000 per year. The BRIGHT program is designed to offer households day one and ongoing energy savings, with all maintenance included for 25 years. This program was made possible through the Inflation Reduction Act, which made nonprofits eligible to receive the solar investment tax credit as a direct payment for the first time. This allows organizations like Capital Good Fund to claim the tax credit on behalf of residents who may not be able to claim the credit themselves due to insufficient tax appetite. Georgia BRIGHT also leverages bulk purchasing, monetization of renewable energy credits, additional tax credits (such as the low-income bonus credit and the Energy Community adder), and impact investments at a concessionary rate to offer participants the lowest possible lease payment and the greatest possible savings. 

Currently, the BRIGHT program is available to income-qualified homeowners in Georgia who have a roof in good condition or space for a ground-mounted system. Approval is not based on credit score, but a holistic evaluation of a household’s ability to repay the lease given the anticipated savings. For now, the program is operating as a pilot with enough capital to serve between 150 and 175 households, but the goal is to make the program a permanent offering with an even wider range of eligibility (for example, expanding to serve renters) in the near future.  We also wish to highlight that this is a first-of-its-kind program that we are proud to be piloting in Georgia.

Q.WHAT PERCENTAGE OF CUSTOMERS TO DATE HAVE OPTED FOR STORAGE?
A.As of February 13, 2024, more than 56% of leases have included storage. This is far above our initial expectations and would likely increase if additional incentives were to become available as part of a virtual power plant program. This uptake has been particularly noteworthy since, while a PV-only system can deliver at least 10% month-one bill savings, adding storage results in a “bill-swap”, or even a slight bill increase. Customers have been opting for storage because of how much the value the benefit of resilience in the face of outages and the ability to up-size their solar system to offset more of their consumption. Were there an incentive of $400/kW of storage, all customers would realize day-one bill savings even with a battery, and uptake would undoubtedly be even higher, as some customers who wanted storage decided to maximize savings with a PV-only system.

Q.HOW LIKELY IS IT THAT GEORGIA WILL RECEIVE SOLAR FOR ALL FUNDING FOR PROJECTS IN GEORGIA POWER TERRITORY?
A.The EPA has indicated that sixty awards will be made, with the general intention of ensuring that each state and territory receives at least one award. Three of the four applications that proposed to do work in Georgia would make investments in Georgia Power territory, including applications by Capital Good Fund and the Georgia Environmental Finance Authority. We obviously can’t know for sure which application the federal government will choose, but the guidance suggested that applications with a statewide focus would be viewed more favorably, so I think it is quite reasonable to assume that a significant amount of Solar for All funding will be spent in Georgia Power territory.
Q.WHAT WOULD BE THE SCALE OF THE SOLAR FOR ALL PROGRAM?
A.Statewide applications for the state of Georgia could be for up to $250 million, and both Capital Good Fund and GEFA requested this amount. By our modeling, this could result in more than 300 MW of solar and nearly 110 MWh of battery storage. However, I will caveat that this battery storage projection is likely an underestimate, since actual adoption rates for storage through the BRIGHT program have been more than double our original assumption and would be much higher with the addition of a VPP incentive.
Q.WHAT WOULD BE THE PERFORMANCE PERIOD?
A.Award notifications are expected in March of this year, and the funding is available for five years. 
Q.WHAT STEPS COULD THE COMMISSION TAKE TO MAXIMIZE THE IMPACT OF THIS HISTORIC INVESTMENT?
A.The biggest thing that the Commission could do to maximize the impact of Solar for All funding would be to create a program structure, like a virtual power plant program, that would simultaneously help more customers access batteries and support grid stability by dispatching those batteries for grid services. 
To put this in perspective, if the Commission were to adopt the $400/kW incentive for residential customers and the $600/kW incentive for commercial customers that Rocky Mountain Power offers through the WattSmart program, we would expect total storage deployment to increase by 10-15% relative to our projections, based solely on our ability to make the awarded capital go further. Specifically, between a VPP incentive and Solar for All Funding, we would expect to originate leases that include storage to 3,200 low-income households and 1,300 community facilities (nonprofits, houses of worship, schools, etc.) over five years, totaling 110 megawatt-hours of battery capacity.  That value does not even consider the persuasive impact of the additional incentive in driving consumer interest. 
Q.WHAT IMPACT WOULD THESE CHANGES HAVE ON LOWER INCOME HOUSEHOLDS?
A.Three impacts that are particularly important to my organization are reductions in energy burden; resilience; and job creation.
All of the households we serve are low or moderate income, and many of those households are burdened with energy bills in excess of $200/month. Solar, particularly with battery storage, is a critical tool for reducing these bills to a more manageable level. Any programs that can support our work to get these technologies into communities will have an amplifying effect. With Solar For All funding and a VPP incentive, we would expect households to save at least 20% on their electric bills in month one.
Another impact would be greater access to resilience. Our customer population has included seniors, individuals living with disabilities, and individuals with medical dependence on electricity. For each of these populations, backup power can have serious quality of life benefits, and in some cases even be a matter of life and death. 
It is important to note as well that resilience can also have a financial impact—for most of the people involved in this proceeding, losing a refrigerator full of food to a power outage would be an inconvenience and a disappointment, but it would not prevent us from eating. However, for a family in need, losing a weeks’ worth of groceries can take a family from having hot, nutritious meals to living on whatever shelf stable, processed alternatives they have on hand. 
Finally, we prioritize working with smaller contractors, including woman- and minority-owned firms such as Be Smart Home Solutions and Better Tomorrow Solar. The BRIGHT program will create hundreds of good-paying local jobs in the coming years, and the addition of batteries to a system creates more revenue-generation opportunities for installers and other companies throughout the value chain.
Q.WHAT OTHER BENEFITS WOULD RESULT FROM THESE CHANGES?
A.There has been a lot of discussion in this proceeding about whether the massive load growth that is anticipated will ultimately raise costs for customers. A lot of this uncertainty depends on whether the projected loads actually materialize on time and at the level anticipated. By offering a modular solution to capacity expansion, VPPs offer a crucial safeguard against overbuilding or building too quickly, offering customers additional protection against price increases.
Moreover, investing in solar and storage is a future-proof investment. When restrictive climate regulations or carbon prices are ultimately put in place, as they are almost certain to be, solar and storage will be able to continue operating without interruption. Oil-, gas-, or coal-fired resources will not. At a minimum, these resources will require significant investments to become fully hydrogen capable or be retrofitted with carbon capture. At worst, they will become stranded assets that must be retired prematurely and replaced with alternative resources. And of course, solar plus storage does not result in the local air pollution or greenhouse gas emissions that stem from fossil fuel-based power generation, an issue of particular importance to lower-income ratepayers that tend to live in areas with worse air quality.
Q.WHAT ARE THE PANEL’S RECOMMENDATIONS FOR THE COMMISION?
A.Our panel has three key recommendations:
The Commission should direct Georgia Power to evaluate the Rocky Mountain Power program and similar program models and bring a VPP program proposal to the Commission for approval within 180 days of the conclusion of this proceeding. This proposal should include proposed upfront and participation incentives and their justifications, administrative cost estimates, estimated implementation timelines, and other relevant information. This process should include opportunities for stakeholder input from parties to this docket.
The RNR tariff should be modified to eliminate system size limits for solar energy systems paired with storage. That is, residential customers should be allowed to have systems greater than 10 kW, and commercial customers should be allowed to have systems greater than the current limit of 125% of their peak demand or 250 kW. This change should be made to maximize the amount of solar energy and battery capacity that is available for utility control via the virtual power plant. Though not directly a concern for utility regulation, we must note that this more efficient use of rooftops, parking lots, and other previously developed or disturbed spaces will reduce the need to convert farm land and forested areas into large solar farms, which is an additional benefit for our environment, community character, and quality of life.
The Commission should direct Georgia Power to develop and file a plan for expediting interconnection and witness testing of customer-sited solar energy and battery storage systems to support maximum resource availability to meet the capacity needs identified in this docket. These costs should be addressed in the 2025 rate case.
