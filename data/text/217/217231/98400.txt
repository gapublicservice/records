BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-JKA-6-1 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided diversity factors which include retail operating company load interactions. All of such information (the “Information”) constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains load diversity factors determined by evaluating retail operating company load interactions. Due to separation protocol standards between Southern Company retail operating companies and Southern Power, this Information cannot be shared with Southern Power or other entities due to resulting market interactions and therefore must be considered trade secret. Public dissemination of the Information would allow Georgia Power’s competitors, suppliers, and customers access to the Company’s anticipated load strategies, thereby bestowing insight into the Company’s planning assumptions and forecasting capabilities to the detriment of the Company and its customers. Further, the Company’s competitors are not required to disclose this type of information and to require the Company to do so would place it at an economic disadvantage.   

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.  


STF-JKA-6-1

Question:
Refer to the Company’s Load and Resource table, “Georgia Power Territorial Base Case Load vs. Existing Capability Table.xlsx”, and the footnote - “(E) Does not consider planning reserve sharing. Reflects GPC's Target Reserve Margin resulting from a System Target Reserve Margin of 25.50% (2024-2026) and 26% (2027 and beyond).”
Please explain the terms and conditions of the “planning reserve sharing” feature of the Southern Company ICC agreement and explain how it would change the table if planning reserve sharing had been considered.
Please provide a revised table that accounts for planning reserve sharing.
Please explain what year the Southern Company need date now is, and whether or not GPC could rely on reserve sharing during any part of the near-term period. Please explain. 
Please provide a comparison of the Company’s current Target Reserve Margin to the Target Reserve Margin from the 2022 IRP as it appears to have changed since the 2022 IRP.    
Please confirm the Company’s Southern Company Target Reserve margin has not changed since the 2022 IRP, and is based on the same SERVM analysis that was performed in the 2022 IRP.  If a new study has been performed, please provide the new study.
Please provide all workpapers used to derive the Georgia Reserve Margin target based on diversity from the Southern Company reserve margin target.  Provide this for both the 2022 IRP and the current IRP and provide it on a short-term and long-term basis.


Response:
The Intercompany Interchange Contract (“IIC”) provides for coordinated planning between the Operating Companies and for the sharing of temporary surpluses and deficits of capacity.  While the Southern Pool affords the participants the ability to rely on temporary surplus capacity on the System, each Operating Company is expected to have adequate resources, including an appropriate level of reserves, to reliably serve its own load obligations.  
Any retail Operating Company that has excess reserves may decide at any point to retire some of the capacity or make wholesale sales predicated on some or all of that capacity provided that such Operating Company is maintaining adequate resources to reliably serve its own obligations. In either case (resource retirement or wholesale sale), the effect would be a reduction in the level of available capacity reserves on the System. Additionally, the other retail Operating Companies may experience increased economic development activity similar to what the Company is experiencing and may decide to not make their surpluses available to the Company while the economic development activity is being assessed.

Should Georgia Power rely on the reserve sharing provision of the IIC, Georgia Power would utilize the other Operating Companies’ temporary capacity surplus in Winter of 2026 to eliminate its capacity deficit. The MPC PPA, which is based on MPC’s surplus capacity, is addressing the Winter 2026 capacity deficit. Beginning in Winter of 2027, Georgia Power’s need with planning reserve sharing would be slightly less due to another Operating Company having a small amount of capacity surplus for Winter of 2027 through Winter of 2040. While this capacity is available to Georgia Power customers in times of need due to participation in the Southern Company pool through reserve sharing, it is only intended as a temporary provision and each Operating Company is responsible for maintaining adequate resources to reliably serve its own obligations.
See STF-JKA-6-1 Attachment A for a revised Load and Resource table that accounts for Georgia Power relying on reserve sharing. To maintain consistency with the Load and Resource table provided at the time of filing, STF-JKA-6-1 Attachment A does not reflect any of the proposed resource additions as requested in the 2023 IRP Update, which would likely have a large impact on the reserve sharing MWs that are available to Georgia Power. Also, see “TS GPC and System IRP Summary Data” filed with the Commission on October 27, 2023, which illustrates the System capacity balance and status of the System at the time the Company filed its 2023 IRP Update.
The Southern Company System capacity need is REDACTED. As stated in part (a), while Georgia Power may have the potential to utilize planning reserve sharing to slightly lessen their capacity need in future years, other retail Operating Companies may decide at any point to retire some of their capacity, make it available for wholesale contracts, or may experience a similar increase in economic development activity that may make their surpluses unavailable. 
The Company’s Target Reserve Margin (“TRM”) for both short term and long term have changed from the 2022 IRP due to the updated diversity factors that are applied to the Southern Company System TRM. Both the 2023 IRP Update and the 2022 IRP utilized the 25.5% short term TRM and 26% long term TRM for the System, but the diversity factors are updated annually based on a historical ten-year average of monthly diversities, resulting in different TRMs for the Company. The 2022 IRP TRM was 24.69% and 25.18% for short- and long-term, respectively. The 2023 IRP Update TRM is 24.43% and 25.00% for short- and long-term, respectively. 
Yes, the System TRM is based on the same SERVM analysis that was performed in the 2022 IRP.
Calculations to derive the Georgia Power TRM based on diversity are provided in TS STF-JKA-6-1 Attachment B. Workpapers used to develop the diversity factors for the 2023 IRP Update are provided in STF-JKA-2-10 Attachment A.

Question:
Regarding the Company’s Request for Information Summary, Section 2.1, the Company notes, “The remaining qualifying submissions either indicated new or planned potential projects.”  Please explain the difference in the Company’s use of the words “new” and “planned.” 

Response:
The Company was seeking information on existing or planned projects as part of the Request for Information (“RFI”) issued in September 2023. As part of the Company’s Technology Acquisition Process, Southern Company evaluates each new vendor’s security maturity in light of the inherent risk presented by the products and/or services they will provide the Company. Security concerns or shortfalls can then inform discussions regarding the need for compensating security controls, additional contractual obligations by the supplier, or (worst case) selection of alternate suppliers. Additionally, as specific technology solutions are integrated with the Company’s technology environments, they are reviewed for conformance with the Company’s technical requirements to reduce security risk while enabling desired business outcomes.
To date, no discussions have been held between the Company and local officials at Moody Air Force Base or Robins Air Force Bases regarding security concerns.
There is no direct communication link or physical tie between the proposed Company facilities and the military bases. These projects will adhere to the physical and cyber security protocols required of all Company facilities. All controls will meet the Company’s stringent communication and network requirements.
The Company prioritizes both physical and cyber security through layered defenses. The Company has established cyber security standards for generation facilities that specify the requirements for network architecture, physical access, electronic access, remote access, backups, system administration/updates, change management, transient cyber assets, and removable media.
