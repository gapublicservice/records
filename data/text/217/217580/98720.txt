I. Introduction
Q. PLEASE STATE YOUR NAME, BUSINESS ADDRESS, AND OCCUPATION.
A. My name is Lisa V. Perry. My business address is 2608 SE J Street, Bentonville, Arkansas 72716. I am employed by Walmart Inc. ("Walmart") as Director, Utility Partnerships - Regulatory.
Q.ON WHOSE BEHALF ARE YOU TESTIFYING IN THIS CASE?
A.I am testifying on behalf of Walmart.
Q.PLEASE DESCRIBE YOUR EDUCATION AND EXPERIENCE.
A.I received a J.D. in 1999 and a L.L.M. in Taxation in 2000 from the University of Florida Levin College of Law. From 2001 to 2019, I was in private practice with an emphasis from 2007 to 2019 in Energy Law. My practice included representing a large commercial client before the utility regulatory commissions in Colorado, Texas, New Mexico, Arkansas, and Louisiana in matters ranging from general rate cases to renewable energy programs. I joined the energy department at Walmart in September 2019 as Senior Manager, Energy Services. My Witness Qualifications Statement is attached as Exhibit LVP-1.
Q.HAVE YOU PREVIOUSLY SUBMITTED TESTIMONY BEFORE THE GEORGIA PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION ("COMMISSION")?
A.No, I have not. 

Q.HAVE YOU PREVIOUSLY SUBMITTED TESTIMONY BEFORE OTHER STATE REGULATORY COMMISSIONS?
A.Yes, I have submitted testimony with state regulatory commissions for Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Florida, Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maryland, Michigan, New York, Ohio, Oklahoma, South Carolina, Texas, Virginia, and Wyoming. I have also provided legal representation for customer stakeholders before the state regulatory commissions for Colorado, Texas, Arkansas, Louisiana, and New Mexico in the cases listed under "Commission Dockets" in Exhibit LVP-1.
Q.ARE YOU SPONSORING EXHIBITS IN YOUR TESTIMONY?
A.Yes. I am sponsoring the exhibit listed in the Table of Contents.
Q.PLEASE BRIEFLY DESCRIBE WALMART'S OPERATIONS IN GEORGIA.
A.As shown on Walmart's website, Walmart operates 213 retail units, 10 distribution centers, three fulfillment centers, and employs over 66,000 associates in Georgia. In fiscal year ending 2023, Walmart purchased approximately $24.8 billion dollars of goods and services from Georgia-based suppliers, supporting over 189,000 supplier jobs.


Q.PLEASE BRIEFLY DESCRIBE WALMART'S OPERATIONS WITHIN THE SERVICE TERRITORY FOR GEORGIA POWER COMPANY ("COMPANY" OR "GPC")
A.Walmart has 125 retail stores, three distribution centers, one fulfillment center, and related facilities that take service from GPC.

II.Purpose of Testimony and Summary of Recommendations
Q.WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF YOUR TESTIMONY?
A. The purpose of my testimony is to respond to the Company's proposed 2023 Integrated Resource Plan ("IRP") Update filed October 27, 2023, along with supporting testimony and documents, in which the Company is seeking, inter alia, approval of new generation resources, power purchase agreements ("PPAs"), new distributed energy resource ("DER") programs, and a new curtailable load, or demand response, program. Specifically, my testimony addresses the Company's proposed DER programs, its proposed commercial and industrial ("C&I") customer demand response program, and opportunities for customers to provide resources that can help the Company meet its increasing customer demand.
Q.PLEASE SUMMARIZE WALMART'S RECOMMENDATIONS TO THE COMMISSION.
A.Walmart makes the following recommendations to the Commission: 
(1)require the Company to work with interested stakeholders to build upon the Company's proposed DER Co-Location Program, DER Customer Owned Program, and Demand Response Plus Energy Credit Program by developing additional C&I programs that provide more options to customers and will capitalize on resources that customers can bring to the Company's system on both the supply-side and demand-side of the equation, such as those mentioned later in my testimony, and any other opportunities that help the Company provide affordable and reliable clean energy solutions that benefit all customers; and 
(2)require the Company seek Commission approval of such program(s) as part of its next statutorily required 2025 IRP.
Q.DOES THE FACT THAT YOU MAY NOT ADDRESS AN ISSUE OR POSITION ADVOCATED BY THE COMPANY INDICATE WALMART'S SUPPORT?
A.No. The fact that an issue is not addressed herein should not be construed as an endorsement of, agreement with, or consent to any filed position.

III.  Description of Proposed 2023 IRP Update
Q.CAN YOU PROVIDE A GENERAL DESCRIPTION OF THE PROPOSED 2023 IRP UPDATE?
A. Yes. Based on my understanding, the Company is experiencing substantial load growth since filing its currently approved 2022 IRP, and, as a way to address this growth, the Company filed the 2023 updated IRP to seek approval of additional resources. Specifically, the Company is asking the Commission to approve additional supply-side resources, including two PPAs, three simple cycle combustion turbine ("CT") resources, and Battery Energy Storage Systems ("BESS"). The Company is also seeking approval of two new DER programs, the DER Co-Location Program and the DER Customer Owned Program, along with a new Curtailable Load Program for C&I customers and expansion of an existing residential demand response program.  
Q.IN ADDITION TO SUPPLY-SIDE RESOURCES AND DEMAND-SIDE PROGRAMS, IS THE COMPANY SEEKING APPROVAL FOR ANYTHING ADDITIONAL AS PART OF ITS 2023 IRP UPDATE?
A.Yes, it is. The Company is also asking the Commission to allow the Company to seek approval of additional capacity prior to its 2025 IRP during instances when the Company's load forecast project interim additional capacity needs. The Company refers to this as "Flex Capacity." Furthermore, the Company is seeking Commission approval to update its transmission system, as needed, to integrate the resources requested in its 2023 IRP Update into the Company's system.
Q.WHAT IS WALMART'S RESPONSE TO THE COMPANY'S PROPOSED 2023 IRP UPDATE?
A.Walmart understands that the Company is seeking approval of different resources to meet growing demand quickly and efficiently. Walmart also appreciates that the Company is putting forth various avenues that it can deploy in order to meet its customer needs that not only provide additional generation resources, but also manage the demand side of the equation. As I discuss in more detail below, Walmart's ongoing efforts to meet its sustainability goals have provided it with valuable experience and access to a variety of clean energy resources that can provide the Company with even more solutions to meet its growing customer demand. In short, Walmart sees customers as being a part of the solution by positioning themselves to provide not only demand-side resources such as demand response, but also generation capabilities that reduce the Company's need to purchase expensive centralized generation.  

IV.  Walmart's Sustainability Goals
Q.HAS WALMART ESTABLISHED CORPORATE SUSTAINABILITY GOALS THAT INCLUDE CLEAN ENERGY AND CARBON REDUCTION GOALS?
A.Yes, it has. Walmart has long had aggressive and significant company-wide renewable energy goals, and on September 21, 2020, Walmart announced new targets as a part of its sustainability goals, including: (1) to be supplied 100 percent by renewable energy by 2035 and (2) zero carbon emissions in its operations, including its transportation fleet vehicles, without the use of offsets, by 2040. Walmart has also set a goal to transition to zero emission buildings by deploying low-impact refrigerants for cooling and electric equipment for heating by 2040. Additionally, on January 9, 2024, Walmart announced a goal to bring 10 GW of 13 new clean energy projects online by the end of 2030, including 1 GW of new onsite solar plus storage, and enabling 2 GW of new community solar projects. 
To date, Walmart has contracted for or currently takes electricity from one or more renewable resources in at least 29 states, including Georgia, and Puerto Rico.
Q.WHAT CHANNELS DOES WALMART UTILIZE TO SECURE CLEAN ENERGY RESOURCES?
A.To meet its clean energy goals, Walmart utilizes three primary channels to secure resources:
Development of off-site resources: These products are typically structured to replace other energy, both physically and on the bill. This mechanism allows Walmart to leverage its scale to drive the best project economics while simultaneously minimizing transaction time and costs. To date, Walmart has contracted for these resources in deregulated markets through Texas Retail Energy, LLC, a competitive electric supplier wholly owned by Walmart that serves as its electric supplier in most deregulated retail markets, to directly serve its load. Walmart has also entered into "Virtual PPAs" and "Renewable Energy Certificate ("REC") Agreements" in deregulated wholesale markets for clean energy assets. These contracts do not directly serve Walmart's load but allow it to bring new large-scale renewable resources to the market by entering into long-term financial agreements for clean energy power and environmental attributes. Walmart will also sign up its facilities for offsite community solar program subscriptions in jurisdictions where those programs are offered. Through these programs, Walmart sites will subscribe to an allocation of a shared offsite solar installation and receive bill credits based on their subscription size, facility power usage, and their eligible utility expenses. In addition to community solar program participation, Walmart is also working to make clean energy accessible and affordable for its customers and members, as part of its commitment to unlock 2 GW of new community solar projects around the United States. As an example, Walmart recently announced its investment in seven community solar projects in Illinois and New York, which will serve low-to-moderate income households, public schools, and religious institutions, among others.    
Development of on-site resources: Walmart is currently developing and will own on-site, behind the meter resources, and has also contracted resources through PPAs and leases that allow performance guarantees. These resources replace grid energy with the expectation that the operating costs for the site are reduced.
Utility partnerships: Walmart works with its utility partners to develop useable commercial and industrial programs and economic structures targeted to function within the confines of the regulatory compact and with minimal impact to non-participating customers. When this option is pursued, Walmart works to ensure that programs it assists in developing can be used by broader groups of customers, not merely by Walmart. Walmart is unique in the large commercial space because it has significant in-house rate and regulatory expertise that it is willing to leverage to create opportunities to move the entire industry forward. Walmart currently participates in programs with 20 utilities across the United States. The largest of these partnerships that have been executed to date include the development of and participation in Florida Power & Light Company's SolarTogether Program, Duke Energy Florida's Clean Energy Connection Program, and the Company's Renewable Energy Development Initiative Program. While Walmart assisted in developing these opportunities, the opportunities are open to other interested large customers, not just Walmart.

V.  Supply Side Opportunities
Q.YOU MENTIONED THAT THE COMPANY IS SEEKING APPROVAL FOR SPECIFIC CUSTOMER-SITED SUPPLY-SIDE RESOURCES THROUGH TWO NEW DER PROGRAMS. COULD YOU PLEASE PROVIDE MORE DETAIL ABOUT THESE DER PROGRAMS?
A.The Company is seeking to offer the DER Co-Location Program to large C&I customers, which would site a Company-owned dispatchable 10 MW or greater back-up generator at the customer's premises. The back-up generator would be a system resource for the Company during normal grid operations, but would then switch to back-up power for the participating customer during a power outage. The economics of the program is based on the cost of the back-up unit itself and the value that it brings to the Company's system as set forth in the accompanying DCL-1 tariff.
Q.WHAT IS THE SECOND PROPOSED DER PROGRAM?
A.The second proposed DER program is the DER Customer Owned Program, which, as its name suggests, provides a credit to C&I customers who own a dispatchable back-up generator with a minimum of 1 MW and maximum of 10 MW capacity, either from one account or through the aggregation of multiple accounts, under the proposed DCO-1 tariff. As with the DER Co-Location Program, the Company would operate the back-up generator as a system resource during normal operations and as back-up power for the participating customer during outages.
Q.DOES WALMART SUPPORT THESE TYPES OF PROGRAMS?
A.Yes, Walmart supports the Company's proposed DER programs and, in fact, has supported a similar program through testimony filed with the Louisiana Public Service Commission for Entergy Louisiana LLC's Power Through program, which provides additional supply-side resources to the utility while giving participating customers resilient back-up power. Walmart looks forward to continuing its conversation with GPC about the proposed DER programs and whether these programs are a good fit for Walmart and its operational needs. 
Q.DOES WALMART BELIEVE THERE ARE ADDITIONAL CUSTOMER-PROVIDED RESOURCES AND OPPORTUNITIES AVAILABLE TO THE COMPANY?
A. Yes, it does. As I mentioned above, as part of its sustainability efforts, Walmart is working to make clean energy accessible and affordable for its customers and members, and it has committed to unlocking 2 GW of new community solar projects around the United States.  
Q.HOW CAN PROJECTS SUCH AS COMMUNITY SOLAR HELP THE COMPANY UNLOCK ADDITIONAL CAPACITY RESOURCES TO MEET ITS GROWING CUSTOMER DEMAND?
A.The structure of a Community Solar project can vary depending on legislative authority, customer resources, and utility appetite for customer involvement. In some states, such as New Jersey, New York, Nevada, and other states, the legislature has promulgated statutes that allow third parties to install smaller community solar projects and re-sell the output from that facility to individual customers. In other states, such as Georgia, where third parties are generally prohibited from re-selling electricity from a solar installation, there are other opportunities for customers to support solar generation resources. For example, Walmart could invest its own capital in solar projects where the utility owns and operates the solar installation, with or without co-located energy storage, as a system resource. This type of arrangement not only provides the utility with access to a greater number of generation resources, but also provides cleaner power for all of its customers. 
Q.ARE THERE ANY OTHER TECHNOLOGIES THAT WALMART BELIEVES CAN ACT AS A RESOURCE TO THE COMPANY'S GENERATION SYSTEM?
A.Yes, there are. As I briefly mentioned above with community solar, customer-owned or customer-financed BESS, similar to the technology that the Company is seeking approval of in this Docket, can also enable the Company to provide additional energy storage that can act as a balancing resource to renewable energy powered generation. 
Another opportunity is leveraging customer-side generation that can provide benefits not only to the individual customer, but also to the utility's system and resource planning as a whole. As I mentioned earlier, Walmart is currently developing and will own on-site, behind the meter resources at its facilities. These resources can act primarily as replacement power for Walmart's operations with any additional power being made available to the utility or to third parties where allowed under community solar structures. 
Q.WHAT IS WALMART'S RECOMMENDATION TO THE COMMISSION REGARDING DER PROGRAMS AND CUSTOMER PROVIDED SUPPLY-SIDE RESOURCES?
A.Walmart appreciates GPC's continued efforts to develop programs that leverage customer sites and resources as not only a way to meet increasing customer demand, but also as a means to provide cleaner power to all customers. To continue this initiative and to leverage new and evolving technology and customer resources, Walmart recommends that the Commission:  
(1)require the Company to work with interested stakeholders to build upon the Company's proposed DER Co-Location Program and DER Customer Owned Program by developing additional C&I programs that provide more options to customers and will capitalize on resources that customers can bring to the Company's system on the supply-side such as those mentioned earlier in my testimony and any other opportunities that help the Company provide affordable and reliable clean energy solutions that benefit all customers; and 
(2)require the Company seek Commission approval of such program(s) as part of its next statutorily required 2025 IRP.
 
VI.  Demand Side Opportunities
Q.YOU MENTIONED THAT THE COMPANY IS SEEKING APPROVAL IN THIS DOCKET FOR SPECIFIC DEMAND SIDE RESOURCES, SUCH AS A NEW CURTAILABLE LOAD PROGRAM FOR C&I CUSTOMERS. IS WALMART INTERESTED IN THESE TYPES OF PROGRAMS?
A.Yes, it is. The Company is seeking approval of its proposed Curtailable Load Program and corresponding DCL-1 tariff for C&I Customers in addition to its currently approved Demand Response Plus Energy Credit Program. Although Walmart is continuing to evaluate its interest in the Curtailable Load Program, at present, Walmart has over a thousand stores participating in demand response initiatives throughout the United States. These programs span multiple Regional Transmission Organizations ("RTOs") and Independent System Operators ("ISOs"), including:  ISO New England Inc., New York Independent System Operator, PJM Interconnection, LLC, Electric Reliability Council of Texas, Midcontinent Independent System Operator, and California Independent System Operator. Walmart also participates in dozens of demand response programs and, at utility or government request, has provided emergency responses outside of formal programs. 
However, to maximize customer engagement and system benefit, Walmart recognizes that multiple demand response programs may be needed to meet the needs of most customers. The benefits of load reduction through these types of programs have been demonstrated time and time again through extreme weather events such as 2021 Winter Storm Uri and excessive heat during summer months. Additionally, demand response resources can potentially delay and/or reduce the need to develop new generation facilities, saving money for the Company's customers. In order to maximize the benefits of supply-side management through a robust suite of program offerings, Walmart supports approval of the Company's proposed Demand Response Plus Energy Credit Program and requests that the Company continue to work with C&I customers to develop additional programs that can meet the needs of the most number of customers.
Q.WHAT IS WALMART'S RECOMMENDATION TO THE COMMISSION REGARDING DEMAND SIDE MANAGEMENT PROGRAMS?
A.Walmart recommends that the Commission: 
(1)require the Company to work with interested stakeholders to build upon the Company's proposed Demand Response Plus Energy Credit Program by developing additional C&I programs that provide more options to customers and will capitalize on resources that customers can bring to the Company's system on the demand side and any other opportunities that help the Company provide affordable and reliable clean energy solutions that benefit all customers; and 
(2)require the Company seek Commission approval of such program(s) as part of its next statutorily required 2025 IRP. 
Q. DOES THIS CONCLUDE YOUR TESTIMONY?
A.Yes.
