BEFORE THE GEORGIA PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION

GEORGIA POWER COMPANY
DOCKET NO. 55378

Direct Hearing Request

BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to this direct hearing request (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided forward-looking estimates of revenues, loads, and costs for specific capacity projects. All of such information (the “Information”) constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains detailed data related to the Company’s load and revenue forecast in addition to the estimated costs for capacity, financing, capital costs, unit, and operation assumptions.  Disclosure of the Information would provide extensive insight into the Company’s projected unit operations. If revealed to the public, a generation wholesaler, power marketer, or original equipment manufacturer could use the Information to arbitrarily tailor proposals with the intention of pricing products and services according to the Company’s expected costs, which could undermine the Company’s ability to procure the best cost products and services for customers. Such disclosure could unfairly allow competitors to artificially manipulate the wholesale market and ultimately harm the Company. Lastly, the Company’s competitors are generally not required to file similar forecast information and to require the Company to do so would put it at an economic disadvantage.

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.

Southern Company Services acts as agent on behalf of the Operating Companies for the monthly settlement of the Intercompany Interchange Contract (“IIC”), both for capacity and energy transactions. All payments are settled between Southern Company Services and each Operating Company. 
As part of the IIC, each Operating Company is expected to have adequate resources to reliably serve its own obligations, and the IIC accounts for temporary surpluses or deficits through reserve sharing. Therefore, the capacity amounts associated with reserve sharing are not directly related to the planning reserves and capacity needs associated with the coordinated integrated resource planning process.

The settlement of capacity is a monthly calculation per the IIC, utilizing inputs related to load responsibility, recognized capacity, equivalent unavailability factors, and capacity rates. BEFORE THE GEORGIA PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION

Direct Hearing Request

BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to this hearing request (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided data supporting the economic analysis of projects proposed in the 2023 IRP Update with sensitive pricing and confidential details (the “Information”). All of such information constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information includes competitively sensitive details specific to the resources under contract, proposed to be acquired or developed. If the Information were made public, competitors, bidders, and suppliers could use the Information to unfairly manipulate the request for proposals process and competitive market to structure future bids and set an artificial price floor to arbitrarily increase prices to the detriment of the Company and its customers. Public dissemination of the Information would undermine Georgia Power’s ability to negotiate the best price and contract terms and could harm the Company’s ability to secure the best cost bids and resources for the benefit of customers. In addition, the terms of the agreements themselves provides for the confidentiality and protection of the Information. Compromising the confidentiality of such Information could harm Georgia Power in current or future negotiations, as counterparties may us the Information as a bargaining tool in negotiations or fear compelled disclosure of key contractual terms. Lastly, the Company’s competitors are not required to reveal or publish similar information.

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information.  Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.

A(Witness Valle)  Yes.  That is part of the forecast.  We have –

A(Witness Valle)  We have a light-duty section of the forecast, and we have a medium-to-heavy duty, which would be capturing what Walmart would be adding to the system.
QAnd that's actually information you could kind of break out so it would be a little bit more visible to those of us that want to see it?

Response:
Of the large load external adjustments modeled in the 2023 IRP Update, Electric Vehicle (“EV”) Manufacturing represents about 2% of the total announced load. EV Battery Manufacturing, which is related but treated as a separate segment, represents more than 6.5% of total announced load. 
 
The Company includes estimates of peak usage for light (i.e., passenger) and medium/heavy-duty vehicles in its 2023 IRP Update. In total, projected growth in peak load from all electric vehicles ranges from less than 0.5% in 2024 to nearly 12% in 2043. In the early years of the forecast, light duty vehicles account for nearly all of the growth. Medium and heavy-duty vehicles start becoming more prevalent in the mid-2030s and eventually outpace the growth in demand from light duty vehicles in the later years of the forecast.
