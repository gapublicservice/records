As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-JKA-4-1 (“Response”). All of such information (the “Information”) constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains monthly projections of load ramp up for new customers projected to come online. Publicly disclosing this information would allow competitors and market participants to know how the load is ramping in and how that could impact demands for energy and supply availability. In turn, if the Information were made public, competitors, bidders, and suppliers could use the Information to unfairly manipulate the request for proposals process and competitive market to structure future bids and set an artificial price floor to arbitrarily increase prices to the detriment of the Company and its customers. Public dissemination of the Information would undermine Georgia Power’s ability to negotiate the best price and contract terms and could harm the Company’s ability to secure the best cost bids and resources for the benefit of customers. Lastly, the Company’s competitors are not required to reveal or publish similar information.  

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. STF-JKA-4-1

Question:
Please refer to the following statement on (or about) page 10: “Given the significance of the Company’s increased projected loads and corresponding capacity needs, Georgia Power cannot wait to conduct the All- Source RFP or wait until the 2025 IRP to address these needs.”  Please provide any analysis or documentation supporting this statement, including consideration of the actual or expected timing of load increases, the capacity available to the Company to serve the load and the length of time required to conduct an All Source RFP.  Please provide a table showing expected load requirements by month and the timelines for conducting an All Source RFP.


Response:
As shown in Figure 4 of the Main Document of the 2023 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) Update, which was filed on October 27, 2023, without the capacity resources proposed in the 2023 IRP Update, Georgia Power has a capacity need as early as the winter of 2025/2026. The Company explained in the Main Document of the 2023 IRP Update that the Request for Proposal (“RFP”) process typically takes two to two and a half years from the selection of an independent evaluator (“IE”) to the certification of resources. 

An RFP timeline is driven by the Commission’s RFP Rules, bid development, generation and transmission evaluation timing, whether the RFP is utilizing a refreshed bid process, contract execution, and Georgia law. Specifically, Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(c)(3) states that the IE shall be retained in time to begin service at least six months prior to the expected issuance of the RFP. Following issuance of the RFP, approximately one and half months are allowed for bid development and submission. Upon receipt of bids, the Company begins the evaluation process. The time to complete bid evaluation can vary depending on whether the RFP utilizes a competitive tier and bid refresh process, as allowed by Commission Rules, and also the volume and technology types of bids. From RFP issuance to evaluation completion was approximately thirteen months in the Company’s 2022-2028 Capacity RFP but did not include transmission evaluation of Battery Energy Storage Solutions (“BESS”), which are expected to be part of future Capacity RFPs. Upon completion of bid evaluation, the Company pursues contract execution with the winning bidders. This took approximately two months in the 2022-2028 Capacity RFP. Finally, the Company must submit the recommended resources to the Commission for certification. In accordance with Georgia law, the certification process can take up to 180 days from submission of the certification application to a final order. Therefore, the typical RFP timeline from IE selection to certification of resources is approximately two to two and a half years. The timeline may vary for future RFPs depending on capacity procurement targets and the complexity of products sought as part of the RFP.

Following certification of RFP resources, to the extent new-build resources were part of the recommended and certified resource portfolio, new generation resources must be constructed to achieve commercial operation before the Company’s capacity need year. As the Company’s RFI indicated minimal existing resources available, new-build resources will be the vast majority of future RFPs. Further, any transmission upgrades required by the new generation resource must be constructed, which can take substantial time given the current long lead times on the procurement and supply of transmission equipment. 

Following a typical RFP timeline of two to two and a half years, if an RFP process commenced now, certification would not be achieved until sometime in calendar year 2026, which is after the Company’s capacity need begins in the winter of 2025/2026, and does not account for the build time for any certified new resources regardless of resource type. Moreover, if the Company waited until the 2025 IRP to propose resources to meet capacity needs in the winter of 2025/2026 through the winter of 2027/2028, the commercial operation for new-build resources would be further delayed, and the Company would not be able to meet needs in the winter of 2026/2027. The development schedules for the Company’s proposed new-build resources are provided in the Technical Appendix to the 2023 IRP Update. See STF-JKA-2-2 Attachment B for the Company’s projected capacity needs based on the current development timelines for the proposed resources in the 2023 IRP Update.

For additional information regarding the timing and complexities of administering an RFP, please see the Company’s response to STF-JKA-4-2.

Please see TS STF-JKA-4-1 Attachment for the monthly load ramp up.

Question:
Please refer to the following statement on page 25: “Consistent with Commission policy, the Company must maintain an appropriate balance between Company-owned resources and purchased power to ensure the reliability of the electric system and meet the energy needs of the customers it is obligated to serve.”  

Does the Company agree the Commission could modify the policy in difficult situations such as this when substantial capacity needs exist which cannot be met quickly and sufficiently with Company-owned resources? Is it the Company position that “Company-owned” means Georgia Power Company-owned, and whether “Southern Company capacity resources under contract to Georgia Power Company” may be considered equivalent to Company-owned capacity. 
See page 30 of testimony, which states that the Company-owned resources would fall below 70% by the winter of 2026/2027 and decrease to approximately 56% by the winter of 2030/2031.  Please provide an analysis, electronically, with all formulae intact, showing all data such as the names of the Company resources, the MWs of the Company resources and the annual sum of all Company resources, and the name of the PPA resources, the MWs of the PPA resources, and the annual sum of all PPA resources, and the percent calculations the Company performed. Please provide this on an annual basis covering 2023 through 2045, or the last year that any modeling was performed in this IRP.
Does the proposed MPC contract count as part of the Company owned 70% or as part of the 30% PPAs?  If it counts as part of the PPA resources, please provide justification why Southern Company resources would be viewed as PPA resources for this policy consideration.  
Does the Company agree that an important element of the decision to sign and seek approval of the MPC contract was the concern that MPC would retire some of its’ capacity absent the proposed sale to Georgia Power? Please explain.
Please explain why the policy of 70% ownership is critical to Georgia Power and explain whether the reasons today are the same as the reasons many years ago when the Commission first implemented this policy.
Explain why Georgia Power attempts to require ownership of battery resources whereas other utilities allow battery resources to be acquired via PPAs. 

Response:
The Commission has the authority to modify any Commission policy, including the policy on the appropriate balance between Company-owned resources and purchased power to ensure the reliability of the electric system and Georgia Power’s ability to meet the energy needs of the customers it is obligated to serve. However, the Company does not recommend the Commission modify its policy or the currently approved 70/30 ratio of Company-owned resources to purchased power. Georgia Power’s Direct Testimony highlights the potential challenges and risks associated with meeting the increased and earlier capacity need identified in the 2023 IRP Update, particularly related to the limited existing resource availability in the market, as exacerbated by the increased competition from other utilities. These challenges emphasize the importance of maintaining the minimum ownership percentage required by long-standing Commission policy to help ensure that the Company can reliably serve customers and that the Commission maintains sufficient oversight of the Company’s obligation to reliably serve customers. 

Company-owned resources refer only to Georgia Power-owned resources and does not include affiliate-owned resources. Therefore, the Mississippi Power power purchase agreement (“PPA”) is appropriately considered as purchased power.

Please see the “Figure 6” worksheet in the “Figures 4, 5, 6, & 9 – Capacity Needs Data for B22, B23, and 2023 IRP Update.xlsx” file located in the “Main Document” folder of the “TS 2023 IRP Update Models and Workpapers,” which was previously provided to the Commission Staff on October 27, 2023, with the 2023 IRP Update filing. Note, the ownership percentages provided in Figure 6 of the 2023 IRP Update Main Document and referenced in Georgia Power’s Direct Testimony are calculated based on the assumption that capacity needs would be met exclusively with PPAs and do not include specific resources from the IRP Update resource plan. See rows 344-349 of STF-JKA-4-11 Attachment for owned and purchased capacity percentages incorporating the Company’s proposed resources from the 2023 IRP Update.

The Mississippi Power PPA is considered purchased power. Each Southern Company retail electric operating company (Georgia Power, Alabama Power, and Mississippi Power) owns and operates its own resources for the benefit of its respective customers, unless the unit is co-owned. Georgia Power has no ownership interest or operational control over the units supplying the Mississippi Power PPA. In fact, the Mississippi Power PPA is not unit specific and may be supplied by any resource in the Mississippi Power fleet. Nevertheless, in the absence of the Mississippi Power PPA, several Mississippi Power resources would be retired and removed from the Southern Company pool. The existence of the Mississippi Power PPA ensures that these resources will continue to be part of the Southern Company pool and that the power produced will be designated for service to Georgia Power’s customers. It is appropriate for the Mississippi Power PPA to be considered a PPA, not a Georgia Power-owned resource.

 
As discussed in the Company’s response to STF-JKA-2-20(a), Mississippi Power was ordered to retire approximately 950 MW of capacity by the end of 2027 or show with detailed evidence why continued operation of the resources is in the best interests of its customers. Therefore, Mississippi Power planned to retire some of that capacity by the end of 2023 and was actively marketing the remaining capacity to be sold outside of the Southern Company system. By purchasing 750 MW from Mississippi Power through this PPA, Georgia Power ensures that this resource not only remains in the Southern Company pool but also provides dedicated, reliable, market-priced capacity and energy to Georgia Power’s customers to meet its burgeoning short-term demand.

Consistent with Commission policy established in 2001, the importance of the policy to maintain a minimum of 70% ownership is to help ensure that the Company can reliably serve customers and that the Commission maintains sufficient oversight of the Company’s obligation to reliably serve customers. The pace and volume of the capacity needs identified in the 2023 IRP Update cannot be met by market resources alone. As evidenced in the Company’s request for information (“RFI”), the capacity market is tight and there are limited existing resources available to meet the timing and volume of the Company’s needs. Therefore, in the absence of sufficient market resources,  the role of Company-owned resources is crucial to meeting customer needs on the system.

As stated in the 2023 IRP Update Main Document, Georgia Power is obligated to provide its customers with reliable electric service and, as such, cannot solely rely on existing or new market resources from independent power producers to provide the capacity needed to reliably serve its customers. The selection of market resources through the Commission’s competitive RFP process can benefit customers, and the Company continues to be supportive of this process. However, the market is neither obligated to serve the Company’s customers, nor is it positioned to fully address the accelerated timing and magnitude of the Company’s emerging capacity needs. To ensure Georgia Power can continue to reliably serve its customers in the face of increased and accelerated projected generation capacity needs, the Company must develop new resources in accordance with Commission Rules through RFP exceptions in the Commission Rules in addition to resources to be procured through the RFP process. 

While Georgia Power seeks approval of company-owned battery energy storage systems (“BESS”) in this IRP Update due to the timing necessary to address capacity needs as early as the winter of 2026/2027, the Company will also be seeking battery resources from the market through the upcoming request for proposals (“RFPs”) approved in the 2022 IRP: the All-Source RFP and the 500 MW energy storage system (“ESS”) RFP. These RFPs will present the market with opportunities to offer energy storage resources to Georgia Power via a PPA (selling energy and capacity to Georgia Power from a new or existing storage asset that remains third party owned), build transfer agreement (selling new energy storage assets proposed to be built by a third party and transferred to the Company upon completion), or asset purchase agreement (selling an existing storage asset to Georgia Power).

Georgia Power’s RFP process is a robust process that is governed by Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04. There are certain rules governing the RFP process that include but are not limited to (i) the six-month requirement between the RFP Service Date and the issuance of the RFP to the market, and (ii) that the draft of the RFP has to be shared with Commission Staff and Independent Evaluator at least one-hundred and twenty days prior to the planned issue date of the RFP. Question:
See page 36 of testimony, which discusses the Robins and Moody AFB BESS resource additions and the 200 MW BESS plus 200 MW solar project, and up to 1,000 MW of BESS. 
Line 8 states that without BESS, the winter planning capacity of the solar resources at the Robins and Moody AFB would be just 10% of nameplate, and with BESS it would be 100%.  Please provide all modeling analyses, reports, etc. performed to derive those values. Provide this electronically, with all formulae intact.    
Please provide all economic analyses that were performed of these Robins and Moody AFB resources. Provide all modeling analyses, reports, etc. performed, electronically, with all formulae intact. 
Page 36 discusses the 200 MW BESS plus 200 MW solar project.   Please state where this project will be installed, and provide all modeling analyses, reports, etc. performed to derive the winter planning capacity value of those two resources. Provide this electronically, with all formulae intact. 
Please provide all economic analyses that were performed of the 200 MW BESS plus 200 MW solar project. Provide all modeling analyses, reports, etc. performed, electronically, with all formulae intact. 
At page 7 of testimony, the Company states it is requesting authority to develop, own, and operate up to 1,000 MW of BESS resources, and at page 37, line 5, the Company states that after identifying additional BESS resources, it will seek Commission approval and certification.  Please explain the difference between seeking authority now from the Commission to develop, own and operate BESS resources, and seeking Commission approval later for certification of those resources. 
Response:
Robins and Moody solar resources are existing resources with a 10% winter Effective Load Carrying Capability (“ELCC”) based on their relatively early deployment and SERVM simulations summarized in STF-JKA-4-20 Attachment A. Adding a 4-Hour Battery Energy Storage System (“BESS”) with the same nominal capacity as the existing solar nominal capacity improves the overall aggregate facility (consisting of both the existing solar and incremental BESS components) to a 100% ELCC value, or 100% of the point of interconnection (“POI”) limit. The SERVM ELCC analysis for a combined solar and BESS facility is summarized in STF-JKA-4-20 Attachment B. See the response to STF-JKA-3-12 for further details on POI limitations.
Please see TS Economic Analysis Technical Appendix, TS Economic Analysis of Capacity Resources.xlsx workpaper, and SAM workpapers provided to Commission Staff on October 27, 2023. 
This project will be installed in REDACTED REDACTED. New solar resources have a winter ELCC value of 5% via simulations performed within the SERVM reliability model and summarized in STF-JKA-4-20 Attachment C. Consistent with Robins and Moody, the overall aggregate facility (consisting of both the solar and incremental BESS components) will equate to 100% ELCC value, 100% of the POI limit at the facility, or 200 MW total facility capacity value as described in part (a).
Please see TS Economic Analysis Technical Appendix, TS Economic Analysis of Capacity Resources.xlsx workpaper, and SAM workpapers provided to Commission Staff on October 27, 2023. 
The Company is seeking the authority to recover the costs associated with development activities on up to 1,000 MW of BESS to ensure it can meet the growing energy demands of its customers with resources to be online by the end of 2027. Approval of this for the 1,000 MW of BESS will allow the Company to begin the multi-year development process for these resources. As incremental BESS opportunities arise, the Company will bring specific project sites and costs to the Commission for review and certification.

STF-JKA-4-28

Question:
What assumptions has the Company made regarding bonus tax credits available for renewable resources (location where resources sited, domestic production of resources, etc.) in modeling of generic resources in the 2023 IRP. Please explain and provide workpapers, electronically with all formulae intact deriving input assumptions.

Response:
Generic renewable resource additions are assumed to receive the 10-year Clean Electricity Production Tax Credit (“PTC”), which includes the bonus tax credits of meeting the workforce requirement and one of the additional bonus tax credits of domestic production of resources or energy community location. Additionally, the Company assumes that storage resources would receive the Storage Investment Tax Credit (“ITC”), which also includes the bonus tax credits of meeting the workforce requirement and one of the additional bonus tax credits of domestic production or energy community location. Based on this assumption, the Company assumes a 40% ITC on top of build and maintenance capital cost as shown in the “SAM Files for Generic Resources” folder located in the “8 TS Resource Mix Study” folder within the “TS Technical Appendix” folder of the “TS 2023 IRP Update Models and Workpapers,” which were previously provided to Commission Staff on October 27, 2023, with the 2023 IRP Update filing. Calculations to derive the PTCs used for generic renewable resources are shown in TS STF-JKA-4-28 Attachment.
