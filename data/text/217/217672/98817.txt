INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-JKA-8-10 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided project specific information related to the transmission upgrades an investments required to support the resource request in the 2023 IRP Update (the “Information”). All of such Information constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains competitively sensitive cost information related to the prices Georgia Power has estimated for transmission equipment and specific details related to the timing of Company’s build out of transmission infrastructure. Public dissemination of the Information would allow Georgia Power’s competitors and suppliers to have access to the costs paid by the Company and insight into the Company’s transmission planning process. Access to the Information would also allow competitors to gain specific insight into the Company’s technical analysis regarding planned projects. Competitors would obtain an unfair advantage because they are not required to reveal similar information and can structure the pricing for competing products based on the Information. In the event the Information was released, it is quite likely that suppliers would use the Information to set the floor in establishing their own prices, thus artificially and inefficiently setting a market price that may not be representative of the best cost that the market could offer. Competitors would also unfairly benefit in having access and insight into the Company’s planning processes and methodologies. This competitive advantage for the Company’s suppliers and competitors would mean that Georgia Power will potentially pay higher prices to suppliers, ultimately harming Georgia Power's customers.

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.

2022 IRP STF-JKA-4-9
Question:
Refer to the Company’s Unit Retirement Study and the MG0 case Aurora runs for the LTCE, Zonal, and Asset Valuation Cases. (Aurora reference files: TS B2022 Final IRP MG0 URS Base LTCE_Archive.zip; TS B2022 Final IRP MG0 URS Base Zonal_Archive.zip; TS Output_B2022_Final IRP URS.xdb in the “Asset Evaluation Archives” folder)
a. Please explain why the Bowen plant annual output MWh drops significantly going from the System Operation Production Cost style analysis (LTCE, Zonal Runs) to the Avoided Cost methodology in the Asset Evaluation case (Bowen 1-4).
b. Does the Company utilize different operating characteristics for the candidate retirement units between the various runs? Please explain.
c. Does the Company’s Asset Evaluation study methodology account for system reliability constraints and operating reserve value of candidate units?
d. Please confirm that the Company estimates that the Bowen units would not run at all in calendar years 2025-2027 and 2032-2036, according to the Asset Valuation dispatch underlying the Unit Retirement Study.
e. Does the Company believe that if it kept the Bowen units online, in a zero carbon price future, that they would not be utilized, or would only operate uneconomically, for 3 consecutive years? Please explain.
Response:
a. The LTCE and Zonal runs are full production cost runs dispatched against load. In these runs, all constraints associated with meeting system requirements needed to serve load are considered, including minimum up and down time of resources, known local reliability constraints, operating reserve requirements, etc. Given the large minimum up and down times and startup time requirements for coal plants along with other system constraints, they generally remain online for extended periods in the zonal analysis as they are not flexible enough to only operate during the period they are needed, often resulting in a net cost. In the Asset Valuation runs, the units are dispatched against a fixed energy price stream only. The Asset Valuation runs maximize net energy benefit for a generating unit assuming a fixed energy price to determine the energy benefit or value of the existing unit and replacement unit in the same environment. In these runs, Plant Bowen does not run when its dispatch cost is greater than the marginal energy price over a commitment period equal to or greater than its minimum up time. This difference results in the Bowen plant annual output being greater in the LTCE and Zonal runs than in the Asset Valuation runs.
b. No, the Company does not utilize different operating characteristics for the candidate retirement units between the various runs.
c. No, the Asset Valuation runs themselves do not include system reliability constraints such as operating reserves. However, the Pre-Retirement Avoided Energy Costs (“PAEC”) thaare used in the Asset Valuation runs are a product of the Zonal runs that do account for system reliability constraints and operating reserve considerations.
d. The Company estimates that, in the Asset Valuation study based on its dispatch against PAEC, the Bowen units do not show economic unit operations in 2025-2027 and 2032-2036 when considering the individual unit economics apart from system needs.
e. The Company believes it is likely that the Bowen plant would run in a zero-carbon price future if the plant were kept online. As stated above regarding LTCE and Zonal runs, which are full production cost runs and more closely resemble how units would be committed and operated for system needs, it is anticipated that Bowen would be needed either for system economic or reliability needs. See TS STF-4-9 Attachment for additional information from the Aurora Zonal run.

a. The Company is seeking approval for the authority to procure 1,000 MW of ESS resources for ownership using a competitive EPC turnkey RFP. As stated in the Energy Storage Procurement Action plan, the Company will return to the Commission for approval and certification of the EPC turnkey RFP results prior to construction. The Company will plan on and use the results of the Renewable Integration Study to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of ESS bids from the EPC turnkey RFP while seeking to add 1,000 MW of ESS.
b. The Renewable Integration Study establishes an economic benefit to integrating renewable resources when flexible resources such as BESS are available to the electric system equivalent to approximately 15% of the solar resource penetration. The Company will
evaluate the results of the EPC turnkey RFP to determine cost-effectiveness for certification of the 1,000 MW.
c. The Renewable Integration Study does not directly evaluate the capacity equivalence of solar resources. Instead, the study determines the cost to mitigate potential balancing violations through the use of operating reserves, specifically those with instantaneous load following capability. The analysis shows that this mitigation cost is significantly reduced by the presence of flexible resources in the energy system equivalent to approximately 15% of the solar resource penetration. This reduced mitigation cost is part of the economic analysis provided in the Battery Storage Cost-Benefit Analysis.
d. Of the 1,000 MW of Company-owned BESS, the Company plans to locate 265 MW at McGrau Ford Battery Facility in North Georgia. The Company is also conducting a state-wide land potential study to identify other possible locations in accordance with the Company’s BESS procurement action plan laid out in Section 13.4 of the Georgia Power 2022 IRP.
e. The Company has completed transmission interconnection studies for its proposed McGrau Ford Battery Facility. Please see TS STF-JKA-6-2 Attachment. Additional transmission studies will be performed as suitable sites are identified and in accordance with the Company’s BESS procurement action plan laid out in Section 13.4 of the Georgia Power 2022 IRP.

Response:
Several steps are involved to move from the Budget 2024 (“B2024”) load forecast to get to the load forecast included in Aurora for the 2023 IRP Update. First, two Metrix ND models are built to get the hourly load shape forecasts, one for weather sensitive load and the other for non-weather sensitive load. These models were part of the initial 2023 IRP Update filing along with the other models. The names of these Trade Secret models are: “GPC - B24 Base - WS.NDM” and “GPC - B24 Base - Non WS.NDM.”
The next step is to calibrate the hourly load shapes to the B2024 unadjusted energy forecast using Metrix LT. This model was also included with the initial filing. The name of this trade secret file is: “GPC_Peak_B24.ltm.”
During the calibration process, the hourly loads for residential, commercial, industrial, MARTA, government lighting, res/com/ind solar, res/com light-duty EV, heavy-duty-medium-duty EV, commercial large customer (com_p95), and industrial large customer (Ind_p95) are generated. The calibrated values for each category are summed up to obtain the total unadjusted Georgia Power hourly load. Please see TS STF-JKA-8-20 Attachment A.
The last step is to convert the total unadjusted hourly load to central time and calibrate it to the B2024 adjusted energy and peak forecast, which includes adjustments for demand side management (“DSM”) and cogeneration, using Metrix LT. TS STF-JKA-8-20 Attachment B contains the Metrix LT model used for this final calibration. The model uses TS STF-JKA-8-20 Attachment C and TS STF-JKA-8-20 Attachment D as inputs for the calibration. The output of this model – Georgia Power total calibrated hourly loads in central time – is what is used in Aurora. Please refer to TS STF-JKA-8-20 Attachment E for the input to Aurora. 
Since the 2023 IRP Update Load and Energy Forecast is based on the whole portfolio of new large customer projects, information on individual projects is not available. The commercial and industrial P95 hourly load forecasts are found in TS STF-JKA-8-20 Attachment A, “Hourly” tab, columns H and M.

Question:
Refer to the Aurora Mix Study and the Change Set, “B2024 Final IRP – Reduce Optimization Noise” 
Please explain what the “reduce optimization noise” supplemental parameter in this change set is trying to accomplish? 
Please explain what necessitated the need for this change set. 


Response:
The following “Reduce Optimization Noise” parameter description has been provided by the Aurora software developer and will be included in the help file for a future update.

When True, this parameter activates logic throughout the simulation that seeks to minimize potential differences in the solution that might occur when the same project is run on different machines. The following steps are taken: 
The constrained dispatch variable objective coefficients are adjusted slightly to reduce the likelihood of multiple optimal solutions existing. 
The constrained dispatch solution is rounded before the results are passed to the commitment optimization logic.
The RHS values for daily limits in the commitment optimization originating from the constrained dispatch logic are rounded in the formulation.
The solution to the month-ahead storage scheduling logic is rounded and the simplex method is forced for the quadratic program solving for the storage schedule. 
The mixed-integer program solving for the LT decisions uses a “Work Limit” instead of a “Time Limit” when solving with Gurobi. 
Note that because the solver can behave slightly differently on different hardware, it is not guaranteed that Aurora will give identical results on different machines. But it is much more likely when this parameter is set to True.

During post-processing analysis work of previous Aurora runs, it was discovered that there were variations in capacity expansion plan results across different machines having only slightly different architectures. This change set is the solution proposed by Energy Exemplar to overcome the differences.

Question:
Even though Batteries have 30% or even higher ITC’s available, won’t the prices of BESS components be driven up in the future, such as from supply chain issues and market demand for batteries?    


Response:
No, the Company does not agree that the prices of Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”) components will generally increase in the future. The responses received from the Company’s 2023 Request for Information (“RFI”) and Request for Proposals (“RFP”) have shown that BESS prices have declined since the highs seen in 2022 and that lead times are well under 18 months. The Company is currently in negotiations to secure competitive market pricing for the BESS.
While market demand is expected to continue increasing for BESS components, global production capacity is also expected to continue to increase across the BESS value chain. The supply-side growth is expected to drive further economies of scale and drive prices down even as demand rises. The BESS industry is still relatively young, and significant gains in technological advancement and manufacturing improvements, which will put more downward pressure on prices, are still expected this decade. In 2023, the Company observed notable growth in investment in energy storage manufacturing projects that will drive these economies of scale and help secure supply chains. Further, industry analysts expect this trend to continue for years. 
BloombergNEF surveyed miners and metal traders in 2023 and found that respondents expect key battery metals to fall farther in 2024. Changes in battery pack prices are largely tied to the cost of such material inputs.1 The industry’s overall transition from using nickel manganese cobalt oxide (NMC) cells to lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells in grid applications places long-term downward pressure on BESS because LFP relies on less expensive and less volatile raw materials, overall. In addition, new lithium deposits are being discovered and a nascent battery recycling industry is growing, both of which will be able to expand the raw material supply base, increase raw material competition, and further support the continuation of long-term price decline.
