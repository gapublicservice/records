AMENDED APPLICATION FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE OF
SOUTHERN RENEWABLE ENERGY ASSOCIATION

COMES NOW, the Southern Renewable Energy Association (“SREA”), and hereby files this Amended Application for Leave to Intervene (“Application”) in the above-referenced docket   pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59,  Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) Rule 515-2-1-.06, and the Commission’s Procedural and Scheduling Order (PSO) issued on November 21, 2023.  In support hereof, SREA respectfully states as follows:
1.
On October 27, 2023, Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power”) filed its Integrated Resource Plan Update (“IRP”).  On December 6, 2023, Georgia Power made a filing indicating that its legal notice was published on December 4, 2023. SREA subsequently filed a timely Application for leave to intervene and participate in this docket on December 6, 2023, within 30 days of Georgia Power’s legal notice.  SREA files this Amended Application for Leave to Intervene for the purpose of requesting to add its new Regulatory Director, Whit Cox, to the service list for this docket as outlined in paragraph 5 herein. 
2.
SREA is a regional trade association that works to promote the responsible development and use of utility-scale wind energy, energy storage, solar energy and transmission throughout the South. Georgia is one of eleven states in which SREA works. SREA believes that the Georgia Power service territory market for utility-scale wind energy, energy storage, solar energy, and transmission, is large enough to incorporate significantly greater amounts of these resources into its IRP than the IRP has included. SREA is a nonprofit trade association consisting of individuals, businesses, and other entities that promote the economic and environmental benefits of utility-scale wind energy, energy storage, solar energy and transmission in Georgia. SREA members have a vital interest in the pending dockets inasmuch as its members develop and deploy facilities to generate and produce energy. The Commission’s actions in this docket will affect development, deployment, or use of the various energy resources in the region and thus will affect the investment decisions made by members of SREA.  Moreover, SREA members want to ensure that they can provide low-cost energy resources in Georgia to reduce prices for consumers and encourage economic development for the state.  As a result, SREA has substantial interests in the outcome of this proceeding and desires to intervene in order to advance and protect those interests.
3.
As required in the Application Requirements adopted by the Commission in the PSO, SREA has identified other intervening parties whose interests are similar to SREA, including the Advanced Power Alliance, Clean Energy Buyers Association, Sierra Club, Georgia Interfaith Power and Light, Southern Alliance for Clean Energy, Georgia Association of Manufacturers, Georgia Solar Energy Association, and Metropolitan Atlanta Rapid Transit Authority. SREA’s rights and interests cannot be adequately represented by any other party to the above-styled docket. SREA’s specific interests in the development of utility-scale solar energy, wind energy, energy storage, hybrid resources, and transmission are not adequately represented by the other pending intervenors in this docket. In particular, transmission system planning is not a focal point of the applications filed by the other pending intervenors. Further, SREA’s participation in this matter will not unduly delay these proceedings or prejudice any other party. 
4. 
SREA respectfully requests that it be granted leave to intervene and participate fully as a party in these proceedings, including the right to present testimony and exhibits, cross-examine witnesses, and file briefs.  SREA has participated in prior Georgia Power IRP proceedings. Our expertise regarding utility-scale wind energy, energy storage, solar energy, transmission and utility resource planning would be valuable in this IRP proceeding. SREA intends to participate in the docket by providing direct testimony regarding but not necessarily limited to IRP capacity expansion methodology and assumptions, energy storage, and transmission planning.
