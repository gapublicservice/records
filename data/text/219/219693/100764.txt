Executive Summary
This Application for the Certification of the Robins, Moody, Hammond, and McGrau Ford Phase II Battery
Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”) projects (“Application”) is made pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-3A-4 and
Commission Rule 515-3-4-.07(2) and in accordance with the Order Adopting Stipulated Agreement issued
by the Georgia Public Service Commission (the “Commission”) approving the associated stipulation
(“Stipulation”) in Georgia Power Company’s (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) 2023 Integrated
Resource Plan (“IRP”) Update in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update Final Order”). As explained in the
Company’s 2023 IRP Update and this Application, 500 megawatts (“MW”) of BESS are necessary to meet
the needs of Georgia Power customers in the winter of 2026/2027 and beyond, and approval of this
Application is in the public interest.

Per Items 7 and 8 of the Stipulation, the Company is authorized to develop, own, and operate up to 500
MW of BESS resources at various sites to be identified by the Company, including the Robins and Moody
BESS projects, to meet customer needs in the winter of 2026/2027. This Application meets the
requirement for the Company to file for expedited certification of the applicable projects once
Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (“EPC”) agreements have been executed. This BESS
development is exempted from the request for proposal (“RFP”) process under Commission Rules 515-3-
4-.04(3)(f)(6) and (7).

The 2023 IRP Update Final Order emphasizes that the “Stipulation represents a reasonable path to
ensuring that Georgia Power can continue to serve its existing and growing customer base with the
reliability that customers value and expect, while continuing to support Georgia’s growing and thriving
economy.”1 Through the approval of the 500 MW of company-owned BESS and the rest of the stipulated
resource plan, the Company’s forecasted incremental revenue associated with the state’s extraordinary
economic growth will have downward pressure on rates of at least $2.89 for the typical residential
customer using an average of 1,000 kilowatt-hour (“kWh”) per month in the next base rate case.2

This Application includes the following portfolio of sites identified by the Company to fulfill the 500 MW
total of 4-hour duration lithium-ion BESS resources:

       •   128 MW Robins BESS in Bibb County, GA,
       •   49.5 MW Moody BESS in Lowndes County, GA,
       •   57.5 MW Hammond BESS in Floyd County, GA, and
       •   265 MW McGrau Ford Phase II BESS in Cherokee County, GA.

These new BESS projects are important capacity resources as part of the Company’s well-balanced and
diversified approach approved in the 2023 IRP Update Final Order. This strategy and approach will allow
Georgia Power to continue providing reliable and cost-effective service as it navigates unique reliability

resource planning challenges, including increased and accelerated generation capacity needs and
increased renewable resource penetration across the system.

The proposed BESS resource portfolio projects specifically meet the needs identified in the 2023 IRP
Update docket in a cost-effective and strategic fashion. Furthermore, each BESS project will produce a
variety of benefits. Each project will leverage existing transmission infrastructure. As such, these projects
save time and avoid additional capital investment otherwise required to construct interconnection
facilities and transmission system upgrades to charge and discharge the BESS. The proposed BESS
resources will also provide energy arbitrage benefits by optimizing energy savings by shifting the energy
from hours with a relatively low system marginal cost to hours with a relatively high system marginal cost.
Furthermore, the resource portfolio helps minimize costs by maximizing economies of scale within the
bounds of the transmission available for each site and by pursuing available tax credits through the
Inflation Reduction Act (“IRA”) and passing those benefits through to customers.

The Robins and Moody BESS projects will be primarily charged by the existing solar resources at each site.
The BESS will thereby firm up the winter planning capacity of the existing solar facilities at both sites by
storing energy that can then be dispatched by system operators to benefit the grid. Without BESS, the
winter planning capacity for the solar resources at each site is 10% of the nameplate capacity of each
facility. With the BESS, each facility can provide 100% of the solar nameplate capacity to meet winter
capacity needs, thus maximizing the utilization and benefit of the existing solar resources and existing
interconnection capabilities.

The Hammond BESS project is a standalone BESS that allows the Company to leverage existing
infrastructure from the retired coal-fired Plant Hammond facility, where transmission capacity has been
identified to be available by winter of 2026/2027. By repurposing Company-owned land and increasing tax
incentives from the IRA, the Hammond BESS provides significant benefit to customers while aiding the
Company in meeting its capacity obligations.

The McGrau Ford Phase II BESS project is also a standalone BESS that will expand on the McGrau Ford
Phase I BESS, that the Commission provisionally approved in the 2022 IRP. The Company is able to leverage
the development and site work, land acquisition, and contracting strategy from McGrau Ford Phase I BESS
in developing the McGrau Ford Phase II BESS. For example, The Company is able to streamline the
contracting and construction process by using the same construction partner and existing land. In addition,
the preliminary design for McGrau Ford Phase I BESS facilitates the Company’s ability to cost effectively
expand the project level substation and generation tie line as compared to constructing a new project level
substation.

The Company entered into EPC Agreements with Burns & McDonnell for the Robins BESS and McGrau Ford
Phase II BESS projects and EPC Agreements with Crowder Industrial Construction, LLC. (“Crowder”) for the
Moody BESS and Hammond BESS projects. Both firms are well known for their extensive experience within
the power industry, demonstrating their capability to contribute significantly to the projects. The
Company’s review of their proposals and engagement with these firms has established a solid foundation
that has validated and reinforced the effectiveness of their execution strategies. The Company has

Description of the Proposed Capacity Resources

The Company seeks to certify four 4-hour duration lithium-iron phosphate (“LFP”) BESS projects, which
will create a total of 500 MW of capacity and associated energy. The Company has contracted for all four
BESS projects to reach commercial operation by November 2026 to meet capacity needs beginning in
winter of 2026/2027. Each project will be comprised of Tesla Megapack LFP battery cell technology and
power conversion systems. Projects are designed with initial storage capacities and augmentation
schedules to ensure they maintain adequate energy capacity over the 20-year life of the resources. Figure
1 below provides additional project details and estimated performance metrics for the proposed BESS
resources:

To facilitate quick deployment, the Robins and Moody BESS resources will utilize existing solar
interconnections and will be charged primarily by the existing Company-owned solar facilities near Robins
and Moody Air Force Bases, respectively. These co-located BESS will, however, be constructed with the
capability to grid-charge if constraints are not present on the transmission system.

The remaining balance of the 500 MW is proposed to be fulfilled by two standalone BESS projects that
have been identified to be cost-effective for customers: the Hammond BESS project, to be located at the
site of the retired coal-fired Plant Hammond, and the McGrau Ford Phase II BESS project, to leverage the
ongoing McGrau Ford Phase I BESS project from the 2022 IRP.

Once developed, the Robins, Moody, Hammond, and McGrau Ford Phase II BESS projects will serve as
dispatchable capacity resources that provide customers with a reliable and economical source of
electricity required by the winter of 2026/2027. In addition, the resources will form a critical component
of the Company’s diverse generation portfolio, helping ensure the Company has the mix of technologies
necessary to provide reliable and resilient electric service for all customers during all hours.

Commission Rule Exception to the RFP Requirement in Rule 515-3-4-.04

Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6) and (7) provide exceptions to the Commission rule that requires the
Company to procure supply-side resources through an RFP process. Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6)
provides that the Commission shall expressly consider in each IRP, and make a determination in each IRP,
whether to exclude from the RFP Process any new supply-side resources identified in the soliciting entity’s
approved IRP. Further, Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(7) articulates the Commission’s policy that
investor-owned utilities under its regulation, such as Georgia Power, shall maintain a minimum percentage
of their capacity as self-owned rate-based assets. Pursuant to the 2001 IRP Final Order, the Commission
established the minimum ratio of self-owned assets to purchased power at 70/30. The Commission Rule
provides that when the utility is nearing or finds that its self-owned capacity would fall below this
minimum percentage (here, 70%), the utility shall inform the Commission of this eventuality in advance of
the RFP Process and seek guidance from the Commission on how to proceed. The Company provided
notice to the Commission that it was close to falling below the minimum ownership percentage as part of
its 2022 IRP and 2023 IRP Update filings.

Accordingly, Items 7 and 8 of the Stipulation exempt these resources from the RFP process under
Commission Rules 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6) and (7) for the 500 MW Company-owned BESS portfolio, including
the Robins and Moody BESS projects. By developing new resources in accordance with these RFP
exceptions in the Commission’s Rules in addition to procuring resources through the typical RFP process,
Georgia Power can continue providing its customers with reliable service and meet the timelines of
customers’ needs. To meet Item 9 of the Stipulation, Georgia Power is also issuing a narrowly tailored RFP
on an expedited schedule to meet the capacity needed to serve customers during the winter of 2027/2028.
It is critical that the Company leverage procurement options to maintain a diverse resource portfolio that
includes market resources, Company-owned resources, and a balance of resource technologies to ensure
a reliable and efficient generation fleet.

        Cost-Benefit Analysis
Included as TS Attachment A to this Application is the Company’s Economic Analysis with a cost-benefit

        Site Selection Analysis
The Company selected the Robins site for its expedited deployment capabilities, primarily attributed to
the existing solar facility at this location which ensures known transmission deliverability. This strategic
choice allows the Company to leverage existing infrastructure, thereby eliminating the need for
constructing new generator step-up (“GSU”) project-level substations and eliminating potential expenses
and long lead time projects associated with interconnection and network upgrades.

        Fuel Use
The Robins solar facility will be the primary fuel source for the Robins BESS facility. The BESS will store the
renewable energy during daylight hours and discharge the energy as needed to support grid operations
and meet capacity needs. Additionally, the Robins BESS will primarily serve as a cost-effective, dispatchable
capacity resource by optimizing energy savings. It will achieve this by shifting the energy from hours with
relatively low system marginal cost to hours with relatively high system marginal cost. While it will
primarily charge from solar energy, it can also charge from the grid if constraints are not present on the
transmission system.

         Battery and Equipment Supply Agreement
In August 2024, the Company finalized a BESA with Tesla for the procurement of an integrated battery
energy storage system, the Megapack 2 XL, for deployment at Robins BESS. This agreement was executed
following a comprehensive Supply Chain RFP process. Manufactured at Tesla's Megafactory in Lathrop, CA,
the basic storage component in the Tesla Megapack 2 XL is the lithium-iron phosphate (“LFP”) battery cell.
These cells are assembled into battery modules and integrated into the Megapack units. Megapack’s
modular design allows for easy scalability, connecting multiple units to meet project requirements. Each
unit can store over 3.9 MWh of energy. The system arrives on-site fully integrated with initial testing
completed, ready to deliver alternating current (“AC”) electrical output.

Safety is crucial in the Megapack design. Each unit undergoes rigorous testing to ensure safe operation.
Tesla’s advanced Battery Management System (“BMS”) monitors and regulates temperature, voltage, and
state-of-charge, ensuring optimal performance. Tesla’s technology is well-proven and reliable with grid-
scale batteries operational in over 65 countries.

The agreement includes a warranty that safeguards against equipment defects and guarantees against
energy capacity degradation. In addition to the BESA, the Company will enter into a Long-Term
Commitments Agreement (“LTCA”), which will provide an enhanced guaranteed energy retention capacity
curve, as well as a Site RTE guarantee for each year of operation.

The executed BESA between Georgia Power Company and Tesla is included in the Technical Appendix to
this Application.

Introduction

As stated in the 2023 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) Update, Georgia’s economy has continued to
thrive, bringing rapid economic expansion and an unprecedented increase in the demand for energy to
the state. This rapid economic expansion requires Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or “GPC”)
to develop or procure additional capacity resources so that it can continue to provide reliable electric
service to its customers. Therefore, Southern Company Services (“SCS”) and Georgia Power (collectively,
the “Company”) evaluated numerous resources that can provide the capacity necessary to meet its
customers’ energy needs during this period of extraordinary economic growth in Georgia. In accordance
with the Order Adopting Stipulated Agreement issued by the Georgia Public Service Commission (the
“Commission”) approving the stipulation in Georgia Power’s 2023 IRP Update Final Order in Docket No.
55378, the Company has identified 500 megawatts (“MW”) of Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”)
to meet customer needs in the winter of 2026/2027 and beyond. Consistent with the analysis provided to
the PSC in the 2023 IRP Update, Georgia Power’s Application for the Certification of the Robins, Moody,
Hammond, and McGrau Ford Phase II BESS (“Application”) includes updated economics that support the
Company’s Application.


Analysis Overview

In the 2023 IRP Update Economic Analysis, the Company compared the costs and benefits of various
resource options to determine the least-cost solutions for meeting its capacity needs. This comprehensive
evaluation considered a range of fuel prices and carbon price paths, resulting in five distinct planning
scenarios. Utilizing the Aurora model, the Company quantified the production cost savings (commonly
referred to as fuel savings). The economic evaluation incorporated the costs of new BESS resources,
including in-service capital costs, maintenance capital costs, O&M costs, and other incremental costs to
customers. The Inflation Reduction Act (“IRA”) benefit of the Investment Tax Credits (“ITC”) for BESS was
also factored into the analysis. Additionally, the renewable integration benefit was assumed as a benefit
for the BESS resources. The cost of the necessary transmission system upgrades was included. The analysis
preserved the period assumed in the 2023 IRP Update economic analysis, which was sufficient for
evaluating resources with varying unit lives and term lengths using the term equalization method.
Therefore, these updated economic evaluations are consistent with the information provided in the 2023
IRP Update. For additional information, please review the 2023 IRP Update Economic analysis in Docket
No. 55378.

500 MW BESS Economic Analysis 
2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update BESS Certification 


Introduction 
As stated in the 2023 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) Update, Georgia’s economy has continued to thrive, bringing rapid economic expansion and an unprecedented increase in the demand for energy to the state. This rapid economic expansion requires Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or “GPC”) to develop or procure additional capacity resources so that it can continue to provide reliable electric service to its customers. Therefore, Southern Company Services (“SCS”) and Georgia Power (collectively, the “Company”) evaluated numerous resources that can provide the capacity necessary to meet its customers’ energy needs during this period of extraordinary economic growth in Georgia. In accordance with the Order Adopting Stipulated Agreement issued by the Georgia Public Service Commission (the “Commission”) approving the stipulation in Georgia Power’s 2023 IRP Update Final Order in Docket No. 55378, the Company has identified 500 megawatts (“MW”) of Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”) to meet customer needs in the winter of 2026/2027 and beyond. Consistent with the analysis provided to the PSC in the 2023 IRP Update, Georgia Power’s Application for the Certification of the Robins, Moody, Hammond, and McGrau Ford Phase II BESS (“Application”) includes updated economics that support the Company’s Application. 
Analysis Overview 
In the 2023 IRP Update Economic Analysis, the Company compared the costs and benefits of various resource options to determine the least-cost solutions for meeting its capacity needs. This comprehensive evaluation considered a range of fuel prices and carbon price paths, resulting in five distinct planning scenarios. Utilizing the Aurora model, the Company quantified the production cost savings (commonly referred to as fuel savings). The economic evaluation incorporated the costs of new BESS resources, including in-service capital costs, maintenance capital costs, O&M costs, and other incremental costs to customers. The Inflation Reduction Act (“IRA”) benefit of the Investment Tax Credits (“ITC”) for BESS was also factored into the analysis. Additionally, the renewable integration benefit was assumed as a benefit for the BESS resources. The cost of the necessary transmission system upgrades was included. The analysis preserved the period assumed in the 2023 IRP Update economic analysis, which was sufficient for evaluating resources with varying unit lives and term lengths using the term equalization method. Therefore, these updated economic evaluations are consistent with the information provided in the 2023 IRP Update. For additional information, please review the 2023 IRP Update Economic analysis in Docket No. 55378.

Executive Summary
This Application for the Certification of the Robins, Moody, Hammond, and McGrau Ford Phase II Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”) projects (“Application”) is made pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-3A-4 and Commission Rule 515-3-4-.07(2) and in accordance with the Order Adopting Stipulated Agreement issued by the Georgia Public Service Commission (the “Commission”) approving the associated stipulation (“Stipulation”) in Georgia Power Company’s (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) 2023 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) Update in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update Final Order”). As explained in the Company’s 2023 IRP Update and this Application, 500 megawatts (“MW”) of BESS are necessary to meet the needs of Georgia Power customers in the winter of 2026/2027 and beyond, and approval of this Application is in the public interest. 
Per Items 7 and 8 of the Stipulation, the Company is authorized to develop, own, and operate up to 500 MW of BESS resources at various sites to be identified by the Company, including the Robins and Moody BESS projects, to meet customer needs in the winter of 2026/2027. This Application meets the requirement for the Company to file for expedited certification of the applicable projects once Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (“EPC”) agreements have been executed. This BESS development is exempted from the request for proposal (“RFP”) process under Commission Rules 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6) and (7).
The 2023 IRP Update Final Order emphasizes that the “Stipulation represents a reasonable path to ensuring that Georgia Power can continue to serve its existing and growing customer base with the reliability that customers value and expect, while continuing to support Georgia’s growing and thriving economy.” Through the approval of the 500 MW of company-owned BESS and the rest of the stipulated resource plan, the Company’s forecasted incremental revenue associated with the state’s extraordinary economic growth will have downward pressure on rates of at least $2.89 for the typical residential customer using an average of 1,000 kilowatt-hour (“kWh”) per month in the next base rate case. 
This Application includes the following portfolio of sites identified by the Company to fulfill the 500 MW total of 4-hour duration lithium-ion BESS resources:
128 MW Robins BESS in Bibb County, GA,
49.5 MW Moody BESS in Lowndes County, GA,
57.5 MW Hammond BESS in Floyd County, GA, and
265 MW McGrau Ford Phase II BESS in Cherokee County, GA.
These new BESS projects are important capacity resources as part of the Company’s well-balanced and diversified approach approved in the 2023 IRP Update Final Order. This strategy and approach will allow Georgia Power to continue providing reliable and cost-effective service as it navigates unique reliability resource planning challenges, including increased and accelerated generation capacity needs and increased renewable resource penetration across the system. 
The proposed BESS resource portfolio projects specifically meet the needs identified in the 2023 IRP Update docket in a cost-effective and strategic fashion. Furthermore, each BESS project will produce a variety of benefits. Each project will leverage existing transmission infrastructure. As such, these projects save time and avoid additional capital investment otherwise required to construct interconnection facilities and transmission system upgrades to charge and discharge the BESS. The proposed BESS resources will also provide energy arbitrage benefits by optimizing energy savings by shifting the energy from hours with a relatively low system marginal cost to hours with a relatively high system marginal cost. Furthermore, the resource portfolio helps minimize costs by maximizing economies of scale within the bounds of the transmission available for each site and by pursuing available tax credits through the Inflation Reduction Act (“IRA”) and passing those benefits through to customers.
The Robins and Moody BESS projects will be primarily charged by the existing solar resources at each site. The BESS will thereby firm up the winter planning capacity of the existing solar facilities at both sites by storing energy that can then be dispatched by system operators to benefit the grid. Without BESS, the winter planning capacity for the solar resources at each site is 10% of the nameplate capacity of each facility. With the BESS, each facility can provide 100% of the solar nameplate capacity to meet winter capacity needs, thus maximizing the utilization and benefit of the existing solar resources and existing interconnection capabilities.
The Hammond BESS project is a standalone BESS that allows the Company to leverage existing infrastructure from the retired coal-fired Plant Hammond facility, where transmission capacity has been identified to be available by winter of 2026/2027. By repurposing Company-owned land and increasing tax incentives from the IRA, the Hammond BESS provides significant benefit to customers while aiding the Company in meeting its capacity obligations. 
The McGrau Ford Phase II BESS project is also a standalone BESS that will expand on the McGrau Ford Phase I BESS, that the Commission provisionally approved in the 2022 IRP. The Company is able to leverage the development and site work, land acquisition, and contracting strategy from McGrau Ford Phase I BESS in developing the McGrau Ford Phase II BESS. For example, The Company is able to streamline the contracting and construction process by using the same construction partner and existing land. In addition, the preliminary design for McGrau Ford Phase I BESS facilitates the Company’s ability to cost effectively expand the project level substation and generation tie line as compared to constructing a new project level substation.
The Company entered into EPC Agreements with Burns & McDonnell for the Robins BESS and McGrau Ford Phase II BESS projects and EPC Agreements with Crowder Industrial Construction, LLC. (“Crowder”) for the Moody BESS and Hammond BESS projects. Both firms are well known for their extensive experience within the power industry, demonstrating their capability to contribute significantly to the projects. The Company’s review of their proposals and engagement with these firms has established a solid foundation that has validated and reinforced the effectiveness of their execution strategies. The Company has experienced personnel in place to provide oversight and appropriate execution of the EPC Agreement by these contractors and is highly confident in their ability to successfully execute these projects. 

In addition, the Company executed Battery and Equipment Supply Agreements (“BESA”) for all four projects with Tesla for the Megapack 2 XL. The Tesla Megapack 2 XL is a utility-scale energy storage solution with a modular design that provides for rapid deployment of BESS for high-capacity, large-scale projects. 
By utilizing the Tesla Megapack 2 XL, the projects leverage the expertise of a top market supplier to procure timely and reliable BESS equipment. The Company has focused on developing relationships with a leading supplier who stands behind operational guarantees to ensure successful project deployment for customers' benefit.

Commission Rule Exception to the RFP Requirement in Rule 515-3-4-.04
Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6) and (7) provide exceptions to the Commission rule that requires the Company to procure supply-side resources through an RFP process. Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6) provides that the Commission shall expressly consider in each IRP, and make a determination in each IRP, whether to exclude from the RFP Process any new supply-side resources identified in the soliciting entity’s approved IRP. Further, Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(7) articulates the Commission’s policy that investor-owned utilities under its regulation, such as Georgia Power, shall maintain a minimum percentage of their capacity as self-owned rate-based assets. Pursuant to the 2001 IRP Final Order, the Commission established the minimum ratio of self-owned assets to purchased power at 70/30. The Commission Rule provides that when the utility is nearing or finds that its self-owned capacity would fall below this minimum percentage (here, 70%), the utility shall inform the Commission of this eventuality in advance of the RFP Process and seek guidance from the Commission on how to proceed. The Company provided notice to the Commission that it was close to falling below the minimum ownership percentage as part of its 2022 IRP and 2023 IRP Update filings. 
Accordingly, Items 7 and 8 of the Stipulation exempt these resources from the RFP process under Commission Rules 515-3-4-.04(3)(f)(6) and (7) for the 500 MW Company-owned BESS portfolio, including the Robins and Moody BESS projects. By developing new resources in accordance with these RFP exceptions in the Commission’s Rules in addition to procuring resources through the typical RFP process, Georgia Power can continue providing its customers with reliable service and meet the timelines of customers’ needs. To meet Item 9 of the Stipulation, Georgia Power is also issuing a narrowly tailored RFP on an expedited schedule to meet the capacity needed to serve customers during the winter of 2027/2028. It is critical that the Company leverage procurement options to maintain a diverse resource portfolio that includes market resources, Company-owned resources, and a balance of resource technologies to ensure a reliable and efficient generation fleet.

Special Design Features
The proposed project will utilize the Tesla Megapack 2 XL, which is a battery storage unit capable of charging and discharging real power and injecting and absorbing reactive power. The system is unique compared to other available BESS in the market because it arrives at site fully integrated and capable of outputting 480V alternating current (“AC”) power. This integration greatly reduces commissioning timelines and allows the Megapack 2 XL thermal system to operate prior to backfeed being available at the site. The Megapack 2 XL features a modular design for increased energy density and includes multiple inverters per container, making the loss of one inverter negligible to the system’s overall performance. Each 4-hour container possesses a storage capacity of over 3,900 kWh.

In August 2024, the Company finalized a BESA with Tesla for the procurement of an integrated battery energy storage system, the Megapack 2 XL, for deployment at Robins BESS. Manufactured at Tesla's Megafactory in Lathrop, CA, the basic storage component in the Tesla Megapack 2 XL is the lithium-iron phosphate (“LFP”) battery cell. Special Design Features
The proposed project will utilize the Tesla Megapack 2 XL, which is a battery storage unit capable of charging and discharging real power and injecting and absorbing reactive power. The system is unique compared to other available BESS in the market because it arrives at site fully integrated and capable of outputting 480V AC power. This integration greatly reduces commissioning timelines and allows the Megapack 2 XL thermal system to operate prior to backfeed being available at the site. The Megapack 2 XL is modular design and includes multiple inverters per container, making the loss of one inverter negligible to the system’s overall performance. Each 4-hour container possesses a storage capacity of over 3,900 kWh.

Battery and Equipment Supply Agreement
In August 2024, the Company finalized a BESA with Tesla for the procurement of an integrated battery energy storage system, the Megapack 2 XL, for deployment at Moody BESS. This agreement was executed following a comprehensive Supply Chain RFP process. Manufactured at Tesla's Megafactory in Lathrop, CA, the basic storage component in the Tesla Megapack 2 XL is LFP battery cell. These cells are assembled into battery modules and integrated into the Megapack units. Megapack’s modular design allows for easy scalability, connecting multiple units to meet project requirements. Each unit can store over 3.9 MWh of energy. The system arrives on-site fully integrated with initial testing completed, ready to deliver AC electrical output. 
Safety is crucial in the Megapack design. Each unit undergoes rigorous testing to ensure safe operation. Tesla’s advanced BMS monitors and regulates temperature, voltage, and state-of-charge, ensuring optimal performance. Tesla’s technology is well-proven and reliable with grid-scale batteries operational in over 65 countries. 
The agreement includes a warranty that safeguards against equipment defects and guarantees against energy capacity degradation. In addition to the BESA, the Company will enter into a LTCA, which will provide an enhanced guaranteed energy retention capacity curve, as well as a Site RTE guarantee for each year of operation.
The executed BESA between Georgia Power Company and Tesla is included in the Technical Appendix to this Application.

Site Selection Analysis
For fulfilling the remaining balance of the 500 MW of company-owned BESS approved in the 2023 IRP Update Final Order, the Company strategically selected the McGrau Ford site to leverage the ongoing McGrau Ford Phase I BESS project, that was provisionally approved in the 2022 IRP. Given the existing site work, land acquisition, and contracting for McGrau Ford Phase I BESS, efficiencies in contracting and construction are obtained by using the same construction partner and Company-owned land. In addition, the preliminary design for McGrau Ford Phase I BESS provides an opportunity to cost effectively expand the project level substation and generation tie line as compared to constructing a new project level substation. This approach not only ensures cost savings but also capitalizes on economies of scale to reduce overall EPC costs.
Fuel Use
The McGrau Ford Phase II BESS will be grid charged, so it will pull energy from the grid as directed through AGC signals. Grid operators will utilize the BESS to provide both energy arbitrage and firm capacity benefits to minimize total system production costs while maintaining system reliability.

Estimated Annual Variable Costs and Operational Data
The McGrau Ford Phase II BESS will serve as a cost-effective, dispatchable capacity resource that will optimize energy savings by shifting the energy from hours with relatively low system marginal cost to hours with a relatively high system marginal cost. The variable costs for the McGrau Ford Phase II BESS include the cost to charge the battery and losses during charging and discharging. These costs are optimized by charging during low-cost periods and discharging during high-cost periods, although the exact cost to charge, and benefits of discharge, vary based on market conditions. 
In addition, the BESS will fulfill a wider array of system and grid requirements beyond the more commonly recognized benefits of capacity value and energy value. BESS can be instrumental in providing cost-effective essential services for grid reliability, particularly operating reserves, leading to reductions in total system operational costs. As the energy mix evolves, these services and the role of BESS will likely become increasingly important, with BESS benefits increasing in response to intermittent resource penetration and potential carbon pressure. Estimated Environmental Impact
Other than impacts associated with land use for the new facility described below, Georgia Power does not anticipate that the BESS facility will have any environmental impacts related to the specific emission, production, or usage data categories outlined in GPSC Rule 515-3-4-.07 (2)(a)(3)(xi). 
Georgia Power has conducted a full suite of environmental assessments and surveys for the McGrau Ford Phase II site. During site development and operations, some intermittent environmental impacts may occur as a result of stormwater runoff due to precipitation. However, these impacts will be regulated by, and in compliance with, applicable state and federal requirements. 
During facility operations, no direct air emissions or water usage are expected except as encountered during an emergency or other unplanned event. Some stormwater runoff is expected as a result of direct rainfall, but this will be controlled by measures installed during facility construction and maintained for the life of the facility. Some limited solid waste disposal may occur as a result of normal operations. Battery recycling is expected as the BESS achieve their expected operational lifespan. Ash, scrubber sludge, high- and low-level nuclear waste will not be produced. Spent nuclear fuel will not be created. Approximately 66 acres of land will be used by this BESS project for the facility footprint, interconnection substation and any associated utility tie line(s), which provides adequate space for future augmentation needs.
Required federal, state, and local permitting have been evaluated as provided in the EPC agreement, and applicable requirements will be met at the appropriate times to meet all compliance obligations.

Special Design Features
The proposed project will utilize the Tesla Megapack 2 XL, which is a battery storage unit capable of charging and discharging real power and injecting and absorbing reactive power. The system is unique compared to other available BESS in the market because it arrives at site fully integrated and capable of outputting 480V AC power with a. This integration greatly reduces commissioning timelines and allows the Megapack 2 XL thermal system to operate prior to backfeed being available at the site. The Megapack 2 XL is modular design and includes multiple inverters per container, making the loss of one inverter negligible to the system’s overall performance. Each 4-hour container possesses a storage capacity of over 3,900 kWh.

Conclusion 
In the 2023 IRP Update Final Order, the Commission concluded that “the Stipulation is a well-reasoned and balanced resolution of the Company’s 2023 IRP Update that will provide significant benefits to Georgia Power’s customers, [including] customer protections, the assurance of downward pressure on customer rates, and the continued resiliency and reliability of the Company’s electric system.” Consistent with the 2023 IRP Update Final Order, Georgia Power submits this application to request the expedited certification of 500 MW of Company-owned BESS projects at Robins, Moody, Hammond, and McGrau Ford Phase II sites. 
These projects are necessary and cost-effective capacity resources to continue to reliably serve the state’s economic growth, beginning in the winter of 2026/2027. The generation capacity provided by these 4-hour, dispatchable resources is a critical benefit of these projects, while the Company is also able to leverage existing infrastructure, including military base solar installations, a retired coal-fired generating site, and development adjacent to current substations. These projects save time and avoid additional capital investment by pursuing opportunities that have transmission capacity available as early as the winter of 2026/2027 to provide the best value to Georgia Power customers. Other benefits of these projects include energy arbitrage capabilities to optimize energy savings by shifting the energy output, including solar energy, from periods of relatively low system marginal cost to hours with relatively high system marginal cost. Additionally, the BESS will fulfill a wider array of system and grid requirements beyond capacity and energy value. It will provide cost-effective essential services for grid reliability, such as operating reserves, leading to reductions in total system operational costs. As the energy mix evolves, these services and the role of BESS will likely become increasingly important, especially with the rise of intermittent resources and potential carbon pressure. The Company will also pursue available tax credits through the IRA for these projects and pass those benefits through to customers.
The request in this Application complies with applicable Commission rules and is in the public interest; therefore, the Company requests that the Commission (1) certify the Robins, Moody, Hammond, and McGrau Ford Phase II projects; and (2) approve the total deemed certified amounts of the projects as shown in Figure 16 below.
