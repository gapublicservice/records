STF-PIA-15-1

Question:
Please refer to the Application for Certification, pg. 4, stating that the Robins and Moody BESS will “be constructed with the capability to grid-charge if constraints are not present on the transmission system.” 
Please describe the constraints that would prevent the BESS from charging from the grid. 
In the economic analysis of the Robins and Moody projects, including the Aurora modeling, did the Company assume the projects are capable of charging from the grid? Please provide a narrative description of the modeling treatment of this constraint. 


Response:
Please see the Company’s response to STF-DEA-9-5. Until firm transmission delivery service has been studied for the Robins and Moody BESS, grid-charging constraints related to congestion or stability issues on the bulk power system are unknown. The Company will investigate firm transmission deliverability service for charging in the future to expand the operational flexibility of the resources.
The Robins and Moody BESS projects will be primarily charged by the existing solar resources at each site. In the Aurora simulations, the Company limited charging to the adjacent solar facility. During real-time operations, the Company will grid-charge when transmission is available and it makes economic sense.  Given that firm transmission delivery for charging is currently unknown, this modeling approach is a reasonable approximation of energy benefits.

Total_Hours_Run: The total number of hours in the Time_Period that the resource operated; both charging and generating.

The units are expected to operate at different levels of output based on the needs of the system in weather-normal conditions in Aurora (inclusive of point of interconnection [“POI”] limitations like at Robins and Moody).  From Attachment B, consider that Total_Hours_Run column factors both hours where the BESS generates as well as hours where it charges. STF-PIA-15-12

Question:
Please provide a detailed explanation regarding how contingency was calculated for each of the proposed BESS projects.


Response:
As outlined in the Company’s Application for Certification of Robins, Moody, Hammond, and McGrau Ford Phase II Battery Energy Storage Systems; Docket No. 55378, the Company has secured multiple fixed price contracts to derisk the battery energy storage projects. The contingency for the Battery Energy Storage System (“BESS”) projects was determined by considering risk factors related to the development and deployment of BESS projects. These factors include market fluctuations, project complexity, supplier lead times, contractual terms, and the clarity and stability of the project scope. The rapid development, the complexity of managing multiple interdependent agreements and the critical need for a 2026 commercial operation date (“COD”) were also considered. Contingency was applied across all projects in consideration of these risks. Any unused contingency funds will not be included in the final cost of the project, and the Company expects to report out on contingency as part of the Construction Monitoring requirements.

STF-PIA-15-2

Question:

Please refer to the Application for Certification, Figures 4-15; please provide a narrative description of each column/row in each figure.

Response:

The values included in Figures 4-15 were provided in accordance with Commission Rule 515-3-4-.07(3). Each figure offers a detailed breakdown of the costs associated with the respective Battery Energy Storage System (“BESS”) projects, including annual depreciation, financing costs, income taxes, fixed Operations and Maintenance (“O&M”), insurance, property taxes, and capital additions. The expenditure plans outline the costs over multiple years, while the Allowance for Funds Used During Construction (“AFUDC”), ad valorem, and sales tax estimates provide a detailed look at these specific costs.
Figure 4, 7, 10, and 13: 
Estimated Annual Depreciation: This represents the annual book depreciation expense for each BESS resource.
Estimated Annual Debt and Equity Financing Costs on Capital Investment: The annual costs associated with financing the capital investment through debt and equity.
Estimated Annual Income Taxes on Capital Investment: The annual income tax expense related to the capital investment inclusive of applicable tax credits.
Estimated Fixed O&M: Fixed O&M costs are the expenses required to keep the BESS operational over its life. 
Estimated Insurance: This is the estimated annual cost of insuring each BESS resource against certain risks. 
Estimated Property Taxes: This represents the annual property tax expenses forecasted for each BESS resource.
Estimated Annual Capital Additions: These are the capital additions expected for the assumed augmentation cycles. 
Figure 5, 8, 11, and 14: 
These figures show the different categories of expenditures for 2024-2026. Below each figure is a section with the heading “Descriptions of each category are provided below,” which offers a narrative description of each row. This section explains the nature of each expenditure category, such as planning, licensing, EPC contract costs, and more.

Figure 6, 9, 12, and 15: 
A narrative description of these figures is provided in the preceding paragraph for each figure. These figures detail the AFUDC, ad valorem, and sales tax estimates. AFUDC represents the cost of financing the construction of the BESS before it is operational. Ad valorem taxes are property taxes, and sales tax estimates are provided for applicable items.
