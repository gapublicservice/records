II.2023 IRP UPDATE
Q.HOW AND WHEN WAS THE NEED FOR THE COMBUSTION TURBINES DETERMINED?
A.On October 27, 2023, the Company filed its 2023 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) Update (“2023 Update”) on the basis that the Company’s latest forecast of new load growth was expected to increase dramatically beyond what had been previously forecast in the 2022 IRP, and that the need for capacity would be realized much sooner than had been expected.  Much of this new load growth is expected to be the result of relatively few high load customers locating in the State of Georgia.  Among various proposed capacity resources and demand side initiatives, the Company requested approval to self-build three dual-fuel CTs at Plant Yates.   

Q.HOW WERE THE ISSUES PRESENTED IN THE 2023 IRP UPDATE RESOLVED BETWEEN STAFF AND THE COMPANY?
A.On March 26, 2024, Staff and the Company, and several other Intervenors, entered a stipulation that was intended to resolve all issues related to the 2023 IRP Update, including approval of the Plant Yates Units 8, 9, and 10 as part of the Company's 2023 IRP Update Plan.  Paragraph 12 of that Stipulation relates to the CTs being requested for Certification in this proceeding by the Company.  Paragraph 12 is repeated below:
The Plant Yates combustion turbines 8-10 ("Yates 8-10") are approved as part of the Company's 2023 IRP Update Plan. Yates 8-10 are exempted from the RFP process under Commission Rules 515-3-4.-04(3)(f)(6) and (7). Accordingly, the Commission shall proceed with a hearing on the Company's Application for Certification of Yates 8-10 filed in this docket on January 31, 2024. Limited to this case, the Company agrees that if the Application is granted, Georgia Power will not seek recovery of any project construction costs that exceed the proposed project construction cost in the Company's January 31, 2024 Application for Certification absent a showing that such costs are the result of circumstances beyond the Company's reasonable control, which are: acts of God that damage necessary facilities; sudden actions of the elements such as floods, earthquakes, pandemic or epidemic, named tropical storms, hurricanes or tornados; terrorism or war; sabotage; riots; labor strikes or work stoppages; change in law; actions related to the Yates 8-10 project from a court or governmental authority; or other similar events beyond the reasonable control of Georgia Power. In the event the Company seeks recovery of additional project construction costs because of any such circumstances, Georgia Power agrees that it shall have the burden to prove that project construction costs above the amount proposed in the Application for Certification were the result of circumstances beyond the Company's reasonable control and were reasonable and prudent. In addition, if the Application is granted, the Company will file semi-annual construction monitoring reports from the date construction begins through the date of commercial operation. Specific reporting requirements will be determined in the certification proceeding.

Q.DOES PARAGRAPH 12 OF THE STIPULATION PROVIDE PROTECTIONS FOR RATEPAYERS?
A.Yes.  First, it states that “…the Company agrees that if the Application is granted, Georgia Power will not seek recovery of any project construction costs that exceed the proposed project construction cost in the Company's January 31, 2024 Application for Certification…” except for events which generally fall under a force majeure clause.   This language caps the cost of the CTs to the estimate provided by the Company in its Application and shifts all risks that are not attributable to force majeure to the Company.  Second, should the Company deem it necessary to request costs above the cap, the burden of showing that those costs are attributable to force majeure and are reasonable and prudent fall on the Company.

III.STAFF CONSTRUCTION MONITORING PROGRAM

Q.WHAT ARE THE PRIMARY OBJECTIVES FOR A STAFF MONITORING PROGRAM ON LARGE CONSTRUCTION PROJECTS?
A.There are three primary objectives for Staff’s monitoring program.  These are presented in no order of importance:
To fulfill Staff’s obligation to promote the public interest by ensuring that ratepayers only pay costs that are reasonable and prudent and by ensuring that electric service is safe, reliable, and reasonably priced.
To monitor construction of the Project to determine whether: the Company is complying with applicable Commission laws, rules, and orders; the Company is utilizing best practices in its construction, oversight, and management; the Project remains on schedule and within the approved cost, and, to the extent that it does not, determine the root causes and if applicable, contributing causes.
To keep the Commission independently informed of the progress of the Project until Commercial Operation; and if necessary, alert the Commission of any material deviation from expectations at any given point in time during the construction, testing, and transfer of ownership of the assets from the Consortium to the Company. 

Q.DOES STAFF AGREE WITH THE COMPANY’S PROPOSED REPORTING REQUIREMENTS AND WHAT DOES STAFF RECOMMEND FOR THE REGULATORY REPORTING OF THE PROGRESS OF THE PROJECT?
A.Staff agrees with the Company’s proposal to file a Report on a semi-annual basis.  [Testimony at 9, line 6].  With respect to the contents of this Report, Staff would like to reserve the right to amend information required in the report based on performance of the Project Management.  Staff also would like to file its own independent Report addressing the Company’s Report, providing Staff’s opinions as to the status of the Project and any risks, variances, or challenges that concern Staff.  Staff recommends that the Company’s semi-annual Report be filed 45 days after the end of the semi-annual period. Staff would then file its Report 75 days hence from the filing of the Company’s Report.   

Q.DOES STAFF BELIEVE MONITORING HEARINGS ARE NECESSARY FOR THIS PROJECT?
A.Not currently.  Staff, however, reserves the right to request a hearing should it be necessary to explore more thoroughly the status of the Project or if Staff believes that the Company has reported costs not actually spent on the project.  The need for a hearing will be dependent upon Georgia Power management’s execution of the Project Plan. 

Q.ARE THERE OTHER REASONS WHY STAFF BELIEVES THAT SEMI-ANNUAL HEARINGS ARE NOT NECESSARY UNLESS IT APPEARS THAT THE PROJECT COULD MATERIALLY DEVIATE FROM THE PROJECT PLAN?
A.Yes.  Fundamentally the risk profile for this Project should be relatively low, and therefore there should be a reasonably high expectation of meeting the budget and the schedule.  CT technology has been built and operated for many decades.  Staff has been able to ascertain that several Units of this exact advanced H technology have been deployed in the United States and are in operation.  The Consortium and Georgia Power should have obtained lessons learned from those prior builds.  Those lessons learned should be incorporated in the initial Yates Project Plan.  Additionally, because of the sequence of constructing Units 8, 9, and 10, the Project lessons learned program should be able to immediately incorporate any lessons learned into the Plan for subsequent Units, thereby reducing risk for those subsequent Units.  At this time, given the level of risk, the Project should not warrant semi-annual hearings.  However, as stated above, should a risk emerge and not be successfully mitigated to an acceptable level, or a lack of budget and schedule adherence exists, that may trigger a request from Staff to hold a hearing.

Q.SHOULD STAFF EXPECT THE PROJECT LEADERSHIP TO DEVELOP AND IMPLEMENT AN INTEGRATED EARNED VALUE MANAGEMENT SYSTEM?
A.Yes.  An Integrated Earned Value Management System (“EVMS”) is industry standard and essential for Project success.  Given this technology has been built, and is in operation, there should be no reason for not developing a robust EVMS for this Project.  All major stakeholders and major subcontractors should coordinate to develop a “integrated” EVMS.  The key elements are a resource loaded fully Integrated Project Schedule, critical path method analyses using both forward and backward passes, integration of engineering and engineering support, integration of quality assurance and quality control, inclusion of all major sub-contractor work, and the allocation of major overheads. 

Q.DO THE FACTORS DESCRIBED ABOVE HAVE THE OVERALL EFFECT OF REDUCING RISK FOR THE PROJECT?
A.Yes, the overall risk to the Project should decrease.
