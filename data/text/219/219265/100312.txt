STF-PIA-24-12

Question: 

Is the current inventory level of distribution transformers sufficient for normal reliable operations?  

Response:

Yes. The Company sets its inventory levels based on factors including known new business projects, capital maintenance activities, forecasts for future new business demands, and material lead times.  The rise in lead times noted in the Company’s responses to STF-PIA-24-9 and STF-PIA-24-10 indicate that current normal operations are different than in 2019. While the Company attempts to secure transformers in sufficient quantities to avoid project delays, there have been instances where new customers may have experienced longer than desired construction schedules while waiting for transformer orders to be fulfilled.

===== DKT 44280 STF-PIA-24 PD/STF-PIA-24-19.docx =====
STF-PIA-24-19

Question: 

Has the Company experienced transformer supply chain delays recently in procuring distribution transformers?  

If so, did this impact what type or size of transformers that were ordered or procured?  
What is the present wait time for each type of transformer?  
Does transformer size and type affect the expected order fulfillment timeframe? If so, please describe.  

Response:

Yes, the Company experienced transformer supply chain delays recently. Accordingly, Southern Company increased the overall number of transformers on order to account for longer lead-time and increased demand for all types and sizes. 

Pole Mount: 20 to 30 weeks
Pad Mount: 30 to 70 weeks

Yes, the lead-time for all types of pad-mounted type transformers continues to be significantly above pre-2020 levels. In 2021, lead-times for pad-mounted units increased to over 100 weeks. The Company has worked to approve new manufacturers and establish new agreements over the past three years. As a result, lead-time for single phase pad-mounted units has been reduced to an average of 30 to 40 weeks and lead-time for three-phase pad-mounted units has been reduced to an average of 60 to 70 weeks. Based on current trends, market demand for pad-mounted type transformers continues to be above historic levels. The Company has begun procuring these type units from additional suppliers to combat the long lead-times but expects the long lead-times for pad-mounted type transformers to continue over the next one to two years until additional production capacity becomes available domestically.

STF-PIA-24-21

Question: 

Has the increased EV charging load changed the need to replace distribution transformers? If so, please explain.  
 
Response:

The Company does not track discrete end uses like EV charging load as drivers for individual distribution transformer replacements. Transformer replacements due to load growth are driven by a variety of end uses. While there are times when a distribution transformer replacement is needed for load growth, the Company has not identified prevailing trends that indicate transformer replacement is a given for every load addition.

STF-PIA-24-6

Question: 

What is the Company’s plan to comply with the U.S. Department of Energy’s Energy Conservation Standards for Distribution Transformers, effective July 8, 2024? 

Response:

While the Energy Conservation Standards for Distribution Transformers (“Standards”) take effect on July 8, 2024, the Standards only apply to transformers manufactured on or after April 23, 2029. Transformer manufacturers are currently reviewing the Standards and efficiency requirements to determine the impact to their transformer designs. After their review, the manufacturers will engage the Company to notify of any required design changes due to the Standards.
