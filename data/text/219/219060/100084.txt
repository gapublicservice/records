            APPLICATION OF GEORGIA INTERFAITH POWER & LIGHT
                        FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE

       Georgia Interfaith Power & Light (“GIPL”), through counsel, hereby applies to the Georgia

Public Service Commission (“Commission”) for leave to intervene in the above-referenced docket

pursuant to O.C.G.A. §§ 46-2-59 and 50-13-14 and Commission Rule 515-2-1-.06. In support of

this application, GIPL respectfully shows the Commission as follows:

                                               1.

       On January 31, 2024, Georgia Power Company filed its Application for Certifications of

three self-build combustion turbines at Plant Yates, for which it seeks Commission approval.

                                               2.

       This application for leave to intervene is timely filed pursuant to the Commission’s

Procedural and Scheduling Order.

                                               3.

       Georgia Interfaith Power & Light is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization headquartered in

Decatur, Georgia. GIPL represents the interests of communities of faith in promoting

environmental justice, energy conservation, energy efficiency, renewable energy, and related

sustainable practices. GIPL engages faith communities in the stewardship of Creation. GIPL is a

growing organization with more than 350 member congregations across the state of Georgia

representing a wide variety of faiths and over 100 congregationally based Green Teams working

to advance GIPL’s objectives. While several of GIPL’s participating congregations are located

throughout the Atlanta metropolitan area, many are located in other areas of the State, including

the Savannah, Macon, Augusta, Acworth, Athens, Gainesville, Sautee-Nacoochee, Dalton,

Clayton, Carrollton, Newnan, Roswell, Ellijay, Kennesaw, Covington, Warner Robbins, and

Canton areas.

                                                 4.

       GIPL has an interest in the Application for Certification of Plant Yates Units 8-10 both on

its own behalf and on behalf of its members. Many of GIPL’s participating congregations and

members of those congregations purchase electricity from Georgia Power Company, both for their

places of worship and for their individual residences. Their costs for electricity will be affected by

the Commission’s actions in this docket.

                                                  5.

       Additionally, GIPL’s core mission is to promote the responsible use of God’s Creation by

advocating for and educating its communities concerning the production of electricity sustainably,

using less electricity through energy efficiency measures, and minimizing the impact of resource

decisions on vulnerable communities. GIPL’s mission is thus directly implicated by Georgia

Power’s self-building of new fossil-fueled resources without testing them against the market.

GIPL’s mission is further implicated by the extent to which the Commission and Georgia Power’s

process for choosing electric resources is transparent and accessible, to provide for member

education and engagement in these important policy choices. GIPL intends to address the concerns

of both itself and its members regarding how Georgia Power’s proposal—and Georgia Power’s

and the Commission’s processes—impact both God’s Creation and the resulting bills that

customers, and in particular low-income customers, pay. As a result, GIPL has substantial and

vital interests in the outcome of these proceedings and seeks to intervene to protect these interests.

No other party in this case shares GIPL’s unique interests.

                                                  6.

In furtherance of its interests and on behalf of its members, GIPL has intervened in many prior

proceedings before the Commission, including: Georgia Power’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan

Update, Docket No. 55378; Georgia Power’s 2022 Rate Case, Docket No. 44280; Georgia Power’s

2022 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) and Demand-Side Management Plan (“DSM”)

proceedings, Docket Nos. 44160, 44161; Georgia Power’s 2020 PURPA Avoided Cost

proceedings, Docket Nos. 4822, 16573, 19279; Georgia Power’s 2019 Base Rate Case, Docket

No. 42516; Georgia Power’s 2019 IRP and DSM proceedings, Docket Nos. 42310, 42311; the

2017-2018 DSM Working Group and the 2014-16 DSM Working Group, Docket Nos. 41253,

38401; Georgia Power’s 2016 IRP and DSM proceedings, Docket Nos. 40161, 40162; Georgia

Power’s Application for Certification of a Green Energy Program and Green Energy Pricing Tariff,

Docket No. 16573; Georgia Power’s 2015 and 2016 Advanced Solar Initiative Prime, Docket No.

38877; the Notice of Inquiry and Workshop to Examine Issues Related to the Value of Renewable

Resources and Distributed Energy Resources, Docket No. 39732; Georgia Power’s 2010 IRP and

DSM proceedings, Docket Nos. 31081, 31082; and Georgia Power’s Seventeenth, Eighteenth,

Nineteenth, Twentieth/Twenty-First, Twenty-Second, Twenty-Third, Twenty-Fourth, Twenty-

Fifth, Twenty-Sixth, Twenty-Seventh, Twenty-Eighth, and Twenty-Ninth Semi-Annual

Construction Monitoring proceedings, Docket No. 29849.

                                                  7.

       The interests of GIPL and its participating congregations are not adequately represented by

other parties to these proceedings. Other parties that have applied to intervene do not have interests

similar to those of GIPL. The applicant’s intervention would not unduly delay the proceedings or

prejudice the rights of other parties.

                                                  8.

       If this application is granted, GIPL, through its counsel, intends to participate fully as a

party in this docket, including cross-examining witnesses, and filing proposed orders and/or briefs.
