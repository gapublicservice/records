GEORGIA POWER COMPANY’S RESPONSE TO AMERICANS FOR AFFORDABLE CLEAN ENERGY’S COMMENTS ON GEORGIA POWER COMPANY’S COMMUNITY CHARGING PLAN

I. INTRODUCTION

Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits this response to the Comments on Georgia Power Company’s Community Charging Plan (“Plan”). On May 8, 2023, the Company filed its first Plan, identifying eleven (11) locations for the placement of electric vehicle (“EV”) charging stations as part of Georgia Power’s Community Charging Program (“Program”).  The Electric Transportation Term Sheet (“Term Sheet”), attached to the Stipulation in Georgia Power’s 2022 Rate Case and approved by the Georgia Public Service Commission on December 30, 2022, allowed interested parties to comment on the Plan within thirty (30) days of filing.  On June 7, 2023, the Americans for Affordable Clean Energy (“AACE”) filed Comments on the Company’s Plan.

II. DISCUSSION

The Term Sheet set forth the general parameters of the Program’s operation.  AACE’s Comments identify certain outstanding issues concerning Program implementation to which Georgia Power responds below:  
1.Replacement locations. While the Term Sheet does not set forth a process for the Company to replace proposed charging locations that are claimed by the market through the contemplated right of first refusal (“ROFR”) process, the term sheet provides a $3 million annual budget for the Program.  In 2023, this budget will provide funding for eleven (11) new locations.  Georgia Power reserves the right to provide replacement locations for any sites claimed by the market through a ROFR under the Program in order to utilize the complete budget.  Any proposed replacement locations will also be subject to all the other requirements of the Term Sheet including a new 60-day ROFR process.  Due to the timing of this year’s filing, Georgia Power may choose to not recommend additional locations in 2023, but the Company may name replacement Community Charging Program locations in 2024 and 2025 based on conditions at that time.  
2.Eligibility. AACE points out the that proposed Blackshear location in Pierce County (Charger 11 in the Plan) is in a Tier 3 county according to the Georgia Department of Community Affairs (“DCA”) 2022 Job Tax Credit Tier map (“2022 DCA Map”).  This is true.  However, Pierce County is Tier 2 county according to the DCA 2023 Job Tax Credit Tier map (“2023 DCA Map”).  For the purposes of Georgia Power’s 2023 Plan this issue is moot because of a ROFR filed by Friendly Express, Inc. on May 8, 2023, but the Company recommends that the Commission use the most current DCA map when determining a proposed location’s eligibility for the Program in each year’s Plan.
3.The 15-mile restriction. The Company accepts AACE’s recommendation to measure the Program’s fifteen (15) mile limitation on charger locations based on a fifteen (15) mile radius around a proposed location.  On April 19, 2023, Geo. H. Green Oil, Inc. (“Green Oil”) filed a ROFR for Georgia Power’s proposed location at the intersection of U.S. 19 and U.S. 41 in Griffin, Georgia.  Green Oil’s ROFR identifies a location at 1552 Highway 85 South, Fayetteville, Georgia for the installation of an EV charging location.  This ROFR is improper as the location identified by Green Oil is beyond the fifteen (15) mile radius of the EV charging location proposed by the Company in Griffin, Georgia.  However, on July 6, 2023, Silver Comet Energy filed a proper ROFR for this same location, so there is no longer a need for the Commission to address Green Oil’s ROFR.


III. CONCLUSION

The Company files these responses to AACE’s comments for Staff’s consideration as the Program is implemented.
