RE:Georgia Power Company’s Update of Base Tariffs; Docket No. 44280

Dear Ms. Tanner:

In accordance with O.C.G.A. § 46-2-25 and the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (“Commission”) Order Adopting Settlement Agreement in Docket No. 44280 (“2022 Rate Case Order”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) hereby files an original and one copy of the updated traditional base tariffs, the Environmental Compliance Cost Recovery (“ECCR”) tariff, the Demand Side Management (“DSM”) tariffs (residential and commercial) and the Municipal Franchise Fee (“MFF”) tariff, along with the temporary rate adjustment riders. The revised tariffs become effective on January 1, 2024.  


The primary updates to the tariffs are as follows:  

Traditional Base Rates – The traditional base rate tariffs will collect an additional $275 million on an annual basis as a result of costs to be incurred by the Company in 2024 associated with additional capital investments and operating expenses previously approved by the Commission. In addition, the Company is recovering incremental revenue erosion of $341 thousand associated with the Time of Use – Food and Drink tariff (“TOU-FD”) conversions from all TOU-FD rate customers as approved by the Commission.

Environmental Compliance—The ECCR tariff will collect $99 million less on an annual basis as a result of ECCR costs to be incurred by the Company in 2024, which is comprised of a $64 million reduction in CCR ARO compliance costs along with a $35 million reduction in traditional ECCR costs excluding CCR ARO compliance costs. The decrease in the CCR ARO compliance costs reflects the actual CCR ARO regulatory asset balance through July 2023, as well as updated CCR ARO estimates for August 2023 through December 2024, as authorized by the Commission in the 2022 Rate Case Order and presented in the latest semi-annual CCR ARO filing in October 2023. In addition, the recovery of CCR ARO compliance costs for 2024 includes the application of the 2021 and 2022 Annual Surveillance Report sharing amounts credited to the CCR ARO regulatory asset. The decrease in traditional ECCR costs excluding CCR ARO compliance costs reflects the costs associated with environmental capital investments and operating expenses previously approved by the Commission.

DSM—The DSM tariffs will collect an additional $11 million on an annual basis as a result of DSM costs to be incurred by the Company in 2024. This increase reflects the incremental costs and Additional Sum associated with the Company’s DSM programs, which were previously approved by the Commission. The increase is partially offset by the true-up of 2022 actual results which resulted in an over-recovery and includes the $1.5 million reduction to 2022 Additional Sum as agreed upon with the Commission’s Public Interest Advocacy Staff.

MFF—The MFF tariff will collect an additional $5 million on an annual basis primarily as a result of the net increases noted above.


TOU-EVC Tariff—A November 2023 version of this tariff is being provided in addition to the tariff that will go into effect in January 2024.  This tariff was requested by stakeholders to be available to customers before January 2024.

PPP Tariff—The Price Protection Products (“PPP-2”) schedule is updated to address customer eligibility requirements.


The total increase reflected in this filing is approximately $191 million, which is approximately $185 million less than the 2024 increase contemplated in the 2022 Rate Case Order. 

Each of the increases/decreases outlined above have been calculated in accordance with the principles established in the 2022 Rate Case Order. Specifically, the Company has (1) utilized the most current CCR ARO compliance cost estimates for purposes of calculating the ECCR tariff; (2) utilized the projections for DSM costs approved by the Commission in Docket No. 44161 and a true-up of 2022 actual results; and (3) adjusted the MFF rate to reflect the updated actual percentage of MFF revenues within municipalities. The 2024 tariff designs for these increases/decreases utilize the most current kWh sales forecast.

The temporary rate adjustment riders will be applied beginning January 1, 2024 to facilitate the proration of the increase on that date.  

If you have any questions, please call me at 404-506-3044.

Sincerely,

/s/ Kelley Balkcom

Kelley Balkcom
Director, Regulatory Affairs

AVAILABILITY:
Throughout the Company's service area from existing lines of adequate capacity.  
APPLICABILITY:
Applicable to all electric service for Commercial customers, at one standard voltage required on the customer’s premises, delivered at one point and metered at or compensated to that voltage.  
Customers must have an annual actual peak demand of not less than 30 kW and no more than 250 kW and either (1) are defined under the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) as 722 Food Services and Drinking Places, or (2) were served on the Time of Use – Multiple Business (TOU-MB-7) tariff as of December 31, 2019 and have been continuously served on TOU-FD since January 1, 2020.  Qualifying accounts will be accepted on TOU-FD on a first come, first allowed basis until the number of accounts equals 6,000 or until December 31, 2025, whichever comes first.
This tariff cannot be used as the base rate in the Customer Base Line (CBL) portion of the Real Time Pricing (RTP) tariffs.

Single or three phase, 60 hertz, at a standard voltage.

DETERMINATION OF REACTIVE DEMAND:
Where there is a power factor of less than Ninety-Five percent (95%) lagging, the Company may, at its option, install metering equipment to measure Reactive Demand.  The Reactive Demand shall be the highest 30-minute kVAR measured during the month.  The Excess Reactive Demand shall be kVAR that is in excess of one-third of the measured actual kW in the current month.  The Company will bill excess kVAR at the rate of $0.36 per excess kVAR.
