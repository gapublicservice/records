       POST-HEARING BRIEF OF GEORGIA SOLAR ENERGY ASSOCIATION (‘GA SOLAR”)
GA Solar respectfully submits its Post-Hearing Brief to the Georgia Public Service Commission

(“Commission”) and pursuant to the Commission’s 2022 Procedural and Scheduling Order in

that above referenced docket.


                                          Introduction


The decision to expand the monthly netting program is a policy decision and at the discretion of

the Commissioners. Significant rate increases of up to $350 per year for residential customers

are expected over the next three years. A modest increase in monthly netting participation to

3% of the Company’s total customer base, or 80,000 customers, would have widespread

benefits, including creation of 2,400+ jobs, boost to Georgia’s GSP, and meaningful annual bill

savings. An expanded monthly netting program will enhance customer choice, offer a variety of

system-wide benefits, and help mature the local rooftop solar market with further reduced PV

prices. All customers would benefit, with increasing distributed generation participation

providing a range of direct and indirect benefits to customers.


Expanding net metering would promote equity across Georgia as well, and with intentional

programming, even more of the benefits of an expanded monthly netting program could flow

into these communities and provide a much-needed financial boost. A dedicated working group

could coordinate financial support from sources such as IRA programs, ensure streamlined

program administration, provide for workforce training, and help to educate and enroll

customers, all in a place-based approach. This working group could also develop a rooftop PV

consumer protections framework.


                                          Recommendations


To realize the benefits of an expanded monthly netting program, including significant job

creation, meaningful economic development impacts, and much needed saving to ratepayers as

outlined above, GA Solar makes the following recommendations:


   1) Customer and capacity limits on the RNR tariff’s monthly netting provisions be lifted

       until the next rate case or until 80,000 customers are subscribed, whichever comes first.

   2) Customers that installed solar after the 5,000-customer cap was reached be allowed to

       opt into the expanded program.

   3) Reject the Company’s request to force rooftop solar PV adopters onto a 3-part rate tariff

       be rejected, and that customers adopting rooftop solar continue to be permitted to

       choose their own service tariff.

     4) A rooftop solar working group be formed to include the Company, PIA Staff, rooftop

           solar installers, community-based organizations, city officials, and others with the

           capacity to:

                a. contribute to defining and executing financial support, partnership, and place-

                      based programming highlighted in this brief and further detailed in Kenneth

                      Sercy’s testimony, and

                b. that the same or similar working group be tasked with collaboratively developing

                      reasonable rooftop PV consumer protections.

The decision on whether to expand the monthly netting program has repeatedly been

characterized in this docket as a policy decision.

states to Georgia means it is a policy decision for the Commission, Company counsel, Hewitson,

said “[t]hat is certainly something that you can read out of this, Commissioner McDonald,”1

is, it sounds like you are just saying the Commissioners have to weigh policy considerations and

                                   The Monthly Netting Pilot Program is a Success


The existing GPC monthly netting program available to up to 5,000 customers was fully

subscribed in approximately 18 months. The program surprised many in how quickly it sold out

and is widely considered a resounding success.4


The pilot program has driven installation of approximately 36.79 MW of customer-sited solar

PV. More rooftop solar was installed in GPC territory since the monthly netting pilot began

than in multiple decades before the pilot. Based on the data reported to the US Energy

Information Administration (“EIA”), GPC had 5.4 MW of residential solar PV through 2019,

which had grown to 21.5 MW of residential solar PV by 2021. This rooftop solar market activity

has provided electric bill savings to thousands of GPC customers, helped drive local economic

growth, and diversified the state’s power grid.

The success of the pilot program implies high ratepayer demand more monthly netting capacity

to help control increasing utility rates and save ratepayers money on their electricity bills. In

monthly netting program.5 However, even with the market growth under the monthly netting

pilot, Georgia has among the 19 lowest shares of rooftop solar in the country, including

substantially trailing other 20 Southeastern states like Florida, Louisiana, South Carolina,

Virginia, North Carolina, and 21 Arkansas.


             Expanded Monthly Netting Will Create Jobs and Improve Georgia’s Economy


Expanded Monthly Netting Will Create Jobs


The solar energy industry currently employs 5,341 Georgians. Georgia Solar Energy

Association’s testimony, authored with support from Kenneth Sercy, indicates that an

expansion of the pilot program at 3% of the Company’s total consumer base, or 80,000

customers, will add another 1,477 jobs directly to the State’s solar industry, over a 25%

increase, and 945 indirect jobs for a total of 2,422 new full-time equivalent jobs in Georgia.

Expanded Monthly Netting Will Improve Georgia’s Economy


Using the same assumptions contained in Table 2 above, the economic impact resulting from an

expanded monthly netting program is two-fold: job income and gross state product.


   A. Income


Direct income is the salary compensation received by the workers in the jobs created. As

indicated in Table 2, direct income resulting from an increase in rooftop solar PV installations

amounts to $286 million. Direct income is attributable to technicians that install the PV systems

onto customer rooftops as well as the management, administrative, and other jobs within the

solar businesses that deliver rooftop solar to customers. The further ripple effect of indirect and

induced incomes results in a income impact of $445 million annually to Georgia residents.


   B. Gross State Product


Gross state product, or GSP, is the value-add of the goods produced by the economic activity

that results from an expanded monthly netting program. For 80,000 participants or 3% of the

Company’s total customer base, an increase in GSP of $93 million is expected for the direct

activity that results from an expanded program and $322 million to GSP when taking into

consideration the indirect and induced activity.


These are important jobs and economic development considerations that have informed past

Georgia Public Service Commission actions – for example, earlier this year the Commission

approved a motion on a biomass RFP in recognition of “…the benefits of biomass as a

renewable resource, and in order to support Georgia’s forest industry and rural job and

economic development growth…”6


              Expanded Monthly Netting Will Save Consumers Money on Electricity Bills


Rates are Projected to Increase through 2025


In this proceeding, the Company is requesting a $1 billion increase, a 12% increase to retail

rates that would “result in an increase of approximately $16.29 to the monthly bill of a typical

residential customer using an average of 1,000 kWh per month.”7


Between the Company’s rate case request in this docket and the potential approval of fuel

balance expenses and partial costs of Plant Vogtle, residential customers may see an increase in

base rates of more than $350 per year by 2025.


Expanded monthly Netting Can Help Consumers Control Increasing Rates


With rates projected to increase, in addition to external inflationary pressure Georgian’s face at

the grocery store and the gas pump, now is the time to give ratepayers more choices to control

                                     Consumer Protections

A growing solar industry can attract a number of out-of-state marketers looking to “get rich

quick” and potentially mislead consumers into making an uneducated purchasing decision.

These out-of-state marketing companies are known to saturate the Georgia market with

advertisements with misleading claims that can induce an unsuspecting customer to over pay

for a system that underperforms what is claimed. In addition, poor installation practices can

mean serious trouble when interconnecting to the grid. A properly installed solar PV array will

last over 25 years, but if the installer takes shortcuts or uses unqualified workers the system

can be a fire hazard, cause roof leaks, or otherwise fail prematurely.


No entity in Georgia, public or private, has done more to address rooftop solar consumer

protection issues than GA Solar. At gasolar.org, consumer protection information can be found

that warns consumers of these bad actors and sales practices and offers guidelines on how to

avoid becoming a victim. In the 2022 legislative session, GA Solar supported consumer

protection bills that called for proper disclosures of material terms, informative consumer

protection videos, and other measures designed to protect consumers. But more can be done if

the monthly netting program is to be expanded.


While a rate case proceeding is not the proper forum to address rooftop solar consumer

protection issues, GA Solar supports the Company’s recommendation to develop a framework

to recommend bad actors to the Consumer Protections division of the Attorney General’s

office. But reporting bad actors after the damage is done is not enough to protect consumers.

In addition to developing this framework, a working group should be formed among the PSC

Staff, the Company, and rooftop solar industry stakeholders to address consumer protection

issues meaningfully and comprehensively on the front end before the Georgia consumers are

harmed and without overly regulating the solar industry. GA Solar will gladly lead this effort

should the Commission make consumer protections a part of it’s decision in this rate case.
