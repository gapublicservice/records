                                          DISCUSSION

   I. GEORGIA POWER’S PROPOSAL TO SUSPEND RNR MONTHLY NETTING AND

   A. GEORGIA LAGS FAR BEHIND BOTH THE U.S. AND THE SOUTHEAST IN

       CUSTOMER-SITED SOLAR GENERATION.

In this proceeding, Commissioners, the Company, intervenors, and public witnesses have attested

to the divergent pathways of solar development in Georgia. On the one hand, as Chair Pridemore

noted in this proceeding, Georgia ranks fifth, and almost fourth, in the U.S. for total solar on the

grid (Tr. 4838, Lines 12-14). On the other hand, according to SACE and Southface Witness Dr.

Marilyn Brown, distributed solar generation represented less than 0.03% of Georgia’s total

electricity generation in 2019 (Docket No. 44280, Document No. 192146, Tr. 4907, Lines 14-

16), and penetration of BTM solar in Georgia was at only 0.2% for residential customers as of

June 2022, ranking Georgia 43rd in the U.S. for rooftop solar (Docket No. 44280, Document

No. 192146, Tr. 4894, Lines 2-3). In short, Georgia is not at a level of residential solar penetration

that warrants valuing excess distributed solar generation to the grid at anything less than the retail


                                                  7

rate. The national average penetration of customer-sited solar is approximately 1.9% (Docket No.

44280, Document No. 192146, Tr. 4901, Lines 11-12). Witness Dr. Marilyn Brown’s assessment

of Georgia’s national ranking in rooftop solar is in accordance with observations from Witness

Thatcher Young (Tr. 4838, Lines 15-16), among others.

           Describing customer-sited solar development in Georgia during the final hearing in this

proceeding, counsel for Georgia Power asked Witness Dr. David Hill, “...we can’t simply just do

nothing knowing that the wolf is coming?” (Tr. 4509, Lines 17-18). In addition to the subsequent

series of thoughtful responses from Witness Dr. David Hill, which closely align with our key

recommendations, SACE and Southface also agree with the poignant observation made by

Commissioner Echols the following day, that, “I mean, it really sounds more like a Labrador

Retriever to me…” (Tr. 4835, Lines 19-20). SACE and Southface Witnesses Dr. Marilyn Brown

and Alden Hathaway provide testimony that supports both Witness Dr. David Hill’s

recommendations for more deliberate stakeholder engagement to refine tariff design, among

others, as well as Commissioner Echols’ observations that rooftop solar might be ratepayers’ ‘best

friend’.

           Net metering policies began in Idaho in 1980, and, as attested by Witness Dr. Marilyn

Brown, and affirmed by Witnesses Kevin Lucas, Thatcher Young, and Will Geise, 33 states

currently have what is often called “true net metering” policies, which credit excess

electricity generated by customers at the retail rate” (Docket No. 44280, Document No.

192146, Tr. 4898, Lines 7-8). Over the last decade, states have continued to adopt net energy

metering (“NEM”), including, for example, South Carolina.


                                                  8

        In the Southeast, as Witness Dr. Marilyn Brown testifies, North Carolina, 7 South Carolina,

and Florida “have state-level policies requiring true net metering, and they also have the highest

rooftop solar penetration rates in the Southeast, averaging approximately 1%” (Docket No. 44280,

Document No. 192146, Tr. 4898, Lines 10-12). These states show clear associations between net

metering policies conducive to customer-sited solar and actual behind-the-meter solar expansion

and penetration within the state. Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia, and Tennessee, however, “don't

allow one-to-one netting of kWh for energy supplied to the grid and are all in the bottom eight

states for rooftop solar penetration” (Docket No. 44280, Document No. 192146, Tr. 4898-4899,

Lines 16-17, 1-2). In those four states, there is also a strong association with bottom-in-the-nation

rankings for customer-sited solar penetration.

        Georgia is not close to meeting the achievable potential for rooftop solar to supply a

valuable peak resource. Georgia Power has the most restrictive customer-sited solar tariff cap

in the Southeast and, perhaps, the nation, at 0.2% of annual peak demand in the previous

year. Many states across the U.S. do not have such caps, including Alabama, Florida, North

Carolina, and Tennessee. South Carolina’s program cap is 2%, not 0.2%, as in Georgia, and

Mississippi’s cap is 3% of annual peak demand (Docket No. 44280, Document No. 192146, Tr.

4899, Lines 21-22).

     FEDERAL FUNDS

    A. CONSUMER AND ENERGY ECONOMICS FUNDAMENTALLY CHANGED

        SINCE GEORGIA POWER CONCLUDED ITS 2022 INTEGRATED RESOURCE

        PLAN (“IRP”) AND FILED ITS 2022 RATE CASE.

The IIJA became law on November 15, 2021, only two and a half months before Georgia Power

filed its 2022 IRP and DSM Application on January 31, 2022. The IRA became law on August 16,

2022, one and a half months after Georgia Power filed its 2022 Rate Case on June 24, 2022.

Taken together, the IIJA and IRA represent one of the largest investments in climate and

energy in U.S. history. As described by Witness Ronald Lehr, the IRA provides more than $413

billion in new funding for climate and energy and the IIJA’s $168 billion in funding largely flows

to transportation, climate, and electricity (Docket No. 44280, Document No. 191855, Tr. 3707,

Lines 21-24). These changes to consumer and utility economics are likely to increase the potential

for consumer benefits during the three-year ARP. SACE and Southface recognize that Georgia

Power could not change its filing deadlines to accommodate potential federal legislation, but now

that legislation is law, the Company must work with the Commission to adjust its revenue forecasts

and requests accordingly through annual reviews of revenue for cost savings during the three-year

ARP.

III. GEORGIA POWER’S T&D REQUESTS

      A. T&D INVESTMENTS REPRESENT A LARGE PORTION OF COSTS INCLUDED

          IN THE COMPANY’S REQUEST, AND THEREFORE MERIT ADDITIONAL

          SCRUTINY AND OVERSIGHT BY THIS COMMISSION.

The Company is asking for rate recovery of $6.8 billion over the three-year ARP for T&D

improvements in this rate request, which includes $2.2 billion in investments in the Company’s

we believe it is of the utmost importance for the Commission to take decisive action to ensure

T&D investments, both now and in the future, are fair and reasonable for inclusion in customer

rates.

general Rate Case where the Commission will have access to more data and can consider them in

a broader context of the Company’s overall rate design.”

       In that same Order, the Commission summarized points from the SACE Brief. Those

additional excerpts from the Order are included below for convenience:

       “SACE further recommended that the Commission exempt low-income residential
