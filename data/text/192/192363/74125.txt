POST HEARING BRIEF OF 
THE METROPOLITAN ATLANTA RAPID TRANSIT AUTHORITY


COMES NOW, the Metropolitan Rapid Transit Authority (“MARTA”) and pursuant to the Georgia Public Service Commission’s August 5, 2022 Second Amended Procedural and Scheduling Order hereby submits this brief in support of MARTA’s request that the Electric Transportation Tariff (“ET Tariff”) be held at current rates and not increased in association with Georgia Power Company’s 2022 Rate Case.   In support of the same, MARTA shows the Commission as follows:
Summary of the Argument

MARTA’s ET Tariff Should Not be Increased 

MARTA’s Public Transportation Services are Crucial to Maintaining Georgia’s Status as the #1 Place to Do Business 

MARTA’s Public Transportation Services are Critical to Attracting Business

MARTA’s Transportation Oriented Developments Also Continue to Attract New Residents to the Metro-Area Growing Georgia Power’s Customer Base

Not Increasing the ET Tariff Enables MARTA to Utilize Funds to Continue Expanding and Enhancing its Service Whereby Stimulating More Economic Growth and Development and New Electric Customers for Georgia Power Company

MARTA Will Not be Fully Exempt from Increases; It Will Still be Subject to Any Rate Increase on its Other 92 Electric Service Accounts 

 Conclusion

Argument

The Electric Transportation Tariff Should Not be Increased 

As Mr. Kevin Hurley testified, MARTA is a powerful driver of new economic development in the State and Metro-Atlanta area.  MARTA’s public transportation services are crucial to maintaining Georgia’s status as the #1 Place to Do Business.  While reliable, affordable energy has been considered attractive to business, reliable, affordable public transportation has proved to be not just attractive but crucial to bringing new business to an otherwise car-centric State and metro Atlanta area.    
MARTA’s public transportation services are key to attracting new corporate hubs, special events, and other attractions to Georgia and the Metro Atlanta area as well as preserving the quality of life that Metro-Atlantans enjoy.   MARTA is unique among Georgia Power’s rate payers in providing critical public transportation services to the Metro Atlanta area.   Such services have been crucial in attracting business to Georgia, and the Atlanta area particularly.   MARTA has been and remains a highly sought-after amenity for businesses looking to locate or relocate to the Atlanta area.   In fact, public transportation – specifically access to MARTA rail – has become one of the most sought-after amenities for business in the region with many referring to it as the trump card in negotiations for corporate relocations.   
Microsoft, Mercedes-Benz, State Farm, and NCR are just a few of the companies which have deliberately located near MARTA rail stations to take advantage of public transportation for their work force and visitors.  Amazon’s interest in Atlanta for Amazon’s HQ2 advanced through Amazon’s site selection process because of access to ten potential sites near MARTA rail stations.   Likewise, MARTA’s Bankhead rail station was a driving factor in Microsoft’s recent decision to develop a 90-acre campus on the Westside of Atlanta near the station.  The Bankhead station is now undergoing an expansion to further support Microsoft’s decision to locate in the area.   Significantly, the State, recognizing the importance of MARTA’s service, for the first time ever included a line item in its state budget to help support the positive impacts of the Bankhead station expansion, which will see benefits beyond just Microsoft.    
MARTA, with its direct connection and rail service from Atlanta Hartsfield-Jackson International Airport also continues to be a major driver in attracting special events to the State and Atlanta region.  MARTA was critical to Atlanta landing major sporting events like the 2019 Superbowl and the upcoming 2026 World Cup.   With its direct connection from the Airport to conference centers, including the Georgia World Congress Center, MARTA is also a crucial factor in attracting conventions and travelers to the metro area, helping the state’s hospitality sector as well.   
MARTA is not just crucial in attracting corporations and events but has proven crucial in reshaping our urban environment through its transportation-oriented developments, which attracts new residents and businesses alike.  MARTA’s transit-oriented development projects have resulted in the development of approximately 1,919 multifamily units, 379,300 square feet of retail, and roughly 2.8 million square feet of office space.   Additional transit-oriented development already in the plans over the next fifteen (15) years includes an additional 9,140 multifamily units, 80,000 square feet of retail space, and 2 million square feet of office space at an estimated cost of $4.75 billion, and more opportunity exists.       
In addition to its transit-oriented developments, MARTA plans to spend more than $3 billion on capital projects over the next ten years, including expansion of services, offering more incentives and opportunities for businesses looking to relocate or grow in Georgia.     Furthermore, MARTA continues to make energy efficiency improvements in its services, including by replacing aging rail cars with more energy efficient cars.    In other words, MARTA continues to make great strides in improving its own energy usage and burden.
By not increasing the ET Tariff MARTA can utilize those funds to expand and enhance its service, whereby further stimulating economic development and new customers for Georgia Power.   MARTA is a much more valuable partner as a driver of economic development – both business and residential development – than as a ratepayer.   Funds expended on MARTA services have an economic multiplying effect.    Georgia Power stands to benefit far more from the revenue generated by the new business and residential customers MARTA brings in than from revenue to be generated by an increase in the ET Tariff.   This also has a benefit on other Georgia Power customers by helping to spread the cost of Georgia Power’s fixed costs over more customers.    Lastly, MARTA will not be fully exempt from the impacts of any rate increase as it has roughly 92 other electric accounts with Georgia Power that are not part of the ET Tariff.   
Conclusion

MARTA’s investment in its infrastructure and public transportation services, particularly rail services, have a great multiplying effect on economic development in Georgia and the Atlanta region, increasing Georgia Power’s electric sales to new customers and spreading the cost of service.  In previous rate proceedings, Georgia Power, the Commission, and others have recognized MARTA’s unique value and contribution in helping to make this State the number one place to do business.   MARTA’s ability to continue offering reliable, efficient, accessible, and affordable public transportation services is critical to the continued success of the State and Metro-Atlanta area in attracting and maintaining new business and being the number one place for business.   MARTA respectfully asks the Commission to grant MARTA’s request to exempt the ET Tariff from any rate increase as part of Georgia Power’s 2022 Rate Case.
