 RESOURCE SUPPLY MANAGEMENT BRIEF


COMES NOW, Resource Supply Management and shows the Commission the following:

Georgia Power’s Rates are a Confusing Mess.
Georgia Power Studies and Analyses Lack Credibility.

Reduce Coming Series of Rate Increases by Putting Vogtle on the Wholesale Market.
Electric Vehicle Chargers Should Pay Their Way With No Georgia Power Competition.
1.
              Georgia Power’s Rates are a Confusing Mess.

Georgia Power brags about having a large number of rates as if that were a good thing. It isn’t. It confuses customers about how to save energy and reduce demand. The Company is changing resident rates to correctly collect demand cost that in the older rate are being collected volumetricly. Yet under cross-examination the Company witness defended the confusing widely used commercial rate which collects part of its fixed cost in a volumetric manner. The witness was unable to proprely answer what was the true demand cost. Georgia Power’s witness even made the doubious assertion that such a confusing method was done by other utilities. 

While Georgia Power made the required Cost-of-Service study, the results were not used in their rate proposals. Over the years the connection between demand costs and demand charges are fading.

Georgia Power’s restrictions on customer response with marginal cost rates creates an artifical need for peaking generation and batteries.
2.
         Georgia Power Studies and Analyses Lack Credibility.

When Georgia Power presents a study or analysis to support some action from the Commission, the study should be assumed to be false.
Take the Vogtle project where Georgia Power said it would cost $14 billion. It’s running $34 billion, and even that is suspicious.               
Then there is overearning after each rate case for the many years now. The Company makes profits higher than the Commission targets because the Company uses inflated operating cost estimates and low-balls projected revenues. It seems to work every time even with a long track record.
In the current case the staff caught Georgia Power using a computer program to inflate the cost of replacing transmission and distribution components before the end of their useful life. We are talking big money here. The Company can only repeat their initial litigation position in response.
The Company’s coal ash remediation number is huge and unrealistic. Here the Company’s goal is to get a softening of environmental regulations. Which brings us now to the creation of vampire plants that still have a useful life. The Company proposes to keep collecting on idle plants.  Naturally, they have studies showing it is better to shutter the facilities than to fix them. If the Commission denied any recovery of closed assets, the studies would be very different; and the plants likely would keep running.   The fact that Georgia Power produces a study is evidence enough to assume the results are to influence rather than be accurate.

3.
Cut Revenue By Dissallowance – Not Securitization

The Commission should ignor calls by some parties to use government bonding authority to bail out bad debt. In this case the bad debt is created by the closure of still useful generating facilities.

Normal businesses write off obsolete and abondoned assets. If Georgia Power is forced to do the same, they would not be so reckless in their investments. Since the Company Board of Directors cannot stop bad investments; perhaps the lenders can after facing default.

4.
Reduce Coming Series of Rate Increases by Putting Vogtle on the Wholesale Market.

The Commission is looking for ways to reduce the stream of requsted new revenues coming over the next few years. The best way is to let the cost of Vogtle be recovered through revenues obtained by selling its output into the wholsale market. Payback may not be quick, but innocent ratepayers should not be stuck with bad debt.

5.
Electric Vehicle Chargers Should Pay Their Way With No Georgia Power Competition.

The staff’s initial position in this case is that the users of Electric Vehicales should have some of their costs paid by by other power customers. This is correct, and further the Company should not compete with private investors who cannot be subdized by non-users of EV chargers. The new EV rate should be available to both new and existing customers.

NOW THEREFORE, The Commission should follow the wise advice offered here.
