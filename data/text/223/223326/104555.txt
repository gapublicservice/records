CLARIFICATION:

CUSTOMER IDENTIFIED RESOURCES ARE SYSTEM RESOURCES

       CEBA greatly appreciates the Commissioners’ interest and questions during the Energy

Committee meeting. During the discussion, it became clear that there may be a lingering

misunderstanding about Customer Identified Resources, which will be system resources.

       Georgia Power procures all resources for the benefit of all customers, including Customer

Identified Resources. Georgia Power will only select clean firm Customer Identified Resources

(i.e., renewables paired with battery storage) that benefit all customers. Georgia Power will

dispatch storage-paired Customer Identified Resources for the benefit of all customers – not for

the sole benefit of the subscribing customers. These same principles apply to the existing CARES

Carbon Free Energy Around the Clock (CFE-ATC) option, in which Georgia Power accounts for

the avoided capacity value that subscribed storage-paired renewable resources provide when

calculating the subscription price.

      To summarize:

      •   Subscribing customers will pay the full cost of Customer Identified Resources through

          their subscription, on top of their normal electric rate.

      •   All Georgia Power customers will receive the full energy and capacity benefits of

          Customer Identified Resources under CEBA’s proposal.

      •   The CARES Program provides credits that reflect the hourly energy value of the

          resources to which customers subscribe. The CARES CFE-ATC option additionally

          reflects the avoided capacity value of storage-paired renewable resources in the

          calculation of the subscription price.

      •   CEBA simply asks that the avoided capacity value of clean firm Customer Identified

          Resources be reflected in the calculation of the subscription price, using the same

          formula as the CARES CFE-ATC option.

To put it simply: Subscribing customers will pay the full cost of Customer Identified

Resources but these resources will be selected and operated for the benefit of all Georgia

Power customers.

                        PROPOSED COMMISSIONER MOTIONS

CEBA Recommendation 2: Direct Georgia Power to account for the avoided capacity value
of clean firm Customer Identified Resources (e.g. renewables paired with storage) in the
subscription price calculation, just as it does in the CARES CFE-ATC tariff.

       To implement CEBA’s Recommendation 2, CEBA requests that the Commission by

Motion insert the following paragraph in the Final Order:

“The Commission finds and concludes that it is reasonable for the Company to account for capacity

value in the CIR option for clean firm resources, such as renewables paired with battery energy

storage. Clean firm resources provide more value to the Company’s system and its customers than

variable renewable resources alone, especially during this period of rapid load growth. CIRs are

off-site, utility-scale, system resources that serve all customers, and will be fully paid for by

subscribing customers. CEBA witnesses testified that large customers are willing to pay higher

subscription prices for clean firm resources through the CIR option if the higher value these

resources provide is recognized through the subscription price. The Company argued that

accounting for the capacity value of a subscription resource was contrary to the regulatory

compact. However, this position is refuted by the fact that the Company recognizes the capacity

value of storage-paired renewable resources that participate in the CARES CFE-ATC option when

it calculates the subscription price for the CFE-ATC option. The Company is therefore directed to

use the same formula to account for capacity value that it uses for the CARES CFE-ATC option

in the CIR option for clean firm resources starting in 2027, which is the first year the Company

forecasts a capacity need. The Company shall ensure that accounting for the incremental capacity

value of clean firm CIRs shall not harm non-participating customers.”

CEBA Recommendation 4: Direct Georgia Power to allow customers to subscribe to up to
5,000 MW of CIRs.1

       To implement CEBA’s Recommendation 4, CEBA requests that the Commission by

Motion insert the following paragraph in the Final Order:

“The Commission finds and concludes that the overall size of the CIR option should be increased

to meet customer demand. Customer demand for clean energy subscriptions currently exceeds the

available supply. Demand for clean energy subscriptions from future new and expanding

customers is also expected to be high. The CIR option is driven by customer demand and resources

will only be selected as part of a second phase of utility scale renewable RFPs if they provide

benefits to all customers, so there is no risk that the Company will procure more CIRs than it needs.

To meet both current and expected customer demand, the Company is directed to allow customers

to subscribe to up to 5,000 MW of CIRs.”
