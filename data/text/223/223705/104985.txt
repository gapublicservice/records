Q2 2025 Large Load Economic Development Report

August 15, 2025


Docket No. 56002


Introduction
Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) has a responsibility to serve the needs of its customers — today and for generations to come. Our long-term integrated resource planning processes, along with the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (“Commission”) triennial Integrated Resource Plan proceedings, serve as the foundation of our ability to provide clean, safe, reliable, and affordable energy to Georgians and to serve the state’s growing economy. Georgia is the number one state to do business due in large part to the effectiveness of this planning process that ensures we are able to meet energy demands today and in the future. 
Since the Company’s last large load quarterly report in May, Georgia’s economy continues to prosper. And as the state’s economic prospects remain bright, the trends leading up to the 2023 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) Update filing and as presented in the 2025 IRP remain consistent, having now continued for more than two years.
The latest data continue to support Georgia Power’s expectation for continued and robust economic growth in Georgia and the timing of new large load customers. As illustrated in this report, the following growth trends continue:
The pipeline of potential economic development projects continues to grow. 
As of June 30, 2025, the total pipeline of economic development projects through the mid-2030s has increased by 4,400 MW to 59,000 MW. Of this, 56,800 MW represent large load economic development projects.
For the near-term winter of 2028/2029, the large load economic development pipeline has increased by 600 MW to 25,800 MW.
Large load customers continue to materialize and progress with construction.
In the near-term, projects that have broken ground represent 3,265 MW of the total 6,261 MW of customer commitments for the winter of 2028/2029.
Table 1 in this report provides a summary of the construction status of the 26 large load projects that have executed a Request for Electric Service (“RFS”) as of June 30, 2025. Thirteen of these projects have broken ground, and thirteen are pending construction.
This Large Load Economic Development Report is provided in accordance with the Order Adopting Stipulated Agreement issued by the Commission in approving the Stipulation in Georgia Power’s 2023 IRP Update in Docket No. 55378 and Order Adopting Stipulation in Georgia Power’s 2025 IRP in Docket No. 56002. To keep the Commission informed during this period of extraordinary economic growth, Item 2 from the 2023 IRP Update Stipulation requires the Company to file quarterly reports that include information identified in Attachment A of the Stipulation and any other pertinent information about large load economic development activity in the 2023 IRP Update Load Forecast and any changes since the previous update. Details about the existing, new, and former projects in the pipeline are provided in the attachment to this report. In addition, as agreed to in the 2025 IRP Stipulation, the Company has included the date that any new project enters the large load pipeline, the announced load of any new project entering the large load pipeline, and new large load projects that have entered into a Contract for Electric Service.
Changes in the Economic Development Pipeline
Since the first quarter 2025 report, as of June 30, 2025, the total pipeline of economic development projects through the mid-2030s has increased from 54,600 MW to 59,000 MW – an increase of 4,400 MW. Of the 59,000 MW in the total pipeline, 56,800 MW represent large load economic development projects. The large load projects have increased by a net 4,700 MW, resulting from the following changes since the last report:
6,000 MW that entered the pipeline; 
A net increase of 400 MW for projects already in the pipeline that increased their projected load; and
1,600 MW that exited the pipeline.
Since our last report, in the near-term (winter of 2028/2029), the total pipeline of projects has increased by 1,200 MW to 28,900 MW, while the large load economic development pipeline has increased by 600 MW to 25,800 MW. Further details about the existing, new, and former projects in the pipeline are provided in the attachment to this report.
Changes in Commitments from Large Load Customers
As the total and large load economic development pipelines have grown and strengthened, the number of commitments to Georgia Power for service from large load customers has remained the same this quarter. One committed project has requested a change in load causing near-term commitments to increase 615 MW to 6,261 MW and total committed load to increase 525 MW to 8,799 MW, representing 26 committed large load projects.
Table 1 below provides a summary of the construction status of the 26 committed large load projects, all of which have executed an RFS with Georgia Power. Thirteen of these projects have broken ground, and thirteen are pending construction. This evidence clearly indicates that these large load customers are materializing and making progress without material delays. In the near-term, projects that have broken ground represent 3,265 MW of the total 6,261 MW of customer commitments for the winter of 2028/2029.

Conclusion
Since March 2025: 
The total economic development pipeline has expanded from approximately 54,600 MW to approximately 59,000 MW through the mid-2030s, representing an increase of 4,400 MW;
Near-term 2028/2029 large loads have increased by 600 MW; and
Customer commitments increased to 8,799 MW and near-term commitments have grown 615 MW to 6,261 MW
Since the filing of the 2023 IRP Update in October 2023:
The total economic development pipeline has expanded from approximately 17,000 MW to approximately 59,000 MW through the mid-2030s, representing an increase of 42,000 MW;
Near-term 2028/2029 large loads have increased by approximately 15,300 MW; and
Customer commitments have expanded from 3,612 MW to 8,799 MW, representing an increase of 5,187 MW.

This information shows that Georgia Power’s load forecast is materializing and that the constructive outcomes of the 2023 IRP Update and 2025 IRP support continued economic growth in Georgia.
The Company will continue to monitor economic growth and will keep the Commission apprised of large load activity consistent with the requirements of the Commission’s Order Adopting Stipulated Agreement in the 2023 IRP Update and Order Adopting Stipulation in the 2025 IRP.
