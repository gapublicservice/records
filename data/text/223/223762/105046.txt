     JOINT APPLICATION OF GEORGIA INTERFAITH POWER & LIGHT AND
         SOUTHFACE ENERGY INSTITUTE FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE

       Georgia Interfaith Power & Light (GIPL) and Southface Energy Institute (Southface),

through counsel, hereby apply to the Georgia Public Service Commission for leave to intervene in

the above-referenced dockets pursuant to O.C.G.A. §§ 46-2-59 and 50-13-14 and Commission

Rule 515-2-1-.06. In support of this application, GIPL and Southface respectfully show the

Commission as follows:

                                                 1.
       On July 30, 2025, Georgia Power Company filed an Application for the Certification of

the All-Source Capacity Power Purchase Agreements and Company-Owned Proposals. It is the

largest single request for certification in Georgia Power’s history.

                                                 2.
       On July 30, 2025, Georgia Power also filed a surprise application for the certification of

“supplemental” resources that consist of nearly 2,000 additional megawatts of supply-side capacity

not previously approved, or even mentioned, as part of an integrated resource plan—including the

2025 IRP, voted on two weeks prior. Georgia Power seeks waiver of the Commission’s

competitive bidding process that ensures billpayers only pay for the most cost-effective resources

available.

                                                 3.
       Georgia Interfaith Power & Light is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization headquartered in

Decatur, Georgia. GIPL represents the interests of communities of faith in promoting

environmental justice, energy conservation, energy efficiency, renewable energy, and related

sustainable practices. GIPL engages faith communities in the stewardship of Creation. GIPL is a

growing organization with more than 350 member congregations across the state of Georgia

representing a wide variety of faiths and over 150 congregationally based Green Teams working

to advance GIPL’s objectives. While several of GIPL’s participating congregations are located

throughout the Atlanta metropolitan area, many are located in other areas of the State, including

the Acworth, Adel, Albany, Athens, Augusta, Brunswick, Canton, Carrollton, Clayton, Covington,

Dalton, Darien, Ellijay, Gainesville, Kennesaw, Macon, Newnan, Roswell, Sautee-Nacoochee,

Savannah, Valdosta, Warner Robins, and Waycross areas.

                                                 4.
       GIPL has an interest in these two certification proceedings both on its own behalf and on

behalf of its members. Many of GIPL’s participating congregations and members of those

congregations purchase electricity from Georgia Power Company, both for their places of worship

and for their individual residences. Their electricity consumption and costs for electricity will be

affected by the Commission’s actions in this docket.

                                                 5.
       Additionally, GIPL’s core mission is to inspire and equip communities of faith to organize,


                                                 2

implement practical climate solutions, and advocate across Georgia on issues of climate change,

environmental justice, and community resilience. GIPL’s mission is thus directly implicated by

Georgia Power’s supply-side choices as proposed in these proceedings. GIPL’s mission is further

implicated by the extent to which the Commission and Georgia Power’s process for choosing

electric resources is transparent and accessible, to provide for member education and engagement

in these important policy choices. GIPL intends to address the concerns of both itself and its

members regarding how Georgia Power’s proposals—and Georgia Power and the Commission’s

processes—impact both God’s Creation and the resulting bills that customers, and in particular

low-income customers, pay. GIPL envisions a Georgia where all people live safely in a healthy

environment, a stable climate, and resilient communities. As a result, GIPL has substantial and

vital interests in the outcome of these proceedings and seeks to intervene to protect these interests.

No other party in this case shares GIPL’s unique interests.

                                                  6.
       In furtherance of its interests and on behalf of its members, GIPL has intervened in many

prior proceedings before the Commission, including: Georgia Power’s 2025 IRP and DSM

proceedings, Docket Nos. 56002, 56003; Georgia Power’s 2023 Biomass Request for Proposals,

Docket No. 44880; Georgia Power’s 2023 IRP Update and Application for Certification of Plant

Yates Units 8-10, Docket No. 55378; Georgia Power’s 2022 Rate Case, Docket No. 44280;

Georgia Power’s 2022 IRP and DSM proceedings, Docket Nos. 44160, 44161; Georgia Power’s

2020 PURPA Avoided Cost proceedings, Docket Nos. 4822, 16573, 19279; Georgia Power’s 2019

Base Rate Case, Docket No. 42516; Georgia Power’s 2019 IRP and DSM proceedings, Docket

Nos. 42310, 42311; the 2017-2018 DSM Working Group and the 2014-16 DSM Working Group,

Docket Nos. 41253, 38401; Georgia Power’s 2016 IRP and DSM proceedings, Docket Nos. 40161,

40162; Georgia Power’s Application for Certification of a Green Energy Program and Green

                                                7.
       Southface is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization that has promoted energy-, water-, and

resource-efficient workplaces, homes, and communities throughout the Southeast for more than

four decades. Southface works with consumers, the construction and development industry, and

policymakers to advance clean energy goals through education, research, advocacy, and technical

assistance. Southface offers market-based solutions that create jobs and support sustainable

communities. Southface's members reside across the state.

                                                8.

       Southface has extensive expertise in the use and installation of the kinds of energy

efficiency and energy management technologies that are the basis of the Company’s proposed

energy efficiency programs. Southface is one of the leading organizations in the Southeast on how

to achieve energy efficiency in buildings. Southface has conducted research and provided

education and technical assistance on such technologies for over 40 years. Southface advises

companies, nonprofits, communities, and residential customers in Georgia and across the

Southeast on how to cost-effectively meet their energy and sustainability goals. Southface brings

unique interests and perspectives to these proceedings.

        In furtherance of its interests and on behalf of its members, Southface has intervened in

proceedings, Docket Nos. 56002, 56003; Georgia Power’s 2022 Rate Case, Docket No. 44280;

Application for the Certification, Decertification, and Amended DSM Plan, Docket No. 44161;

                                                 10.

        The interests of GIPL and its participating congregations and Southface are not

adequately represented by other parties to these proceedings. Other parties that have applied to

intervene do not have interests similar to those of GIPL or Southface. The applicants’

intervention would not unduly delay the proceedings or prejudice the rights of other parties.

                                                 11.

        If this application is granted, GIPL and Southface, through its counsel, intend to participate

fully and jointly in this docket, including cross-examining witnesses, potentially filing direct

testimony, and filing proposed orders and/or briefs. GIPL and Southface, through counsel, have

so far in these proceedings been denied access to trade secret materials by Georgia Power; the

absence of the ability to review basic facts about the sources at issue makes it difficult to clearly

articulate a full basis for intervention.
