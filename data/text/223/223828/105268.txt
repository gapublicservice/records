Question: 
See page 14 of the Application, which says an in-depth cost-benefit economic analysis was performed for the BESS projects that advanced to the Competitive Tier.  
Was this economic analysis performed just comparing BESS projects?  If so, was this considered a screening step to weed out some of the BESS projects that were ultimately evaluated against other types of resources?
That page states indirect costs were included in the analysis. Please identify and quantify the indirect costs that were included in the analysis.
That page states potential risks were included in the analysis. Please identify and quantify the risks that were included in the analysis.

Response:

No. The economic analysis that led to the battery energy storage systems (“BESS”) projects that advanced to the Competitive Tier was not performed only comparing BESS projects to each other. All submissions into the RFP were consistently evaluated and ranked wholistically at each stage of the RFP process. There was no screening step used to “weed out” some of the BESS projects.

Indirect costs included in the analyses for BESS projects include the costs of power used to grid charge, transmission delivery costs, and term equalization costs. 

The costs of power used to grid charge are not isolated in the analyses but are accounted for in the energy benefits determination. The Aurora model optimizes when grid power is used to charge BESS projects in order to discharge in the future and save production costs. The change in production costs before and after adding a resource represents the energy benefits that are included in the evaluation. 

Transmission delivery costs represent the project’s attributable portion of the costs of bulk transmission system projects that are needed to deliver firm transmission of energy from, and in the case of a storage resource, to the resource. 

Term equalization costs are used to evaluate resources with different useful lives, start dates, or end dates. The evaluation timeframe was set to encompass the resource with the earliest start date and the resource with the latest end date. In time periods when a specific resource starts later than or ends earlier than the set evaluation timeframe, replacement capacity costs are included. For the calculation of term equalization costs, please see STF-PIA-1-1 Attachment A TRADE SECRET, Section 2 – Evaluation, subfolder (c) Short List, specifically the Capacity RFP Evaluator file.

Potential risks evaluated and quantified for BESS projects in the RFP include the impacts on energy benefits related to forecasted system fuel pricing, potential greenhouse gas emissions regulation, load forecasts, and future expansion technology options. Although not quantified individually, these risk impacts were encapsulated in the range of scenarios used in the evaluation. For energy benefits quantified for each scenario, please see the ranking files that include individual planning scenario rankings in STF-PIA-1-1 Attachment A TRADE SECRET, Section 2 – Evaluation, subfolders (a) through (c).

Response:

If a Bid is determined to be a lease, there is an equity cost to Georgia Power when the Power Purchase Agreement (“PPA”) is placed on the Company’s balance sheet. This is caused by the rating agency treatment of a lease, in accordance with FASB ASC Topic 842.
For a Finance Lease, Georgia Power must capitalize the PPA as an asset on its balance sheet and must offset it by a debt obligation on the liability side of the balance sheet. The addition of the debt obligation on the balance sheet requires the Company to recapitalize to maintain the appropriate debt and equity ratio. As a result, there are direct equity costs incurred by Georgia Power when a PPA is capitalized and reflected on the balance sheet as a Finance Lease. The equity cost of a bid determined to result in a Finance Lease, if selected, is evaluated by determining the net cost (lease cost less depreciation and tax implication) of capitalizing the PPA.
For an Operating Lease, the PPA is also added to the balance sheet but without directly affecting the debt and equity ratio on the Company’s books. However, rating agencies impute debt onto Georgia Power’s balance sheet in their evaluation of the credit rating for the Company. Therefore, the Company must still recapitalize to maintain the target debt and equity ratio needed to ensure its credit rating is not impacted by the PPA. There is a direct equity cost premium associated with this treatment.
The additional annual revenue requirement cost to the Company is calculated for the Operating Lease or Finance Lease. The result is the equity cost applied to the bid.
