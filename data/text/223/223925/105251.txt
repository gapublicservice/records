STF-PIA-33-2

Question: 

Refer to the response to STF-PIA-29-4.  Provide a narrative of how the Company uses the Distribution Resource Management Tracking Application to determine resource needs before a storm. Include any materials the Company uses such as guidelines, procedures, or models (e.g., decision trees) to determine resource needs. 

Response:

The Company uses the Distribution Resource Management (“DRM”) tracking application for major storms that necessitate allocation of resources from one area to another. For major events such as Hurricane Helene, Georgia Power assesses resource requirements by referencing similar historical weather events from the DRM database. Based on the actual resources that were used in those prior storms, the DRM estimates the resources required for anticipated major storms, such as number of personnel, crew leader contact, assigned work location, and resource type.
In the case of Hurricane Helene, based on the storm’s forecasted path through the Gulf, Hurricane Michael from 2018 was identified as a relevant comparison based on its location and projected trajectory. The Company reviewed the resource allocations that were required to respond to Hurricane Michael to inform planning for manpower acquisition, prestaging, and post-landfall restoration activities.

STF-PIA-33-1

Question: 

Refer to the response to STF-PIA-29-3. Indicate when the Company expects to have the new storm modeling tools developed and operational. In addition, provide an update on the specific tools that are in development and/or operational.

Response:

The Company implemented a storm modeling tool, Storm Prediction, Estimated Restoration Times and Reporting (“SPEAR”), in July 2025. This storm analytics tool delivers:
Predicted incident counts;
Forecasted customer impacts;
Hourly timing estimates;
Geospatial impact forecasts, both at the state and region levels; and
Weather metric predictions.
As part of the SPEAR application, the Company also recently implemented a manpower tracking tool called GridCritical, which will be used to efficiently onboard, track, and accommodate resources assigned to restorations efforts.  This information will feed directly into the SPEAR application to assist in customer restoration times.
In addition to SPEAR, the Company is currently testing two additional modeling tools (StormImpact Prediction Models and WeatherSentry) to use for benchmarking purposes.
StormImpact: Provides proprietary weather and hurricane models that are customized with Georgia Power’s historical storm effects to predict infrastructure damage and outages for any scale event.  
WeatherSentry: Robust platform that provides insights from proprietary weather prediction models on storm impacts using historical data to show damage, outages, and flooding. Among other capabilities, this system also provides data on traffic and evacuation routes to route restoration crews if necessary.
