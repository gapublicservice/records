STF-PIA-17-3

Question: 

Please explain how a third party could derive Georgia Power trade secret results using Staff’s results.

Response:

By publishing any of the trade secret inputs or outputs of the Load Realization Model (“LRM”), a third party could calculate the risk adjustment factors produced by the LRM to convert the large load pipeline announced load to expected metered load or forecasted load. Given the proprietary nature of the LRM and the competitive advantage it offers, the Company would be unwilling to provide  insights into its output to potential competitors. The Company marks its own outputs of the LRM as trade secret, and the results of Staff calculations using the Company’s model should also be treated accordingly.

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-17 PD/STF-PIA-17-1.docx =====
STF-PIA-17-1

Question: 

Please refer to Table 28 on page 83 of the Trokey-Drugan-Pol testimony in Docket 56002. The Company designated the values calculated by Staff in the columns “Staff P50 LRM” and “Staff LRM Adjustment” as trade secret. Please demonstrate how public dissemination of the Staff calculated values would harm Georgia Power customers.

Response:

The Load Realization Model (“LRM”) is a proprietary, Company-produced model aimed at quantifying the specific elements of uncertainty the Company has identified when forecasting large load electricity demand. By quantifying the elements of uncertainty present in the Company’s large load pipeline, the Company can produce a unique, risk-adjusted large load forecast specific to Georgia Power and compete to serve  large load customers while also adequately planning a reliable system. This valuable proprietary model allows the company to  appropriately balance new growth opportunities with potential risk. Given that the Company publishes the large load pipeline in its quarterly large load economic development reports, public dissemination of any trade secret LRM inputs or outputs would give competitors insight into the Company’s proprietary model, allowing them to use the model for their own benefit and eroding the Company’s ability to compete with them. In this case, competitors are either electric service providers within Georgia or electric service providers outside of Georgia that are competing for the same large load customers. In addition to hindering the Company’s ability to compete with other electric suppliers,  public dissemination  of the LRM outputs, including Staff’s calculated values, could harm customers by undermining the economic benefits associated with economic growth in Georgia.
