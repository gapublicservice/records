HEARING BRIEF OF THE 
GEORGIA SOLAR ENERGY INDUSTRIES ASSOCIATION, INC.

Comes now, the Georgia Solar Energy Industries Association, Inc. (“GSEIA”) and, pursuant to the Procedural and Scheduling Order (“PSO”) entered in the above-styled dockets, files this Hearing Brief.  Based on the evidentiary record, GSEIA shows that the following directives should be included in the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (the “Commission”) order in this docket:
Georgia Power Company (the “Company”) is directed to roll over all megawatts (“MW”) approved by the Commission in prior IRPs, the 2025 IRP, and future IRPs but unfulfilled in any Distributed Generation (“DG”) Requests for Proposals (“RFPs”) to DG RFPs and programs for which the Company seeks approval in the 2025 IRP and/or in future IRPs. 
The Company is directed to reconstitute the DG working group with the goal of refining the program guidelines for DG RFPs and DG Power Purchase Agreement (“PPA”) terms resulting from the 2025 IRP and future IRPs.  Staff, the Company, and stakeholders shall prepare and file with the Commission a comprehensive report of all future working group meetings, including topics/disputes discussed, resolutions of disputes/topics discussed, and any unresolved disputes/topics discussed.  The working group shall be conducted to strive to ensure that the Commission’s policy decisions are effectuated and fulfilled so that the DG solar MWs that the Commission has seen fit in prior and this IRP, and sees fit in future IRPs, for advancement of solar in the State of Georgia, are fully procured. 
In current and future DG RFPs in which the Company permits bidders to pair solar plus storage, if a bid that pairs solar plus storage is not selected, the Company shall permit the bidder to refresh the bid, without incurring additional bid fees, as a standalone solar (i.e., solar without storage) project. 
Between now and the filing of the next IRP, the Company is directed to collaborate and communicate with staff and stakeholders regarding Virtual Power Plant (“VPP”) programs in other states as well as the Company’s Customer-Site Solar + Storage Pilot Program to implement best practices and lessons learned in VPP programs proposed by the Company in the next and future IRPs. 
Rather than present a Proposed Order, GSEIA attaches hereto Exhibit “A” which sets forth proposed directives GSEIA respectfully requests the Commission include as part of its order in the above styled docket.
GSEIA represents 12 members, primarily based in Georgia.  (T. 3067:12-14).  It focuses its advocacy on standalone solar DG and behind-the-meter solar projects ranging in size from 1 MW to 6 MW.  (T. 3067: 14-17).  GSEIA has actively participated in the Company’s tri-annual IRP proceedings since 2013.  (T. 3067:11-12).  DG solar in Georgia has grown over the past (15) years due to the Commission’s, its staff’s, and the Company’s recognition of the benefits that DG solar provides to ratepayers, the Company’s system, and the State of Georgia.  This hearing brief focuses on the issues that are pertinent to the continued success of DG solar in Georgia.  
Staff and the Company agree that roll over of unprocured MWs in DG RFPs has been the historical practice of the Commission and the Company.  That practice should continue.

Hearing testimony established the importance of ensuring that MW that the Commission has in prior IRPs authorized and in future IRPs will authorize the Company to acquire but remain unprocured roll over to future RFPs.  Continuing the practice of unprocured MW rolling forward to future RFPs will ensure that the Commission’s policy decisions for DG solar are implemented as thoroughly and successfully as possible. 
The Company anticipates load growth of 9.4 gigawatts by the winter of 2034 to 2035.  (T. 636:24-637:4).  DG solar generation helps the company meet its load growth projects, while meeting corporate customers’ demand for sustainability goals and avoiding regulatory risk related to greenhouse gas emissions.  (T. 638:15-639:21, T: 640:22-641:3).  DG solar also has unique locational benefits as it places generation closer to load.  (E.g., T. 641:13-643:22).  In this IRP, the Commission proposes for the Renewable Cost Benefit (“RCB”) Framework to be modified to replace the deferred transmission investment component of the RCB Framework with a geographically differentiated transmission system cost benefit factor, which further recognizes DG solar’s unique locational benefits.  (E.g., T:1131:1-20).  The Company’s Renewable Integration Study also recognizes that the Company and its system can accept significant increases in solar penetration while maintaining reliability.  (T. 1133:12-16).
The Company proposes in the 2025 IRP for previously unprocured utility scale solar MW to roll forward to and be procured in future utility scale solar RFPs.  This has been the Company’s and the Commission’s historical practice for unprocured utility scale solar MWs. (T. 1156:22-1158:8).  And it has been the Commission’s historical practice for DG solar MW.  (See e.g., T. 1158:2-8, T. 1159:3-9; T. 1996:2-4).  There are substantial unprocured DG solar MW from prior DG RFPs.  (T. 1158:9-1159:2).  The Company admits that whether those unprocured DG solar MWs should roll forward is a policy decision.  (T. 1161:5-6 (“[I]t would be up to the Commission to decide the disposition of unfilled megawatts.  Again, historically, those unfilled megawatts have been rolled forward per order of the Commission.”)).
Although the Company does not propose in this IRP for previously unprocured DG solar MW to roll forward to future IRPs, Commission Public Interest Advocacy (“PIA”) Staff recommends that such MWs should roll forward on a go-forward basis.  (T. 1995:14-1995:1).  In other words, DG MW unprocured in RFPs prior to the 2025 IRP should roll forward to RFPs proposed in this and future RFPs, and DG MW unprocured in future RFPs should roll forward to successive RFPs.  (Id.). 
Given that the Commission’s and the Company’s historical practice has been for DG solar MW unprocured in an RFP to roll forward to future DG RFPs, and Commission PIA Staff supports the continuation of this practice, the Commission should direct the Company to roll forward into the RFPs proposed by the 2025 IRP and/or in future IRPs DG solar MWs that were not or are not procured in any solar DG RFPs. 
Staff and the Company recognize that the DG Working Group has been productive; therefore, it should be continued and its results should be reported to the Commission.  

The Company recognizes that for the Company’s system “to realize the benefits that distributed generation and renewables in general can provide, . . . RFPs need to be flexible and need to adapt.”  (T. 1162:21-1163:1).  The last DG working group met approximately 6 times and was attended by representatives of the Company, Commission Staff, and solar DG developers.  (T. 1166:19-22; T. 1167:19-20).  During those working group meetings, the Company received feedback directly from the solar DG developers.  (T. 1166:19-22).  The Company developed the enhancements it proposes to in the 2025 IRP “based directly on th[e] feedback” it received from developers during working group meetings.”  (T. 1167:2-3).  The Company agrees that the working group allows RFPs to “be more flexible and adaptive; thereby allowing the [C]ompany to ensure it processes as many of the [MW] . . . that th[e] Commission has authorized.”  (T. 1168:25-1169:7).
Commission PIA Staff also supports continuation of the working group, as necessary “if there are issues that developers[,] . . . the Staff and the [C]ompany need to work or try to improve the DG RFP process.”  (T. 1997:16-24).  GSEIA’s witnesses testified that there are significant issues with the RFP process and PPAs that developers, Commission Staff, and the Company need to work through to ensure that the Company procures as many of the MWs the Commission has authorized or will authorize the Company to acquire.  (T. 3085:5-3087:19).
Accordingly, the Commission should direct the Company to reconstitute the DG working group with the goal of refining the program guidelines for DG RFPs and terms for DG PPA resulting from the 2025 IRP and future IRPs.  Additionally, the Commission should direct the Staff, the Company, and stakeholders to prepare and file with the Commission a comprehensive report of all future working groups, including topics/disputes discussed, resolutions of disputes/topics discussed, and any unresolved disputes/topics discussed. 
The Company recognizes that the DG RFPs resulting from the 2024 IRP will be the first DG RFPs where DG solar has been permitted to be paired with storage, and there are lessons that will be and need to be learned in those RFPs.  As a result, bidders should be permitted to refresh a solar plus storage bid as a standalone solar bid, if its solar plus storage bid is not selected.

In the 2025 IRP, the Company proposes to acquire 100 MW of DG solar (with or without storage), though two RFPs, one in 2026 and one in 2027, each seeking 50 MWs.  In those RFPs, the Company proposes to permit bidders with bids not selected for the Target List to elect to “buy-down the price of the bid to meet the average Total Net Benefits of the selected portfolio.”  This buy down process will be implemented if there is unmet demand in the CARES DG subscription program.  
The Company also proposes an extended phase of the RFP to allow projects to “be submitted into the RFP at, or below, a price that results in a Total Net Benefit for the additional bid(s) that matches the average Total Net Benefits form the first phase of the RFP.”  But this option would be made available only if there is “an unmet CARES DG customer subscription need after the first phase of the RFP.”  This “buy-down” would allow bidders to “reprice their submission to a price that would equal the total net benefit average of the entire portfolio.  It basically is a form of a bid price refresh . . .  and would allow additional procurements on that part of the competitive RFP.”  (T. 1164:19-23; T. 1165:16-18).
The DG RFPs proposed in this IRP would be the first DG RFPs in which the Company would permit bidders to pair solar resources with storage.  (See e.g., T. 1135:16-22).  There are presently no stand-alone DG solar resources paired with storage on the Company’s system.  (T. 1156:8-14).  As a result, the Company admits that there are lessons that need to be “learned about pairing batteries with DG solar.”  (T. 1135:23-1136:1).  Such lesson to be learned, include “[h]ow the systems can operate and how the company can dispatch them and what their response is like, what that value is offered to the system.”  (T. 1137:9-12).  Thus, both bidders and the Company will have to work together to ensure that the DG RFPs proposed in this IRP, which will permit, for the first time, DG solar to be paired with storage, are successful.  
Accordingly, GSEIA proposes that in current and future DG RFPs in which the Company permits bidders to pair solar plus storage, if a bid that pairs solar plus storage is not selected, the Company should permit the bidder to refresh the bid, without incurring additional bid fees, as a standalone solar (i.e., solar without storage) project. Doing so would help the Company achieve its goal to “run an RFP that’s fair, that allows market participants to put their best projects forward, and that allows the [C]ompany, along with the staff and the IE, to select the portfolio of projects that [the Company] deem[s] [to] provide value to all  . . . customers.”  (T. 1175:13-18).  
The Company admits that there are lessons to be learned about VPP.  As a result, the Company should utilize industry, VPP experts to accelerate its ability to learn such lessons. 

GSEIA’s witness, Ani Bacak, testified regarding the grid and customer benefits that VPPs can provide.  While the Company proposes in the 2025 IRP for a Customer-Site Solar + Storage Pilot Program, that pilot program is not a true VPP program because as Company Witness Robinson Testified: “Yes[, there is a distinction between a VPP, like that discussed by GSEIA Witness Bacak, and Distributed Energy Resource Management System (“DERMS”)] . . . The DERMS that we’re implementing is centralized DERMS.  [VPP] is mostly . . . what are called grid-edge DERMS.”  (T. 647:19-25).  The Company will use a third-party aggregator to administer the pilot program.  But, Company Witness Robinson also acknowledged, in response to a question from Commissioner Echols, that 
[O]ne of the benefits to a centralized DERMS system is that you can interface with grid-edge DERMS system, as you mentioned, those systems like our temp check system. So in the future, you would be able to incorporate those grid-edge DERMS into the overall DERMS system and bring centralized control back to the balancing area as a whole.

(T. 422:13-21).  In other words, the resources in the pilot program will be controlled via an aggregator through a grid-edge DERMS, instead of a centralized DERMS.  (T. 1146:19-25).  And another program that will use a grid-edge DERMS to interface with the Company’s centralized DERMS is the TempCheck Program (T. at 421-422).
The intent of the Customer-Site Solar + Storage Pilot Program is “for the [C]ompany to learn about deployed batteries, either stand-alone or paired with solar . . . as well as customer behavior when dealing with these collocated assets.”  (T. 1148:9-16).  So, there are significant lessons to be learned as the pilot program progresses and as the next IRP approaches.  To that end, there are industry experts, such as Ms. Bacak, who can be a valuable resource to the Company, the Commission, and Commission Staff as the Company collects data and interprets data from its pilot program.  For that reason, GSEIA requests that the Commission direct the Company, between now and the filing of the next IRP, to collaborate and communicate with staff and stakeholders regarding VPP programs in other states as well as the Company’s Customer-Site Solar + Storage Pilot Program to implement best practices and lessons learned in VPP programs proposed by the Company in the next and future IRPs. 
GSEIA’s Recommendations

Based on the evidentiary record as discussed above, GSEIA shows that the following directives should be included in the final order in this docket:
The Company is directed to roll over all MW approved by the Commission in prior IRPs, the 2025 IRP, and future IRPs but unfulfilled in any DG RPFs to DG RFPs and programs for which the Company seeks approval in the 2025 IRP and/or in future IRPs. 
The Company is directed to reconstitute the DG working group with the goal of refining the program guidelines for DG RFPs and DG PPA terms resulting from the 2025 IRP and future IRPs.  Staff, the Company, and stakeholders shall prepare and file with the Commission a comprehensive report of all future working group meetings, including topics/disputes discussed, resolutions of disputes/topics discussed, and any unresolved disputes/topics discussed.  The working group shall be conducted to strive to ensure that the Commission’s policy decisions are effectuated and fulfilled so that the DG solar MWs that the Commission has seen fit in prior and this IRP, and sees fit in future IRPs, for advancement of solar in the State of Georgia, are fully procured. 
In current and future DG RFPs in which the Company permits bidders to pair solar plus storage, if a bid that pairs solar plus storage is not selected, the Company shall permit the bidder to refresh the bid, without incurring additional bid fees, as a standalone solar (i.e., solar without storage) project. 
Between now and the filing of the next IRP, the Company is directed to collaborate and communicate with staff and stakeholders regarding VPP programs in other states as well as the Company’s Customer-Site Solar + Storage Pilot Program to implement best practices and lessons learned in VPP programs proposed by the Company in the next and future IRPs.
