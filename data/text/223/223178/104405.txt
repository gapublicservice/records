MOTION TO EXTEND THE COMMISSION’S PROCEDURAL SCHEDULE TO PRESERVE STATUTORY INTERVENOR RIGHTS
INTRODUCTION
Pursuant to O.C.G.A. §§ 46-2-20, 46-2-59, and 50-13-13, and consistent with the Commission’s obligations under the Georgia Administrative Procedure Act and constitutional due process principles, GA WAND, by and through undersigned counsel, respectfully moves the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) to amend the procedural schedule adopted in its May 20, 2025 Procedural and Scheduling Order in Docket No. 44280. Specifically, Movant requests that the Commission extend the deadline for intervenor direct testimony to no earlier than thirty (30) days after the statutory intervention deadline of June 24, 2025, and reschedule other dependent procedural dates accordingly.
PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND
On May 20, 2025, the Commission issued a Procedural and Scheduling Order initiating a compressed timeline to evaluate the Joint Petitioners’ proposal to extend Georgia Power Company’s Alternate Rate Plan (“ARP”) for an additional three years, thereby suspending the requirement for a general rate case filing due July 1, 2025. The Commission set a deadline of June 12, 2025 for intervenor direct testimony—twelve (12) days before the statutory intervention deadline of June 24, 2025, as calculated from the first public notice date of May 25, 2025.
This compressed schedule deprives prospective intervenors of their statutory right under O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59(c) to meaningfully participate in this contested case. By requiring direct testimony from intervenors before the end of the intervention period granted by Georgia state law, the Commission’s procedural schedule renders the procedural safeguards granted by Georgia law ineffective.
LEGAL BACKGROUND
Under O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59(c), “[a]ny… person desiring to intervene must file an application for leave to intervene within 30 days following the first published notice of the proceeding.” This statutory intervention right is a critical component of the due process protections afforded to affected parties in Commission proceedings. The statute guarantees prospective intervenors a 30-day window after notice is issued to review filings, assess impacts, and determine whether and how to participate.
In this case, however, the Commission has scheduled the deadline for intervenor direct testimony for June 12, 2025, while the last day to file a timely intervention is June 24, 2025. The effect is to:
Require testimony before the end of the intervention period granted to prospective intervenors by law;
Curtail the opportunity for expert analysis or legal preparation;
Deny meaningful participation in the hearing process.
LEGAL ARGUMENT
1. The current procedural schedule violates O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59 and undermines statutory rights under Georgia State law to intervene.
State law entitles intervenors to 30 days after notice publication to decide whether to participate. Yet, under the Commission’s current schedule, the singularly most important and meaningful opportunity for intervenors to participate—through the submission of expert testimony—is set twelve days before that period ends. 
This truncation of time undermines rights granted to intervenors under Georgia state law. The statutory purpose of the 30-day intervention window is to provide parties a reasonable opportunity to evaluate the case and prepare a response. It creates a conflict between a party’s right to take the full time allowed by law and the need to submit meaningful pre-filed testimony. The Commission should not adopt procedures that make compliance with one requirement functionally impossible without forfeiting another.
2. The schedule imposes unreasonable burdens on potential intervenors, violating principles of due process.
In practice, the schedule also puts unreasonable burdens on prospective intervenors by requiring the submission of expert witness testimony just twenty-three (23) days after the Commission’s procedural order and before even the end of the thirty (30) day intervention period granted to intervenors by law. This timeline precludes intervenors from the submission of rigorous expert testimony and contributing to the record meaningfully. For example, intervenors who may be interested in analyzing why Georgia Power customers are not seeing a net reduction in rates after Georgia Power’s own experts continually testified that new large loads on their system were putting “downward pressure on rates” throughout the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan proceeding in Docket Nos. 56002 and 56003, likely do not have the time to perform such an analysis due to the truncated procedural timeline, and intervenors whom have exercised their right under Georgia state law to intervene within the thirty-day period and after the testimony deadline have certainly been prevented from analyzing the details of the agreement to extend the ARP. The purpose of this motion is to provide additional time not just for GA WAND, but also all other intervenors, to be able to have a more meaningful opportunity to participate in the Commission’s decision-making process.
3. No exigency justifies rushing a major policy decision without stakeholder input.
The Joint Petitioners request to forgo a general rate case for three more years—an action with long-term financial implications for ratepayers and structural consequences for Commission oversight. This is not a routine procedural action, but a substantial deviation from the default requirement of a general rate case.
A fair process to evaluate that request should not be sacrificed for speed. Extending the schedule by one month would allow more robust participation without violating parties’ procedural rights, jeopardizing Commission authority, or substantially delaying final resolution.
REQUESTED RELIEF
WHEREFORE, for the foregoing reasons, Movant respectfully requests that the Commission:
Amend its May 20, 2025 Procedural and Scheduling Order to set the deadline for intervenor direct testimony no earlier than July 24, 2025, or at least 30 days after the statutory intervention deadline of June 24, 2025;
Adjust other deadlines (including rebuttal testimony and the Commission’s hearing dates) to accommodate this change;
Grant such other and further relief as the Commission deems just and proper.
