INTRODUCTION
On January 31, 2024, the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) entered its Order Adopting Stipulation (the “Vogtle Prudence Order”) in Docket No. 29849, Georgia Power Company’s Application to Adjust Rates to Include Reasonable and Prudent Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 Costs (the “Vogtle Prudence Proceeding”). In the Vogtle Prudence Order, the Commission adopted a Stipulation (the “Vogtle Prudence Stipulation”), which, among other things, required Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) to “propose and support in the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) and Demand Side Management (“DSM”) Certification dockets a base case of DSM performance savings targets of at least .75% of annual retail sales.”
On January 31, 2025, Georgia Power filed its 2025 IRP, as well as its Application for the Certification, Decertification, and Amended DSM Plan (“DSM Application”), in Docket Nos. 56002 and 56003, respectively. Pursuant to the plain terms of the Vogtle Prudence Order, the Company’s DSM proposed case incorporated the 0.75% DSM savings target (the “Proposed Case”) and the Company supported the Proposed Case throughout the course of the 2025 IRP proceeding. On July 9, 2025, the Public Interest Advocacy Staff (“PIA Staff”) filed a Stipulation between the Company and PIA Staff that is intended to resolve all issues in the 2025 IRP and DSM Certification dockets (the “2025 IRP Stipulation”).
On July 10, 2025, Georgia Interfaith Power & Light (“GIPL”) and Southface Energy Institute (“Southface,” and collectively, “GIPL/Southface”) filed a Motion to Enforce the Vogtle Settlement, alleging that the 2025 IRP Stipulation “supports the wholesale rejection of [the DSM performance savings] target,” and that “[b]y signing a stipulation counseling against the Vogtle DSM terms, Georgia Power has abandoned its ‘support’ of its proposed case, in egregious violation of the Vogtle Stipulation.” As explained more fully below, Georgia Power fulfilled its obligations under the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation and the Company’s commitment to that obligation has provided benefits through the DSM plan proposed for approval in the 2025 IRP Stipulation. GIPL/Southface’s purported expectations about the interplay between the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation and the 2025 IRP Stipulation are unrealistic and unsupported by the plain language of the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation, and their allegations are completely without merit. The Commission should accordingly deny GIPL/Southface’s motion.
ARGUMENT
The Company fulfilled the plain terms of the Vogtle Prudence Settlement.
Item 15 of the Prudence Stipulation required Georgia Power Company to “propose and support” in the 2025 IRP and DSM Certification dockets a base case of DSM performance savings targets of at least 0.75% of annual retail sales. GIPL/Southface argue that, by signing the 2025 IRP Stipulation, Georgia Power has somehow failed to support its own Proposed Case, abandoned its support for the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation, and “brazenly violate[d]” a Commission order.
On the contrary, the Company both proposed the 0.75% DSM savings target and supported that same target throughout the IRP proceeding. The 0.75% DSM savings target was part of the Company’s Proposed Case, which was included in the Company’s initial filing. The Company also thoroughly supported its Proposed Case throughout discovery, answering multiple questions about the Proposed Case and the impact of a 0.75% DSM savings target. In direct and rebuttal testimony and respective hearings, the Company affirmed and reaffirmed its support for the Proposed Case.
There is no question that a 0.75% DSM savings target was proposed in the 2025 IRP and that the Company has supported that savings target throughout the course of the proceeding. Therefore, it is indisputable that the Company fulfilled the plain terms of the Vogtle Prudence Settlement. 
The requirement that Georgia Power Company “propose and support” a base case of DSM performance savings targets of at least 0.75% of annual retail sales does not prohibit Georgia Power from compromising in settlement.
Despite the ample evidence of the Company’s adherence to and fulfillment of the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation and Order, GIPL/Southface contort the plain meanings of “propose” and “support” to argue that the Company somehow missed its mark because the 0.75% DSM savings target was not included in the final version of the 2025 IRP Stipulation. “Propose” is defined as “to form or put forward a plan or intention.” “Support” is defined as “to promote the interests or cause of, to uphold or defend as valid or right, to argue or vote for.” Yet nowhere in either of these definitions or in the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation is there justification to interpret  “propose” and “support” to mean that the Company cannot reach reasonable compromises for DSM, or any other matter the Company supported, through the constructive settlement process that has been the hallmark of IRPs over the past several decades.
It is undisputed that the Company formed and put forward a plan—i.e., proposed—a DSM case that included a 0.75% DSM savings target. Additionally, throughout discovery, hearings, and briefing, the Company promoted a 0.75% DSM savings target, upheld and defended the 0.75% savings target, and argued for adoption of the 0.75% DSM savings target—i.e., the Company supported the DSM savings target. 
Notably, the Company proposes and supports all aspects of its case throughout the course of an IRP proceeding. However, not all of those aspects survive the give-and-take of settlement negotiations. Yet GIPL/Southface would have this Commission believe that any aspects of the Company’s case that are not adopted in a stipulation were somehow abandoned by the Company. Take, for example, the Company’s proposed hydro modernization projects, for which the Company has fought since the 2019 IRP. The continuation of this program, like the 0.75% DSM savings target, was proposed in the Company’s initial 2025 IRP filing and supported throughout the course of the 2025 IRP proceeding. The Company similarly proposed unit uprates at Plant Hatch and supported that proposal throughout the course of the 2025 IRP proceeding. Neither of these proposals were adopted as initially filed and supported by the Company in the 2025 IRP Stipulation, and yet, no party that participated in the 2025 IRP could make a reasonable argument that the Company abandoned or failed to propose or support those aspects of the Company’s case. 
What GIPL/Southface seek in their motion and claim to have been their “expectation” when agreeing to the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation—an unyielding position and refusal to compromise on the issue—is patently unreasonable and runs contrary to the nature of constructive settlement, which requires that parties compromise in the interests of reaching a mutually acceptable outcome. Taking GIPL/Southface’s suggestion to its logical conclusion, the Company could never support any stipulation or agree to anything less than what the Company initially proposed. That outcome makes no sense, runs contrary to the constructive regulatory environment in Georgia, and ignores the benefits of the constructive settlements the Commission has routinely adopted in IRP cases, which have provided certainty, compromise, and fairness to all parties. Georgia Power would not have agreed to the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation had it prohibited Georgia Power from entering a stipulation in this proceeding. Accordingly, the Commission should reject GIPL/Southface’s unreasonable position.
The 0.75% DSM savings target from the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation has already benefitted all customers.
Parties to the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation negotiated and agreed to, among many other items, the 0.75% DSM savings target. Because of that settled position in the Vogtle prudence proceeding, the Company filed its 2025 IRP with a Proposed Case that included a significantly increased DSM savings target compared to prior IRPs, and which contained hundreds of millions of dollars of upward pressure on rates. Consistent with the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation, the Company proposed this case and supported it throughout the 2025 IRP proceeding. Absent the inclusion of the 0.75% savings target in the Vogtle Prudence Proceeding, the Company likely would have filed the 2025 IRP with a significantly lower DSM savings target that put less upward pressure on rates. In other words, parties advocating for higher DSM savings targets received substantial benefit from the Vogtle Prudence Proceeding because the Company proposed the significant 0.75% DSM savings target, which had the beneficial effect of raising the scale of the DSM plan that was to be evaluated by other Parties in the proceeding. 
The nature of settlement agreements in IRPs has resulted in compromise positions on the DSM plan, with PIA Staff often serving as a primary party in proposing revisions and alterations to the Company’s Plans. The same process of compromise took place in this case. Ultimately, the final, negotiated DSM savings target of 0.51%, while different from the 0.75% that GIPL/Southface strenuously demand, still results in one of the highest DSM savings targets to be approved in recent history, and certainly better than what may have occurred if the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation had not been approved by this Commission. To illustrate, the previous 2022 IRP involved a Company-proposed DSM portfolio with energy savings of approximately 433,000 MWh, and a Staff-proposed DSM portfolio with energy savings of approximately 430,000 MWh. Likewise in the 2019 IRP, both Company and Staff proposed portfolios that would have resulted in about 375,000 MWh of energy savings. Both cases were resolved with settlement positions that were a compromise between the Company and Staff positions, resulting in an amount of energy savings somewhere in between the litigated positions.
In contrast here, and as acknowledged by GIPL/Southface in their motion, the Company’s position in the 2025 IRP (which accounts for the 0.75% DSM savings target) would result in energy savings of approximately 741,000 MWh—nearly twice as much as the energy savings in 2019 and more than one and a half times as much as the energy savings in 2022. The settled position in this case, which accounts for the lower savings targets in Staff’s litigated position, likewise would result in an amount of energy savings of approximately 503,000 MWh—higher than any of the energy savings in either of the previous IRPs. By negotiating a 0.75% DSM savings target in the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation, and due to the Company’s proposal and support of this DSM savings target during the IRP proceeding, parties to the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation ensured that DSM savings in this case were higher than they have ever been in the last two cases. Put simply, GIPL/Southface and all Georgia Power customers have benefitted immensely from the 0.75% DSM savings target.
CONCLUSION
The position taken by GIPL/Southface in their Motion to Enforce the Vogtle Settlement is unreasonable, illogical, and not supported by the plain meaning of the words used in the Stipulation. It is the responsibility of the Intervenor, not the Company, to ensure that the Intervenor understands the terms of the stipulation they are signing, and to clarify positions if they are unclear. In this case, the plain and direct meaning of “propose and support” requires no additional clarification, and the Commission should reject any attempt by GIPL/Southface to inject uncertainty into the plain meaning of these words. Georgia Power fulfilled its obligations under the Vogtle Prudence Stipulation and no reasonable interpretation of that document could determine otherwise. For the reasons set forth above, the Commission should deny GIPL/Southface’s motion. 
Respectfully submitted, this 14th day of July 2025.
