SOUTHERN RENEWABLE ENERGY ASSOCIATION
APPLICATION FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE 

The Southern Renewable Energy Association (“SREA”) hereby files this Application for Leave to Intervene (“Application”) in the above-referenced dockets pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59 and Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) Rule 515-2-1-.06.  In support hereof, SREA respectfully states as follows:
On July 30, 2025, Georgia Power filed an Application for the Certification of the 2029-2031 All-Source Capacity generating resources requesting that the Commission issue an order granting a certificate of public convenience and necessity (“Certificate”) for the selected generation resources. On July 30, 2025, Georgia Power filed an Application for the Certification of Supplemental Resources to be added in the years 2028-2031, representing capacity potentially available outside of the 2029-2031 All-Source Capacity RFP process. On August 5, 2025, the Commission issued a Procedural and Scheduling Order (“PSO”) finding that “the All-Source and Supplemental Resources dockets will be considered concurrently.”
SREA is a non-profit industry-led initiative that works to promote the responsible development and use of utility-scale wind energy, energy storage, solar energy, and transmission throughout the South, including in Alabama, Arkansas, Georgia, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, and Tennessee. ​SREA members have an interest in the proceeding because they include businesses that develop, own, and operate facilities that generate and produce energy and capacity.  SREA’s members also include customers that desire to have more access to clean and renewable sources of energy and capacity.  A major focus of SREA’s regulatory work includes participating in regulatory proceedings in the South, including in Georgia.  SREA has an interest in ensuring that renewable energy and storage are given fair and factually based consideration in procurement processes used by public utilities, including Georgia Power. As a result, SREA has substantial interests in the outcome of this proceeding and desires to intervene in order to advance and protect those interests. 
In compliance with the first of the intervention application requirements adopted by the Commission in the PSO, there are currently no other intervening parties with similar interests to SREA. Still, SREA anticipates that our specific interests in the development of utility-scale solar energy, wind energy, energy storage, hybrid resources, and transmission are not and will not be adequately represented by the other pending intervenors in this docket.  Further, SREA’s participation in this matter will not unduly delay these proceedings or hinder the rights of other parties. 
Pursuant to the second intervention application requirement set forth in the PSO, SREA states its present intention to submit direct testimony by expert witness Miriam Makhyoun, CEO of EQ Research. Broadly speaking, the testimony of SREA’s expert witness will address subjects including the evaluation of renewable energy and storage, Georgia Power’s self-ownership of proposed resources, and best practices from utilities in other jurisdictions.
