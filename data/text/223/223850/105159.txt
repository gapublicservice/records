===== DKT 56298 & 56310 All-Source RFP & Supp Res PD/STF-PIA-5-12.docx =====
STF-PIA-5-12

Question: 

Referring to the Company’s Main Panel Rebuttal Testimony in Docket No. 56002 & 56003, Exhibit 1, section 7.1.5, please define “Timeline Review” and explain:
a. What are the costs associated with completing a Timeline Review? 
b. What portion of these costs is the prospective customer responsible for paying? 
c. What information is the customer required to provide in order to complete a Timeline Review? 
d. How long does it typically take to begin a Timeline Review either after the customer has expressed interest in becoming a Georgia Power customer (if no Transmission Study has been completed) or after a Transmission Study has been performed?
e. How long does it typically take to perform a Timeline Review?

Response:

Timeline Review is the Company’s evaluation of an implementation timeline to determine the solution along with the scope, design, and construction of the required transmission and distribution infrastructure to accommodate a customer’s requested load ramp. The Timeline Review process takes into consideration outage coordination, land acquisition, and material and labor availability necessary to implement the customer’s request. The Timeline Review is performed after the Transmission Study provides the method of service. 

The Timeline Review is included in the $100,000 Planning and Study fee referenced in STF-PIA-5-11. The costs associated with Timeline Review include but are not limited to utilizing necessary staff to review planning study output, vetting high-level solutions for electrical service, and creating high-level schedules based on material, constructability, outage coordination, and other necessary activities to complete the project.

Prospective customers that are 100 MW or greater pay 100% of the costs associated with Timeline Review. Prospective customers that are less than 100 MW do not pay the costs associated with Timeline Review.

Customer information required to complete a Timeline Review includes, but is not limited to, annual customer load ramp with peak metered load, preferred service voltage, a high-level engineering description of on-site electrical service requirements, and a requested date for service.

Timeline Review is conducted only after the completion of the Transmission Study and may commence within one business week thereafter.

The Timeline Review process typically requires 4 to 8 weeks, depending on project size, complexity of system impacts, and the volume of requests.

STF-PIA-5-11

Question: 

Referring to the Company’s Main Panel Rebuttal Testimony in Docket No. 56002 & 56003, Exhibit 1, section 7.1.5, please define “Transmission Study” and explain:
a. What are the costs associated with completing a Transmission Study?
b. What portion of these costs is the prospective customer responsible for paying?
c. What information is the customer required to provide in order to complete a Transmission Study?
d. How long does it typically take to begin a Transmission Study after the customer has expressed interest in becoming a Georgia Power customer?
e. How long does it typically take to perform a Transmission Study?

Response:

Refer to Sections A.3 and A.4 in Technical Appendix Volume 3 of Georgia Power’s 2025 Integrated Resource Plan filing, Docket No. 56002, for a definition of Transmission Study.

Prospective customers pay a $100,000 Planning and Study fee for customer requests 100 MW or greater, which covers the entire cost of the Transmission Study and other costs associated with the Company’s initial evaluation of the request (e.g. Timeline Review). The costs specific to a Transmission Study vary depending on the complexity of the request to serve a prospective customer’s load and the impacts that service may have on the grid. 

See the Company’s response to subpart (a) for customer requests 100 MW or greater. Customer requests under 100 MW do not pay the costs for a Transmission Study.

The Company requests that a prospective customer provide the project location, an annual or quarterly load ramp, power factor, type of load, and requested in-service date in order to complete a Transmission Study. This information is collected through an intake form provided to all prospective customers seeking electric service from Georgia Power. See the Company’s response to STF-PIA-2-1 in Docket Nos. 56298 & 56310 for customer intake form submissions received since January 2025.

A Transmission Study will typically begin 30-90 days after the customer has expressed interest in becoming a Georgia Power customer, subject to the prospective customer’s timely submission of all project intake requirements, which is the first step before the Company can begin any evaluation of the request.

As stated in the Company’s response to subpart (a), a Transmission Study can vary in complexity, which affects the time required to complete the study. Typically, a Transmission Study will take a minimum of 6-8 weeks.
