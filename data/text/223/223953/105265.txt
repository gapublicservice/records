Question: 

Does Georgia Power track service quality measures related to its call center customer service? If so, please identify the measures.

Response:

Georgia Power employs a comprehensive approach to monitoring and reviewing calls from a quality perspective to ensure exceptional customer service. This involves tracking several key performance indicators, with the main evaluation being the Quality Assurance Scores, which are derived from evaluations of call recordings. These evaluations assess whether representatives adhere to Company standards in communication, problem-solving, and overall service delivery, with a quality expectation goal of 85% or higher on all calls. By reviewing these calls, Georgia Power can identify training opportunities, recognize exemplary performance, and ensure compliance with service protocols. This quality monitoring process not only helps with maintaining high service standards but also aids in continuously improving customer experience by addressing any identified gaps and implementing best practices across the call center.

STF-PIA-34-13

Question: 

What role does DSM play in informing rate design at GPC?

Response:

Demand side management (“DSM”) is a key concept in rate design. Sending cost causal pricing signals encourages efficient system utilization. For example, time-of-use rates encourage customers to use energy during off-peak times, demand charges encourage managing peak usage, and hours use demand rates encourage higher load factors.

April 2023: Call Back Assist (CBA)
Description: Implemented a feature allowing customers to request a callback if wait times exceed a certain threshold.
Successes: Reduced customer frustration and decreased call abandonment rates, leading to improved customer satisfaction. After Call Surveys for CBA and our research shows that when customers engage with CBA, they rate the agents higher.
Challenges: Initial technical challenges in integrating with existing call center infrastructure led to some delays in full-scale implementation.

February 2024: Service Order Texts
Description: Customers automatically receive text updates for service order requests.
Successes: Enhanced customer communication and transparency, resulting in higher customer engagement.

March 2024: Mobile App Push Notifications for Billing Alerts
Description: Implemented push notifications for billing alerts through the mobile app.
Successes: Increased customer awareness Challenges: Minor issues with notification settings not syncing correctly for some users.

June 2024: Virtual Assistant for Self-Service
Description: Launched a virtual assistant for self-service options like payment arrangements and outage reporting.
Successes: Increased efficiency in handling routine inquiries, freeing up agents for more complex issues.
Challenges: Initial limitations in the virtual assistant's ability to handle nuanced customer queries.
July 2024: Avaya Oceana Workspaces Migration
Description: Migrated from eGain to Avaya Oceana Workspaces for email routing. Email routing is a system that directs emails to the most appropriate agent based on customer context, business rules, and agent skills.
Successes: Improved routing efficiency and agent productivity.
Challenges: Transition period required additional training for agents to adapt to the new system.

July 2024: Text to PAY
Description: Introduced a text-based payment option for customers with a saved bank account.
Successes: Simplified payment process..

August 2024: Mobile App Push Notifications for Outage Alerts
Description: Added outage alerts to mobile app push notifications.
Successes: Improved customer awareness and reduced inbound calls during outages.

September 2024: Avaya Experience Portal 8.0 Upgrade
Description: The Voice Response Unit (“VRU”) is an automated phone system that interacts with calls using pre-recorded or computer-generated speech to provide information, collect relevant details, and route calls to the correct department/consultant. Upgraded to a new version of the application supporting the VRU.
Successes: Enhanced system stability and performance.

September 2024: Lightico Application Launch
Description: To establish service, customers may be required to provide documentation that contains personal identifiable information. The Company implemented an automated method for customers to send such information through an application channel that streamlines the process.
Successes: Improved security and efficiency in handling sensitive customer documents while enhancing customer experience.
Challenges: Limited initial adoption due to agent unfamiliarity with the process.


October 2024: Agency Pledge VRU Personalization
Description: An agency pledge is an agreement made by a social service agency or charity to pay all or part of a customer’s bill. VRU now announces agency pledges on customer accounts. 
Successes: Preempts the need to speak to a live agent.

October 2024: Sprout Social Media Deployment
Description: Replaced Sprinklr with Sprout for social media management.
Successes: Improved social media engagement and monitoring capabilities.

December 2024: VRU After Call Survey
Description: Introduced after-call surveys for business and Spanish-speaking customers.
Successes: Gathered valuable feedback to improve customer service.
Challenges: Response rates varied, requiring adjustments to survey delivery methods.

December 2024: VRU Text Message Offer for Service Order Calls
Description: Offered text message links for online service order completion.
Successes: Reduced live agent calls and improved customer convenience.

Question: 

Please share what customer surveys GPC has performed in the past 3 years. Provide results as well as statistical analysis of the Customer Service Satisfaction surveys. If no surveys were performed, please explain.

Response:

Georgia Power employs a multi-pronged approach to customer feedback, leveraging both industry-standard benchmarks from syndicated studies and internal survey tools to directly poll customers about their experiences and sentiment. Some of those surveys include those that measure customer satisfaction, loyalty, product satisfaction, channel satisfaction, and brand image.

For its customer satisfaction corporate goals, Georgia Power leverages the national J.D. Power Electric Utility Customer Satisfaction Index for residential and general business benchmarks and Net Promoter Score (“NPS”) loyalty surveys for large commercial & industrial customers with an assigned account manager.   

J.D. Power Electric Utility Customer Satisfaction Study

Georgia Power subscribes to and is included in J.D. Power’s Electric Utility Customer Satisfaction Studies for both residential and business customers. These studies assess satisfaction across eight dimensions of Price/Cost, Safety & Reliability, Trust, Ease of Doing Business, Digital Channels, People, Problem Solving, and Information Provided (Communications).

Performance: Scores are reported on a 1,000-point scale and the results are included as a portion of the Company’s operational metrics. See the table below for 2022 through 2024 results. Managed Account Net Promoter Score® (MANPS) Surveys

Managed accounts are surveyed using the NPS methodology. This methodology measures loyalty by asking customers in this segment to imagine if they could choose their electric utility, how likely would they be to recommend Georgia Power to a friend or colleague, with a 0 being would not at all recommend to a 10 being would most definitely would recommend. Scores are determined by taking the percent of customers who gave a 9 or 10 rating and subtracting the percent of customers who provided a 0 – 6 rating and multiplying by 100. ((% 9s and 10s) - (% 6s and below)) x100. Ratings of a 7 and 8 are discarded.   

Performance: NPS scores are tracked quarterly. See the table below for 2022 through 2024 results. Through the second quarter of 2025, Georgia Power’s score was 75 (scale of -100 to 100), which is considered outstanding based on marketing research standards applied across most industries.

Other Surveys Conducted: 

Customer Experience “Companion Surveys”
These internal surveys complement J.D. Power results and are used to assess overall satisfaction, trust, and ease of doing business, as well as including questions about certain experiential factors such as customer contact channels used, outages experienced, and communications awareness. They are administered quarterly for both residential and business customers.

Transactional Surveys
These are used for specific customer interactions to help understand the customer experience when engaging with Georgia Power. Transactional surveys are short, targeted surveys administered immediately after a customer interaction. They are designed to measure satisfaction with specific touchpoints such as agent-managed service calls, self-service IVR transactions, and digital engagement experiences. They include measures such as likelihood to recommend the transaction channel, satisfaction with the experience, and effort. Additional transactional surveys include those for Post-Grid Improvement (survey sent to customers impacted by the Grid Improvement Program), Georgia Power Land Right-of-Way (“ROW”) (survey where the Company has purchased land for ROW), Georgia Power Property Managers (survey of property managers’ experience with their portal).  

Product Satisfaction Surveys
•Georgia Power gathers customer feedback through surveys related to their enrollment and installation experiences across various regulated and unregulated programs and solutions. While many of these surveys are ongoing, some are conducted as one-time opportunities to gather targeted insights. The surveys are typically short and focus on satisfaction, experience, and NPS ratings. Some programs that are surveyed include: Solar Installation, Small Commercial Direct Install, EV Charger Rebate Program Participation, Electric Transportation Make Ready Program Participation, AllConnect, and Vivint.

Image and Brand
•Image and brand surveys are conducted annually and include focus groups of 20-30 customers as well as a quantitative study of 2,000 customers to gauge customer sentiment and awareness around Georgia Power brand perception, customer needs, messaging impact, etc.
