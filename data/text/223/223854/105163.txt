STF-PIA-4-20

Question: 

Term Equalization Component 
Did the Company also evaluate the bids without a term equalization component to isolate the impact of the term equalization component? If so, what were the Company’s findings?

Please explain the rationale and derivation of the term equalization component in the years after a system need occurs, but before a resource is available for acquisition.

Does the Company believe the term equalization ($/kW-yr) value compares reasonably closely to the bid price of market alternatives? If the Company expects the term equalization value to be somewhat higher or lower than the bid price of market alternatives, please explain the reason it could be higher or lower.

Please explain the reason the Company believes it is reasonable to use a term equalization component following the end of the life of various resources that were bid in to the RFP.

Did the Company evaluate basing the term equalization value on the ECC cost of a CT? If so, please provide the ranking results and the workpapers associated with that evaluation. If not, explain why not.

Has the Company always based the term equalization value on the ECC cost of a CC? If not, explain why previously the Company did not base the term equalization value on the ECC cost of a CC.

Explain why the Company believes it would be reasonable for the term equalization value to be negative in any years?

Does the Company believe that energy benefits could exceed the total revenue requirements of a new CC over the life of the unit in the Company’s economic analysis?

Response:

The Company did not conduct any comparative total net cost analyses or rankings excluding term equalization; however, the term equalization costs are categorized separately. Please refer to the ranking files in STF-PIA-1-1 Attachment A TRADE SECRET, folder 2 – Evaluation, subfolders (a) through (c), for the individual cost components including term equalization.

Georgia Power uses term equalization at the front end is used to reflect the value that resources provide based on their start dates. For instance, when the Company anticipates a capacity shortage, a resource scheduled to begin later cannot address immediate needs and is therefore considered less valuable than one starting sooner. To measure these cost differences, the Company projects capacity costs for the periods before resources come online. The Company, along with the Staff and the Independent Evaluator (“IE”), decided to use the economic carrying cost (“ECC”)—including energy benefits—of a generic combined cycle (“CC”) unit as the projected capacity cost for term equalization purposes. 

The term equalization approach and the corresponding capacity cost estimates were collaboratively determined by the Company, Staff, and the IE in November 2024, prior to the start of the evaluation process. Setting these modeling assumptions—such as term equalization—before evaluation begins is essential to maintain the fairness and credibility of the request for proposals (“RFP”). When the term equalization method was selected for the 2029-2031 All-Source RFP, the Company believed that the $/kW-yr value reflected a reasonable estimate of costs for market-based alternative capacity. However, since the average capacity costs for RFP and Supplemental Resources are higher than the term equalization values set in November 2024, the Company now expects that market alternative capacity costs will exceed those initial projections.

Georgia Power uses term equalization at the back end to reflect the value that resources provide based on their term length or asset life. For instance, a resource that exists longer to serve capacity needs is considered more valuable than one with a shorter term or asset life. To measure these cost differences, the Company projects capacity costs for the periods after resources end. The Company, along with the Staff and the IE, decided to use the ECC of a CC—including energy benefits—as the projected capacity cost for term equalization purposes. 

The Company did not evaluate RFP or Supplemental Resources using the ECC of a combustion turbine (“CT”) for term equalization costs. Prior to the evaluation, the Company assessed projected capacity costs using generic CC, CT, and battery energy storage resources. The generic CC was determined to provide the lowest cost capacity – including energy benefits – and was selected to be used in the term equalization approach for the evaluation. The term equalization approach and the corresponding capacity cost estimates were collaboratively determined by the Company, Staff, and the IE in November 2024, prior to the start of the evaluation process. Determining the evaluation methodology in consultation and agreement with Staff and the IE in advance is consistent with how the Company conducts all of its RFPs.

Historically, the Company has aimed to base term equalization on the resource projected to provide the lowest cost capacity, which has not always been a CC. This strategy helps reduce the cost differential that could otherwise disadvantage shorter-term resources in the evaluation process. Influencing factors include shifts in energy demand, fuel price forecasts, technological progress, and changing environmental regulations—all of which have made the energy benefits of CCs more pronounced compared to CTs. At the start of this RFP, the ECC, including energy benefits, for combined cycle resources was estimated to be lower than for other technologies under consideration.

Term equalization costs represent the expense of securing replacement capacity—including energy benefits—during times when a resource is unavailable in the evaluation period. If the annual energy benefit from the replacement resource exceeds its annual cost, this results in a net benefit or negative cost. Thus, it is reasonable for a shorter-term resource to be more quickly succeeded by an alternative that offers greater energy benefits, leading to a negative term equalization value.

While economic or regulatory changes in the future could result in higher energy benefits than those reflected in the current scenarios, the Company’s analysis found that, for all new CC resources considered for certification, the total net present value of energy benefits would not surpass the total net present value of their revenuerequirements. However, resource energy benefits are not a function of the resource revenue requirements, and a situation could arise where the energy benefits of a resource exceed the total revenue requirements over the life of the resource.
