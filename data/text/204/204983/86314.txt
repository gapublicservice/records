       RE: Georgia Power Company's Income Qualified Discount Opportunities

Dear Mr. Wooldridge:

        Please accept the following recommendations regarding the expansion of income
qualified discount opportunities by Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or “the
Company”). These comments are submitted on behalf of the Southern Environmental Law
Center (“SELC”), which represents Georgia Interfaith Power & Light, and on behalf of the City
of Savannah, City of Atlanta, City of Decatur, DeKalb County, and the Athens-Clarke County
Unified Government (collectively, “the Georgia Coalition of Local Governments”), and on
behalf of Georgia Watch. As interested parties in the 2022 Georgia Power Company Rate Case,
we appreciate the opportunity to comment.

        We appreciate Georgia Power and the Georgia Public Service Commission
(“Commission”) carefully considering the expansion of the income qualified discount program to
serve more Georgians in addition to the seniors who are currently served. An expanded income
qualified discount program will help working families, seniors, and disabled customers across
Georgia adjust more gradually to coming shocks from recently-approved rate increases from
excess fuel costs and anticipated increases when Vogtle Units 3 and 4 are placed in service.

         We provide the below recommendations with the full awareness that any ratepayer-
funded program will, by definition, result in upward pressure on rates for non-participants.
However, we believe that it is in the best interest of all Georgians to take reasonable measures to
protect the health and safety of our neighbors, relatives, and employees. It is neither unique nor
new for Georgia Power to shift costs between customers when the Commission views such shifts
to be in the best interest of ratepayers and Georgia as a whole. Recently, for example, customers
have been paying significant inter-generational cost shifts, as customers’ bills have increased to
fund the cost of constructing the Vogtle nuclear plant, regardless of those customers’ expectations
of still being a customer when the plants come online in the future. See also 2022 Georgia Power
Rate Case, Docket No. 44280, Direct Testimony of Georgia Interfaith Power and Light, Justin
Barnes, p. 12-18 (Nov. 3, 2022).

       Further, we support ratepayer-funded income qualified discount programs in the
extraordinary current context of rapidly rising rates:

      •    In 2022, approximately 10% of residential accounts were disconnected for nonpayment
           of bills. For context, the Congressional Research Service estimates that across the country
           approximately 1% of residential households are disconnected each year. See
           Congressional Research Service, “Electric Utility Disconnections” (Jan. 31, 2023),
           available at https://crsreports.congress.gov/product/pdf/R/R47417/2.

      •    From January 2023 to January 2025, we estimate that rates will increase approximately
           $45 per month for the average residential customer, which will only exacerbate the
           disconnection crisis.

Finally, we acknowledge that we have not had the ability to conduct an expert evaluation of each
recommendation, however, we also recognize that Georgia Power is in the best position—with
access to information and modeling—to conduct such analysis.


Recommendations
        We ask Georgia Power to evaluate the below options, listed in order of preference, using
the following principles:
      •    Principle 1: The costs for these programs should be borne by all customer classes. The
           protection of the health and safety of customers classified as low-income is in the best
           interest of all Georgians.
      •    Principle 2: No program should result in more than a $1 per month increase to the
           average residential bill, or the proportional increase to commercial or industrial bills. This
           recommendation aligns with the recommendation of Georgia Watch in 2019.
      •    Principle 3: All newly enrolled participants who own their residence must sign up for an
           energy efficiency assessment through Energy Assistance for Savings & Efficiency
           (“EASE”). However, eligibility for the low-income discount programs should not depend
           on a participants’ ability to receive energy efficiency services or upgrades.

       RE: Georgia Power Company's Income Qualified Discount Opportunities

Dear Mr. Wooldridge:

        Please accept the following recommendations regarding the expansion of income
qualified discount opportunities by Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or “the
Company”). These comments are submitted on behalf of the Southern Environmental Law
Center (“SELC”), which represents Georgia Interfaith Power & Light, and on behalf of the City
of Savannah, City of Atlanta, City of Decatur, DeKalb County, and the Athens-Clarke County
Unified Government (collectively, “the Georgia Coalition of Local Governments”), and on
behalf of Georgia Watch. As interested parties in the 2022 Georgia Power Company Rate Case,
we appreciate the opportunity to comment.

        We appreciate Georgia Power and the Georgia Public Service Commission
(“Commission”) carefully considering the expansion of the income qualified discount program to
serve more Georgians in addition to the seniors who are currently served. An expanded income
qualified discount program will help working families, seniors, and disabled customers across
Georgia adjust more gradually to coming shocks from recently-approved rate increases from
excess fuel costs and anticipated increases when Vogtle Units 3 and 4 are placed in service.

         We provide the below recommendations with the full awareness that any ratepayer-
funded program will, by definition, result in upward pressure on rates for non-participants.
However, we believe that it is in the best interest of all Georgians to take reasonable measures to
protect the health and safety of our neighbors, relatives, and employees. It is neither unique nor
new for Georgia Power to shift costs between customers when the Commission views such shifts
to be in the best interest of ratepayers and Georgia as a whole. Recently, for example, customers
have been paying significant inter-generational cost shifts, as customers’ bills have increased to
fund the cost of constructing the Vogtle nuclear plant, regardless of those customers’ expectations
of still being a customer when the plants come online in the future. See also 2022 Georgia Power
Rate Case, Docket No. 44280, Direct Testimony of Georgia Interfaith Power and Light, Justin
Barnes, p. 12-18 (Nov. 3, 2022).

       Further, we support ratepayer-funded income qualified discount programs in the
extraordinary current context of rapidly rising rates:

      •    In 2022, approximately 10% of residential accounts were disconnected for nonpayment
           of bills. For context, the Congressional Research Service estimates that across the country
           approximately 1% of residential households are disconnected each year. See
           Congressional Research Service, “Electric Utility Disconnections” (Jan. 31, 2023),
           available at https://crsreports.congress.gov/product/pdf/R/R47417/2.

      •    From January 2023 to January 2025, we estimate that rates will increase approximately
           $45 per month for the average residential customer, which will only exacerbate the
           disconnection crisis.

Finally, we acknowledge that we have not had the ability to conduct an expert evaluation of each
recommendation, however, we also recognize that Georgia Power is in the best position—with
access to information and modeling—to conduct such analysis.


Recommendations
        We ask Georgia Power to evaluate the below options, listed in order of preference, using
the following principles:
      •    Principle 1: The costs for these programs should be borne by all customer classes. The
           protection of the health and safety of customers classified as low-income is in the best
           interest of all Georgians.
      •    Principle 2: No program should result in more than a $1 per month increase to the
           average residential bill, or the proportional increase to commercial or industrial bills. This
           recommendation aligns with the recommendation of Georgia Watch in 2019.
      •    Principle 3: All newly enrolled participants who own their residence must sign up for an
           energy efficiency assessment through Energy Assistance for Savings & Efficiency
           (“EASE”). However, eligibility for the low-income discount programs should not depend
           on a participants’ ability to receive energy efficiency services or upgrades.
