COMMENTS OF
AMERICANS FOR AFFORDABLE CLEAN ENERGY
ON GEORGIA POWER COMPANY’S COMMUNITY CHARGING PLAN

Comes now, AMERCANS FOR AFFORDABLE CLEAN ENERGY (“AACE”), an Intervenor in the above styled docket and pursuant to the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (“Commission”) Order Adopting Settlement Agreement as Modified, files these Comments on Georgia Power Company’s (the “Company” or “Georgia Power”) Community Charging Plan (the “Plan”), showing the Commission, as follows:
AACE intervened in the above styled Docket to address issues related to electric vehicle (“EV”) charger deployment. AACE members own and operate 784 convenience stores, public travel facilities and truck stops in Georgia at which they provide goods and services, primarily consisting of retail consumer goods, vehicle repair and service products, food, and fuel to the traveling public and at which they desire to provide EV charging.  AACE signed the Settlement Agreement entered between the Company and the Commission’s Staff.  
The Electric Transportation Term Sheet (“ET Term Sheet”), Attachment 2 to the Settlement Agreement, set forth the terms and conditions of the Company’s Community Charger program.  On May 8, 2023, the Company filed its first Plan, identifying eleven (11) locations for the placement of EV charging stations.  The ET Term Sheet allowed interested parties to comment on the Plan within thirty (30) days of filing.  Since this is the first Plan presented to the Commission and since all parties are cooperatively working to deploy this new service, AACE believes that the Commission should take this opportunity to assess the ET Term Sheet and address implementation issues that are arising related to the Plan.
Compliance with Community Charger Criteria:
The ET Term Sheet set the criteria by which a Community Charger can be deployed.  It required the Company to: 
Identify the location of up to (but not more than) eleven Community Chargers:  The Company has identified eleven (11) chargers, and the Plan is compliant with this requirement.
Focus on underserved areas (rural and income qualified) which are not located within one (1) mile of a designated Alternative Fuel Corridor (“AFC”) or any location funded through the Georgia Electric Vehicle Infrastructure Deployment Plan:  None of the Company’s proposed Community Chargers are located within one (1) mile of an AFC or a location funded through the infrastructure deployment plan.  The Plan complies with this requirement.  
Located in Tier 1 and 2 Counties identified by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs (“DCA”):  Charger No. 11 proposed to be installed at U.S. 84 and Main Street in Blackshear, is located in Pierce County which is designated as a Tier 3 County by DCA.  It does not meet the requirements of the ET Term Sheet. The remaining ten (10) chargers identified in the Plan are compliant.
Implementation Issues:
Given developments to date, the following implementation issues are arising and will need to be addressed:
Number of Community Chargers:  The ET Term Sheet clearly limits the total number of Community Chargers, including those as to which a right of first refusal (“ROFR”) is exercised, to eleven (11).
Distance for exercise of a ROFR:   The ET Term Sheet allows private EV charging service providers to claim a location within fifteen (15) miles of a Community Charger location proposed by the Company.  However, it does not define how to measure fifteen (15) miles.  AACE recommends that a fifteen (15) mile radius around the proposed Community Charger should be used.
Exercise of ROFR by multiple private EV charging service providers:  The EV Term Sheet provides no guidance in the event more than one (1) private EV charging service provider exercises the ROFR for a particular Community Charger location.  It must be clear that if at least one (1) competing EV charging service provider installs an EV charger at a Community Charger location that the ROFR has been fully exercised. 
AACE appreciates the opportunity to continue working with the Staff, the Company and interested parties to address these issues regarding the implementation of the Community Charging Program as set forth in ET Term Sheet.
