 8   Q.     BASED ON YOUR REVIEW, DID THE COMPANY’S ANALYSIS FOLLOW ITS

 9          CONVENTIONAL PLANNING PROCESSES?

13          additional cases known as “Area Max” scenarios.


14   Q.     IS THE PROCESS USED BY THE COMPANY TO EVALUATE THESE

15          PROJECTS        CONSISTENT         WITH      INDUSTRY-STANDARD             PLANNING

16          PROCESSES?

17   A.     Mostly, with a few notable exceptions. The use of four seasonal base case scenarios is

20          Daylight Shoulder, and Dusk Shoulder base cases were used to evaluate these projects. The

21          Company uses standard procedures and software to evaluate the cases.

 1            Based on our experience in other jurisdictions, we have identified a few elements of the

2             Company’s analysis that are less common transmission planning practices. The use of Area

3             Max cases is a more conservative approach taken by the Company than that of many

4             transmission utilities. In the Area Max scenarios, generation within a specific geographic

5             area is evaluated at its maximum output, typically at or near Pmax 7 while N-1 contingency

6             analysis 8 is performed to identify thermal overloads and voltage constraints. Area Max

7             cases are developed by the Company for each season and planning year. Area Max cases

8             are not a direct requirement under North American Electric Reliability Corporation

 9            (“NERC”) reliability standards. 9 While these cases can identify overloads during extreme

10            grid conditions, they represent potentially unlikely circumstances where a particular region

11            is being dispatched at or near it’s maximum rated limit. Typically, this is a low-probability

12            circumstance, and thus reflects conservative planning.


13   Q.       ARE OTHER SCENARIOS THAT WERE USED BY THE COMPANY TO

14            EVALUATE THESE PROJECTS CONSISTENT WITH INDUSTRY-STANDARD

15            PLANNING PROCESSES?

16   A.       Yes. The Company also evaluates various generator outage scenarios for each planning

17            year and season. In these analyses, an N-1 contingency analysis is performed with a pre-

18            existing generator outage. These cases are known as N-G-1, with “G” signifying generator.

19            The N-G-1 cases constitute a P3 contingency scenario as defined in NERC TPL-001-5
