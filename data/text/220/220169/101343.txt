Q.WHAT CONCERNS Does Staff have regarding the contingency amount REQUESTED BY the COMPANY As part of the certified cost for the five bess projects?
A.Staff has concerns regarding the risk category Changes in Import Tariffs or other Regulatory Impacts being included in the Company’s contingency request. If a change in law does occur and Georgia Power incurs costs associated with changes in tariffs or other regulatory impacts being implemented, under the Commission’s IRP Act, the Company would be allowed to request recovery of such costs even if the costs are not included in the certified amount. Staff would likely support the recovery of such costs if associated directly with the implementation of the change in law.  However, if the change in law does not occur but the contingency for those costs has been included in the certified cost, there is no mechanism available to challenge the reasonableness of any project cost unless the project costs exceed the approved certified amount. In other words, if Georgia Power spends the contingency amount on something different, ratepayers would have no opportunity to challenge the reasonableness of such costs. The Commission should preserve the right of the ratepayers to challenge costs based on the legal standard of reasonableness.

Q.PLEASE DESCRIBE THE RENEWABLE INTEGRATION BENEFIT.
A.The Renewable Integration Benefit derives from the operational value of BESS to a grid with more intermittent renewable resources. During the 2022 IRP proceeding, the Company filed the Renewable Integration Study conducted by Astrapé Consulting (Docket No. 44160. Georgia Power Company 2022 Integrated Resource Plan, Volume 1. Renewable Integration Study. November 18, 2021). That study concluded that the addition of battery storage resources lowers the cost of reliably integrating renewable resources. This cost reduction is reflected in the Renewable Integration Benefit.
Q.HOW DID THE COMPANY QUANTIFY THE RENEWABLE INTEGRATION BENEFIT?
A.The Company used the results of the analysis Renewable Integration Study to quantify the benefit of the BESS projects on a $/kW basis. The Company compared the production cost deltas between two sets of Aurora model runs: a case with 3,000 MW of incremental solar and no new BESS capacity, and a case with 3,000 MW of incremental solar and 1,150 MW of BESS capacity. The Company divided the reduction in production costs due to the BESS by the total BESS capacity to yield a value of the BESS in $/kW.
In the benefits analysis, the Company credited the BESS projects with a Renewable Integration Benefit by multiplying the project capacity by the $/kW value. Q.PLEASE DESCRIBE THE COMPANY’S AUGMENTATION PLANS FOR THE FIVE BESS PROJECTS. 
A.“Augmentation,” in the context of battery storage projects, refers to periodic modifications to the battery storage facilities to maintain the desired performance characteristics. Over time, the ability of BESS to store energy degrades, so a project that was able to deliver four hours of output at the time of commissioning will lose a portion of that duration. The owner of a BESS can make additional capital investments in the facility to maintain the target duration. This is important such that the projects can maintain the same reliability value to the grid.
Q.WHAT ARE THE PRIMARY AUGMENTATION STRATEGIES USED IN THE INDUSTRY?
A.There are two primary approaches to augmentation. First, a BESS owner can overbuild the battery project at the time of commissioning, to provide a buffer for the target duration and extend the time before an augmentation investment is made. The second approach is to build the project to the desired energy level at commissioning and make augmentation investments once the battery degrades and can no longer deliver the desired duration (e.g. 4 hours).
Q.WHICH OF THE AUGMENTATION STRATEGIES IS THE COMPANY PROPOSING TO UTILIZE?
A.The Company proposes to use both strategies. For the Robins and Moody Projects, the Company is sizing the projects to their full target size at commissioning and starting augmentation sooner XXXXXX. For the Hammond and McGrau Ford Phases I and II Projects, the Company is slightly overbuilding the projects and beginning augmentation later XXXXXXX. The Company told Staff during informal discussions that the Company is pursuing this strategy in order to provide a balanced approach. For example, the project will need to be taken out of service for a short period when the augmentation investments are put in service, and with the Company’s approach, all five of the projects will not need to be taken out of service during the same year for the investment.

Q.PLEASE DESCRIBE THE COMPANY’S DECOMMISSIONING PLANS FOR THE FIVE BESS PROJECTS.
A.As part of the total certified cost requests, the Company included decommissioning and dismantlement costs which represent additional estimated costs at the end of the life of the BESS for site remediation. It is Staff’s understanding that the Company plans to recycle the recyclable materials of the Tesla Megapack 2 XL after the 20-year expected life. The Company did not include any additional value or cost of the recycled BESS in the estimated decommissioning costs. Q.PLEASE DESCRIBE THE REPORTING REQUIREMENTS FOR THE FIVE BESS PROJECTS THAT HAVE ALREADY BEEN APPROVED BY THE COMMISSION. 
A.As part of the 2023 IRP Update Order, the Commission ordered the Company to file semi-annually, Construction Monitoring Reports for the Robins and Moody BESS Projects; however, the Order stated that specific reporting requirements for the 500 MW of BESS would be determined during the certification proceeding. For the McGrau Ford Phase I Project, the approved 2022 IRP Stipulation requires quarterly filings but did not specify reporting requirements. 
Q.HOW FREQUENTLY DID THE COMPANY PROPOSE TO FILE THE CONSTRUCTION MONITORING REPORTS FOR THE FIVE BESS PROJECTS?
A.In the Compliance Filing, the Company stated that it will file quarterly Construction Monitoring Reports for the McGrau Ford Phase I Project; however, the Compliance Filing was silent on what information would be provided in the reports. For the Robins, Moody, Hammond, and McGrau Ford Phase II Projects, the Company proposed to file semi-annual Construction Monitoring Reports and provide information regarding capital expenditures and financial cost data, detailed schedule reporting on critical path activities, status updates on the procurement of any new licenses and permits, variances in major contracts, and the procurement status of long-lead time equipment (Company’s Direct Testimony, p. 17, lines 12-21). 
Q.DOES STAFF AGREE THAT SEMI-ANNUAL REPORTING REQUIREMENTS ARE SUFFICENT GIVEN THE FAST CONSTRUCTION TIMELINE AND CAPITAL-INTENSIVE NATURE OF THE BESS PROJECTS?
A.No. Staff does not agree that semi-annual reporting is sufficient when considering the fast-approaching COD and capital-intensive projects. It is Staff’s position that Construction Monitoring Reporting for these projects should be done more often than twice a year. 
Q.WHAT IS STAFF’S RECOMMENDATION REGARDING THE FREQUENCY OF CONSTRUCTION MONITORING REPORTING AND THE INFORMATION TO BE PROVIDED IN THE REPORT?
A.Staff recommends quarterly Construction Monitoring Reporting for the Robins, Moody, Hammond and McGrau Ford Phase II BESS Projects to allow the Commission to stay properly informed regarding the status of the construction and associated spending for these projects. The Commission has already approved a quarterly reporting requirement for the McGrau Ford Phase I Project. In addition to the information that the Company has agreed to provide, Staff recommends that the following information also be provided in the quarterly reports: contingency assessment, CWIP, AFUDC, and Ad Valorem balances.
