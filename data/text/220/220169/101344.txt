III. DISCUSSION OF THE TRANSMISSION ANALYSIS AND RESULTS
Q. PLEASE CHARACTERIZE THE COMPANY’S FILING WITH RESPECT TO THE TRANSMISSION ANALYSIS PERFORMED ON THE FOUR FINAL BESS SITES.
A. The Company’s transmission analysis studied the Series 2024 Summer Peak (S), Off-Peak (O), Daylight Shoulder (D), and Dusk Shoulder (H) Base Cases for the study years 2027 – 2034 using base load levels. 
Q.BASED ON YOUR REVIEW, DID THE COMPANY’S ANALYSIS FOLLOW ITS CONVENTIONAL PLANNING PROCESSES?
A.Yes. The Company’s analysis is consistent with its conventional planning process in the study cases used and the methods for transmission analysis. The analysis included evaluating seasonal cases and scenarios for generating units out of service as well as additional cases known as “Area Max” scenarios.
Q.IS THE PROCESS USED BY THE COMPANY TO EVALUATE THESE PROJECTS CONSISTENT WITH INDUSTRY-STANDARD PLANNING PROCESSES?
A.Mostly, with a few notable exceptions. The use of four seasonal base case scenarios is consistent with standard planning processes. These cases represent the expected load and generation levels forecasted for the respective planning years. Summer Peak, Off-Peak, Daylight Shoulder, and Dusk Shoulder base cases were used to evaluate these projects. The Company uses standard procedures and software to evaluate the cases.
Based on our experience in other jurisdictions, we have identified a few elements of the Company’s analysis that are less common transmission planning practices. The use of Area Max cases is a more conservative approach taken by the Company than that of many transmission utilities. In the Area Max scenarios, generation within a specific geographic area is evaluated at its maximum output, typically at or near Pmax while N-1 contingency analysis is performed to identify thermal overloads and voltage constraints. Area Max cases are developed by the Company for each season and planning year. Area Max cases are not a direct requirement under North American Electric Reliability Corporation (“NERC”) reliability standards. While these cases can identify overloads during extreme grid conditions, they represent potentially unlikely circumstances where a particular region is being dispatched at or near it’s maximum rated limit. Typically, this is a low-probability circumstance, and thus reflects conservative planning. 
Q.ARE OTHER SCENARIOS THAT WERE USED BY THE COMPANY TO EVALUATE THESE PROJECTS CONSISTENT WITH INDUSTRY-STANDARD PLANNING PROCESSES?
A.Yes. The Company also evaluates various generator outage scenarios for each planning year and season. In these analyses, an N-1 contingency analysis is performed with a pre-existing generator outage. These cases are known as N-G-1, with “G” signifying generator. The N-G-1 cases constitute a P3 contingency scenario as defined in NERC TPL-001-5 Transmission System Planning Performance Requirements. As part of the NERC Criteria, corrective re-dispatching of appropriate generation resources is allowed as a corrective action after the initial N-G scenario.
As discussed in Section IV, it is these two types of scenarios, Area Max and N-G-1, which caused the overloads that triggered the need for transmission system upgrades for McGrau Ford Phase II.  
Q.DO YOU HAVE ANY OTHER OBSERVATIONS ABOUT THE COMPANY’S PLANNING PROCESS?
A.Yes. The Company submitted the interconnection request for the McGrau Ford Phase II Project prior to the April 2024 compliance deadline for FERC Order 2023, meaning that the study is currently ongoing as a part of the 2024 transitional cluster study process which began in June 2024. The results of this study are expected in November 2024. The results of this study could impact interconnection costs for the project. We recommend that the Company provide a copy of this study when complete, and an evaluation of how the results might impact the expected costs of the project.
In addition to the potential impact on costs, the transitional cluster study did not mandate that ATTs be evaluated to determine whether the overloads could be mitigated without the need for potentially more costly transmission network upgrades. Therefore, the Company did not evaluate any ATTs. The Company states that they will begin evaluating ATTs as mandated by FERC Order 2023 once annual cluster studies commence in 2025. This study approach is consistent with the version of the tariff that was effective when the interconnection request was submitted for the McGrau Ford Phase II Project.
Finally, as was the case in the 2023 IRP Update transmission analysis, the transmission planning cases in this docket included certain generators that had been identified and ordered retired in the 2022 IRP. These generators were dispatched throughout this transmission analysis.
Q.WHAT WERE THE PRIMARY CONCLUSIONS OF THE COMPANY’S ANALYSIS OF THE FOUR BATTERY SITES?
A.The Company’s analysis concluded that the Robins, Moody, and Hammond sites could be interconnected without any transmission upgrades but identified a need for two upgrades associated with the interconnection of the McGrau Ford Phase II Project.
