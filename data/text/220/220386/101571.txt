Question:
Please describe and explain in further detail the technical review process (known as preliminary vetting stage)?


Response:
The technical review process, also known as the preliminary vetting stage, involves a capacity review, transmission study, engineering design, timeline review, and economic analysis. Once these steps are complete, Company management evaluates the projects prior to extending a contract for electric service.

BEFORE THE GEORGIA PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION

GEORGIA POWER COMPANY
DOCKET NO. 55378

Data Request No. STF-PIA-19-2

BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-PIA-19-2 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided confidential data on the Company’s large load customers (the “Information”). All such Information constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains sensitive commercial information that is integral to Georgia Power’s competitive positioning within Georgia. In addition, maintaining the confidentiality of proposed customer project details is of paramount importance. If the Information were disclosed without customer permission, it would harm the trust relationship that Georgia Power maintains with its customers and the ability of Georgia Power to implement its rates and programs in the most efficient and economic manner for customers. The release or exposure of the Information, even in part, could be detrimental to Georgia Power’s ability to make competitive offers in the marketplace by providing other electric service providers insights into the Company’s cost structure and pricing, which may undermine the Company’s competitiveness for customer choice opportunities, which would have a negative impact on all retail customers. Lastly, the Company’s competitors are not typically required to make public this type of information and to require the Company to do so would place it at an economic disadvantage.  

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.

STF-PIA-19-3

Question:
What is the minimum load, in megawatts, to be considered a large load customer?


Response:
As further described in STF-DEA-5-1, for purposes of the Company’s large load economic development tracking and forecasting, the Company considers “large load customers” to be commercial customers at 115 MW and industrial customers at 45 MW, respectively. For purposes of the large load exception to the Georgia Territorial Electric Service Act, please see the requirements of O.C.G.A. § 46-3-8.

STF-PIA-19-4

Question:
Is there a penalty for canceling a project when there is a contract for service in place?  If yes, how is the penalty established? For example, is there a volumetric (-dollars per MW) fee anticipated or a standard fee?


Response:
There is currently no penalty for a customer cancelling or terminating a Contract for Electric Service with Georgia Power. However, a customer who breaches a contract by terminating before the end of an agreed-upon term may be liable for, and Georgia Power may pursue, damages arising from or related to the customer’s breach. This may include, without limitation, unpaid Real Time Pricing Customer Baseline Load charges, Time of Use – Supplier Choice Monthly Access Charges, Power and Light contract minimum Billing Demand charges, and Excess Facilities charges for any remaining portion of the term, in each case as specified in the contract.

Question:

The latest estimate of the customer’s ramp and level of contracted capacity have been used to make these estimates. They may vary from the information provided in the second quarter report.
