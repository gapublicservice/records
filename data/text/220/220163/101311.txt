BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-PIA-16-4 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided confidential data on the Company’s contracts, transactions, and process for working with large load customers (the “Information”). All of such Information constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains sensitive commercial information that is integral to Georgia Power’s competitive positioning within Georgia. In addition, maintaining the confidentiality of proposed customer project details is of paramount importance. If the Information were disclosed without customer permission, it would harm the trust relationship that Georgia Power maintains with its customers and the ability of Georgia Power to implement its rates and programs in the most efficient and economic manner for customers. The release or exposure of the Information, even in part, could be detrimental to Georgia Power’s ability to make competitive offers in the marketplace by providing other electric service providers insights into the Company’s cost structure and pricing, which may undermine the Company’s competitiveness for customer choice opportunities, which would have a negative impact on all retail customers. Lastly, the Company’s competitors are not typically required to make public this type of information and to require the Company to do so would place it at an economic disadvantage. 

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information. 


STF-PIA-16-4

Question:
Please list any projects where the Company considered, approached, or pursued a facility outside of its service territory, but later determined that it was ineligible under the Territorial Act? Please specify why the project was ineligible under the Territorial Act.

Response:
Below are examples of projects the Company initially pursued but later determined to be ineligible for choice of electric service provider under the large load exception of the Territorial Act. The Company does not track projects outside of its service territory that were initially considered but later determined to be ineligible for choice of electric service provider. 

REDACTED: Georgia Power worked with two data centers (first REDACTED REDACTED and then REDACTED) to evaluate service to a premises in Lithia Springs. In evaluating the project, Georgia Power learned that the local electric membership corporation (“EMC”), in whose territory the premise was located, had already installed a padmount transformer with a permanent meter at the premises. As such, Georgia Power considered the premises to be already served, and communicated to the prospective customer that Georgia Power was not able to provide electric service.
REDACTED: A potential customer was developing a facility in Georgia Power’s service territory, but an EMC had corridor rights associated with the premises. Initially, the developer thought the premises would have an initial connected load over 900 kW. However, once the Company received the panel schedules for the facility, it determined the initial connected load would not exceed 900 kW and the EMC should serve the premises due to the aforementioned corridor rights.
REDACTED REDACTED: This project related to the development of a logistics center within an EMC’s service territory. The developer of the project contacted Georgia Power to provide electric service to the premises and communicated that the load would be over 900 kW. However, upon examination, the panel schedules did not verify connected load of 900 kW or greater. Therefore, Georgia Power communicated to the developer that the EMC had the right to serve the premises. 
REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED: Representatives from REDACTED contacted Georgia Power to discuss service to an elevated water tank being installed to provided water to industrial customers. After reviewing the territory maps and panel schedules, Georgia Power determined the premises, which was in EMC service territory, did not meet the 900kW threshold of the large load exception to the Territorial Act. Therefore, Georgia Power determined the premises was not eligible for a choice of electric service provider and communicated this to REDACTED.
