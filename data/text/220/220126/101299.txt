BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-PIA-18-1 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided information and data supporting the proposed projects, including sensitive terms and conditions contained within the Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (“EPC”) Agreements and the Battery and Equipment Supply Agreements (“BESA”), pricing information, resource operational capability data, and development timelines, as well as certain information regarding the Company’s proprietary planning processes, financial data, technology screening assumptions, generic technology and resource cost information, specific resource and technology cost information, economic analyses, including confidential details regarding current and future transmission projects (the “Information”). Certain portions of the Information are trade secrets of Georgia Power and Southern Company and their affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.
 The trade secret portions of the Information derive economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from their disclosure or use. Specifically, the trade secret portions of the Information contained herein include competitively sensitive pricing and operational capability data, including project development timelines where applicable, specific to the resources under contract and proposed to be developed. If the Information were made public, competitors, bidders, and suppliers could use the Information to unfairly manipulate the request for proposals process and competitive market to structure future bids and set an artificial price floor to arbitrarily increase prices to the detriment of the Company and its customers. Public dissemination of the Information would undermine Georgia Power’s ability to negotiate the best price and contract terms and could harm the Company’s ability to secure the best cost bids and resources for the benefit of customers. 
The trade secret portions of the Information also contain competitively sensitive cost information related to available technology options, processes and data used by Georgia Power in analyzing resource addition schedules, financial data used in the Company’s resource analyses, and other confidential details including data supporting resource economic evaluations. Public dissemination of the trade secret portions of the Information would allow Georgia Power’s competitors and suppliers to have access to such processes and strategies and thereby gain an unfair competitive advantage in the marketplace. Competitors would obtain an unfair advantage because they are not required to reveal similar information and can utilize such trade secret portions of the Information to manipulate pricing and timing of supply to the disadvantage of Georgia Power. Competitors would also unfairly benefit in having access and insight into the Company’s planning processes and methodologies. This competitive advantage for the Company’s suppliers and competitors would mean that Georgia Power will potentially pay higher prices to suppliers, ultimately harming Georgia Power and its customers.  
The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.  

STF-PIA-18-1

Question:
Please refer to the Company’s Application for Certification, Figures 4, 7, 10, and 13; please provide a narrative description of the values found in the Estimated Annual Capital Additions column and how those values were calculated. 


Response:
The estimated annual capital additions are predominantly for augmentation cycles. The augmentation is estimated by tracking the REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED and calculating the additional Megawatt-hours needed when the system degrades to REDACTED of its nameplate capacity. The amount of incremental energy storage added to the site (i.e. augmentation) will be enough to reenergize the site to REDACTED of the nameplate energy capacity. The site will eventually degrade back to REDACTED some years later, at which point an additional augmentation will be needed. The cost of augmentation was calculated by multiplying the amount of Megawatt-hours needed to bring the facility from REDACTED of its nameplate energy capacity by the forecasted price curves.
Some relatively small capital costs are reflected in the first years of service for certain resources. These costs are associated with remaining construction-related activities that are expected to be incurred after each BESS resource is placed in service. Therefore, these costs are captured in the estimated annual capital additions column in the figures and associated modeling. 
The calculations for the estimated annual capital additions can be found in the SAM files previously provided to Commission Staff on August 16, 2024.

STF-PIA-18-10

Question:
Please refer to the Response to STF-PIA-17-5, noting that the Hammond and McGrau Ford projects “energy-capacity overbuild, which requires a different augmentation schedule from that of Robins and Moody BESS.”
How much are each of the projects overbuilt?
Please explain how the Company determines whether augmentation should be achieved by overbuild or through earlier augmentation investments, as with the Robins and Moody projects.  If the Company conducted any analysis to support its decision, please provide the results.

Response:
All sites require a slight overbuild to account for real and reactive power losses to ensure the BESS can meet Point of Interconnection (“POI”) requirements:  
Robins – REDACTED, 
Moody – REDACTED, 
McGrau Phase II – REDACTED, and 
Hammond – REDACTED.
The Company's approach to determining whether augmentation should be achieved through overbuild or earlier augmentation is guided primarily by strategic considerations rather than a specific quantitative analysis. One key consideration was the ability to alternate outage scheduling and portfolio diversification. Varied overbuild starting points can aid in the need for alternate outage schedules across the BESS portfolio.
Company ownership provides flexibility to adapt to changing storage needs, manage degradation, and optimize outage schedules. The Company can balance available tax incentives and declining BESS costs with system reliability and needs to adjust plans if the most appropriate periods for augmentation change. While the Company does not have a detailed financial analysis for this specific assumption, this strategy provides flexibility and diversification of the portfolio to ensure efficient and cost-effective storage solutions.

STF-PIA-18-14

Question:
Please explain why the McGrau Ford Phase II injection is studied at -133 MW in the charging scenarios (for all Off-Peak and Daylight Shoulder scenarios), and not as -265 MW. This is seen in REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED provided by Georgia Power. 

Response:
For screening as a capacity resource, BESS must be able to fully charge in 4-hrs in Summer (or nameplate if greater than 4-hrs) and within 8-hrs in Winter. To optimize BESS performance, it is crucial to preserve a certain amount of grid charging capabilities according to the duration capabilities of the BESS, as well as understanding the timeframe between seasonal system load peaks. This concept ensures that the battery can recharge efficiently and be ready to discharge during periods of high demand. 
The McGrau Ford Phase I project was designated at 265 MW charging; however, as a 2-hour BESS, only 132.5 MW are required for that system. Therefore, for the addition of the Phase II BESS as a 4-hour BESS, the current preservation methodology necessitates only an incremental 133 MW to cover the remaining portion required for both Phase I and Phase II of the McGrau Ford BESS projects. This strategic allocation helps ensure that the BESS can effectively meet its operational targets and support grid reliability.
The following summarizes the designations needed for both McGrau Ford Phases I and II:
McGrau Ford Phase I is a 265 MW/hr 2-hr battery (-132.5 MW required).
McGrau Ford Phase II is a 265 MW/hr 4-hr battery (-265 MW required).
Total Charging Capabilities Required: -397.5 MW

STF-PIA-18-18

Question:
Regarding the Company’s SAM files, please provide an explanation for the assumption that BESS augmentations are eligible for the Base + Labor + Domestic Content ITC for years after the IRA is set to expire.


Response:
The Investment Tax Credit (“ITC”) benefits from the Inflation Reduction Act (“IRA”) are set to phase out at the later of either 2032 or when the nationwide electric sector reaches a 75% reduction in carbon emissions from 2022 levels. Given the current pace of carbon emission reductions, the Company has assumed that IRA incentives will extend through the augmentation periods for each BESS resource in the economic analysis.
Additionally, the Company has assumed that achieving domestic content requirements for augmentation is feasible. This assumption is primarily based on the expectation that U.S. solar and BESS supply chains will mature over time, thus providing opportunities to meet domestic content criteria and benefit from the associated ITC.
The Company will continue to monitor any changes in tax policy, battery performance, and augmentation costs, and adjust augmentation strategies accordingly to the benefit of customers.
