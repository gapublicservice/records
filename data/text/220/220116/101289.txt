STF-ASR-3-10

Question:

Company statement: 
“The remaining tax credits of over $2 million will also be utilized for the benefit of the customers but will be subject to Normalization.” 
How will the $2 million tax credits be reflected in ASR rate base and Operating Income statement over time?

Response:

As stated in the Company’s response to STF-ASR-3-2, the final amount of Tugalo Hydro Modernization ITC generated in 2023  is $15.6 million. The unsold portion of tax credits, therefore, is $3.6 million ($15.6 million - $12 million), instead of $2 million.
Subject to IRS normalization rules, the $3.6 million of unsold ITCs will not be included in rate base. Once the credits are utilized on the Company’s federal tax return, the amortization of the $3.6 million will be reflected in operating income as a credit to amortization expense over the depreciable life of Tugalo Units 1&2.

STF-ASR-3-11

Question:

Company statement: 
“The economics of selling tax credits even at a discount (for example market is in the low ninety cents on the dollar for ITCs) will trigger a more beneficial ratemaking structure for customers, via lower rate base.” 

What happens to the cash received from selling ITCs?

Response:

The cash received from selling the ITCs in 2024 was recorded as a debit to FERC account 131 Cash and a credit to FERC account 236 Taxes Payable. The balance of the ITCs recorded to FERC account 255 Accumulated Deferred Investment Tax Credits, which is included as a reduction to rate base upon the sale of the ITCs, is lowered to reflect the discounted proceeds from the sale.

STF-ASR-3-13

Question:

Please explain how rate base is “lowered” from selling ITCs. What are the specific adjustments to rate base line items?

Response:

The rate base is lowered by transferring the sold portion of the unamortized ITC liability into a separate FERC account 255, which will be included in rate base as a new line item upon the sale. Under IRS normalization rules, the unamortized ITC balance is not included in rate base if the credits are not sold.

STF-ASR-3-19

Question:

Provide a description of the regulatory accounting treatment of the ITC monetization.

Response:

By selling the ITCs, they are no longer subject to IRS normalization rules. For the ITCs that are sold, the Company will transfer them into a separate FERC account 255 and include the balance as a reduction to rate base for customers. In addition, the amortization of the sale proceeds from FERC account 255 to FERC account 411.4 will be treated above the line in operating income for retail cost of service.

===== DKT 44280 STF-ASR-3/STF-ASR-3-7.docx =====
STF-ASR-3-7

Question:

Company statement: 
“… a public utility may elect to either reduce rate base by the Unamortized ITC or reduce cost of service by amortization of the credit over the life of the asset.  The Company’s long-standing election for regulatory treatment is to reduce cost of service by amortization of the credit.”
Please explain the difference between “reduce rate base by the Unamortized ITC” and “reduce cost of service by amortization of the credit over the life of the asset”. Specifically, identify the impact on ASR rate base and Operating Income statement under both options.

Response:

The statement referenced above reflects the regulatory treatment election under IRS normalization rules. When ITCs are generated, they are recorded to FERC account 255 Accumulated deferred ITC. Once the credits are utilized on the Company’s tax return, the Company would amortize these credits over the life of the related asset to FERC account 411.4 Investment tax credit adjustments under amortization expenses in the income statement.
Under the election to “reduce rate base by the unamortized ITC,” the unamortized ITC balance in FERC account 255 would be applied as a reduction to rate base for customers, but the amortization of the ITC in FERC account 411.4 would be treated below the line. Therefore, there would be no operating income impact for retail cost of service under this election. 
Under the election to “reduce cost of service by amortization of the credit over the life of the asset,” the amortization of the ITC in FERC account 411.4 would be treated above the line for retail operating income, but the unamortized ITC balance in FERC account 255 would be excluded from rate base.
