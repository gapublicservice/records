STF-ASR-4-1

Question:

Plant Held for Future Use (PHFFU). Refer to the response to STF-ASR-1-1. This response states that in 2023, the Company made an addition to PHFFU for the purchase of land related to the 265 MW McGrau Ford BESS project, which was authorized by the Commission Order in Docket No. 44160. Is this parcel of land related to the Phase 2 project? Explain fully.

Response:

The land purchased by the Company in 2023 related to the 265 MW McGrau Ford BESS project referenced in response to STF-ASR-1-1 was initially for the Phase I project. However, the land is suitable to accommodate both McGrau Ford BESS Phases I and II.

STF-ASR-4-2

Question:

Plant Held for Future Use (PHFFU). Refer to the response to STF-ASR-1-2. As it relates to the $149,000 of timber sales that were credited to the St. Joe Timber Land (located in Stewart County) in PHFFU, was the balance in PHFFU (i.e., rate base) for the Stewart County land decreased by the $149,000? Explain fully how the Company accounted for the Timber Land sales proceeds and provide the accounting entries.

Response:

Yes, the balance in PHFFU for the St. Joe Timber Land (located in Stewart County) decreased due to the $149,000 from timber sales recorded in 2023. The journal entry was to credit PHFFU FERC account 105 and debit deferred credit FERC account 253 where the sale proceeds were originally recorded.

STF-ASR-4-3

Question:

Vegetation Management. Refer to the response to STF-ASR-1-19 and the table below.


As shown in the above table, for 2023 the Company’s actual recorded expenses for Distribution Asset Management, Distribution Vegetation Management and Transmission Vegetation and Right of Way Management were substantially under budget by the amounts and percentages shown.

Why were the actual 2023 expenses that are associated with each of category of asset and vegetation management expense shown in the above table so far below the budgeted amounts for 2023? Specifically address whether for each category, how much of the actual cost being below budget was due to (1) fewer than planned miles being cleared, (2) lower contractor line clearing costs, or (3) other. Please explain each other factor.

For each category of expense shown in the above table, what was the planned/budgeted number of miles to be cleared for 2023 and how? Explain fully.

For each category of expense shown in the above table, what was the actual number of miles that were cleared during 2023? Explain fully.

How did the Company accomplish the actual 2023 line clearing? Explain fully.

Response:

For the vegetation management program, the plan is to trim each circuit in a four-year window. Within the four-year window, the Company was able to address the vegetation backlog in 2021 and 2022 due to the additional O&M spending that was allocated to the program. Entering 2023, the Company had flexibility to maintain reliability while spending below budget as a result of the additional work completed in 2021 and 2022; therefore, there were fewer miles cleared in 2023 than planned. Likewise, Distribution asset management was able to pull 2023 work forward into 2021 and 2022; however, this is not measured in “miles.” 

Below were the budgeted miles/acres for the vegetation management program in 2023.

Distribution Vegetation Management budgeted 11,200 miles.
Transmission Vegetation Management budgeted 700 miles.
Transmission Right of Way 
42,000 acres of herbicide budgeted.
20,000 acres of mowing budgeted.

Please note, Distribution asset management is not part of the vegetation management program and is not measured in miles or acres.

Below were the actual miles/acres for the vegetation management program in 2023.

Distribution Vegetation Management completed 5,491 miles.
Transmission Vegetation Management completed 470 miles.
Transmission Right of Way
42,000 acres of herbicide completed.
20,000 acres of mowing completed.

Please note, Distribution asset management is not part of the vegetation management program and is not measured in miles or acres.

The Company accomplished the line clearing in 2023 via contractors for both the Distribution Vegetation Management and Transmission Vegetation Management programs.
