APPLICATION FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE OF
THE METROPOLITAN ATLANTA RAPID TRANSIT AUTHORITY


COMES NOW, the Metropolitan Atlanta Rapid Transit Authority (hereafter “MARTA”), pursuant to O.C.G.A. §§50-13-14 and 46-2-59 and Commission Rule 515-2-1-.06, and by and through counsel, hereby files this Application for Leave to Intervene (“Application”) in the above-referenced docket.  In support thereof, MARTA respectfully shows the Commission as follows.
1.
 On January 21, 2026, the Commission issued a procedural and scheduling order (“PSO”) in this docket.   Under the PSO, Georgia Power (sometimes referred to herein as the “Company”) is scheduled to file its application and direct testimony for adjustments for the recovery of under-recovered storm damage by 4:00 p.m.  This filing is the result of a Stipulation approved by the Commission on July 31, 2025 in this docket. Pursuant to the PSO, MARTA files this Application for leave to intervene and participate in the proceedings.   The Application is timely filed.  
2.
MARTA is a public authority created by the MARTA Act, Georgia Laws 1965, page 2243, et. seq., as amended from time to time.  Its mission is to advocate and provide safe, multi-modal transit services that advance prosperity, connectivity, and equity for a more livable region.   
MARTA provides service to 2.2 million residents of Metro Atlanta and is one of the top 10 transportation agencies in the United States.  Its combined bus and rail services support over 24,000 jobs throughout Atlanta and the State of Georgia.  MARTA’s rail service involves over 330 rail cars capable of carrying passengers to any of its 38 stations, and its 550 buses provide service along 1,800 miles of road on 113 routes.  MARTA currently operates six electric buses with plans to expand its fleet of electric vehicles.  MARTA’s services help drive economic development in and around the metro-region.  MARTA’s footprint in the metro-area is fairly significant, with 905.7 acres of property, including 388.7 acres directly adjacent to its MARTA stations.  MARTA purchases electricity from Georgia Power to power its electric rail service and provides Electric Vehicle charging stations to its patrons and employees.  Cost effective, reliable energy is important to its operations and services.  Energy costs have a significant impact on MARTA’s mission, operations, and its customers.  
3.
In furtherance of its interests and on behalf of its customers MARTA has intervened in other rate case proceedings before the Commission, including Georgia Power’s 2013 (Docket No. 36989), 2010 (Docket No. 31958), 2019 (Docket No. 42516), 2022 (Docket No. 44280) rate cases. 
4.
MARTA has substantial interests in the proceedings related to this docket on its own behalf and on behalf of its employees and customers.  Georgia Power is MARTA’s electric service provider and its customers are subject to the impacts of the Company’s services and rates.  MARTA seeks to intervene in this proceeding to continue to protect its interests and the interests of its employees and customers.   
5.
MARTA’s interests and those of its customers are not adequately represented by other parties to these proceedings, including the other intervening parties, and MARTA’s intervention and participation in this matter will not unduly delay the proceedings or prejudice the rights of other parties.  If this application is granted MARTA intends to fully participate in this docket, including through the submission of direct expert testimony from MARTA staff and/or outside experts.
6.
MARTA respectfully requests that it be granted leave to intervene and participate fully as a party in these proceedings, including the right to present testimony and exhibits, cross-examine witnesses, and file briefs.
7.
MARTA respectfully requests that the following persons receive all notices, correspondence and copies of pleadings, orders and other materials in this docket, and that all communications regarding this docket be directed to:
