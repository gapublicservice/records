FUEL COST RECOVERY
SCHEDULE: “FCR-27”
          PAGE                                 EFFECTIVE DATE                                  REVISION      PAGE NO.
          1 of 2       With Bills Rendered for the Billing Month of June, 2026                 Original        10.40


                                                    APPLICABILITY:

This schedule is applicable to and becomes a part of each retail rate schedule in which reference is made
to the Fuel Cost Recovery Schedule unless an account is billed on a Time of Use Fuel Cost Recovery
Schedule.

                                                          RATE:

All bills rendered subject to the Fuel Cost Recovery Schedule shall be respectively increased in an
amount equal to:

    June through September:
       Secondary Distribution customers .................................................... 3.8158¢ per kWh
       Primary Distribution customers ......................................................... 3.7475¢ per kWh
       Transmission customers .................................................................... 3.7214¢ per kWh

    October through May:
       Secondary Distribution customers .................................................... 3.8657¢ per kWh
       Primary Distribution customers ......................................................... 3.7965¢ per kWh
       Transmission customers .................................................................... 3.7700¢ per kWh


Fuel Costs shall be the cost of:

   (1) fossil, nuclear, bio-mass (including renewable) fuel and emission allowances (including
       credits, offsets, taxes, tariffs or other mechanisms intended to establish a market price for
       carbon, carbon dioxide and/or other greenhouse gases) consumed in the Company's own
       plants, and the Company's share of fossil, nuclear, and bio-mass (including renewable) fuel
       and emission allowances (including credits, offsets, taxes, tariffs or other mechanisms
       intended to establish a market price for carbon, carbon dioxide and/or other greenhouse
       gases) consumed in jointly owned or leased plants; plus
   (2) the identifiable fossil, nuclear, bio-mass (including renewable) fuel and emission allowance
       costs (including credits, offsets, taxes, tariffs or other mechanisms intended to establish a
       market price for carbon, carbon dioxide and/or other greenhouse gases) associated with
       energy purchased for reasons other than identified in (3) below; plus
   (3) the net energy cost of energy purchases, exclusive of capacity or demand charges
       (irrespective of the designation assigned to such transaction) when such energy is
       purchased on an economic dispatch basis. Included therein may be such costs as the
       charges for energy purchases and the charges as a result of scheduled outages, all such
       kinds of energy being purchased by the buyer to substitute for its own higher cost of
       energy; less

SCHEDULE: “FCR-27”
          PAGE                             EFFECTIVE DATE                             REVISION    PAGE

   (4) the cost of fossil, nuclear, bio-mass (including renewable) fuel and emission allowances
       (including credits, offsets, taxes, tariffs or other mechanisms intended to establish a market
       price for carbon, carbon dioxide and/or other greenhouse gases) recovered through
       intersystem sales when sold on an economic dispatch basis; less
   (5) retail portion of gains resulting from the sale of any emissions allowances; less
   (6) seventy-five percent of net gains from wholesale market opportunity sales; plus or minus
   (7) net gains and losses incurred under the Natural Gas and Oil Procurement and Hedging
       Program implemented in Docket No. 16134-U; plus or minus
   (8) carrying costs on over or under recovered fuel balance calculated at the Company’s short-
       term debt rate and excluding the first $15 million of any under recovered cost; plus or
       minus
   (9) other costs or credits as determined by the Commission for inclusion in, or reduction of,
       recoverable fuel costs.

                                               DEFINITIONS:

TRANSMISSION CUSTOMERS:

Customers who receive electrical service from a transmission line of 46 kV or higher, or from a substation
or a pole-mounted or pad-mounted transformer, which is dedicated to them and whose metering is at or
emulated to 46 kV or more.

PRIMARY DISTRIBUTION CUSTOMERS:

Customers who receive electrical service from a distribution line at a voltage between 2.4 kV and 25 kV,
or a substation or a pole-mounted or pad-mounted transformer whose high-side and low-side voltage is
between 2.4 kV and 25 kV.

SECONDARY DISTRIBUTION CUSTOMERS:

Any customer not defined as a Transmission or Primary Distribution Customer.


                                    INCOME QUALIFIED DISCOUNT:

Qualifying customers certified by the Company will be eligible for a monthly bill discount at their primary
residence pursuant to the requirements of the Income Qualified Discount Service Rider Schedule.


                                   GENERAL TERMS & CONDITIONS:

The adjustment calculated under this rider is subject to change in such an amount as may be approved
and/or amended by the Georgia Public Service Commission.

Service hereunder subject to Rules and Regulations for Electric Service on file with the Georgia Public
Service Commission.

II.OVERVIEW OF THE FILING 
Q.PLEASE EXPLAIN WHY THE COMPANY IS SEEKING AN ADJUSTMENT TO ITS FUEL COST RECOVERY RATES AT THIS TIME.
The Commission’s May 25, 2023 Order Adopting Stipulation in Docket No. 44902 (“FCR-26 Order”) requires Georgia Power to file new FCR rates on or before February 28, 2026. In compliance with the Commission’s FCR-26 Order, the Company is proposing to adjust its current fuel rates with the rates set forth in the FCR-27 Application and supporting minimum filing requirements (“MFRs”) filed in this docket on February 17, 2026. 
Q.PLEASE PROVIDE AN OVERVIEW OF THE COMPANY’S FILING.
A.Georgia Power utilizes a diverse fleet of generation resources to economically and reliably meet the energy needs of its customers. The Company recovers the cost of the fuel that it uses to generate electricity, as well as the cost to purchase energy from various resources – including natural gas and renewable resources, through the Company’s fuel rates. The fuel costs incurred by the Company, by statute, are directly passed through to customers. 
With this filing, the Company is proposing to adjust its current fuel rates to set fuel rates at an appropriate level to recover the Company’s future fuel costs and projected under-recovery of FCR-26, as projected in the two-year FCR-27 test period from June 1, 2026 through May 31, 2028. Based on the recovery of previously incurred fuel costs over the three-year period as approved by the Commission in the previous FCR-26 proceeding, virtually all of that substantial balance is projected to be collected by May 31, 2026. Consequently, the Company will lower rates for customers as reflected in this filing.
Based on average annual fuel rates, the proposed FCR-27 rate changes are projected to decrease the monthly bill of a typical residential customer using an average of 1,000 kWh per month by approximately $5.74. Contemporaneous with this fuel rate filing, the Company has also filed a Storm Damage Recovery Application. Taken together, these filings will result in a reduction of customer bills by $1.32 per month for a typical residential customer.
Additionally, as a part of this filing, the Company recommends modifying the Interim Fuel Recovery Mechanism (“IFR”) to better align the IFR threshold to the magnitude of the total fuel expenses incurred by the Company on an annual basis. This modification will provide greater rate stability for customers by allowing FCR rates set in this filing to absorb natural fuel price volatility and normal fluctuations in the fuel over- or under-recovery balances on a monthly basis.
Finally, the Company proposes to expand its natural gas hedging program by increasing both the percentage of budgeted burn that may be hedged and the length of the hedging window. This expansion is warranted given the projected growth in natural gas burn in the Company’s budget. By enhancing the scope of the hedging program, the Company will be better positioned to mitigate exposure to price fluctuations and provide greater stability in fuel costs for the benefit of customers. 
Q.WHAT IS DRIVING THE NEED TO REDUCE THE COMPANY’S FUEL RATES?
A.The proposed decrease in fuel rates is primarily driven by the continued recovery of $2.2 billion of previously under-recovered fuel balance from FCR-26, as expected, with the remaining balance at $310 million as of December 2025. This decrease is partially offset by the under-recovery of fuel expenses accumulated since the FCR-26 rate went into effect on June 1, 2023, which is $212 million as of December 2025, and is projected to grow to $300 million by May 31, 2026. The FCR-26 under-recovery is primarily driven by higher levels of coal generation, and actual natural gas prices that were lower than what was projected in Real-Time Pricing (“RTP”) revenues in the FCR-26 budget. In addition, there are increasing fuel expenses in the forecasted periods primarily driven by projected increase in natural gas prices compared to the natural gas price update incorporated for fuel rates in FCR-26 and the higher load requirement projected in the test period. We explain these factors in greater detail later in this testimony.
Even with these factors putting upward pressure on rates, the recovery of prior under-recovered fuel balance more than offsets these items and the result is a net decrease to customers of $388 million on an annual basis, as shown in Exhibit___(ADH/MSB-7), Page 1. As a result, the Company seeks to adjust its fuel rates to recover its projected fuel costs for the FCR-27 test period as further described below.

Historic Fuel Costs
Q.PLEASE ELABORATE ON THE CHANGES THAT HAVE OCCURRED IN THE FUEL MARKETS SINCE JUNE 2023 WHEN THE COMPANY SET THE FCR-26 RATES.
A.After experiencing a high level of price volatility with strong demand following the impacts of the Russia-Ukraine war in 2022, coal prices remained relatively stable over the 2023 through 2025 term. Coal prices began to decline in 2023 as coal purchasing activity slowed with falling natural gas prices. Coal inventories rose to elevated levels across the entire U.S. power sector through 2023, 2024, and into 2025. While coal prices fell with the decline in domestic demand, inflation-driven increases in mining, labor, and equipment costs created a new, higher price floor. Strong international demand also helped keep prices stable in 2023 and 2024. As domestic coal inventories returned closer to historical levels in 2025, purchasing activity accelerated and prompted a slight rise in prices. At the same time, international demand softened, which helped limit overall price movement. The combination of recovering domestic demand and easing international markets resulted in a modest overall price increase in 2025.
Natural gas prices remained volatile from 2023 through 2025 even as they moved below the extreme highs seen in 2022. In 2023 and 2024, strong production levels and an absence of extended extreme weather periods created a looser supply–demand balance, keeping Henry Hub prices low. By 2025, rising liquified natural gas (“LNG”) export demand and steady domestic consumption tightened balances and pushed prices higher. Over the three-year period, Henry Hub spot prices ranged from $1.23 to $12.97, averaging $2.53 in 2023, $2.25 in 2024, and $3.53 in 2025.
Q.FOR THE FCR-27 HISTORIC PERIOD, HOW DID THE COMPANY’S ACTUAL FUEL COSTS DIFFER FROM THE COMPANY’S BUDGETED FUEL COSTS? 
A.The Company’s actual fuel costs for operations during the historic period were $185 million, or 2.2%, above budgeted costs. The primary factor impacting actual costs was higher than expected coal generation, which was partially offset by lower cost of natural gas generation and purchases driven primarily by the lower prices of natural gas.
 Q.PLEASE FURTHER EXPLAIN THE INCREASE IN COAL GENERATION DURING THE HISTORICAL PERIOD. 
The coal generation during the historical period was approximately 22.3 million MWh, or 141%, higher than budget. This higher-than-expected generation was used to serve additional load during the historical period, primarily due to the lower marginal cost of delivered coal, which averaged $3.11/MMBtu, or 36% lower than the marginal cost for delivered coal reflected in the FCR-26 budget. A key driver in these lower costs is favorable contract negotiations for coal transportation services in which the Company negotiated 20% to 30% rate reductions. As part of the overall coal procurement strategy, the Company seeks to maintain a consistent supply chain to mitigate coal supply constraints and ensure reliable, predictable, and sustainable coal deliveries in volatile fuel markets. This approach will ensure reliability of supply in times of high market demand while optimizing fuel costs over time.

At the same time, natural gas will serve as a critical fuel source to support the substantial growth in electricity demand. Although production is expected to increase to meet rising demand, supply growth faces risks, including infrastructure constraints and continued capital discipline among producers. Q.HOW DOES THE COMPANY’S PROJECTED ENERGY SALES IN THE TEST PERIOD COMPARE TO THE FCR-26 BUDGET TEST PERIOD?
A.The Company anticipates significant increase in total energy sales driven primarily by higher retail load of 35.7 million MWh, or an increase of 21%. Wholesale sales are also projected to increase by 3.7 million MWh, or an increase of 94%, driven by new wholesale contracts signed since FCR-26. On a total basis, the projected load is 39.4 million MWh higher, or 22%. To serve this additional load, the Company projects increases across all of its mix of generation portfolio. More specifically, the Company projects higher generation of coal, natural gas, and nuclear fuel of 264%, 15%, and 5%, respectively. Similar to the historical period, the significant increase in coal generation during the test period is primarily attributed to the lower marginal cost of coal in FCR-27, which is 33% below the marginal coal cost assumptions included in FCR-26. Likewise, the FCR-26 budget did not include any operational must-run requirements for the Company’s generation fleet, but FCR-27 budget includes must-run requirements for Plant Bowen to address system constraints and ensure operational reliability. In addition, the Company’s solar generation and its solar PPAs combined are also expected to produce 46% higher generation during the FCR-27 test period. The higher generation of resources during the FCR-27 test period is projected to result in an additional $1.4 billion in fuel expenses. This increase will be mostly offset by the revenues from additional energy sales projected in the test period from both retail and wholesale customers.
Q.        WILL THE INCREASE IN LARGE LOAD CUSTOMERS IMPACT FUEL COST RESPONSIBILITY FOR EXISTING CUSTOMERS? 
A.         No. The increase in fuel expenses due to higher forecasted generation from resources reflects the growth in demand, which includes economic development growth from large loads. These large load customers have already provided benefits to customers by allowing the Company to keep base rates stable over the next three years. Moreover, the Company has been able to commit to a large load revenue forecast that will provide downward pressure on rates in the Company’s next base rate case. Therefore, the increase in generation is in part due to securing the loads that will provide this value. Georgia Power is not proposing to change the structure or framework for how fuel costs are recovered from current or future customers.

Additionally, much of the KWh sales from these large load customers is priced at RTP. For RTP load, a large load customer’s fuel contribution reflects the marginal costs incurred by the Company in each hour. Further, large load customers bear the risk of higher fuel prices and are assessed those costs directly during higher cost hours. Thus, large load customers will bear the appropriate cost responsibility in each hour for the fuel cost they impose on the Company and the overall revenues collected from these customers are projected to provide significant benefits that will reduce rate pressures for all Georgia Power customers.
Q. ARE THERE OTHER FACTORS PROVIDING DOWNWARD PRESSURE ON PROJECTED FUEL COSTS IN THE FCR-27 TEST PERIOD?
A.Compared to the FCR-26 test period, the fuel price for coal and nuclear generation, as well as purchases, are projected to decrease during the FCR-27 test period. The $/MWh fuel cost for coal and nuclear generation are projected to go down by 31% and 9%, respectively. The reduction in the delivered coal price forecast is based on newly negotiated transportation service agreements entered into by the Company since FCR-26, which provide significantly lower transportation prices for customers. The reduced fuel prices for coal and nuclear generation projects lower fuel expenses of $153 million and $42 million, respectively, during the FCR-27 test period. Likewise, the fuel price of affiliate and non-affiliate energy purchases from the pool are expected to be lower by 11% in FCR-27 test period, which results in $180 million of lower projected fuel costs. The update of lower natural gas prices in approved FCR-26 budget did not adjust the fuel expenses associated with energy purchases from the pool and therefore, the energy purchases reflected much higher underlying natural gas prices and resulted in higher fuel expenses in FCR-26 test period.
Q.ARE THERE ANY ADJUSTMENTS TO NET FUEL COSTS IN THE PROJECTED FCR-27 TEST PERIOD?
A.Yes, the Company procured firm transportation from Transco for future generation of new combined cycle units at Plants Bowen and Wansley, which were approved by the Commission through the All-Source Certification in 2025. The capacity payments for this pipeline are projected to begin in November 2027, prior to the in-service dates of those units. The Transco Southeast Supply Enhancement was the only project that could provide firm transportation service to Plants Bowen and Wansley to meet the units’ planned commercial operation dates. The Company was one of several shippers in this project and the project in-service date was driven by the requirements of a much larger shipper. Therefore, timely procurement of capacity to support Plants Bowen and Wansley required the Company to participate in a project that will be available prior to commercial operation of the plants. For this firm transportation capacity projected in the FCR-27 test period, the Company plans to monetize the capacity in the market to generate revenues and have reflected the potential proceeds from the sale to FCR to offset the firm transportation expenses in this filing.
VI.NATURAL GAS HEDGING
Q.IS THE COMPANY PROPOSING ANY CHANGES TO ITS CURRENT NATURAL GAS HEDGING PROGRAM?
A.Yes. While the current hedging program has worked as intended and continues to provide benefits for customers, the Company proposes to expand the hedging program given the expectation for increasing exposure to natural gas based on the increase in natural gas burn projections in the Company’s budget. 
Q.WHY DOES THE COMPANY ENGAGE IN NATURAL GAS HEDGING?
A.The Company engages in natural gas hedging to help mitigate the risk of upward price volatility in the natural gas market. Natural gas fired generation is projected to be the largest energy source for Georgia Power during the FCR-27 test period, with approximately 40% of the total projected fuel costs coming from natural gas. Because the cost of natural gas is a significant portion of the Company’s fuel costs and remains subject to significant volatility, it is appropriate for the Company to mitigate its natural gas costs by fixing and/or capping a portion of the prices it pays for gas. This benefits customers by providing greater stability in the Company’s fuel costs and reducing the impact of increasing gas prices.
Q.ARE NATURAL GAS PRICES STILL SUBJECT TO VOLATILITY?
A.Yes. Natural gas prices remain subject to significant volatility. As shown in Chart 3 below, this volatility can be seen in the wide range of spot prices at Henry Hub, which ranged from $1.23 to $12.97 per MMBtu in 2023 through 2025. Even small movements in gas prices can impact the Company’s fuel balance and, ultimately, costs to customers. For a Company like Georgia Power that anticipates consuming more than 250 million MMBtu of natural gas per year, a robust hedging program serves as a critical tool to mitigate upward price volatility for customers. 
Chart 3

In fact, as recently as late January 2026, Henry Hub natural gas prices rose above $30 per MMBtu during Winter Storm Fern, illustrating the ongoing potential for volatility in natural gas markets.
Q.WHAT ARE THE CHANGES REQUESTED BY THE COMPANY TO THE CURRENT HEDGING PROGRAM?
A.Based on the Commission approval in Docket No. 44902, the Company is currently allowed to hedge 40% of its budgeted natural gas burn up to 36 months. The Company is requesting the limits of the hedging program be increased to 60% of its budgeted natural gas burn up to 48 months. These adjustments will provide greater protection for customers against increasing exposure to price volatility in the natural gas market as seen historically in Chart 3 above. 
Q.IS THE PROJECTED NATURAL GAS BURN EXPECTED TO INCREASE IN THE FORECASTED PERIODS?
A.Yes. On an annual basis, the MMBtu of natural gas burn projected in the test period in FCR-27 is 522 million MMBtu compared to the 461 million MMBtu that were projected in the test period in FCR-26 Budget. This higher burn volume leads to increased exposure to price volatility in the natural gas market. By locking in additional percentage of natural gas over a longer horizon, the hedges can serve to reduce this significant price risk for the benefit of customers.
VII.INTERIM FUEL RIDER 
Q.SHOULD THE IFR MECHANISM BE CONTINUED?
A.Yes. The IFR mechanism should be continued because it helps mitigate the accumulation of a substantial over-recovered or under-recovered fuel balance, thereby benefiting customers and the Company. The Commission initially approved the IFR mechanism in FCR-21, and it has served as a useful tool for customers and the Company since then. 
Q.ARE ANY CHANGES TO THE IFR MECHANISM NECESSARY AT THIS TIME?
A.Yes. The Company proposes to increase the IFR threshold from the current amount of $200 million approved by the Commission to $300 million.
Q.HOW WOULD THIS CHANGE TO THE IFR MECHANISM BE APPLIED?
A.Consistent with the currently approved IFR mechanism, the $300 million will be applied in either direction of over-recovery or under-recovery balance with the current maximum percentage of 40% of cumulative rate change unaltered from the current approved level. The Company proposes that the timing and the filing requirements remain the same. The increase in threshold will provide greater rate stability for customers and will allow increased flexibility to absorb natural fuel price fluctuations and normal swings of over or under-recovery balances on a monthly basis. 
VIII.RENEWABLE RESOURCE PROCUREMENT COST RECOVERY
Q.HOW DOES GEORGIA POWER RECOVER ITS COSTS FOR DEVELOPING AND EVALUATING RENEWABLE REQUESTS FOR PROPOSALS?
A.Georgia Power structures its Renewable Requests for Proposals (“RFPs”) to collect Bid Fees and Winner’s Fees to defray the cost of the RFP process, to hire the Independent Evaluator, and to evaluate the submissions received in the RFP. Where the Bid Fees and Winners’ Fees do not cover the full cost of Georgia Power’s RFP administration and evaluation costs, those costs are currently recovered through the fuel clause.
Q.WHERE DID THE COMMISSION AUTHORIZE THE COMPANY TO RECOVER THESE COSTS THROUGH THE FUEL CLAUSE?
A.In resolution of Georgia Power’s 2016, 2019, and 2022 IRPs, the Commission approved Stipulations that specifically provided for the cost recovery of all Utility Scale and Distributed Generation RFP-related costs to go through fuel to the extent they were not recovered elsewhere. Since the benefits of renewable energy procured through PPAs accrue to customers in the form of lower fuel costs (and thus lower FCR rates) over time, it is appropriate to recover the costs associated with procuring these energy-benefiting resources through the FCR.
Q.WHAT OTHER RENEWABLE COSTS OR REVENUES FLOW THROUGH FUEL?
A.As presently approved, all RFP-related costs and revenues not otherwise recovered by Bid Fees or Winners Fees are recovered through the fuel clause. Such costs may include the Company’s labor costs from dedicated procurement personnel and the time spent by internal partners supporting the development of a given RFP (e.g. Transmission, Resource Planning, Environmental Affairs, etc.) as well as the cost of the Independent Evaluator, costs for the economic and transmission evaluations, and termination fees. For example, bid security retained by Georgia Power if a Short List bidder elects not to execute a PPA after being named to the Short List is credited to the fuel clause to the benefit of all customers. The same is true of any termination payment received for a PPA that is terminated early. In addition, once PPAs are in effect, all payments to counterparties are processed through the FCR.
The Commission also approved the Company to recover through fuel any incremental renewable and nonrenewable resources (“RNR”) costs not otherwise recovered through the RNR program as well as the charges collected from customers participating in the Company’s Clean and Renewable Energy Subscription (“CARES”) Program. Specifically, the Commission provided as follows: 
The following will be included in the fuel clause and recovered through the Fuel Cost Recovery Mechanism (“FCR”)” all revenues collected through the CARES Program, with the exception of the additional sum as described in Paragraph 23, and (ii) all appropriate costs that are not recovered elsewhere by the Company and are incurred in connection with the procurement of the aforementioned resources for subscription in the CARES Program. CARES Program costs and revenues which are to be included in FCR include but are not limited to the following: (i) the costs to implement and administer the CARES Program, (ii) the bid fees collected in connection with the CARES Program, the fees collected from subscribing customers, and (iii) the PPA that are executed to supply the CARES Program, including any payments for PPAs made by participants. 
Q.WHAT IS GEORGIA POWER’S REQUEST REGARDING RECOVERY OF RENEWABLE RFP RELATED COSTS GOING FORWARD?
A.Georgia Power requests that the Commission continue to authorize Georgia Power to recover all Renewable RFP (utility scale and distributed generation) and renewable subscription program-related costs not otherwise recovered by the Company through Bid Fees and Winner’s Fees through the fuel clause, consistent with past practice. 
IX.CONCLUSION
Q.IS THE COMPANY PROPOSING A SPECIFIC DATE BY WHICH TO FILE ITS NEXT FUEL CASE? 
A.No. The Company is not proposing a specific date for its next fuel case. Instead, the Company proposes to continue its semi-annual reporting of projected monthly fuel revenues, fuel expenses, monthly over or under collections, and projected fuel balances through the FCR-27 test period to help ensure actual fuel costs are recovered as expected. 
With the Company’s request in this case, the Company anticipates recovering the FCR-26 under-recovered fuel balance over 36 months, which extends beyond the FCR-27 projected test period by 12 months. By maintaining the flexibility to set the date for the next fuel case—rather than setting a firm date now that aligns with the end of the FCR-27 projected test period—the Company and the Commission will monitor the fuel balance and potentially eliminate the FCR-26 under-recovered fuel balance before the next fuel case is filed. The Company’s recommended change to the IFR mechanism will also enable the Company and the Commission to respond in a more timely and effective manner to fuel price volatility, thereby further mitigating the need to establish a fixed date for the next fuel case at this time.
For these reasons, a less prescriptive and more flexible, collaborative approach to determining the date of the next fuel filing is appropriate. Additionally, by not setting a specific date for the next filing, the Company and the Commission will avoid the need for formal deferral orders if future circumstances demonstrate that the fuel rates approved in this proceeding are sufficient beyond the FCR-27 test period.
Q.Please summarize the company’s request in this filing.
A.For the reasons set forth herein, the Company respectfully seeks approval of its Fuel Cost Recovery Application, which includes the following requests:
To set fuel rates to recover fuel costs as projected in the Company’s FCR-27 test period (June 2026 through May 2028); 
To increase the amount by which the Company can adjust rates through the IFR mechanism, giving the Company and the Commission more flexibility to timely address fuel price volatility; 
To expand the natural gas hedging program to better mitigate volatility of natural gas prices in the market; and
To authorize Georgia Power to recover all Renewable RFP (utility scale and distributed generation) and renewable subscription program-related costs not otherwise recovered by the Company through Bid Fees and Winner’s Fees, be recovered through the fuel clause.
Q.DOES THIS CONCLUDE YOUR TESTIMONY? 
A.Yes.
