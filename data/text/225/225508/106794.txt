COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before March 23, 2026. 


DEFINITIONS

As may be used in this document:  
1.“The Company,” “Georgia Power” or “The Utility” means Georgia Power Company and its present and former officers, employees, agents, representatives, directors, and all other persons acting or purporting to act on behalf of said Company.
The term “you” and “your” refer to “the Company.”
The term “person” means any natural person, corporation, corporate division, partnership, other unincorporated association, trust, government agency, or entity.
The term “document” or “documentation” shall have the broadest possible meaning under applicable law.  “Document” or “documentation” means every writing or record of every type and description that is in the possession, custody or control of the Company including, but not limited to, correspondence, memoranda, e-mails, drafts, workpapers, summaries, stenographic or handwritten notes, studies, notices, publications, books, pamphlets, reports, surveys, minutes or statistical compilations, computer and other electronic records or tapes or printouts, including, but not limited to, electronic mail files; and copies of such writing or records containing any commentary or notation whatsoever that does not appear in the original.  The term “document” or “documentation” further includes, by way of illustration and not limitation, schedules, progress schedules, time logs, drawings, computer disks, chart projections, time tables, summaries of other documents, minutes, surveys, work sheets, drawings, comparisons, evaluations, laboratory and testing reports, telephone call records, personal diaries, calendars, personal notebooks, personal reading files, transcripts, witness statements and indices.
The term “referring or relating to” means consisting of, containing, mentioning, suggesting, reflecting, concerning, regarding, summarizing, analyzing, discussing, involving, dealing with, emanating from, directed at, pertaining to in any way, or in any way logically or factually connected or associated with the matter discussed.
“And” and “or” as used herein shall be construed both conjunctively and disjunctively and each shall include the other whenever such construction will serve to bring within the scope of these discovery requests any information that would not otherwise be brought within their scope.
The singular as used herein shall include the plural and the masculine gender shall include the feminine and the neuter.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a person other than a natural person means to state:  the full name of the person and any names under which it conducts business; the present or last known address of the person; and, the present or last known telephone number of the person.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a document means to provide with respect to each document requested to be identified by these discovery requests a description of the document that is sufficient for purposes of a request to produce or a subpoena duces tecum, including the following:
the type of document (e.g., letter, memorandum, etc.);
the date of the document; the title or label of the document;
the Bates number or other identifier used to number the document for use in  litigation; the identity of the originator;
the identity of each person to whom it was sent;
the identity of each person to whom a copy or copies were sent;
a summary of the contents of the document;
the name and last known address of each person who presently has possession, custody or control of the document;
if any such document was, but is no longer, in your possession,  custody or control or is no longer in existence, state whether it: (1) is missing or lost; (2) has been destroyed; or (3) has been transferred voluntarily or involuntarily, and if so, state the circumstances surrounding the authorization for each such disposition and the date of such disposition.
“Identify”, “identifying” or identity” when used in reference to a communication should be read to include information regarding the date of the communication, whether the communication was written or oral, the identity of all parties and witnesses to the communication, the substance of what was said and/or transpired and, if written, the identity of the document(s) containing or referring to the communication.

Does the Company or Southern Company utilize any cybersecurity self-assessment frameworks (e.g., NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF), DOE Cybersecurity Capability Maturity Model (C2M2), or other)? 
If yes, when were these frameworks first implemented? 
Please provide a summary of the most recent evaluation results. 
Identify the number of cyber threats directed at the Company, by month, during the past year (2025). 
Were there any confirmed cyber breaches in 2025? If yes, for each incident: 
Describe immediate mitigation actions taken. 
Describe longer-term corrective measures implemented. 
Provide copies of all E-ISAC Bulletins in which the Company or Southern Company issued cybersecurity notifications in 2025. 
Provide copies of all cybersecurity-related notifications or information shared with GBI, CISA, CRISP, E-ISAC, U.S. Government agencies, or other relevant entities in 2025. 
Identify the number of physical security threats directed at the Company, by month, during the past year (2025). 
Were there any confirmed physical security breaches in 2025? If yes, for each incident: 
Describe immediate mitigation actions taken. 
Describe longer-term corrective measures implemented. 
Provide copies of all physical security notifications or information shared with GBI, GA Emergency Management and Homeland Security, CISA, E-ISAC, U.S. Government agencies, or other relevant entities in 2025. 


Provide all security-related notifications, updates, and status reports—including executive summaries, presentations, and/or slides—presented to executives in 2025 that address cyber or physical security. 
How does senior management communicate and oversee policies and procedures for assessing cybersecurity risks to organizational missions and business functions? 
How does Georgia Power define and assess: 
Overall enterprise risk? 
Cybersecurity-specific risk? 
What performance goals has Georgia Power adopted to ensure continuity of essential services while managing cybersecurity risk? 
How does Georgia Power maintain records of security threats? 
How is this information organized (e.g., benchmarks, goals, metrics)? 
What categories and subcategories are used for cyber and physical security information? 
For example, using E-ISAC subcategories: 
Cybersecurity: Malware, Ransomware, Threats, Phishing, Scanning, Initial Access, Others. 
Physical Security: Ballistic Damage, Vandalism, Intrusion, Theft, Assault, Suspicious Activity, Surveillance, Threats (online, phone, in person), Others. 
Please provide an update on current and potential impacts of artificial intelligence on Georgia Power’s operations. Are there any recent findings or case studies regarding malicious use of AI? 


What does Georgia Power identify as the greatest challenges in improving cybersecurity and physical security practices across critical infrastructure? 
What sources of cyber threat intelligence does Georgia Power rely on? How is this intelligence integrated into operational defenses? 
How does Georgia Power determine the appropriate level of investment in cybersecurity and physical security measures? 
What benchmarks or industry standards are used to guide spending decisions? 
How does the Company evaluate cost-effectiveness of security controls? 
What processes are in place to prevent unnecessary duplication of cybersecurity tools or services? 
Has Georgia Power conducted any third-party audits or independent assessments to validate that security spending aligns with risk exposure? If yes, provide summaries of findings and recommendations. 
What was Georgia Power’s total annual expenditure on cybersecurity programs for each of the past five years (2021–2025)? Break down costs by major categories (e.g., personnel, technology, training, compliance, incident response). 
How have these costs changed year-over-year, and what factors drove increases or decreases? 
What percentage of the overall IT budget is allocated to cybersecurity each year for the past five years? 
How does Georgia Power forecast future cybersecurity spending? What assumptions or risk scenarios inform these projections? 
Has Georgia Power implemented any cost-saving measures in its cybersecurity program without compromising security posture? Provide examples and outcomes.
