Bowen Unit 4 (June 7, 2024)
As a result of the Plant Bowen Unit 4 outage, the Company conducted an apparent cause analysis (“ACA”). An ACA is a structured, limited-scope investigation used to identify the apparent cause of an incident. ACAs are faster and less resource-intensive investigations than Root Cause Analyses, typically used to implement prompt corrective actions for single-cause events. The Bowen Instrumentation and Controls (“I&C”) team investigated the cause of the trip using Foxboro Distributed Control System (“DCS”) alarm journals and Burner Management System (“BMS”) signal trends. Control logic captured the BMS first-out as “low air flow;” however, one of the BMS air flow trips had pulsed true without a logical basis. Further investigation of that wiring circuit discovered that post-outage wire removal work was being done in the cabinet that contained that signal wire, among many others. As a result of the ACA investigation, the Company concluded that a mistake made by the electrical contractor working in that cabinet is the most plausible cause of the signal trends. The Company did not uncover evidence to the contrary or that supported any other cause. The Company has not asserted any claims against the contractor but continues to investigate its legal options. 

McDonough Unit 6 (September 25-26, 2024)
McDonough Unit 6, Event 10, resulted in a D1-6A Generator Turbine (“GT”) trip due to an air supply valve being closed by the contractor on the Thermostatic Control Assembly (“TCA”) regulating valve. Because this event was a Generation Availability Data System (“GADS”) event that resulted in a loss of megawatts (“MW”), a Corrective Action Report (“CAR”) was generated. This outage resulted from the contractor’s unfamiliarity with valve positioning. As a result of the outage, the Company held discussions with contractor leadership and all other contractors on site to emphasize the importance of involving the control room when conditions change. The Company has not asserted any claims against the contractor but continues to investigate its legal options. 

McIntosh 10 (April 19, 2024)
As a result of the McIntosh Unit 10 outage, Plant McIntosh completed a CAR to document the related findings. The apparent cause of this event was related to a contractor removing old electrical wire in the vicinity of a control system cabinet, causing a loose connection and tripping the steam turbine. The Company has not asserted any claims against the contractor but continues to investigate its legal options.

Extending the hedging window to 48 months allows hedge transactions to be executed across a broader range of market conditions and entry points, reducing the likelihood that a disproportionate share of hedges are placed during any single higher-priced period.  Increasing the maximum hedge volume to 60% reflects the continued growth in the Company’s natural gas burn and provides a greater portion of that exposure with price stability. The Company has not employed any third-party studies nor commissioned formal internal studies evaluating the proposal to expand the parameters of the hedge program. Rather, the Company relied on management’s experience administering the hedging program across multiple market conditions and the program’s objective of reducing exposure to adverse price volatility.
While longer-dated hedges may reflect prevailing market liquidity conditions and risk premia embedded in forward prices, these characteristics are inherent features of the natural gas forward market rather than incremental costs created by the hedging program.  The Company does not view the extension from 36 months to 48 months as introducing a material change in these characteristics or in the overall risk profile of the program, and considers the proposed window consistent with the objective of enhancing fuel cost stability.
The Company does not expect the extension of the hedging window from 36 to 48 months to materially increase liquidity risk or collateral requirements.
