Question: 

Please explain how the Company calculates the fuel cost used in determining the marginal cost of generation (item 2c above). Please explain whether this calculation is based on the contractual cost of fuel supply and transportation, the spot market price of fuel at that time, or other factors. If the coal supply or transportation contracts include any provisions for liquidated damages or other penalties for failing to meet minimum delivery quantities, take-or-pay provisions, or similar mechanisms, please explain if and how those provisions are accounted for in the fuel cost used in determining the marginal cost of generation from each coal unit.

Response:

Please see MFRP-13.5 submitted in the Company’s initial filing submitted February 17, 2026 for an overview of how marginal cost of fuel was determined for the projected test period. 
The marginal replacement costs for each gas-fired unit are typically updated once each gas trading day. These costs include the best estimate of the next day’s gas prices based on Intercontinental Exchange (“ICE”) average index pricing for the generating unit’s supply plus any pipeline fuel, transportation, and fuel use tax adders. 
The marginal replacement costs for each oil-fired unit are typically updated once each gas trading day. These costs include the best estimate of the next day’s fuel oil prices based on the Oil Price Information Services (“OPIS”) 10 AM rack average index pricing for the generating unit’s supply terminal plus a transportation adder to have oil delivered to the unit based on the current fuel oil supply agreement. 
Transportation for uncommitted coal is reflected in marginal replacement cost using the applicable active contract rate or recent carrier quote for the relevant origin/destination and time period. If the applicable transportation contract contains minimum‑volume / take‑or‑pay provisions with liquidated damages (or similar shortfall charges), the marginal replacement cost of transportation component reflects those provisions only to the extent they affect the incremental cost of additional deliveries based on the timing of need:
When forecast deliveries are below the contractual minimum, the component of transportation costs covered by the shortfall/liquidated damages is treated as fixed and excluded from the marginal replacement cost for incremental deliveries up to the minimum; and
Once forecast deliveries are at or above the contractual minimum, marginal replacement cost reflects the full applicable transportation rate for additional deliveries.

STF-PIA-1-11

Question: 

Has the Company evaluated the costs and benefits of modifying any of the coal or gas combined cycle units to reduce its minimum output level? If yes, please provide all analysis and documentation. If not, why not?

Response:

No, the Company has not evaluated the costs and benefits of modifying the coal or gas combined cycle units for the sole purpose of reducing the minimum load. However, coal and gas combined cycle units that undergo upgrades, such as fuel conversions for coal units and increased base load or improved heat rate for gas combined cycles, are evaluated for possible reductions in minimum load. Reducing the minimum load is limited by the design limitations of unit equipment, including emission control systems. Examples include exhaust flow minimums on steam turbines to prevent heavy water droplet erosion and low boiler exit temperatures that impact emissions equipment performance. A common limitation in the gas combined cycle fleet is steam attemperator capability at lower loads on the gas turbines. Therefore, minimum output reductions are only considered as part of other upgrades or fuel conversions where the unit specific limiting equipment can be modified.

STF-PIA-1-13

Question: 

Please explain why Bowen is primarily fueled from Appalachian and Illinois Basin coal mines instead of lower-cost Powder River Basin coal mines.

Response:

The coal units at Plant Bowen became operational from 1971 to 1975 and were designed for Bituminous coals primarily sourced from Appalachian and Illinois Basin (“IB”) coal mines. The use of Powder River Basin (PRB) coal was very limited in the 1970’s and didn’t really become widespread in the Southeast until the 80’s and 90’s with the western railroad expansion and 1990 Clean Air Act Amendments. The Company explored switching Plant Bowen over the years and the various studies have shown that converting the coal units at Plant Bowen to be able to burn PRB coals would require a large amount of capital investment. In addition, even though the PRB coal is cheaper than Appalachian and IB coal on average per ton basis, there is a capacity reduction associated with PRB in comparison due to lower coal quality of subbituminous coal. Given the significant capital investment required and the lower expected capacity output with PRB coal, the switching of Plant Bowen to PRB coal would be uneconomic for customers.

STF-PIA-1-7

Question: 

Please explain the commitment and dispatch process by which the Company decides which generators will be committed, including the inputs into that process.

Response:

The Company commits and dispatches generation resources through a centralized process designed to reliably meet forecasted system load at the lowest total production cost, consistent with reliability, operational, transmission, and regulatory constraints. Decisions are made for the entire pooled fleet, without regard to individual resource ownership. 

Production cost models are used to determine which units to commit (which units are on or off) and, once committed, the level at which each unit should be dispatched (output between high and low limit). The goal of the models is to select the combination of generating units that can meet forecasted load and reserve requirements at minimum total system cost, subject to all operational constraints. Inputs to the models include marginal fuel replacement costs, variable operating and maintenance expenses (“VOM”), in-plant fuel handling costs, emission allowance replacement cost, and incremental transmission losses. Generator characteristics include, but are not limited to, current unit status, heat rates, start-up costs, ramp rates, high/low limits, minimum up/down times, and planned/forced outages as well as operational limitations related to environmental, transmission, and fuel constraints.

STF-PIA-1-4

Question: 

Please explain how the Company determines which production costs are considered variable (and not fixed) on a short-term basis for determining the marginal cost of generation from a unit for purposes of economic dispatch.

Response:

On a shortterm, operational basis, the Company defines variable production costs as those that would not have been incurred but for the decision to produce an additional unit of energy (in megawatt hours (“MWh”)) from a specific generating unit. Costs that do not change with shortterm output decisions are treated as fixed and are excluded from dispatch calculations.  

As stated in Section 3.1 of the Intercompany Interchange Contract (“IIC”) the following items are considered as “variable dispatch costs”:

Marginal Replacement Fuel Cost
Cost of the incremental fuel required to produce additional MWhs.
Variable Operations & Maintenance (VOM)
O&M costs that vary with energy production (e.g., consumables, wear-related maintenance). 
Excludes fixed labor, salaried staffing, and routine fixed maintenance.
As specified in the IIC, all operating material, non-labor, and on-site contract labor charged to FERC Accounts 502 and 505 as well as all maintenance material, non-labor, and contract labor charged to FERC Accounts 512, 513, and 553.
In-Plant Fuel Handling Costs
Costs directly associated with moving, processing, or handling fuel as generation increases.
As specified in the IIC, in-plant fuel handling expenses are charged to FERC Account 501.
Emission Allowance Replacement Costs
Incremental NOx and SO₂ allowance costs attributable to additional generation.
Incremental Transmission Losses
Cost of additional energy required to serve load due to transmission losses. 
Reflected through loss factors in dispatch equations.
