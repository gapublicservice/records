STF-PIA-2-9

Question: 

Please see the statement on page 19 of the direct testimony of Mr. Houston and Mr. Berrigan that customers must begin paying firm gas transportation charges for pipeline expansions for the new Bowen and Wansley combined cycle generators “in November 2027, prior to the in-service dates of those units.”

a.Please document the Company’s efforts to secure a firm transportation agreement with service and payment commencing closer to the expected in-service dates for the Bowen and Wansley gas combined cycle generators.
b.If the Bowen or Wansley gas combined cycle units experience delays that push their in-service dates past current expectations, will the cost of the unused firm transportation reservation (net of any revenues from selling that capacity in the market) during that delay period be recovered from ratepayers?

Response:

a.  Transco’s Southeast Supply Enhancement Project (“SSE”) was the only project that the Company could have participated in for additional firm transportation (“FT”) service to meet the additional gas needs for the new combine cycle units at Plants Bowen and Wansley. Transco conducted an open season for the SSE project as required by FERC to solicit additional shippers for the SSE project. There have not been any other projects on Transco to date that the Company could have participated in that would have met the in-service date closer to the needs of the new combine cycle units. The in-service date of the additional FT was driven by other shippers and Transco’s desire to complete the project as soon as possible. 

b. While the Company currently does not expect any delay of in-service dates of those units, if there are any such delays, the Company will remarket any excess FT to the shippers and marketers on Transco to recover any cost of unneeded FT during the period prior to the new combine cycle in-service dates similar to its efforts to recover those costs during the projected test period as reflected in the FCR-27 budget. Any remaining FT costs net of revenue proceeds from the sale of capacity will be charged to the fuel clause to be recovered from customers.

For the three-year period covered by FCR-26 rates, covering June 1, 2023, to May 31, 2026, for each hour in which a coal or gas combined cycle unit operated even though its total variable O&M costs ($/MWh) (item 2b above) is greater than the day-ahead forecasted locational marginal energy cost at that location (item 1i above), please explain why the unit operated. Response:

The Company’s system of records does not produce detailed output records of why a coal or gas combined cycle unit operated even though its total variable O&M costs ($/MWh) were greater than the system lambda in a given hour. The primary function of the Company’s dispatch model is to minimize production cost while maintaining all necessary constraints (minimum up time, minimum down time, reserves, startup costs, minimum and maximum capacity, ramp rates, etc.). A combination of these and other constraints such as reliability, operational, transmission, and regulatory constraints could potentially result in the unit’s operation even though its total variable O&M costs ($/MWh) were greater than the system lambda.

Question: 

Please see the statement on page 17 of the direct testimony of Mr. Houston and Mr. Berrigan that the “FCR-27 budget includes must-run requirements for Plant Bowen to address system constraints and ensure operational reliability.' Please explain in detail:

a.What expectations for must-run requirements for Plant Bowen are included in the FCR-27 budget (such as the quantity of must-run generation and the time periods when it occurs)?
b.Why those must-run designations are required “to address system constraints and ensure operational reliability”?

Response:

Please see STF-PIA-2-8 Attachment for the must-run requirement assumptions for Plant Bowen included in this filing for the projected test period. In responding to this data request, the Company discovered the STF-PIA-1-2 Attachment D provided in the Company’s response to STF-PIA-1-2 included incorrect data for the must-run requirement assumptions included in the FCR-27 Budget. Specifically, REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED. The Company is providing the corrected data for the must-run requirement assumptions in this response. In addition, please see STF-PIA-1-10 Attachment A in the Company’s response to STF-PIA-1-10 for the projected minimum capacity for Plant Bowen included in the FCR-27 Budget.

In accordance with NERC Reliability Standards for planning and operating the bulk electric system, the Company performs regular analysis identifying potential transmission reliability needs (e.g. must-run) to maintain the reliability of the transmission system.  Planning may identify that generation is needed in certain locations and at certain time periods to mitigate system constraints due to generation and/or transmission contingencies.  Plant Bowen is uniquely situated such that generation at its location helps mitigate numerous anticipated power flow and thermal constraints during the FRC-27 test period.  Due to the need to ensure coal availability for Plant Bowen, it was necessary for Planning to provide guidance to model higher commitment and run times for Bowen units during the budgeting process.  In the shorter-term operating horizon, studies are performed using updated assumptions (generation and transmission outages) and system conditions (temperature and load forecast) to determine actual unit commitment needs to address system constraints and maintain reliability.
