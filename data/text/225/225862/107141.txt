This annual surveillance report for 2025 has been prepared in accordance with the ratemaking principles
established by the Georgia Public Service Commission (GPSC or the Commission) in Docket Nos. 4007,
6292, 6739, 9355, 14000, 18300, 19758, 25060, 31958, 36989, 42516, and 44280.

Pursuant to the GPSC’s Order Adopting Settlement Agreement issued in Docket No. 44280 (Order), earnings
above the upper limit of the 9.50% to 11.90% return on equity range are subject to sharing, with 40% being
applied to regulatory assets, 40% being directly refunded to customers, and the remaining 20% retained by
the Company. For the 12 months ended December 31, 2025, there were no earnings in excess of the 11.90%
equity range.

This filing contains six sections. These sections are as follows:

         •   Section 1 -- Retail Rate of Return Summary
         •   Section 2 -- Detailed Rate of Return Calculation
         •   Section 3 -- Regulatory Adjustments Worksheet
         •   Section 4 -- Wholesale Adjustments
         •   Section 5 -- Additional Supporting Schedules
         •   Section 6 -- Workpapers for the Regulatory Adjustments in Section 3

Sections 1 - 4 support the retail rate of return calculation and describe the ratemaking principles used in the
filing. Section 5 contains additional information requested by the GPSC Staff, such as the Company's Income
Statement, Balance Sheet, and a comparison of actual versus budgeted non-fuel O&M expenses. Section 6
includes workpapers supporting regulatory adjustments made in Section 3, as requested by the GPSC Staff
or as required in previous Commission Orders.

                        SUMMARY OF RATEMAKING PRINCIPLES USED IN
                           COMPUTING RETAIL RETURN ON EQUITY


The annual surveillance report has been prepared in accordance with the following ratemaking principles.

    1.    Nuclear refueling outage expenses are accounted for on a normalized basis, as established in
          Docket No. 4007.

    2.    Depreciation expense and reserve were reflected at the rates determined by the Commission in
          Docket No. 44280, beginning January 1, 2023. Accelerated Plant Vogtle depreciation recorded
          pursuant to the Accounting Order issued in Docket No. 6292 is also included in associated
          accumulated depreciation provision for nuclear production assets. Depreciation expense and
          reserve for Plant Vogtle Unit 3 and Common capital and construction cost placed in service in July
          2023 and Plant Vogtle Unit 4 capital and construction cost placed in service in April 2024 were
          reflected at the rates determined by the Commission in Docket Nos. 43838 and 29849.

    3.    Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, nuclear decommissioning expenses are
          not being accrued for Plants Hatch Units 1-2 and Plant Vogtle Units 1-2. Pursuant to the
          Commission’s Orders in Docket Nos. 43838 and 29849, $7.6 million and $7.8 million of nuclear
          decommissioning expenses are being accrued for Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4, respectively, to reflect
          funding to the external trust.

    4.    For the purpose of computing income taxes, pro forma interest expenses were calculated by
          applying the actual surveillance period weighted cost of debt to actual rate base.

    5.    Cash working capital was determined using the lead/lag days from the Company’s study as
          approved in Docket No. 44280.

    6.    Expenses associated with the Nuclear Energy Institute have been excluded from retail operations.

    7.    Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 18300, profits from off-system capacity/margin
          sales were allocated to retail using an allocation ratio of 80%.

    8.    Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 3840, 75% of the economy energy profits is
          returned to retail customers through the fuel clause, with 25% retained by the Company.

    9.    Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 3270, the Corporate Headquarters lease was
          reflected as an operating lease rather than as a finance lease.

    10.   Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 3840, institutional and goodwill advertising
          expenses have been removed from retail expenses.

    11.   Payables associated with capitalized materials and supplies were reflected as a reduction to rate
          base.

    12.   The wholesale portion of gains and/or losses from the sale of emission allowances was removed
          from retail operating income. The retail portion of such gains and/or losses is returned to customers
          through the fuel clause.

                     FOOTNOTES TO SECTION 3 REGULATORY ADJUSTMENTS

1.    EXTERNAL NUCLEAR DECOMMISSIONING
      This adjustment to rate base removes the external reserve for nuclear decommissioning from the Asset
      Retirement Obligation (ARO) Regulatory Liability (Account 254), as the Commission has directed nuclear
      decommissioning expenses be reflected based on external funding and the Company has no use of the funds.
      See Section 6, Workpaper 1 for details of this adjustment.

2.    CORPORATE HEADQUARTERS LEASE
      The Corporate Headquarters building is a finance lease on the Company’s books. This adjustment to rate base
      and operating income reflects this lease as an operating lease consistent with the Commission’s Order in
      Docket No. 3270. See Section 6, Workpaper 2 for details of this adjustment.

3.    CALCULATION OF ADJUSTED CASH WORKING CAPITAL
      This adjustment to rate base reflects the effects of the regulatory adjustments on cash working capital. See
      Section 6, Workpaper 3 for details of this adjustment.

4.    ACCUMULATED DEFERRED INCOME TAXES (ADITs) RELATED TO CONSTRUCTION WORK
      IN PROGRESS (CWIP)
      This adjustment to rate base removes, for retail ratemaking purposes, all accumulated deferred income taxes
      related to construction work in progress. See Section 6, Workpaper 4 for details of this adjustment.

5.    UNREGULATED OUTDOOR LIGHTING
      This adjustment to rate base and operating income reflects the removal of revenues and costs associated with
      Unregulated Outdoor Lighting Services. See Section 6, Workpaper 5 for details of this adjustment.

6.    DEMAND SIDE MANAGEMENT (DSM) PROGRAMS
      This adjustment to rate base and operating income removes the revenue for the additional sum associated with
      the Company’s DSM program pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 36499 and the ADITs
      associated with the DSM over/under-recovery and Residential Investment for Saving Energy (RISE) pilot
      program regulatory asset. The adjustment also effectively removes the carrying charge on DSM over/(under)-
      recovery since it is credited to customers through the DSM tariff. See Section 6, Workpaper 6 for details of
      this adjustment.

7.    INSTITUTIONAL & GOODWILL ADVERTISING
      This adjustment to operating income removes all institutional and goodwill advertising expenses from retail
      operating expenses. See Section 6, Workpaper 7 for details of this adjustment.

8.    ECONOMY & OPPORTUNITY SALES ENERGY PROFITS
      In Docket No. 3840, the Commission ruled 75% of profits on economy energy sales be returned to retail
      customers through the fuel clause, with the Company retaining the remaining 25%. This adjustment to
      operating income removes the 25% from income. See Section 6, Workpaper 8 for details of this adjustment.

9.    NUCLEAR ENERGY INSTITUTE (NEI) DUES
      This adjustment to operating income removes the expenses associated with NEI from operating expenses. See
      Section 6, Workpaper 9 for details of this adjustment.

10.   SOUTHERN COMPANY GENERATION NON-TERRITORIAL SALES
      This adjustment to operating income removes the wholesale revenues and expenses associated with non-
      territorial power marketing transactions. See Section 6, Workpaper 10 for details of this adjustment.

11.   PURCHASED POWER AGREEMENT ADDITIONAL SUMS
      This adjustment to operating income removes the revenue for the additional sums associated with various
      PPAs pursuant to the Commission’s Orders in Docket Nos. 4900, 22528, 37854, 38877, 41596, 41734, 34218,
      42625, 43085, 44160, 43814, and 44942. See Section 6, Workpaper 11 for details of this adjustment.

12.   CARRYING CHARGE ON OVER/(UNDER)-RECOVERED FUEL COSTS
      This adjustment to operating income effectively removes the carrying charge on over/(under)-recovered fuel
      costs to revenues since they are credited to customers through the fuel clause. See Section 6, Workpaper 12
      for details of this adjustment.

13.   FERC BOUNDARY LAND / LICENSING FEES
      This adjustment to operating income reflects removal from retail operations of any shoreline maintenance
      costs in excess of licensing fees collected from lake lot lessees in association with FERC Boundary Land,
      pursuant to the Order issued in Docket 25060. See Section 6, Workpaper 13, for details of this adjustment.

14.   WIRELESS CO-LOCATION REVENUES
      This adjustment to operating income reflects removal of twenty percent of any revenues net of program
      expenses associated with the Co-location Wireless Equipment program that utilizes the Company’s regulated
      assets pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280. See Section 6, Workpaper 14 for details of
      this adjustment.

15.   WHOLESALE TO RETAIL MARKET DIFFERENTIAL ADJUSTMENT
      This adjustment to operating income adds a market differential adjustment to retail cost of service relating to
      certain wholesale assets transferred to retail rate base pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No.
      26550. See Section 6, Workpaper 15 for details of this adjustment.

16.   OFF-SYSTEM CAPACITY/MARGIN SALES
      In Docket No. 18300, the Commission ruled 80% of off-system capacity/margin sales be returned to retail
      customers, with the Company retaining the remaining 20%. This adjustment to operating income removes the
      20% from income. See Section 6, Workpaper 16 for details of this adjustment.

17.   CAPITALIZED PPA INTEREST EXPENSE
      This adjustment to operating income removes the interest portion of the capacity payment associated with the
      West Georgia, Dahlberg, Monroe, and Mid-Georgia finance lease PPAs. See Section 6, Workpaper 17 for
      details of this adjustment.

18.   EXECUTIVE FINANCIAL PLANNING SERVICES
      This adjustment removes from operating income any executive financial planning services expenses
      attributable to Georgia Power Company, Southern Company Services, and Southern Nuclear employees
      pursuant to the Commission's Order in Docket No. 44280. See Section 6, Workpaper 18 for details of this
      adjustment.

19.   STOCK-BASED COMPENSATION
      This adjustment removes from operating income any stock-based compensation expenses attributable to
      Georgia Power Company, Southern Company Services, and Southern Nuclear employees pursuant to the
      Commission's Order in Docket No. 44280. See Section 6, Workpaper 19 for details of this adjustment.

20.   PLANT VOGTLE UNIT 3 & COMMON EQUITY FINANCING COSTS
      This adjustment includes the deferred equity financing cost of Plant Vogtle Unit 3 and Common capital and
      construction costs excluded from base rates or the NCCR tariff until Plant Vogtle Unit 4 commercial
      operation, pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 43838. The equity financing cost is recorded for

21.   CALCULATION OF SYNCHRONIZED INTEREST EXPENSE

      expense applicable to rate base. See Section 6, Workpaper 21 for details of this adjustment.

  1) In July 2025, the FASB issued ASU 2025-05, Financial Instruments – Credit Losses (Topic 326): Measurement of Credit Losses for
     Accounts Receivable and Contract Assets (ASU 2025-05), which allows an entity to elect a practical expedient for measuring expected
     credit losses on current accounts receivable and current contract assets arising from transactions accounted for as revenues from
     contracts customers. This expedient allows an entity to assume that current economic conditions as of the balance sheet date do not
     change for the remaining life of the asset. ASU 2025-05 is effective for fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2025 and interim
     periods within fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2026. As permitted, the Registrants have elected to early adopt the practical
     expedient as of December 31, 2025 and applied its provisions prospectively to the provision for uncollectable accounts. The adoption
     of ASU 2025‑05 did not have a material impact on the consolidated results of operations, cash flows or financial condition of the
     Registrants.


  2) On April 17, 2025, the State of Georgia enacted tax legislation that reduces the corporate income tax rate from 5.39% to 5.19%
     effective for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025. HB 111 lowers the state's income tax rate for both individuals and
     corporations. The legislation triggered a Q2 2025 remeasurement of the Company’s existing accumulated deferred income tax
     balances, resulting in the establishment of a regulatory liability representing an accrual for certain tax benefits.

===== Section 1/Section 1 - Summary of Ratemaking Principles.docx =====
SUMMARY OF RATEMAKING PRINCIPLES USED IN
COMPUTING RETAIL RETURN ON EQUITY


The annual surveillance report has been prepared in accordance with the following ratemaking principles. 

Nuclear refueling outage expenses are accounted for on a normalized basis, as established in Docket No. 4007.

Depreciation expense and reserve were reflected at the rates determined by the Commission in Docket No. 44280, beginning January 1, 2023.  Accelerated Plant Vogtle depreciation recorded pursuant to the Accounting Order issued in Docket No. 6292 is also included in associated accumulated depreciation provision for nuclear production assets. Depreciation expense and reserve for Plant Vogtle Unit 3 and Common capital and construction cost placed in service in July 2023 and Plant Vogtle Unit 4 capital and construction cost placed in service in April 2024 were reflected at the rates determined by the Commission in Docket Nos. 43838 and 29849. 

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, nuclear decommissioning expenses are not being accrued for Plants Hatch Units 1-2 and Plant Vogtle Units 1-2. Pursuant to the Commission’s Orders in Docket Nos. 43838 and 29849, $7.6 million and $7.8 million of nuclear decommissioning expenses are being accrued for Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4, respectively, to reflect funding to the external trust.

For the purpose of computing income taxes, pro forma interest expenses were calculated by applying the actual surveillance period weighted cost of debt to actual rate base.

Cash working capital was determined using the lead/lag days from the Company’s study as approved in Docket No. 44280.

Expenses associated with the Nuclear Energy Institute have been excluded from retail operations.
Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 18300, profits from off-system capacity/margin sales were allocated to retail using an allocation ratio of 80%.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 3840, 75% of the economy energy profits is returned to retail customers through the fuel clause, with 25% retained by the Company.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 3270, the Corporate Headquarters lease was reflected as an operating lease rather than as a finance lease.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 3840, institutional and goodwill advertising expenses have been removed from retail expenses.

Payables associated with capitalized materials and supplies were reflected as a reduction to rate base.

The wholesale portion of gains and/or losses from the sale of emission allowances was removed from retail operating income.  The retail portion of such gains and/or losses is returned to customers through the fuel clause.

No donations, lobbying expenses, or executive tax preparation fees were included in retail expenses; these expenses are recorded below the line.

Proceeds from waste ash sales were included as a reduction to actual retail expenses on an “as it falls” basis.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 3936, revenues and costs associated with Unregulated Outdoor Lighting have been excluded from retail operations.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Orders in Docket Nos. 4900, 25036, 34218, 37854, 38877, 41596, 41734, 42625, 43085, 44160, 43814, and 44942, the Company is allowed to record an “additional sum” related to purchased power agreements (PPAs).  During 2025, the Company recorded such sums in association with: Mid-Georgia; Dahlberg; Heard County (Exelon); Blue Canyon Wind; Advanced Solar Initiative (ASI) Prime and REDI PPAs; Harris; West Georgia; Distributed Generation (DG) Solar PPAs, Wansley 7, Harris 2 and Monroe 1&2, Utility Scale Renewable RFP 2022/2023, and 2023 DG RFP. These “additional sums” are effectively removed from retail operating income for surveillance reporting purposes.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, the Company expensed $31.4 million for storm damage reserves.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, the Company expensed $5.1 million for environmental remediation reserves. 

Effective January 1, 2003 and December 31, 2005, the Company adopted SFAS No. 143 – “Accounting for Asset Retirement Obligations” and FASB Interpretation No. 47 – “Conditional Asset Retirement Obligations”, respectively (collectively now ASC 410).  The Company records the appropriate regulatory assets and liability adjustments on the balance sheet to reconcile the treatment of costs under ASC 410 and the current treatment of such costs for regulatory accounting purposes. The Company has received approval from the Commission allowing the continued accrual of other future retirement costs for long-lived assets that the Company does not have a legal obligation to retire. 

The Company adopted SFAS No. 158 – “Employers’ Accounting for Defined Benefit Pension Plans and Other Postretirement Plans” (now ASC 715) effective December 31, 2006.  The Company records the appropriate regulatory assets and liability adjustments on the balance sheet to recognize the treatment of costs under ASC 715 and the current treatment of such costs for regulatory accounting purposes. 

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 42516, the ECCR tariff includes the asset retirement obligation costs for compliance with Coal Combustion Residual Rule, which are being recovered over a rolling four-year period pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, twenty percent of any revenues net of program expenses associated with the Co-location Wireless Equipment program on the Company’s regulated assets have been removed for retail regulatory purposes.  

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 25060, any costs attributable to the shoreline management program in excess of revenues collected through the FERC Boundary Land and Associated License fees have been removed from retail expenses.  

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 25060, any gains and losses related to the Flat Bill program have been recorded above the line and included in retail operations.  

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket Nos. 40161, 42310, and 44160, all revenues and costs associated with the Company’s renewable programs have been recorded above the line and included in retail operations.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, the Company is allowed to record an “additional sum” related to its DSM programs. This “additional sum” is effectively removed from retail operating income for surveillance reporting purposes.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, the excess amortization of the remaining net book values of Plant McIntosh Unit 1 and Plant Langdale Units 5-6 were reclassified as a regulatory liability and have been fully amortized through December 2025. Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 42310, the remaining net book values of Plant Hammond Units 1-4 have been reclassified as a regulatory asset and are being amortized over a period equal to the remaining useful life approved by the Commission in Docket No. 36989, as approved by the Commission in Docket No. 42516.
Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, capital and O&M costs for the electric vehicle infrastructure and initiatives as well as the associated revenues and tax credits are included in the Company’s retail cost of service.
Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, any revenue erosion in 2023 through 2025 from the conversion of certain retail customers to the Time of Use – Food and Drink tariff (“TOU-FD”) is deferred to a regulatory asset and amortized and recovered from all TOU-FD customers in the following year.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 42516, certain expenses associated with the implementation of software and cloud computing projects are being deferred to a regulatory asset. Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, the projected balance as of December 31, 2022 is being amortized over a 5-year period beginning January 1, 2023. 
Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 42516, certain incremental COVID-19 costs were deferred to a regulatory asset and have been fully amortized through December 2025 as approved by the Commission in Docket No. 44280.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44160, the remaining net book values of Plant Wansley Units 1, 2, and 5A CT and Plant Boulevard Unit 1 CT were reclassified as regulatory assets. Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, a portion of depreciation expense for Plants Scherer Units 1-3 and Bowen Units 1-2 is being deferred over the remaining useful lives as approved by the Commission in Docket No. 44280.

Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, the remeasurement of accumulated deferred income taxes associated with the reduction of the state income tax rate from 5.39% to 5.19% effective January 1, 2025, was deferred to a regulatory liability following the signing of House Bill 111 in April 2025.

   Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Docket No. 44280, 26 U.S. Code § 45U nuclear production tax credits (PTCs) earned by the Company in 2024 have been deferred to a regulatory liability. INSTITUTIONAL & GOODWILL ADVERTISINGThis adjustment to operating income removes all institutional and goodwill advertising expenses from retail operating expenses.  See Section 6, Workpaper 7 for details of this adjustment.

ECONOMY & OPPORTUNITY SALES ENERGY PROFITSIn Docket No. 3840, the Commission ruled 75% of profits on economy energy sales be returned to retail customers through the fuel clause, with the Company retaining the remaining 25%. NUCLEAR ENERGY INSTITUTE (NEI) DUESThis adjustment to operating income removes the expenses associated with NEI from operating expenses.  See Section 6, Workpaper 9 for details of this adjustment.

SOUTHERN COMPANY GENERATION NON-TERRITORIAL SALESThis adjustment to operating income removes the wholesale revenues and expenses associated with non-territorial power marketing transactions.  See Section 6, Workpaper 10 for details of this adjustment.

PURCHASED POWER AGREEMENT ADDITIONAL SUMSThis adjustment to operating income removes the revenue for the additional sums associated with various PPAs pursuant to the Commission’s Orders in Docket Nos. 4900, 22528, 37854, 38877, 41596, 41734, 34218, 42625, 43085, 44160, 43814, and 44942.  See Section 6, Workpaper 11 for details of this adjustment.

CARRYING CHARGE ON OVER/(UNDER)-RECOVERED FUEL COSTSThis adjustment to operating income effectively removes the carrying charge on over/(under)-recovered fuel costs to revenues since they are credited to customers through the fuel clause.  See Section 6, Workpaper 12 for details of this adjustment.

FERC BOUNDARY LAND / LICENSING FEESThis adjustment to operating income reflects removal from retail operations of any shoreline maintenance costs in excess of licensing fees collected from lake lot lessees in association with FERC Boundary Land, pursuant to the Order issued in Docket 25060.
