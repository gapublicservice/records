                                           Background

This Internal Auditing report presents the results of the Georgia Power Company Storm Reserve
Cost Review. This engagement was performed at the request of GPC management.

Hurricane Helene made landfall on September 26, 2024, as a Category 4 hurricane. For Georgia
Power, Hurricane Helene was the most destructive storm in the company’s 140-year history. More
than 1,000 miles of electric supply lines were impacted and over 1.5 million customers lost power.
Within a week, by October 3, 2024, Georgia Power had restored power to more than 1.2 million
customers (80% of those affected). The initial estimated total restoration costs related to this
hurricane totaled approximately $1.1 billion. Georgia Power has refined its estimate of total storm
cost incurred to be $878 million as of March 31, 2025, with $732 million charged to the Company’s
storm damage reserve after a portion of the amounts originally included in the storm damage
regulatory assets were capitalized to property, plant, and equipment.

                                            Objective

The objective of this engagement was to review the processes and assess the appropriateness of
charges to the GPC Storm Reserve. Due to the severity of the impacts of the storm and the
swiftness of the Company’s response, particular emphasis was placed upon evaluating the potential
for charges being inappropriately assigned to the Storm Reserve.

Jamie Gollott, Audit Manager, Candra Oster, Senior Auditor and Blake Thomas, Audit Advisor,
conducted the engagement under the direction of Brigham Young, Internal Auditing Manager.
Inquiries can be directed to Todd Warren, Chief Audit Executive, at 678-697-1398.

                                              Scope

The scope of this engagement included all categories of charges to the GPC Storm Reserve for
Hurricane Helene in 2024.

Procedures performed during the engagement included a review of the GPC Storm Reserve
governance processes and procedures including potential regulatory requirements, performing
walkthroughs with responsible personnel, accounting of and categorizing Storm Reserve expense
data, understanding processes for identifying and re-classifying capital expenditures, review of
internal and third-party expenses, and review of accruals and associated processes.

The period covered by this engagement was as of March 31, 2025.

                                           Conclusion

In conclusion, there is an opportunity to strengthen the overall governance of the processes related
to storm costs. GPC is currently working on addressing the improvement opportunities noted in
this review.

                           Findings and Recommendations

Governance Enhancements
Finding           Governance of storm-related expenses should be strengthened.

Background          Formalized governing processes should be more widely deployed in the
                    oversight and management of storm-related expenses including defined
                    oversight activities for storm-related procurement charges to ensure
                    compliance with GPC Storm Accounting Guidelines, Business Expense
                    Guidelines and the Southern Company Contracts Manual. We noted the
                    following exceptions as part of our review:

                    Some internal expenses had limited support or did not fully comply with
                    established procedures. In comparison to the total dollars spent, there
                    were minimal instances identified where straight-time labor ($277K) and
                    procurement card expenses ($47K) were inappropriately charged to the
                    Storm Reserve. In addition, the basis for fleet allocation charges was
                    unable to be determined.

                    There were instances where third-party invoices were paid and approved
                    without fully compliant support and/or did not align with contract terms
                    and conditions. Some third-party vendors utilized for storm recovery
                    services did not have up to date contracts in place. There were instances
                    where non-Purchase Order invoices were utilized even when contracts
                    with third-party vendors were in place.

                    Some outstanding accruals related to IOUs / manpower / mutual
                    assistance were unsupported.
Recommendation      The Company should ensure defined roles and responsibilities, and
                    documentation requirements are communicated to all responsible
                    personnel. Oversight activities should be strengthened to validate storm-
                    related charges, including adherence to internal guidelines and
                    procedures.

                    Procure to pay processes should be followed including ensuring contracts
                    and work authorizations are in place and communicated to appropriate
                    personnel. GPC personnel should strengthen its processes to ensure
                    vendors are held accountable for complying with their respective
                    contracts. Supply Chain Management should be engaged when new
                    vendors are needed or when changes are needed to existing contracts.
                    Base camp leaders should be responsible for the review of invoices prior
                    to payment to the respective vendors.

                    Processes for reconciling outstanding non-invoiced vendors to remaining
                    accruals balance should be developed and performed. Follow-up of these

Management’s   As further documented below, the Company is undertaking additional
Action Items   activities to increase focus and communication of the Company’s Storm
               Accounting Guidelines, Southern Company Business Expense
               Procedures, and the Southern Company Contracts Manual. These efforts
               will be further strengthened by reinforcing policies and guidelines prior
               to storms and other emergency situations to ensure responsible personnel
               understand requirements of associated governing procedures.

               The Company has reclassified certain labor dollars and procurement card
               expenses identified by Internal Audit that were inappropriately charged
               to the Storm Reserve.
               In addition to Internal Audit’s sample testing of invoices and expense
               statements, the GPC Budgeting organization will complete an additional
               review of invoices and expense statements related to Hurricane Helene
               by the end of the year. A reclass of any inappropriate charges in the Storm
               Reserve will be recorded in December 2025.

               Procure to pay processes for storms and other emergency situations have
               been strengthened by the following actions:

                  •   Mutual Assistance (“MA”) invoices will be reviewed within the
                      GPC Finance organization prior to approval by the Company’s
                      Storm Manager.
                  •   As part of the Southeastern Electric Exchange Mutual Assistance
                      agreement, the Company is working with other members to
                      review and strengthen the guidelines around invoicing and
                      payment in order to shorten the timeline for receiving the initial
                      estimates and final invoices, as well as clarify what supporting
                      documentation is expected as part of the invoice package. Earlier
                      estimates will facilitate more accurate accruals for the MA
                      companies.
                  •   Since May 2025, the Company has had secured contracts with ten
                      base camp vendors. In addition to the base camp vendors, the
                      Company has entered into various contracts with additional storm
                      restoration vendors that provide damage assessments and
                      distribution line restoration. This effort provides the Company
                      with a more proactive approach for contract-based billing for
                      storm related expenses.
