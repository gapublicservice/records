Question: 

Referring to the foot note for Actual Completion Year *. ‘The year construction is complete, and the asset is placed in service.  Trailing charges may occur past initial completion to finalize billing and field close the project.’ 
What year does ‘Present Year Budget’ refer to?
What year does ‘Present Year Actuals’ refer to?
Explain the negative amounts sometimes seen for Actuals.
Explain in detail the actual expenditure for each feeder listed below including the date of expenditure.
A5612-IYG
A6512-CFT
B1032-ADV
Q4122-CKZ
Q4132-CKZ
V0522-CVJ


Response:

The year in ‘Present Year Budget’ refers to the project’s budget for the current fiscal year of the filing. For the Grid Investment Program Semi-Annual Report June 2025 report, that would reflect year 2025.

The year in ‘Present Year Actuals’ refers to the project’s actuals to date for the current fiscal year of the filing. For the Grid Investment Program Semi-Annual Report June 2025 report, that would include actuals to date through the end of June 2025.

The two most common drivers of negative amounts seen in actuals are material returns and/or accrual reversals. 
Material returns will result in a credit to a project as it progresses through the accounting month-end closeout process and unused materials are returned to stock. 
Accrual reversals that were higher than the actual expenses will result in a credit to the project. 

The expenditures for these feeders were incurred to enhance system reliability. Key spending areas included:

Engineering and Design: Detailed planning to enhance the reliability of each feeder, in accordance with the feeder’s investment package types.

Land Acquisition: Costs incurred to obtain the necessary land rights to install equipment and materials on these feeders.

Construction: Costs incurred to procure and install equipment and materials on these feeders.

Please see STF-PIA-37-4 Attachment for the monthly expenditures of each feeder from January 2023 through June 2025 as provided in the report.

STF-PIA-37-11

Question: 

Explain the process the Company has developed to optimize GIP projects that may be included with the need for projects to serve increasing large load.

Response:

The Grid Investment Program (GIP) objectives remain unchanged: distribution investments focus on improving customer reliability and transmission investments focus on replacing aging system assets. To that end, each investment package targets underperforming feeders and aging transmission lines and substations. The Company upgrades facilities to current standards as part of GIP projects, which may increase capacity; however, the purpose of the GIP is not tied to increasing large load and its investments are not used to fund projects for large load growth. If large loads affect a GIP project, the Company may continue the original scope of the GIP project or cancel the GIP project and initiate a non-GIP project to accommodate any impacts from large load. This optimization process is coordinated through cross functional stakeholder meetings to align work scopes and ensure efficient execution.
