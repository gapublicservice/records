COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before March 17, 2026. 

DEFINITIONS

As may be used in this document:  
1.“The Company,” “Georgia Power” or “The Utility” means Georgia Power Company and its present and former officers, employees, agents, representatives, directors, and all other persons acting or purporting to act on behalf of said Company.
The term “you” and “your” refer to “the Company.”
The term “person” means any natural person, corporation, corporate division, partnership, other unincorporated association, trust, government agency, or entity.
The term “document” or “documentation” shall have the broadest possible meaning under applicable law.  “Document” or “documentation” means every writing or record of every type and description that is in the possession, custody or control of the Company including, but not limited to, correspondence, memoranda, e-mails, drafts, workpapers, summaries, stenographic or handwritten notes, studies, notices, publications, books, pamphlets, reports, surveys, minutes or statistical compilations, computer and other electronic records or tapes or printouts, including, but not limited to, electronic mail files; and copies of such writing or records containing any commentary or notation whatsoever that does not appear in the original.  The term “document” or “documentation” further includes, by way of illustration and not limitation, schedules, progress schedules, time logs, drawings, computer disks, chart projections, time tables, summaries of other documents, minutes, surveys, work sheets, drawings, comparisons, evaluations, laboratory and testing reports, telephone call records, personal diaries, calendars, personal notebooks, personal reading files, transcripts, witness statements and indices.
The term “referring or relating to” means consisting of, containing, mentioning, suggesting, reflecting, concerning, regarding, summarizing, analyzing, discussing, involving, dealing with, emanating from, directed at, pertaining to in any way, or in any way logically or factually connected or associated with the matter discussed.
“And” and “or” as used herein shall be construed both conjunctively and disjunctively and each shall include the other whenever such construction will serve to bring within the scope of these discovery requests any information that would not otherwise be brought within their scope.
The singular as used herein shall include the plural and the masculine gender shall include the feminine and the neuter.    
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a person other than a natural person means to state:  the full name of the person and any names under which it conducts business; the present or last known address of the person; and, the present or last known telephone number of the person.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a document means to provide with respect to each document requested to be identified by these discovery requests a description of the document that is sufficient for purposes of a request to produce or a subpoena duces tecum, including the following:
the type of document (e.g., letter, memorandum, etc.);
the date of the document; the title or label of the document;
the Bates number or other identifier used to number the document for use in litigation; the identity of the originator;
the identity of each person to whom it was sent;
the identity of each person to whom a copy or copies were sent;
a summary of the contents of the document;
the name and last known address of each person who presently has possession, custody or control of the document;
if any such document was, but is no longer, in your possession, custody or control or is no longer in existence, state whether it: (1) is missing or lost; (2) has been destroyed; or (3) has been transferred voluntarily or involuntarily, and if so, state the circumstances surrounding the authorization for each such disposition and the date of such disposition.
“Identify”, “identifying” or identity” when used in reference to a communication should be read to include information regarding the date of the communication, whether the communication was written or oral, the identity of all parties and witnesses to the communication, the substance of what was said and/or transpired and, if written, the identity of the document(s) containing or referring to the communication.

Refer to MFR-4 filed on February 17, 2026 that summarizes costs by categories for all the 2022-2025 storms combined at line 1, which shows $7.269 million in storm costs remain in the storm regulatory asset from “Regular Payroll, Related Benefits/Other Loadings, and Payroll Tax Costs - GPC Employees.”  Refer also to the panel Direct Testimony of Cleve L. Fann, Josh J. Stallings, and Matthew S. Berrigan at 25, line 12, that mentions “excess straight time.” Confirm that 100% of the $7.269 million regular payroll and related costs for GPC employees remaining in the storm cost regulatory asset is made up of “excess straight time.” If not confirmed, explain why not.

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-PIA-29-17 that stated that at the time of the response, “no casualty loss deductions for the remaining tax basis of assets retired for Hurricane Helene have been estimated for 2024 tax return.”  
Provide an update describing whether a casualty loss deduction was estimated and taken on the 2024 income tax return when filed and the impact on the balance of ADIT as of December 31, 2025. 
If the casualty loss deduction was taken, provide copies of all workpapers relied upon to calculate the deduction in electronic format with all formulas intact. 
If the casualty loss deduction was not taken, then explain specifically why the Company did not take this deduction. 
Indicate if the casualty loss deduction was taken for each of the other storms at issue in this proceeding. If it was not taken, then explain specifically why the Company did not take this deduction for each of the other storms at issue in this proceeding. In addition, provide the remaining tax basis of all assets retired as a result of each storm at issue in this proceeding, including Hurricane Helene.

Confirm that the Company discontinued depreciation expense on the assets retired in conjunction with each of the storms at issue in this proceeding. If confirmed, then provide the reduction in depreciation expense by month as a result of these retirements for each storm.

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-PIA-29-18 that stated that at the time of the response, “no tax repair allowance deductions for the tax basis of new assets installed have been estimated for Hurricane Helene for the 2024 tax return.”  Provide an update describing whether a tax repair allowance deduction was estimated and taken on the 2024 income tax return when filed and the impact on the balance of ADIT as of December 31, 2025. If one was taken, provide copies of all workpapers relied upon to reflect the casualty loss deduction in electronic format with all formulas intact.

Provide a copy of the 2024 federal income tax return.

Refer to Exhibit___(CLF-JJS-MSB) Pg. 1 at line 8, which shows an amount of $30.521 million currently in rates applicable to storm damage. Refer also to MFR 7.1 at line 17, which shows the current storm damage accrual amount of $31.419 million.
Provide the calculation of the $30.521 million shown on Exhibit___(CLF-JJS-MSB) Pg. 1 at line 8 in electronic format with all formulas in place along with all supporting documentation necessary to calculate this amount.
Explain all reasons why the two amounts differ.

Refer to the Direct Testimony of Cleve L. Fann, Josh J. Stallings, and Matthew S. Berrigan at 29-30 related to the Company’s proposal to utilize IRC 45U Production Tax Credits to partially offset and lower the revenue requirement deficiency associated with storm recovery in its filing.  Refer also to MFR 7.2 and Exhibit___(CLF-JJS-MSB) Pg. 5, which shows the Company’s proposal to use the amortization of the PTCs as a reduction to rate base and amortization expense for the test period from June 1, 2026 through May 31, 2027.  
Describe the Company’s rationale for using 25% of the 45U PTC regulatory liability for this purpose. Address why the Company did not propose a greater or lesser percentage for this purpose and identify and describe all impediments to using a greater percentage for this purpose.
Confirm that 25% of the 45U PTC regulatory liability to be used to offset the storm cost revenue requirement deficiency will be fully utilized by May 31, 2027.  
Indicate whether the Company intends for there to be another rate increase effective June 1, 2027 after the amount used to lower the revenue requirement deficiency in rates has been fully utilized.  Explain your answer.

Refer to the panel Direct Testimony of Cleve L. Fann, Josh J. Stallings, and Matthew S. Berrigan at 12 wherein they state: “This process was developed using successful past practice and industry best practices and has evolved over time.” 
Provide all support relied on for the claim “this process was developed using . . . industry best practices,” including, but not limited to, all studies and analyses comparing the Company’s practices to those of other utilities and ranking the Company’s practices at the time of each study or analysis in comparison to other utilities to ensure the Company’s practices were the “industry best practices.”
Provide all support relied on for the claim “this process was developed using . . . industry best practices,” with respect to the use of technology to determine resource requirements in total and locationally. Identify all technology tools used for this purpose, especially during the Company’s response to Hurricane Helene.
Provide all support relied on for the claim “this process was developed using . . . industry best practices,” with respect to the use of technology to manage the resources acquired to minimize costs as storm response activities were performed and then completed, including the locational movements of those resources, the sizing of those resources, and the release of those resources. Identify all technology tools used for this purpose, especially during the Company’s response to Hurricane Helene.
Identify and describe all metrics, tools, and technology used by the Company to balance or optimize the quantity of resources/cost of resources against restoration times, if any.
