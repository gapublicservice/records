STF-SCR-3-5

Question: 

Is the Company proposing to make any adjustments to the Municipal Franchise Fee, Environmental Cost Recovery Tariff, or Demand Side Management Tariff in response to the other adjustments required in this proceeding? Please explain how any adjustments (or non-adjustments) are in compliance with the ARP Stipulation.

Response:

Yes, the Company will propose adjustments to lower the Environmental Cost Recovery (“ECCR”) and Demand Side Management (“DSM”) tariff rider percentages in response to the adjustment of traditional base rate tariffs. The adjustments to these rider tariffs will offset the increase in revenue from those rider tariffs that would have resulted from an increase to traditional base rates for storm recovery as proposed in this filing. No adjustment will be made to the Municipal Franchise Fee (“MFF”) given the percentage of MFF obligation paid to the cities by the Company is directly tied to the total retail revenues collected. 

Therefore, the adjustment to base rates for storm cost recovery will have no net impact on ECCR or DSM revenue collections. The adjustments to ECCR and DSM will reduce the collection of revenue from these riders in an amount equal to the increase in revenue that would have resulted from higher traditional base rate tariffs. 

While the ARP Stipulation does not specifically contemplate adjustments to these base tariff riders as a result of storm cost recovery, this approach is aligned with the ARP Stipulation language to not adjust base rates except for storm damage costs. Further, the ARP Stipulation provides for the true up of MFF and DSM tariffs until the Company’s next base rate case.

The Company does not track the number of customers Georgia Power’s customer service teams have guided through the process of participating in LIHEAP. In addition to the number of customers currently enrolled, the Company estimates that approximately 115 thousand more customers may be eligible for the IQD program based on the age and household income criteria.

STF-SCR-3-8

Question: 

Refer to page 18, lines 4-8 of the Fann, Stallings and Berrigan testimony. For the Georgia Power Outage Map, please explain how the company evaluates, and provide any metrics available, for how the Company measures its clarity, accuracy, and usefulness.

Response:

The Company’s Outage Map is evaluated for clarity, accuracy, and usefulness through operational validation and customer engagement indicators during storm events. Accuracy is assessed by comparing information displayed on the Outage Map to underlying outage management system data and field reports as outages are identified, updated, and restored.

Clarity and usefulness are evaluated through internal reviews and customer interactions across digital and social media channels. For example, during Hurricane Helene, the Company monitored customer engagement and sentiment related to outage communications, including interactions associated with outage information. The Company’s corporate affairs team continually monitors engagement and sentiment from our customers from initial interaction through case resolution. 

Total customer interactions during Hurricane Helene exceeded the Company’s total 2024 year-to-date interactions prior to the storm, reflecting an increase of more than 100% during the storm period. To support outage communications, the Company’s social media team published more than 500 individual pieces of content over a 22-day period, reaching more than 15 million viewers. Overall, positive sentiment significantly outweighed neutral and negative sentiment throughout the storm response, with approximately 78.5% of sentiment classified as positive.
