PUBLIC INTEREST ADVOCACY STAFF’S RESPONSE TO JOINT PETITION FOR REHEARING, RECONSIDERATION, CLARIFICATION, AND ORAL ARGUMENT

The Public Interest Advocacy Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (hereinafter “Staff”) hereby file this Response to the January 9, 2026 “Joint Petition for Rehearing, Reconsideration, Clarification, and Oral Argument on Behalf of Georgia Interfaith Power and Light, Southface Institute, The Sierra Club, and the Southern Alliance for Clean Energy.” In their Petition, intervenors in the above-captioned proceedings, Georgia Interfaith Power and Light (“GIPL”), Southface Institute (“Southface”), and the Sierra Club (hereinafter collectively referred to as “the Petitioners”), seek various remedies with respect to the Commission’s Final Order adopting a Stipulation between Staff and Georgia Power Company (hereinafter referred to as “Georgia Power” or “the Company”).
In general, the Petitioners challenge the sufficiency and legality of the Commission’s final order, issued on December 30, 2025, pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-3A-4 et seq., which provides for the certification of capacity resources proposed by the Company to meet the electricity needs of its customers during the 2029-2031 period. 
As discussed more fully in the Commission’s Order, the Company filed applications on July 30, 2025 for certificates of public need and convenience for twenty-eight (28) capacity resources to come online between 2029 and 2031. Generally, the resources consist of five combined-cycle (“CC”) natural-gas powered generating units, nine Company-owned Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”), two paired Solar and BESS facilities, and eleven power purchase agreements (“PPAs”). Seventeen of the requested resources were identified by the Company pursuant to an All-Source Request for Proposals (“RFP”) under which the Company solicited competitive bids and proposals beginning in 2024. Eleven of the requested resources were identified and negotiated by the Company outside of a competitive RFP. As correctly pointed out by the Petitioners, the total amount of capacity resources for which the Company sought certification represents an expansion of the Company’s overall service capacity of unprecedented magnitude.
As was discussed throughout the proceedings in this case, most of the capacity for which Georgia Power is requesting certification is the result of projected load from large-load data center projects. Staff’s primary concern, accordingly, has been that certification of expensive capacity assets to serve these projects would result in increased costs to the Company and increased rates for the Company’s customers if the anticipated load does not materialize as forecasted.
To resolve those concerns, Staff and the Company entered into a Stipulation allowing for certification of the proposed capacity resources conditioned on certain guarantees made by the Company. During the presentation of evidence in this case on December 16, 2025, PIA Staff Witness Robert Trokey testified in support of the Stipulation, stating that it addressed the core concern of protecting ratepayers from speculative load forecasts by safeguarding existing customers from premature and excessive investment. The Stipulation requires Georgia Power to impute the projected revenues from large load customers from 2029-2031, which ensures at least $8.50 cents per month in downward pressure on residential rates during that period, even if the new load does not materialize as forecasted. Notably, Georgia Power assumes a financial risk through 2031, which provides a significant buffer for ratepayers.
Staff is also charged with closely monitoring Georgia Power’s compliance and will make recommendations as necessary. Normally, the Company has the right to request piecemeal increases in the approved construction costs during the construction process. However, per the Stipulation, the Company will not seek to recover any cost increases on any project sooner than six months before commercial operation date. 

ARGUMENT AND CITATION OF AUTHORITIES
Under O.C.G.A. § 46-3A-3(a), after January 31, 1992, no utility shall commence the construction of an electric plant, sell an existing plant or any portion thereof which is included in the retail rate base or which has been certified, enter into a long-term purchase of electric power, or make expenditures for a demand-side capacity option for serving the utility's Georgia retail customers without having first obtained from the commission a certificate that public convenience and necessity requires, or will require, such construction, sale, purchase, or expenditure.
Georgia Power’s Application for the Certification of Capacity Resources is governed by O.C.G.A § 46-3A-4(a), which provides: 
The commission shall issue a certificate upon a finding that there is or will be a need for the proposed capacity resource at the time that the proposed resource is proposed to be utilized to assure an economical and reliable supply of electric power and energy (emphasis added) for the Georgia retail customers of a utility, that the certificate is required by the public convenience and necessity, and the certificate complies with the provisions of this chapter and the rules of the commission.

The clear intent of this code section is to ensure that Georgia’ electric service rate payers are assured an economic and reliable source of electricity from their public utilities. The Stipulation between Staff and Georgia Power will provide precisely the economic and reliable service the statute contemplates. By agreeing to impute projected large-load revenues, such that a minimum of $8.50 of downward pressure is placed on rates for existing customers, the Company has assumed the risk of shifting costs, which was a primary issue for Staff and the public in this case. Thus, the public is assured an economical and reliable supply of electric power which will not burden them with increased electric rates in the Company’s next rate case.
Petitioners argue that the Company has failed to demonstrate a “need” for the additional capacity sought by Georgia Power to serve retail customers by 2031. As part of its All-Source Certification Application, the Company presented testimony and data that forecasted load growth in excess of 8,400 MW by 2031. This forecast, including its assumptions and the methodology of the Load Realization Model, were subjected to scrutiny and criticism by the Petitioners, Staff, and other intervenors. However, the Commission does not need to adopt the Company’s load forecast in order to meet the requirements for certification. O.C.G.A. § 46-3A-4(a) requires the finding of a current or future capacity need at the time that the proposed resource is proposed to be utilized. It is reasonable for the Commission to find a need for the proposed assets after considering the testimony of the parties, along with the materialization of committed load that occurred both before and after such testimony was made. 
The Petitioners also argue that the Commission’s Order is legally insufficient, claiming that it does not show the certified resources will be needed “at the time that the proposed resource is proposed to be utilized.” However, in Paragraph 1, of the Commission’s “Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law” in the Order, the Commission clearly specified that: “The Commission further finds that the evidence presented supports the finding that the resources will provide a reliable and economical supply of electric power when needed by Georgia retail customers….” (emphasis added). The Petitioners fail to show how the language used in the Commission Order differs materially from the statutory language. By finding that the resources will provide reliable and economical electric power when needed by customers, the Commission has clearly demonstrated that the resources will be needed at the time the proposed resources are proposed to be utilized.
The Petitioners further assert that the Commission’s Final Order approving the Stipulation is deficient pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-3A-5(b) for failing to explicitly adopt a load forecast. However, no provision in § 46-3A-5, modifies the requirement in § 46-3A-4(a) that the Commission “shall” issue a certificate provided the Company otherwise meets the requirements of that code section.


CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons, Staff respectfully requests the Commission DENY the Petitioners’ Petition for Rehearing, Reconsideration, Clarification, and Oral Argument in its entirety.
