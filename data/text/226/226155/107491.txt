All nuclear fuel costs are amortized to FERC account 518-00001, Nuclear Fuel Expense, and recovered through FCR, although nuclear fuel costs transfer through different accounts on the balance sheet as fuel moves through the phases of procurement, construction, and in-service as explained in further below.
 
The Company procures uranium concentrates (U3O8), conversion services (UF6), and enrichment services (EUP) as needed to maintain adequate inventory for future use. The charges related to uranium concentrates, conversion services, and enrichment services are tracked by reload in FERC account 120.2, Nuclear fuel materials and assemblies-stock (“Stock”) account. The cost components of the fuel cycle (i.e., uranium, conversion, and enrichment) held in FERC account 120.2, Stock, are moved to FERC account 120.1, Fuel in Process, incrementally at the time such component would enter the construction process.    Once the construction of the nuclear fuel is complete, the total cost is returned to FERC account 120.2, Stock, until the time it is ready to load into the reactor during the refueling outage. During the refueling outage, a portion of the existing fuel in the core in FERC account 120.3, Nuclear fuel assemblies in Reactor (“Reactor”), is discharged to FERC account 120.4, Spent Nuclear Fuel, and new fuel is reclassed from the FERC account 120.2, Stock, to the FERC account 120.3, Reactor. Accordingly, multiple generations of fuel assemblies are present in the reactor core at any given time. Fuel costs for each reload in FERC account 120.3, Reactor, are allocated to fuel assembly groups proportionate to the anticipated thermal energy produced by each fuel assembly and the cost of fuel is amortized to FERC account 518-00001, distributed over the periods when the thermal energy is produced.

Given the complexity of various stages of the fuel cycle and related accounting process and the multitude of different FERC accounts within the accounting of nuclear fuel, the components are not readily available at the requested cost level when nuclear fuel is expensed. Please see MFRH-11 for the monthly nuclear fuel expense by plant unit during the historical period.

Please see STF-JKA-5-8 Attachment A for the breakdown of monthly costs in nuclear fuel in process during the historical period, which is recorded to construction work in progress (“CWIP”) and excluded from retail cost of service.

STF-JKA-5-23

Question: 

Please provide an update since the last FCR proceeding of the efforts the Company has made to harden generating units against severe weather impacts.  

Response:

Since filing the last FCR proceeding, Southern Company and Georgia Power continue to investigate and improve, where required, combined cycle (“CC”) and simple cycle combustion turbine (“CT”) equipment as well as steam unit equipment that could be impacted by a winter storm. NERC EOP-012-3, which updated NERC EOP-012-2, requires each generator owner to implement freeze protection measures to protect Generator Cold Weather Critical Components that provide the capability to operate at the generating unit(s)’ Extreme Cold Weather Temperature with sustained concurrent twenty (20) mph (32 km/h) wind speed for (i) a period of not less than twelve (12) continuous hours, or (ii) the maximum operational duration for intermittent energy resources if less than twelve (12) continuous hours. NERC EOP-012-3 compliance was required by October 2025. Previous Southern Company and Georgia Power design standards to meet NERC EOP-012-2 required plant designs or design modifications to include measures for equipment operation down to zero-degree Fahrenheit temperatures for a 72-hour period with 25 mile per hour winds.

Work scope execution has been ongoing to address the original zero-degree temperature requirement, which will exceed the revised NERC expectations to operate at the defined Extreme Cold Weather Temperature above as required by NERC EOP-012-3. The Company has completed majority of the work in progress at Plant Robins CT, Plant McIntosh CC and CT, Plant McDonough CC and CT, Plant McManus CT, Plant Bowen, Plant Scherer, Plant Yates 6-7, and Plant Wilson, with minor work scope remaining at only certain plants. In addition to efforts taken in response to NERC EOP-012-2 and EOP-012-3, Georgia Power added radiant heaters to inverter housing at GPC-owned solar sites at Robins Solar, Fort Stewart, and Fort Gordon.
