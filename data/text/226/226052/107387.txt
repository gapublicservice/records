I. Background
Reporting Period: July 1, 2025 – December 31, 2025

Plant Sites Covered: Bowen and Scherer

Background: On May 9, 2024, the Environmental Protection Agency (“EPA”) finalized the
111 Greenhouse Gas (“GHG”) Rules for new and existing sources in the power sector. The
rules require states to develop plans to establish performance standards for existing
sources based on the “presumptively approvable” standards published by EPA. For existing
coal-steam resources, compliance can be demonstrated by one of three pathways: (1) retire
by 2032; (2) co-fire natural gas by 2030 and retire by 2039; or (3) install and operate
carbon capture and storage (“CCS”) by 2032 with no forced retirement date. As an outcome
of the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) filed with the Georgia Public Service
Commission (“GPSC”) (Docket No. 56002), Georgia Power Company (“the Company”) was
authorized to pursue the natural gas co-firing pathway as the compliance strategy for Plants
Bowen and Scherer. Per the stipulated agreement, the Company is filing this report to
update the Commission on the status of the 111 GHG Rules and progress on the evaluation
of the natural gas co-firing pathway for Plants Bowen and Scherer.

II. Regulatory
Current Legal Status of 111 GHG Rules:

The following updates on the legal status of the 111 GHG Rules reflect action since the last
update the Company provided in the 2025 Environmental Compliance Strategy (“ECS”) filed
on January 31, 2025, through the semi-annual period ending December 31, 2025.

On February 19, 2025, and April 25, 2025, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit
granted EPA's motions requesting an abeyance and then a continuing abeyance,
respectively, of the litigation over the 2024 111 GHG Rules.

On June 17, 2025, EPA published a proposed rule that, if finalized, would repeal all or a
portion of the 2024 111 GHG Rules. The proposed rule includes a primary proposal and an
alternative proposal. Under the primary proposal, EPA would repeal all GHG emissions
standards promulgated under Section 111 of the Clean Air Act for fossil fuel-fired power
plants, including both the 2015 and 2024 111 GHG Rules, based on a finding that these
sources do not contribute significantly to global climate change. Under the alternative

proposal, EPA would repeal the emission guidelines for existing units, as well as the 90%
CCS requirement for new combustion turbines (“CTs”) operated over a 40% annual capacity
factor, which includes both simple cycle and combined cycle CTs. New CTs would still be
required to comply with the efficiency-based standards in the 111 GHG Rules. These
standards are numerical (lb CO2/MWh) limits that depend on the capacity factor and fuel
mix of the individual source.

According to EPA’s Unified Agenda released in September 2025, EPA expected to issue a
final rule addressing the repeal of the 2024 111 GHG Rules by December 2025. However,
due to regulatory delays, subsequent action is now expected in spring 2026.

Impact on Continued Operation of Plants Bowen and Scherer:

If finalized as proposed, the repeal of the 111 GHG Rules would reduce compliance costs
and influence the continued operation of Plants Bowen and Scherer. Both the primary and
alternative repeal pathways eliminate requirements for modified operation and retirement
of existing coal-steam units. For the Company, this repeal would mean avoiding costs to
convert boilers to co-fire natural gas and coal, costs to build new gas laterals, and natural
gas transportation costs expected in a co-firing scenario. In addition, without mandated
retirement, replacement generation for coal-steam units will not be required to address the
111 GHG Rules. The repeal is expected to provide long‑term customer cost savings by
avoiding incremental environmental retrofit capital expenditures and deferring the need for
higher‑cost replacement capacity, while allowing continued operation of Plants Bowen and
Scherer through the end of their useful lives.

III. Technical Solutions Overview and Compliance Actions
Summary of Technical Solutions Considered or Implemented:

The 111 GHG Rules offer multiple compliance pathways for existing coal units; however, the
Company is evaluating the natural gas co-firing pathway consistent with the compliance
strategy approved by the Commission. As the proposed repeal is not yet final and thus the
111 GHG Rules remain in effect, the Company must continue investing to assess this
potential compliance pathway.

This technical solution consists of retrofitting the existing coal-fired boilers at Plants Bowen
and Scherer with natural gas-fired burners. These burners would be added to and in some
cases may replace some of the coal-fired burners in each boiler. Because the boilers could
fire coal in a co-firing scenario, emissions controls to reduce emissions of nitrogen oxides,
sulfur dioxides, particulate matter, and mercury would remain in service. Specific emissions
controls are unique to each boiler and summarized in more detail in the Company’s ECS
filed on March 31, 2026.

The Company has engaged General Electric Vernova (“GE”) to conduct engineering studies
at Plants Bowen and Scherer to evaluate natural gas co-firing. In these studies, GE inputs

historical operating data into a computer model of each boiler retrofitted with natural gas
burners to evaluate boiler operating and emissions performance. The Company has
requested GE study two scenarios: 100% gas firing and another percentage equivalent to
the minimum megawatt load for each unit. These two scenarios are important to
understand unit performance across the operating range and provide flexibility while
maintaining 111 GHG Rules compliance. The 111 GHG Rules’ 40% co-fire presumptively
approvable standard is determined on an annual heat input basis. Therefore, the study of a
full gas-conversion scenario will evaluate the option where the Company could operate
each unit on gas or coal while considering fuel costs and environmental compliance. At the
time of this filing, GE has completed modeling of Plant Bowen units and continues
preliminary modeling for the Plant Scherer units. The Company anticipates providing more
detail on the results of the studies during the next semi-annual compliance report after
reports are finalized.

In addition to boiler retrofits, another key component of the co-firing compliance pathway
is gas infrastructure. At present, neither Plant Bowen nor Plant Scherer has gas service to
the facility. Gas pipeline laterals to each site would need to be constructed as part of this
compliance strategy. Construction of a gas lateral to Plant Bowen is planned to support the
recently approved Units 7-8 combined cycle CTs. For both facilities, further evaluation
would be required to determine gas supply and firm transportation (“FT”) availability and
timing to satisfy the 40% co-firing requirement. If the company were to conclude sufficient
gas is not available to meet the presumptively approvable standard by 2030, the Company
would pursue an alternate standard through the State Plan development process subject to
EPA approval. Given the proposed repeal of the 111 GHG Rules, the Company is not
currently seeking to secure FT or other gas contracts specifically for natural gas co-firing at
Plants Bowen and Scherer.

IV. Capital Investment Forecast
With final action on the proposed repeal of the 111 GHG Rules pending, the Company has
not and does not plan to invest significant capital to comply with the 111 GHG Rules for
Plants Bowen and Scherer. The Company will complete the ongoing engineering studies to
support the 111 GHG Rules compliance strategy.

The Company is deferring the costs of the engineering studies being performed by GE
through preliminary survey and investigation (“PS&I”). A summary of the PS&I spend for
