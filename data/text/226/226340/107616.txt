Question: 

Refer to STF- PIA-2-3 Attachment C TRADE SECRET.xlsx

a.Please provide the reason for each must run designation for each unit and month.
b.For each month, provide supporting documentation and any workpapers determining the need for must run designation.

Response:

The units were placed on the day-ahead transmission must-run list at the direction of the Reliability Coordinator (“RC”) based on the RC’s assessment of operational requirements to maintain transmission system reliability (e.g., voltage support, avoidance of transmission line overloads, etc.).

While the RC function is performed by Southern Company employees within Bulk Power Operations, the RC is responsible for reliability across the entire Southeastern Reliability Coordinator Area. This area includes transmission assets and generating units owned by Southern Company–affiliated entities as well as nonaffiliated entities, including those owned by other utilities and Independent Power Producers (“IPPs”). In this role, the RC function operates on behalf of all members of the RC Area and is required to treat all entities equally, regardless of ownership. Because reliability issues typically involve nonpublic transmission information subject to FERC Standards of Conduct, the RC does not disclose the specific reasons a unit is designated as mustrun beyond stating that the designation is necessary to maintain transmission system reliability.

Response:

Confirmed.

Please see STF-JKA-7-5 Attachment. The Company does not track renewable integration costs. Please see the response to subpart (c) below. 

Renewable integration costs are reflected operationally through incremental production costs associated with committing and holding existing flexible resources online to manage renewable variability. In practice, these integration impacts materialize through normal system dispatch and appear in production and fuel costs, even though they are driven by the need to manage renewable variability rather than by additional load. Therefore, the renewable integration costs are embedded in the fuel costs recovered through the fuel tariff and captured in the FCR over/under balance.

Response:

a.The fuel cost or credit values for Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”) in the referenced attachment represent the difference between the energy cost incurred to charge the generation and the energy credit, or benefit, provided to the grid for the discharge of the generation. Given the optimal use of the BESS is to charge during low peak hours and discharge during high peak hours, the benefit of the BESS is shown as a net credit to the overall fuel cost for customers. Please note, the self-owned BESS during the historical period is assumed to be charged from and discharged to the pool and therefore, any net impact shown for the BESS has a direct offset to the pool purchases.
b.Please see STF-JKA-7-2 Attachments A and B for the cost components of BESS from October 2024, when Mossy Branch BESS first became operational, to December 2025. STF-JKA-7-2 Attachment B provides for the year-to-date correction of the net BESS costs through June 2025 booked in July 2025.
c.Please see STF-JKA-7-2 Attachment B. July 2025 includes a year-to-date correction of the net BESS costs through June 2025 following the methodology as explained in the response to subpart (a) above.
