===== DKT 44280 STF-PIA-40/STF-PIA-40-10.docx =====
STF-PIA-40-10

Question: 

Other than the Right of First Refusal (ROFR) period, explain how the Company is ensuring that it does not compete with or crowd-out private market investments.

Response:

The Company complies with the 2022 Base Rate Case Order, including the Electric Transportation Programs Term Sheet for Community Charging, which ensures it does not compete or crowd-out private market investment. In addition to the ROFR, the Term Sheet states:

The Plan will focus on underserved areas -- both rural and income qualified, but which are not located within one (1) mile of a designated Alternative Fuel Corridor or any other location for which funding is provided through the Georgia Electric Vehicle Infrastructure Deployment Plan. 

Rural areas and Income Qualified areas will be defined as “counties identified as Tier 1 and 2 by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs (DCA).” See: https://www.dca.ga.gov/sites/default/files/jtc2022.pdf [dca.ga.gov].

===== DKT 44280 STF-PIA-40/STF-PIA-40-8.docx =====
STF-PIA-40-8

Question: 

If available, provide the cost-benefit and economic impact analysis used by the Company for each of its community charging stations. Provide all formulas in an excel workbook.

Response:

The Company has not performed a specific cost-benefit analysis for each of its community charging stations. 
The Community Charging program benefits Georgia Power’s customers and communities throughout the state as demand from residential, commercial, and industrial customers alike continues to increase for electric transportation technologies, which increases the demand and need for the infrastructure required to support these technologies. The Community Charging program enables greater adoption of these technologies by providing public charging along travel corridors throughout the state, especially in rural and underserved communities that have not received many similar infrastructure investments from the private sector.
