Dear Ms. Tanner:

Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) hereby submits the
enclosed tariff information reflecting the Commission’s approval and adoption of the
stipulation between the Public Interest Advocacy Staff, Georgia Power, the Georgia
Association of Manufacturers, the Metropolitan Atlanta Rapid Transit Authority, and
Resource Supply Management, which was initially filed in the above-referenced dockets
on May 12, 2026. The tariff information includes redline and clean versions of the fuel
tariffs and Temporary Base Rate Adjustment Riders shown on the first tab of the enclosed
electronic spreadsheet. Beginning June 1, 2026, these riders will be used in the billing
process to facilitate application of the approved base rate adjustment pending full
implementation of final non-fuel tariff rates. The adjustments will be applied in the
appropriate manner as shown on the second tab of the spreadsheet.

The Company intends to make a full compliance filing by the end of June 2026.

Please call me at 404-506-7106 should you have any questions regarding this filing.

Fuel Costs shall be the cost of:

   (1) fossil, nuclear, bio-mass (including renewable) fuel and emission allowances (including
       credits, offsets, taxes, tariffs or other mechanisms intended to establish a market price for
       carbon, carbon dioxide and/or other greenhouse gases) consumed in the Company's own
       plants, and the Company's share of fossil, nuclear, and bio-mass (including renewable) fuel
       and emission allowances (including credits, offsets, taxes, tariffs or other mechanisms
       intended to establish a market price for carbon, carbon dioxide and/or other greenhouse
       gases) consumed in jointly owned or leased plants; plus
   (2) the identifiable fossil, nuclear, bio-mass (including renewable) fuel and emission allowance
       costs (including credits, offsets, taxes, tariffs or other mechanisms intended to establish a
       market price for carbon, carbon dioxide and/or other greenhouse gases) associated with
       energy purchased for reasons other than identified in (3) below; plus
   (3) the net energy cost of energy purchases, exclusive of capacity or demand charges
       (irrespective of the designation assigned to such transaction) when such energy is
       purchased on an economic dispatch basis. Included therein may be such costs as the
       charges for energy purchases and the charges as a result of scheduled outages, all such
       kinds of energy being purchased by the buyer to substitute for its own higher cost of
       energy; less

                                              DEFINITIONS:

TRANSMISSION CUSTOMERS:

Customers who receive electrical service from a transmission line of 46 kV or higher, or from a substation
or a pole-mounted or pad-mounted transformer, which is dedicated to them and whose metering is at or
emulated to 46 kV or more.

PRIMARY DISTRIBUTION CUSTOMERS:

Customers who receive electrical service from a distribution line at a voltage between 2.4 kV and 25 kV,
or a substation or a pole-mounted or pad-mounted transformer whose high-side and low-side voltage is
between 2.4 kV and 25 kV.

SECONDARY DISTRIBUTION CUSTOMERS:

Any customer not defined as a Transmission or Primary Distribution Customer.

                                             DEFINITIONS:
ON-PEAK:
The On-Peak period is defined as the hours starting at 2:00 p.m. and ending at 7:00 p.m. Monday
through Friday for the calendar months of June through September (Summer Months). The above hours
on days in which the following holidays are observed shall be considered Off-Peak: Independence Day
and Labor Day.
OFF-PEAK:
The Off-Peak period is defined as the hours between 7:00 a.m. and ending at 11:00 p.m. for weekends,
holidays, and the calendar months of October through May. The Off-Peak period for the calendar months
of June through September (Summer Months) is defined as the hours between 7:00 a.m. and 2:00 p.m.
and the hours between 7:00 p.m. and 11:00 p.m. Monday through Friday.
SUPER OFF-PEAK
The Super Off-Peak period is defined as the hours between 11:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m. Monday through
Sunday for all calendar months.

Customers who receive electrical service from a distribution line at a voltage between 2.4 kV and 25 kV, or
a substation or a pole-mounted or pad-mounted transformer whose high-side and low-side voltage is
between 2.4 kV and 25 kV.

Any customer not defined as a Transmission or Primary Distribution Customer.
