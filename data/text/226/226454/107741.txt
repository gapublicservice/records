===== DKT 44280 STF-ASR-9 PD/STF-ASR-9-71.docx =====
STF-ASR-9-71

Question:

For each temporary difference identified in response to the previous question, indicate whether the Company believes the related ADIT is protected or unprotected.  If the Company believes that the ADIT is protected, provide all citations to the IRC, Regulations, PLRs, and all other authoritative sources that address whether the ADIT is protected or unprotected.

Response:

ADIT accounts are either “protected” and subject to the Internal Revenue Code (“IRC”) normalization requirements, or “unprotected” and not subject to normalization requirements. Protected accounts are those that are the result of book-tax depreciation timing differences. To be entitled to accelerated methods of depreciation for tax purposes, the Company must use a normalization method of accounting as required by IRC sections 168(f)(2) and 168(i)(9). Specifically, protected ADITs result from the excess of tax depreciation deductions allowable under IRC section 168 (Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System and Accelerated Cost Recovery System), and depreciation deductions that would be allowable under IRC section 167(l).  Unprotected ADITs, which include all ADITs other than protected (regardless of whether they are property-related such as basis differences), are not addressed in the IRC nor the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (“TCJA”).  These regulations do not pertain to book-tax timing differences with respect to state income taxes.

As such, in the Company’s response to STF-ASR-9-70, the Company classified federal excess accelerated depreciation ADITs in FERC accounts 281 and 282 as protected and all other regulated temporary items as unprotected and not subject to IRS normalization requirements. As previously stated, the IRC requires the Company to use normalization as a prerequisite to claim accelerated depreciation. If these tax benefits are not normalized in the ratemaking process, the utility loses the right to claim the benefits. In addition, section 13001(d)(1) of the TCJA states that a normalization method of accounting shall not be treated as being used with respect to any public utility property for purposes of section 167 or 168 of the IRC if the taxpayer, in computing its cost of service for ratemaking purposes and reflecting operating results in its regulated books of account, reduces the excess tax reserve more rapidly or to a greater extent than such reserve would be reduced under the average rate assumption method.

Question: 

Prepaid pension asset. Is any amount for Southern Company Services' net pension asset included in the Company's retail jurisdictional rate base for 2025, by line item, on the Company's 2025 ASR filing Schedule 2, page 1 of 2? If not, explain fully why not. If so, identify how much by FERC account. If exact amounts are not available, provide the Company's best estimates and show in detail how such estimates were calculated.

Response:

No, the Company does not include any of Southern Company Services’ (SCS) net pension asset in retail rate base. The capitalized portion of the pension costs, which are billed to the Company by SCS on a monthly basis, are included in rate base. The billed amounts are imported into the Company’s general ledger and distributed among the various FERC accounts included in the Company’s 2025 ASR filing Section 2, page 1 of 2.

The estimates of the pension cost billed to the Company are not easily determined. This is because pension amounts billed by SCS to the Company, and subsequently distributed among the various FERC accounts in the Company’s general ledger, are calculated by the use of allocations and cost pools. Some of the allocations used in the process utilize dynamic allocation rates, which use direct labor dollars as the allocator. Therefore, there is not a consistent allocation rate that can be applied to recalculate exact amounts billed to the Company because the direct labor billed to the Company changes each month. Also, during the initial SCS billing process, the pension dollars are accumulated in a cost pool and comingled with other benefits before being allocated to the Company’s direct labor charges.  Because the pension costs are not allocated as individual dollars, are comingled with other benefits cost, and are incorporated into direct labor charges, it would be extremely difficult to estimate the amount of pension costs billed to the Company.

12.0 Obsolete Materials
Obsolete materials are items that have lost their intrinsic value and must be removed for disposition. Items identified and verified as obsolete materials MUST be removed from stock within 90 days of identification and adjusted from inventory on the company’s books.
Identification and Approval
Obsolete materials cannot be identified and approved by the same employee. Plant obsolete material may be identified by the system owner (engineer). T & D obsolete material may be identified by a staff engineer. Plant management and T & D management must have documentation of appropriate review of obsolete material.
Items become obsolete in one of two ways:
1. Technology
Changes in technology render an item unusable.
• Parts support an asset that is no longer used.
• The part is no longer used when it has been replaced by a different part.

2. Shelf-Life Expirations
• Items lose quality over time.
• Items are deemed obsolete if they exceed established life standards.

A report of slow-moving material should be reviewed annually. It is the responsibility of Supply Chain Management (Transmission, Distribution, Substation) and system owners (Plants) to insure the evaluation every item on the report as soon as practicable. System upgrades and changeouts should include a review of potential stranded material. Guidelines for plant obsolescence review and material disposition are detailed in the Southern Company Generation Playbook.
For items stocked at the MDC, the MDC will notify region locations of pending obsolete material items. The locations will be allowed four weeks to return any items to the MDC. After the four week period, the region supervisor will work with the storekeeper to insure appropriate disposition of obsolete items.
Inventory adjustments entered in Maximo must include “obsolete” as the adjustment reason. Any other information necessary to explain the adjustment is also included in the “comments” field. Documentation must be retained locally for one year for audit purposes. Contact the appropriate financial analyst for appropriate obsolete materials project and task.
Fleet obsolete material adjustments must be approved by the System Administrator and the Fleet Financial Analyst.
Surplus Material
Upon conclusion of a project, surplus standard approved materials should immediately be taken into stock.
Materials which are non-standard, or which are known to be slow moving or obsolete, should not be placed in stock. The Supply Chain Management Supervisor should contact the Investment Recovery Manager (or designee) to determine the disposition of the materials.

The Company’s vegetation management plan is as follows:
Transmission Herbicide – Work plan consists of two-fifth system miles to be sprayed each year.

Incompatible Species Identification - Scheduled based on the forester’s assessment of the condition of lines within their territories, with respect to planted incompatible species on the Right-of-Way. Distribution Right-of-Way Improvement – New easements are acquired for circuit segments based on an assessment of vegetation conditions, criticality of circuit, and segment performance.  Approximately 200-400 miles of worst performing distribution right-of-ways are improved each year.

Interest on customer deposits is not included in the average cost of debt and preferred securities because it is not a part of the Company’s retail financing structure. Please see Section 1, Page 1 of the Company’s 2025 ASR filing for the proportion and cost of each component of retail financing structure.

The amount of interest on customer deposits included in the 2025 ASR is $13.048 million, which represents the tax effected amount of $17.374 million provided in part ‘a’ above and is reflected in Section 2, Page 2 of 2, “Interest on Customer Deposits”.

The interest rates used to compute interest on these deposits vary and are determined by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC”).
