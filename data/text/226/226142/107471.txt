SUMMARY OF UTILITY FUEL HEDGING PRACTICES:

Each of the Southern Company operating companies (OPCOs), including
Southern Power, utilize hedging instruments to manage fuel price risks. SCS
utilizes fixed for floating financial swaps as well as financial options in its hedging
activities. These transactions are executed “over-the-counter” primarily with
financial institutions and producers.

SCS executes hedge transactions as agent for Alabama Power, Georgia Power,
and Mississippi Power.* SCS assists in planning, analyzing, and identifying hedge
opportunities for this group of operating companies. Execution of hedge
transactions for this group occurs as a result of having received previous approval
from the appropriate operating company executives. This approval provides SCS
with the guidance and latitude to hedge certain quantities of gas within certain price
ranges under specific hedge strategies. Once approval is granted, SCS may
hedge within these approved limits. During periods of sustained upward price
movement, SCS may, with appropriate approval of operating company executives,
utilize a hedging execution approach similar to a volume-cost-averaging
methodology.

Gas trades are entered into Attaché Financial, the SCS deal capture system, for a
specific operating company. Costs for each OPCO of a particular trade are
assigned to and accounted for a specific company. For example, when SCS
executes a hedge for Georgia Power, it is entered into Georgia Power’s gas book
via the SCS hedge accounting system (Attaché Financial).

Pages 2 and 3 contain a summary of the administrative costs of the hedging
program and a summary of hedging gains and losses.

Additionally, SCS operates per the applicable hedging orders issued by each
Public Service Commission. In 2007, the Georgia Public Service Commission
issued a Hedging Policy Statement as an amendment to the ongoing Hedging
Program in Docket No. 22403. In 2012, the Georgia Public Service Commission
revised the Hedging Policy Statement in Docket No. 16134. In 2015, the Georgia
Public Service Commission revised the parameters of the hedging program in
Docket No. 39638. In 2020, the Georgia Public Service Commission revised the
parameters of the hedging program in Docket No. 43011.


* SCS executes hedge transactions for Southern Power at Southern Power’s
direction.

SUMMARY OF UTILITY FUEL HEDGING PRACTICES:

Each of the Southern Company operating companies (OPCOs), including
Southern Power, utilize hedging instruments to manage fuel price risks. Most
hedge transactions are simple fixed-for-float financial swaps executed with a
financial institution, or bank with whom SCS hedges. Occasionally, call or put
options might also be executed.

SCS executes hedge transactions as agent for Alabama Power, Georgia Power,
Gulf Power, and Mississippi Power.* SCS assists in planning, analyzing and
identifying hedge opportunities for this group of operating companies. Execution
of hedge transactions for this group occurs as a result of having received
previous approval from the appropriate operating company executives. This
approval provides SCS with the guidance and latitude to hedge certain quantities
of gas within certain price ranges under specific hedge strategies. Once
approval is granted, SCS may hedge within these approved limits.

Each gas trade is entered into GEM Trader, the SCS deal capture system for a
specific operating company. SCS, as agent for each of the Southern Company
OPCOs, enters into hedge transactions with financial counterparties for each
specific OPCO individually, not as a group, and the costs for that particular
hedge are assigned to and accounted for that specific company. For example,
when SCS executes a hedge for Georgia Power, it is entered into Georgia
Power’s gas book via the SCS hedge accounting system (GEM Trader). The
attached Microsoft Excel workbook contains a summary of the administrative
costs of the hedging program and a summary of hedging gains and losses. In
addition, the attached PowerPoint files include correspondence related to
hedging strategy and recommendations.


* SCS executes hedge transactions for Southern Power at its direction.

SUMMARY OF UTILITY FUEL HEDGING PRACTICES:

Each of the Southern Company operating companies (OPCOs), including
Southern Power, utilize hedging instruments to manage fuel price risks. Most
hedge transactions are simple fixed-for-float financial swaps executed with a
financial institution, or bank with whom SCS hedges. Occasionally, call or put
options might also be executed.

SCS executes hedge transactions as agent for Alabama Power, Georgia Power,
Gulf Power, Mississippi Power, and Savannah Electric and Power.* SCS assists
in planning, analyzing and identifying hedge opportunities for this group of
operating companies. Execution of hedge transactions for this group occurs as a
result of having received previous approval from the appropriate operating
company executives. This approval provides SCS with the guidance and latitude
to hedge certain quantities of gas within certain price ranges under specific
hedge strategies. Once approval is granted, SCS may hedge within these
approved limits.

Each gas trade is entered into GEM Trader, the SCS deal capture system for a
specific operating company. SCS, as agent for each of the Southern Company
OPCOs, enters into hedge transactions with financial counterparties for each
specific OPCO individually, not as a group, and the costs for that particular
hedge are assigned to and accounted for that specific company. For example,
when SCS executes a hedge for Georgia Power, it is entered into Georgia
Power’s gas book via the SCS hedge accounting system (GEM Trader). The
attached Microsoft Excel workbook contains a summary of the administrative
costs of the hedging program and a summary of hedging gains and losses. In
addition, the attached PowerPoint files include correspondence related to
hedging strategy and recommendations.


* SCS executes hedge transactions for Southern Power at its direction.

STF-JKA-5-22

Question: 

Please explain how the Company meets the current hedging limits approved by the Commission in Docket No. 44902.

a.Does the Company interpret the 36-month limit from the date of the “Deal Date” to the “Deal End” or from the “Deal Start” to “Deal End?”
b.Does the Company interpret the 40% of projected burn as the total “Opco Volume Per Month” for each month from the “Deal Start” to “Deal End?” Please explain.
c.Please provide the calculations for the total hedged amount (%) compared to the projected burn for each month of the historical period. Please reconcile the volumes deriving the % hedged metric to the values provided in response to STF-JKA-2-3.
d.Please explain if the Company’s hedging percent is derived based on the fuel budget used in the FCR Case (FCR-26) or some other metric of projected burns. Please compare the utilized projected burns to those provided in the Company’s MFRP-6.2 provided in FCR-26.

Response:

The Company interprets the 36-month limit based on the prompt-month contract at the time the transaction is executed. Under this interpretation, a hedge complies with the 36-month limit if its Deal End is no more than 36 months forward from the then-current prompt month.

The Company evaluates compliance with the 40% of projected burn limit on a hedge-month basis by comparing the total “OPCO Volume per Month” hedged for each applicable hedge month to the projected natural gas burn for that same month, as reflected in the most recent Energy Budget (“EB”) available for the applicable period.

STF-JKA-5-22 Attachment A calculates the monthly hedged amounts and reconciles the volumes deriving the percentage hedged to the values provided in the Company’s response to STF-JKA-2-3.

Hedged percentages are based on the volumes hedged compared to the annual Energy Budget burn projections.  Please see STF-JKA-5-22 Attachment B for a comparison of the natural gas burn projections used for calculating hedge volumes and the projected natural gas burn in MFRP-6.2 from FCR-26.

SUMMARY OF UTILITY FUEL HEDGING PRACTICES:

Each of the Southern Company operating companies (OPCOs), including
Southern Power, utilize hedging instruments to manage fuel price risks. SCS
utilizes fixed for float financial swaps as well as financial options in its’ hedging
activities. These transactions are executed “over-the-counter” primarily with
financial institutions.

SCS executes hedge transactions as agent for Alabama Power, Georgia Power,
Gulf Power, and Mississippi Power.* SCS assists in planning, analyzing and
identifying hedge opportunities for this group of operating companies. Execution
of hedge transactions for this group occurs as a result of having received
previous approval from the appropriate operating company executives. This
approval provides SCS with the guidance and latitude to hedge certain quantities
of gas within certain price ranges under specific hedge strategies. Once
approval is granted, SCS may hedge within these approved limits.

Each gas trade is entered into Attaché Financial, the SCS deal capture system,
for a specific operating company. SCS, as agent for each of the Southern
Company OPCOs, enters into hedge transactions with financial counterparties
for each specific OPCO individually, not as a group, and the costs for that
particular hedge are assigned to and accounted for that specific company. For
example, when SCS executes a hedge for Georgia Power, it is entered into
Georgia Power’s gas book via the SCS hedge accounting system (Attaché
Financial). The attached Microsoft Excel workbook contains a summary of the
administrative costs of the hedging program and a summary of hedging gains
and losses. In addition, the attached PowerPoint files include correspondence
related to hedging strategy and recommendations.

Additionally, SCS operates per the applicable hedging orders issued by each
state’s Public Service Commission. In 2007, the Georgia Public Service
Commission (PSC) issued a Hedging Policy Statement as an amendment to the
ongoing Hedging Program in Docket No. 22403. As part of the amended order,
the Company provides an Implementation schedule to the Georgia PSC Staff
before the beginning of each quarter.

In June 2010, the Company partially suspended a portion of the VCA hedging
program in response to the deferred start-up of Plant McDonough. Because the
in-service dates of Plant McDonough Units 4, 5 and 6 were delayed, the
forecasted gas usage had to also be adjusted. Therefore, the Company stopped
adding new hedges for January 2011 – December 2011 to prevent the possibility
of becoming over-hedged. This reduction in planned hedge volumes was
submitted to the PSC in the 3rd Quarter 2010 Implementation schedule.
