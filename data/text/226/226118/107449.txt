For each cost, provide data describing how the cost was incurred (staff labor hours by department, overhead allocations, legal and regulatory support, procurement and evaluation functions, customer subscription program support, etc.)

Please see STF-PIA-5-1 Attachment, tab “FCR Renewables Costs.” Please note, the Energy Storage System (“ESS”) RFP is a capacity RFP; therefore, the associated requests for proposals (“RFP”) costs are not recovered through FCR.

Please see STF-PIA-5-1 Attachment, tab “Bid and Winner's Fees.” 

Please see STF-PIA-5-1 Attachment, tab “RFP Expenditures.” Please note, certain cost categories such as administrative costs, evaluation costs, subscription program costs, and customer-facing costs referenced in the question above are included with all other costs as noted in the attachment. The Company records the costs into “labor,” “contract labor,” and “other expenses,” as provided in the attachment and does not track the costs specific to certain categories identified in the question above. 

Please see STF-PIA-5-1 Attachment, tab “IE Costs.” The Company does not track Independent Evaluator (“IE”) costs in the requested cost categories.

Please see STF-PIA-5-1 Attachment, tab “Internal GPC Costs.” Please see the Company’s response to STF-PIA-5-4 for the accounting process used by the Company to record renewable RFP program costs. For Georgia Power internal labor, each employee in the various departments within the Company who works on the renewable RFP programs tracks their time and expenses dedicated to each program using the project code assigned to each program. This type of work includes, among other functions, legal and regulatory support, procurement and evaluation functions, and customer subscription program support. The Company does not track these costs by work types identified in the question above.

Please see STF-PIA-5-1 Attachment.

STF-PIA-5-3

Question: 

Provide Georgia Power’s explanation and supporting documentation for the rationale that recovering renewable RFP and Program-related costs through the FCR mechanism, rather than ratebase, benefits ratepayers. Include any internal analysis, cost-benefit evaluations, customer impact assessments, or PSC directives the Company relied upon when determining that FCR treatment was appropriate.

Response:

For more than ten years, Georgia Power has recovered renewable power purchase agreement (“PPA”), and the RFP-related costs to procure those PPAs, offset with revenues received from RFP participants such as bid fees, winner’s fees, and delay damages, through FCR. As approved by the Commission in the Company’s 2016, 2019, and 2022 Integrated Resource Plans (“IRP”), Georgia Power recovers various renewable program administrative costs through the fuel clause that are directly associated with the procurement of renewable resources through Commission-approved RFPs, without which the PPAs would not exist. As supported by the Commission in prior IRPs, renewable PPAs are energy resources that reduce the energy generated from thermal resources, such as natural gas and coal, therefore directly reducing the fuel costs for those resources and providing fixed-price energy benefits to all customers. Per the Commission’s RFP Rule,  Georgia Power is required to develop and issue competitive RFPs for new supply side resources and, similar to the revenues from RFP participants, the administrative costs incurred are expenses directly tied to renewable PPAs. Further, in Docket No. 44847, the Commission reaffirmed Georgia Power’s ability to recover RFP-related and CARES subscription program costs through the fuel clause. Accordingly, these costs are recovered through the fuel clause and excluded from base rates contemplated in base rate cases. Given the RFP costs could be difficult to estimate, having the costs recovered through the FCR ensures retail customers only pay for actual costs incurred by the Company.

Question: 

Describe in detail the accounting process used to track renewable RFP/Program related costs within the FCR mechanism. Identify all accounts, subaccounts, and internal cost codes used to categorize expenditures associated with each RFP.

Response:

All request for proposals (“RFP”) related costs are recorded to FERC account 55500125: REDI Program Expense. The Company uses project codes within its accounting systems to identify and track renewable RFP/Program related costs for each RFP. Please see below for the project codes used to identify the costs associated with renewable RFP costs for each RFP program.

Project Code - 11044353: GPC Renewable Development – CARES 2023
Project Code - 11044354: GPC Renewable Development – CARES 2025
Project Code - 11045201: GPC Renewable Development – 2023 IRP DG RFP
Project Code - 11074031: GPC Renewable Development – 2024 DG RFP
Project Code - 11101297: GPC Renewable Development – 2026 DG RFP
