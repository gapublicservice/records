COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before June 22, 2026.

(Please refer to the January 29, 2026 Incremental Battery Energy Storage System (“BESS”) Effective Load Carrying Capability (“ELCC”) Presentation and supporting documentation)
The slides provided on 1/29/26 note that Georgia Power has conducted “sensitivity testing” on 8-hour and 2-hour batteries. Please provide those results or provide a reference to where the files are located.

Is Georgia Power planning to conduct ELCC analysis on thermal resources that bid into the All-Source RFP? If so, please provide such analysis.  If not, please explain why not?  If Georgia Power is planning to use generic values by resource type, please provide any analysis for those values.
In the “Solar Expansion Results-2026” spreadsheet, the results show unexpected trends for winter accreditation.  For example, in the “Combination” tab, the winter Load Carried value is notably higher for 15,000 MW compared to 30,000 MW.  The values are relatively small, but the trend is counterintuitive. What factors might explain these results?
Please provide a narrative explanation for why the additional solar resources improve the BESS ELCC.
In the analysis of solar and BESS combined buildout, the impact of adding solar has an inconsistent impact.  For example, for the cases with 1,000 MW of BESS, the combined buildout of BESS plus solar appears to have a lower total accreditation than the sum of the individual elements, but with more BESS, the cases with combined BESS plus solar carries more load than the sum of the individual results. This effect is reversed for all other BESS tranche sizes.  Please explain.
The “Battery ELCC Modeling Steps” document states, “NOTE: The purpose of these combined simulations is not to determine the ELCC value of a new solar plus battery facility, but to understand how total system battery ELCC value is impacted by various amounts of embedded solar.” Please explain why this should not be used to determine the ELCC value of new solar and batteries.
In prior discussions, Georgia Power has stated that the Company’s system needed energy-rich resources. Please explain why the ELCC of solar is still minimal and provide the current ELCC value.
In the PowerPoint “ELCC Timeline-2021-2026”,  the description of the 2025 ELCC Study states “Winter Demand and Data Center Load Reduce BESS ELCC”.  Please explain how added Data Center load affects BESS ELCC and whether such effects are realized in the ELCC results since such load was “carved out” of the weather-driven load shapes.
Please explain why Georgia Power completed a “focused series of simulations” instead of producing a new independent set of ELCC values by duration. Is it a matter of timing? If so, please describe the amount of time needed to complete new independent studies for each set of durations. 
Please explain why the ELCC for 8-hour batteries is so low.
Has Georgia Power ever completed an ELCC analysis on thermal resources to test the validity of the study? If not, would the Company be willing to complete such a study?
Does Georgia Power intend to conduct a separate ELCC study for hybrid solar plus storage resources as the simulations were “not designed to determine the ELCC value of a specific solar‑plus‑battery facility”? Is Georgia Power proposing to only use the capacity of the BESS when evaluating solar plus storage resources? If so, please explain why.
 With reference to Exhibit G – Overview of Evaluation Methodology, explain in full detail why the ELCC values appear to be the same for (a) standalone energy storage systems (ESS) and (b) ESS paired with renewable generation resources sharing the same point of interconnection (POI). State whether separate SERVM model runs were performed for paired resources, and if not, provide the complete basis for assigning identical ELCC values.
Please explain why Georgia Power Company selected the year 2029 as the study year for determining ELCC values, even though selected resources will be procured and placed in service in 2032 and 2033. Describe any adjustments applied to translate 2029 modeling results to later year system conditions.
Please explain why the modeling relied on the 1973–2022 weather dataset instead of a more recent 20year period (e.g., 2006–2025). Identify all analyses comparing the long-term dataset to a recent dataset and provide Georgia Power’s justification for including earlier decades that may not reflect current extreme weather patterns.
Please explain the origin and justification for the assumption that 5 GW of high load factor load was carved out and modeled as “flat” (not responsive to weather variability). Quantify how this assumption affects modeled peak loads and the resulting ELCC values for incremental BESS tranches.
Please explain and provide all supporting evidence for the load factor value assigned to the large load carveout. Identify whether Georgia Power relied on actual historical customer profiles, projections, or generic assumptions. Provide analysis showing how ELCC values change under alternative assumed load factors. What load factor is assumed for large load customers in the ELCC model? 
Does the ELCC modeling assume that the Georgia Power system is fully islanded and not interconnected with neighboring utilities. If so, explain the technical and operational basis for this assumption. Describe the expected direction and magnitude of ELCC changes if the model were run using an interconnected system configuration.
Provide the document titled “2026 Battery ELCC Modeling Steps”. 
With reference to the 2026 Battery ELCC Modeling Steps document, explain why the modeling assumes thermal resources are capable of operating at full nameplate capability during system peak conditions. Provide all forced outage, unforced outage, cold weather derate, and historical winter peak performance data applied in the ELCC model.
Please explain whether solar, BESS, and thermal resources are modeled with the same or different forced outage and unforced outage parameters during peak periods. If they differ, provide the specific outage rate values used for each technology type and the basis for those values.
With reference to the modeling approach described in the Battery ELCC Modeling Steps document (section describing iterative SERVM simulations) and the Incremental BESS ELCC Presentation, provide the estimated timeline for recalculating ELCC values under alternative modeling assumptions, including: (a) different large load factor assumptions; (b) a non-islanded system configuration; (c) a more recent weather dataset; and (d) updated winter load shapes. Include estimates of the number of SERVM simulations required per tranche, the amount of compute time required, and whether Georgia Power uses parallel processing to complete these simulations.
Does Georgia Power propose to use the same ELCC values for distributed generation and transmission-interconnected resources? If so, please explain why the capacity accreditation should be equivalent for resources interconnected at the distribution level, rather than grossed up for system losses.
Regarding the change in ELCC values between the 2025 IRP, 2029-2031 All Source RFP, and the draft RFP Exhibit G for the 2032-2033 All Source RFP: 
Explain in detail why the ELCC values reflected in response to STFJKA110 differ significantly from the ELCC values included in Exhibit G, including all factors contributing to the reduction in ELCC values. 
Identify all Company decisions, modeling changes, or assumptions that contributed to the shift in results. 
Provide all supporting documentation, workpapers, or model output files necessary to understand the differences. 
Provide a detailed sidebyside comparison of the study calculations for ELCC calculation (2025 IRP, 2029-2031 All Source and 2032-2033 All Source RFP: 
Identify the modeling platforms and versions used. 
Describe the formulas, computational structures, and analytical steps applied in each updated study. 
Identify all assumptions used in each study. 
Identify any differences in scenario design or model configuration between the three ELCC studies. 
Identify all inputs and data sources used for each ELCC calculation: 
Load shapes, including hourly or subhourly time resolution. 
Resource performance assumptions (e.g., outage rates, renewable profiles, storage operating assumptions). 
Transmission assumptions. 
Fuel price assumptions. 
Weather or climate data sets. 
For each input, provide the original source and identify whether it was modified by the Company. 
Provide all data center load shape assumptions used in each ELCC calculation: 
Hourly or subhourly profiles. 
Interconnection location of data center load.
Any adjustments, scenario variants, or scaling factors applied. 
The source(s) of the data center load shape(s). 
A description of how data center load was incorporated into each SERVM model run. 
Identify all modeling steps included in the 2026 battery ELCC calculation. 
Identify and describe all changes from the steps used in the 2025 ELCC analysis. 
Provide supporting documentation for any revised modeling procedures. 
Provide the file titled “BESS Expansion ELCC Results2026.xlsx”: 
Identify all differences between these results and the 2025 ELCC results. 
Describe any changes in inputs, assumptions, data sets, resource configurations, or computational methodology. 
Provide all intermediate model output files that show how these results were derived. 
Regarding paired solarplusstorage ELCC calculations: 
State whether any separate SERVM model runs were performed for paired resources (solar plus storage) for either the 2025 ELCC calculations or the ELCC values included in Exhibit G for the draft RFP. 
If such runs were performed, provide all model inputs, outputs, assumptions, and results. 
If no such runs were performed, please explain why. 
Describe in detail the calibration steps used in the 2026 ELCC calculation: 
Identify all calibration parameters. 
Identify adjustments made to load, resources, weather years, model settings, and lossofload metrics. 
Explain the rationale for each calibration step. 
Identify all changes from the calibration steps used in the 2025 ELCC calculation. 
Regarding ELCC sensitivities for data center load: 
State whether any ELCC sensitivity runs were performed using different assumed data center load factors or alternative load shapes. 
If yes, provide all assumptions, input files, load shapes, and results for each sensitivity case. 
If no, explain why no such sensitivity analysis was conducted. 
The battery storage resources selected as a result of the 2029-2031 RFP received ELCC credits of 80%-100%. Please estimate the ELCC value of those resources if they were evaluated using the Company’s updated study.
