Make Ready Program 
Georgia Power’s Make Ready Program aims to lower cost barriers for business customers interested in installing electric vehicle (“EV”) chargers that serve the public good. This initiative supports the increasing demand for electric transportation solutions by enabling Georgia Power to design, own, install, and maintain EV charging infrastructure from behind the customer's meter up to—but not including—the actual charger. To participate, customers must install at least six Level 2 Chargers ("L2") or a single Direct Current Fast Charger ("DCFC"). Dual port L2 chargers are permitted if at least six parking spaces are designated specifically for EV charging.
In 2025, Georgia Power experienced a 44% year-over-year increase in Make Ready Program applications compared to 2024. The retail segment remained the most active customer group, representing nearly half of all submissions. Georgia Power received 260 applications in 2025, up from 182 in the previous year. Applications progress through stages at varying rates, influenced largely by the applicant's readiness to become a charging station owner or operator and the length of the applicant’s internal approval processes. As a result, the annual number of submitted applications will invariably differ from the number of projects under construction and the count of completed installations, reflecting diverse project timelines. Applications are cancelled if they fail to meet the program's qualifications or if there are site-specific installation complications; otherwise, no application is rejected or denied.  
Installing chargers and infrastructure is a detailed process that requires time. Make Ready construction timelines are affected by factors such as supply chain delays for utility equipment, with transformers sometimes needing 6-12 months lead time and switchgear needing 12-16 weeks lead time. In 2025, the Company completed 86 Make Ready projects and continued work on 70 more which are expected to finish in 2026.
In 2025, the company spent $11,805,576 on 164 Make Ready projects. These expenses cover projects started before 2025, worked on during 2025, and finished in 2025. The chart titled "2025 Make Ready Capital Spend & Charger Counts by Project" (pages 4-7) breaks down spending by location. Through these projects, 1,025 electric vehicle chargers were installed, including both L2 and DCFC. Of these new chargers, 586 came from projects completed in 2025. On average, each site received about six chargers, with each charger costing roughly $11,518. The average cost per charger rose from 2024 to 2025 due to an increase in DCFC installations which are more expensive than L2s. In 2024, 58 DCFC units were added, compared to 126 DCFC installed in 2025.
Georgia Power not only completes Make Ready projects successfully but also surveys participants for feedback on their experience with the Make Ready Program. The company uses the Net Promoter Score (NPS) methodology, which ranges from -100 to 100. Any score above zero is considered positive, indicating more participants would recommend Georgia Power’s Electric Transportation services than those who would not. In 2025, thirty-one participants responded to the survey, resulting in an impressive NPS of 93. Most respondents (86%) agreed that the Make Ready Program helped them accelerate or expand plans to transition to charging stations. This highlights the program’s effectiveness in meeting customer needs and advancing EV charging infrastructure across Georgia.

Community Charging Program 
Georgia Power’s Community Charging Program is designed to support electric vehicle charging needs in underserved areas. In March 2025, Georgia Power submitted eleven (11) locations for approval, starting the 60-day right of first refusal process (ROFR). PSC Staff recommended eight of the eleven locations be approved.   The company filed to replace the three locations and two of those were approved.  There was one location, Alma, that was denied through the Right of First Refusal process in 2024.  The 18-month window passed without the intervenor breaking ground within the allotted time frame and it became the 11th site approved for 2025.  
Georgia Power worked with property owners to obtain the rights to build and operate chargers at ten (10) sites, but we were unable to successfully locate a site host for one (1) site, Mount Vernon.  Construction on the sites began in the 3rd quarter of 2025. All new community charging sites include two DC Fast Chargers (DCFC) and no level II chargers.
Georgia Power also performed site upgrades at (2) two existing Community Charging Program locations, which provided an increase in the kW output of chargers and improves the driver experience.  
The Community Charging location at 2175 US Highway 441 S, Dublin GA 31021 was upgraded with new technology. The site consisted of a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC and a ChargePoint CT4020-HD-GW L2 prior to the upgrade. The upgrade added two CPE280-800-NA-1A3M2-3A1M2 DCFC chargers and is now capable of dispensing 160 kW output to a single vehicle or split power to charge two cars simultaneously.  The CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC removed from the Dublin site was used to upgrade the community charging station located at 696 First Avenue, East Ellijay GA 30540from  one (1) ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD and (1) ChargePoint CT4020-HD-GW L2 to two (2) ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC chargers and the station is now capable of dispensing 125 kW output to a single vehicle or split power to charge two cars simultaneously.  The Company continues to evaluate the program and potential upgrade sites to improve the driver experience.    
In 2025, Georgia Power booked $3,118,535 as capital expenses in 2025.  There were twenty (20) different projects which include the 2025 new approvals, site upgrades and carryover from previous installations.  Carry-over billing from prior year projects is unavoidable as construction and billing timelines do not always coincide within the same calendar year. The net revenue associated with Community Charging Stations was $2,590,990 for 2025.

ET Rebate Programs 
Georgia Power’s ET Rebate Programs are designed to help reduce cost barriers to customers seeking to adopt ET technologies. These programs are designed for both residential and business customers and include program specific requirements.  In 2025, our Net Promoter Score was 74, indicating high satisfaction among our participants.  
The total rebate budget authorized in the 2022 rate case was $6,700,000, which translates to approximately $2,250,000 per year for the three-year order. $4.5M of budget was new and specific to the 2022 rate case ruling and was requested to support customers that may not be able to utilize the Make Ready program.  The remaining $2.2M was other O&M dollars that had traditionally been allocated to ET rebates over the last decade. 
In 2025, the residential rebate was $150 per L2 charger.  We processed over 3,800 rebates, which represented a 12% increase over the previous year.  The increase can be attributed to an increase in marketing efforts and additional charger options being made available on Georgia Power’s marketplace. The “Instant Rebate” offered on the Georgia Power Marketplace, makes up for 71% of the residential rebates.  This instant rebate is immediately applied to the cost of the charger at check out, and no additional action for getting the rebate is required.  
The Residential Builders Rebate, which provides a rebate to builders who install electric vehicle plugs, continues to be underutilized. Many builders are either unaware or don’t see the value in taking the time to provide the necessary documentation. We did not process any residential builder rebates in 2025.
The Business Charger Rebate paid out 100 rebates in 2025 and spent about 75% of our anticipated budget.  This is compared to 10 paid out rebates in 2024.  The challenge in the business market compared to the residential market is installation time.  If a project is not under construction within the first six months of the year, it will likely not qualify to get the rebate until the following year.  With some of the marketing initiatives and program enhancements we have made to the program, we expect to keep seeing growth in participation.    
With the overall budget underspend, we executed several strategies to help generate awareness to help drive participation which should help in 2026 as well. Our rebate web page traffic increased from 4,985 in 2024 to 13,089 in 2025. Below are some of the initiatives we took.

ET Vehicle to Grid Pilot Program
In the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan, a Vehicle to Grid Pilot was approved for The Company to establish a pilot using School Buses to determine if Vehicle to Grid was feasible and if vehicles could be considered a viable grid resource.  
In late fall of 2025, we hired a Project Manager to oversee the pilot program.  Most of the time in 2025 involved research and the development of terms and conditions for participants. Based on our early discussions, we know there is a limited number of school systems with electric buses, and our program design will need to be beneficial enough for participants to get their agreement to participate.

Make Ready Program

Georgia Power’s Make Ready Program aims to lower cost barriers for business customers interested in
installing electric vehicle (“EV”) chargers that serve the public good. This initiative supports the
increasing demand for electric transportation solutions by enabling Georgia Power to design, own,
install, and maintain EV charging infrastructure from behind the customer's meter up to—but not
including—the actual charger. To participate, customers must install at least six Level 2 Chargers ("L2") or
a single Direct Current Fast Charger ("DCFC"). Dual port L2 chargers are permitted if at least six parking
spaces are designated specifically for EV charging.

In 2025, Georgia Power experienced a 44% year-over-year increase in Make Ready Program applications
compared to 2024. The retail segment remained the most active customer group, representing nearly
half of all submissions. Georgia Power received 260 applications in 2025, up from 182 in the previous
year. Applications progress through stages at varying rates, influenced largely by the applicant's
readiness to become a charging station owner or operator and the length of the applicant’s internal
approval processes. As a result, the annual number of submitted applications will invariably differ from
the number of projects under construction and the count of completed installations, reflecting diverse
project timelines. Applications are cancelled if they fail to meet the program's qualifications or if there
are site-specific installation complications; otherwise, no application is rejected or denied.

Installing chargers and infrastructure is a detailed process that requires time. Make Ready construction
timelines are affected by factors such as supply chain delays for utility equipment, with transformers
sometimes needing 6-12 months lead time and switchgear needing 12-16 weeks lead time. In 2025, the
Company completed 86 Make Ready projects and continued work on 70 more which are expected to
finish in 2026.

In 2025, the company spent $11,805,576 on 164 Make Ready projects. These expenses cover projects
started before 2025, worked on during 2025, and finished in 2025. The chart titled "2025 Make Ready
Capital Spend & Charger Counts by Project" (pages 4-7) breaks down spending by location. Through
these projects, 1,025 electric vehicle chargers were installed, including both L2 and DCFC. Of these new
chargers, 586 came from projects completed in 2025. On average, each site received about six chargers,
with each charger costing roughly $11,518. The average cost per charger rose from 2024 to 2025 due to
an increase in DCFC installations which are more expensive than L2s. In 2024, 58 DCFC units were added,
compared to 126 DCFC installed in 2025.

Georgia Power not only completes Make Ready projects successfully but also surveys participants for
feedback on their experience with the Make Ready Program. The company uses the Net Promoter Score
(NPS) methodology, which ranges from -100 to 100. Any score above zero is considered positive,
indicating more participants would recommend Georgia Power’s Electric Transportation services than
those who would not. In 2025, thirty-one participants responded to the survey, resulting in an impressive
NPS of 93. Most respondents (86%) agreed that the Make Ready Program helped them accelerate or
expand plans to transition to charging stations. This highlights the program’s effectiveness in meeting
customer needs and advancing EV charging infrastructure across Georgia.

Community Charging Program

Georgia Power’s Community Charging Program is designed to support electric vehicle charging needs in
underserved areas. In March 2025, Georgia Power submitted eleven (11) locations for approval, starting
the 60-day right of first refusal process (ROFR). PSC Staff recommended eight of the eleven locations be
approved. The company filed to replace the three locations and two of those were approved. There
was one location, Alma, that was denied through the Right of First Refusal process in 2024. The 18-
month window passed without the intervenor breaking ground within the allotted time frame and it
became the 11th site approved for 2025.

Georgia Power worked with property owners to obtain the rights to build and operate chargers at ten
(10) sites, but we were unable to successfully locate a site host for one (1) site, Mount Vernon.
Construction on the sites began in the 3rd quarter of 2025. All new community charging sites include two
DC Fast Chargers (DCFC) and no level II chargers.

Georgia Power also performed site upgrades at (2) two existing Community Charging Program locations,
which provided an increase in the kW output of chargers and improves the driver experience.

The Community Charging location at 2175 US Highway 441 S, Dublin GA 31021 was upgraded with new
technology. The site consisted of a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC and a ChargePoint
CT4020-HD-GW L2 prior to the upgrade. The upgrade added two CPE280-800-NA-1A3M2-3A1M2 DCFC
chargers and is now capable of dispensing 160 kW output to a single vehicle or split power to charge two
cars simultaneously. The CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC removed from the Dublin site was used to
upgrade the community charging station located at 696 First Avenue, East Ellijay GA 30540from one (1)
ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD and (1) ChargePoint CT4020-HD-GW L2 to two (2) ChargePoint
CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC chargers and the station is now capable of dispensing 125 kW output to a
single vehicle or split power to charge two cars simultaneously. The Company continues to evaluate the
program and potential upgrade sites to improve the driver experience.

In 2025, Georgia Power booked $3,118,535 as capital expenses in 2025. There were twenty (20)
different projects which include the 2025 new approvals, site upgrades and carryover from previous
installations. Carry-over billing from prior year projects is unavoidable as construction and billing
timelines do not always coincide within the same calendar year. The net revenue associated with
Community Charging Stations was $2,590,990 for 2025.

ET Rebate Programs

Georgia Power’s ET Rebate Programs are designed to help reduce cost barriers to customers seeking to
adopt ET technologies. These programs are designed for both residential and business customers and
include program specific requirements. In 2025, our Net Promoter Score was 74, indicating high
satisfaction among our participants.

The total rebate budget authorized in the 2022 rate case was $6,700,000, which translates to
approximately $2,250,000 per year for the three-year order. $4.5M of budget was new and specific to
the 2022 rate case ruling and was requested to support customers that may not be able to utilize the
Make Ready program. The remaining $2.2M was other O&M dollars that had traditionally been
allocated to ET rebates over the last decade.

In 2025, the residential rebate was $150 per L2 charger. We processed over 3,800 rebates, which
represented a 12% increase over the previous year. The increase can be attributed to an increase in
marketing efforts and additional charger options being made available on Georgia Power’s marketplace.
The “Instant Rebate” offered on the Georgia Power Marketplace, makes up for 71% of the residential
rebates. This instant rebate is immediately applied to the cost of the charger at check out, and no
additional action for getting the rebate is required.

The Residential Builders Rebate, which provides a rebate to builders who install electric vehicle plugs,
continues to be underutilized. Many builders are either unaware or don’t see the value in taking the time
to provide the necessary documentation. We did not process any residential builder rebates in 2025.

The Business Charger Rebate paid out 100 rebates in 2025 and spent about 75% of our anticipated
budget. This is compared to 10 paid out rebates in 2024. The challenge in the business market
compared to the residential market is installation time. If a project is not under construction within the
first six months of the year, it will likely not qualify to get the rebate until the following year. With some
of the marketing initiatives and program enhancements we have made to the program, we expect to
keep seeing growth in participation.

With the overall budget underspend, we executed several strategies to help generate awareness to help
drive participation which should help in 2026 as well. Our rebate web page traffic increased from 4,985
in 2024 to 13,089 in 2025. Below are some of the initiatives we took.

ET Vehicle to Grid Pilot Program

In the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan, a Vehicle to Grid Pilot was approved for The Company to establish
a pilot using School Buses to determine if Vehicle to Grid was feasible and if vehicles could be considered
a viable grid resource.

In late fall of 2025, we hired a Project Manager to oversee the pilot program. Most of the time in 2025
involved research and the development of terms and conditions for participants. We spoke with
companies that are in this space including Bluebird, Highland Fleet, and Heliox as well as a school system
that currently has electric buses. Based on our early discussions, we know there is a limited number of
school systems with electric buses, and our program design will need to be beneficial enough for
participants to get their agreement to participate.
