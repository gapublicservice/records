JURISDICTION AND PROCEEDINGS
This matter comes before the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) on the application of Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) to set new fuel cost recovery (“FCR”) rates.  The Official Code of Georgia Annotated Section 46-2-26(a)(1) defines "fuel costs" as the cost of fuel “as defined in the utility company’s tariffs in effect on July 1, 1979, as such tariffs may be changed from time to time by order of the commission as provided by law.” "Utility" is defined as “any retail supplier of electricity subject to the rate-making jurisdiction of the commission.” O.C.G.A. § 46-2-26(a)(2).  In this Docket, Georgia Power Company is seeking this agency’s approval to recover fuel costs that the Company has identified as being qualified as fuel costs as defined by state law. Georgia law requires a public hearing on each electric utility's Fuel Cost Recovery (“FCR”) proposal, which is to be held no less than 10 days after the utility's filing. Not later than 90 days after the utility's filing, this Commission must issue an order that states the base fuel rates to be used by the utility during the next three consecutive calendar months, or until changed as provided in law. If such an order is not issued within this timeframe, the fuel rates proposed by the utility become effective by operation of law. O.C.G.A. § 46-2-26(e).
On January 21, 2026, the Commission issued a Procedural and Scheduling Order.  In the Procedural and Scheduling Order, the procedures that were to be followed in this docket were identified along with corresponding dates on which designated events were set to occur with respect to the Company’s application.
Petitions to intervene were filed by the Georgia Association of Manufacturers (“GAM”), Georgia Interfaith Power and Light jointly with Southface Energy Institute, Inc., Resource Supply Management (“RSM”), Metropolitan Atlanta Rapid Transit Authority, and by Sierra Club jointly with NRDC and the Southern Alliance for Clean Energy (“SACE”). No objection was made to any of these petitions for intervention.
Georgia Power filed with the Commission its application to set new FCR rates on February 17, 2026. The Company’s application included the pre-filed testimony of Adam Houston, Vice President and Comptroller for Georgia Power, and Matthew Berrigan, Assistant Comptroller for Georgia Power. In its application the Company requested that the new fuel cost recovery tariff (FCR-27) become effective for bills rendered beginning June 1, 2026.
On April 9, 2026, GAM pre-filed the testimony of Jeffry Pollock, Sierra Club, NRDC, and SACE pre-filed the testimonies of Jeremy Kalin and Michael Goggin, and GIPL and Southface pre-filed the testimony of Albert Lin. On April 10, 2026, Public Interest Advocacy Staff (“PIA Staff” or “Staff”) pre-filed the testimony of Tom Newsome, Philip Hayet, Anthony Sandonato, and Leah Wellborn. April 9, 2026, PIA Staff pre-filed the testimony of Joseph Reynolds. On April 23, 2026, the Company pre-filed rebuttal testimony of Adam Houston, Matthew Berrigan, Lee Evans, John Ohlson, and Earl Berry. 
On May 5-6, 2026, the Commission held a hearing to consider the Company’s application to set new FCR rates. On May 12, 2026, PIA Staff and the Company entered into a Stipulation which was intended to resolve all of the substantive issues in this proceeding between the two parties (as well as issues related to the concurrent Storm Cost Recovery Docket #44280). The Stipulation was filed that day. GAM subsequently joined as a Stipulating Party on May 14,, 2026. RSM joined as a Stipulating Part on May 27, 2026, and MARTA joined on May 28, 2026. A copy of the Stipulation is attached hereto as Attachment A.
On May 28, 2026, the Commission Advisory Staff recommended adoption of the Stipulation without modification and the Commission subsequently approved the Advisory Staff recommendation and adopted the proposed Stipulation in this matter. Due to the complexity of this matter and the short statutory deadline for the issuance of an order, the Commission issues this order as a short order.  The Commission will promptly issue a more detailed order in this matter further explaining its decisions, findings and conclusions
II.FINDINGS OF FACT AND CONCLUSIONS OF LAW
1.The Stipulation
As recommended by Advisory Staff, the Commission finds and concludes that the proposed Stipulation is a reasonable resolution of the issues in this case and that adoption is in the public interest. The Commission finds that the testimony and evidence received by the Commission fully supports the agreement reached by parties to this proceeding. The Commission acknowledges that alternative findings can be made.  However, the Commission further finds that the resulting fuel cost recovery rates and the agreements among the parties on the ancillary issues addressed by the Stipulation are just and reasonable resolutions of said issues.
ORDERING PARAGRAPHS
The Commission decides, based upon its evaluations and determinations as set forth in the preceding Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law, and upon the evidence of record, that Ordering Paragraphs memorializing its directives regarding the Company’s fuel cost recovery allowance are to read as follows:
1.
WHEREFORE IT IS ORDERED that the Stipulation (Attachment A) agreed to by Georgia Power Company and the Public Interest Advocacy Staff is hereby accepted and adopted as to all Fuel Cost Recovery provisions set forth therein.  
2.
ORDERED FURTHER that the FCR-27 fuel cost recovery rates proposed by the Company, as modified by the Stipulation, are just and reasonable and shall be effective for bills rendered beginning June 1, 2026. 
3.
ORDERED FURTHER, that Georgia Power Company shall file its next Fuel Cost Recovery application on or before February 28, 2026, with revised fuel rates to go into effect June 1, 2026, unless a later filing date is agreed to by the PIA Staff or ordered by the Commission.
4.
ORDERED FURTHER that the FCR-27 fuel balance shall be reduced by $13.0 million as of May 31, 2026, consistent with the Stipulation.
5.
ORDERED FURTHER that the Interim Fuel Rider (“IFR”) mechanism is hereby modified as follows: (a) the maximum amount by which fuel rates may be adjusted during an FCR cycle is 40% above or below the approved FCR-27 rates; (b) the IFR threshold is increased from $200 million to $300 million, applied to the current period cumulative over- or under-recovered fuel balance; and (c) when the $300 million threshold is exceeded in either direction, the Company shall, within five days of its IFR notification filing, submit for Commission consideration a proposed IFR rate adjustment to amortize the over- or under-recovered balance over a reasonable and financially prudent period of time, subject to Commission approval, modification, or rejection at the Commission’s discretion. Notwithstanding the foregoing, IFR rate adjustments shall occur no more than once per calendar quarter.
6.
ORDERED FURTHER that the Commission shall further review the methodology by which the Company credits Real-Time Pricing (“RTP”) revenue to the FCR revenues and the allocations the Company is making under such methodology in this Docket. This proceeding shall be concluded by December 31, 2026, and the Commission may make modifications if appropriate. The implementation of such modifications, if any, shall be credited to fuel revenues as determined by the Commission, and any corresponding change to base revenues shall be recorded in a regulatory asset or liability and addressed in the next base rate case. As part of such proceeding, the Company shall provide Commission Staff, within 45 days of the date of the order adopting this Stipulation, complete hourly RTP generation and dispatch data including cost and energy (MWhs) for the FCR-27 historical period and projections for the current FCR-27 period. Staff may issue data requests seeking additional information related to the methodology and allocations.
7.
ORDERED FURTHER that, effective upon the effective date of FCR-27 rates, Georgia Power’s natural gas hedging program shall be modified from the parameters approved by the Commission in FCR-26 (Docket No. 44902) to permit the Company to hedge up to 20% of its budgeted natural gas fuel burn in any given month, with a maximum hedging window of 36 months. For those months where the Company currently exceeds these limits, the existing hedges may remain in place.
8.
ORDERED FURTHER that, effective June 1, 2026, the Company shall discontinue recovery of renewable requests for proposals (“RFP”) administration and renewable subscription program costs through the fuel clause to the extent such costs do not equal associated revenues recovered from RFP participants, including Bid Fees, Winner’s Fees, and PPA termination payments. To the extent costs incurred on or after June 1, 2026 do not equal associated revenues, the difference shall be recorded in a regulatory asset or liability account and submitted for consideration and disposition in the Company’s next base rate case proceeding. The Company shall provide Staff a complete accounting of renewable RFP and subscription program costs and revenues prior to the next base rate case proceeding, consistent with non-renewable RFPs.
9.
ORDERED FURTHER that the Company shall limit coal purchases at prices higher than market price to 15% of total coal purchases during the FCR-27 test period.
10.
ORDERED FURTHER, that all findings, conclusions, statements, and directives made by the Commission and contained in the foregoing sections of this Order are hereby adopted as findings of fact, conclusions of law, statements of regulatory policy, and orders of this Commission.
11.
ORDERED FURTHER that the Company shall continue to provide Staff, on June 30th and December 31st of each year, updated projected monthly fuel revenues, fuel expenses, monthly over- or under-collections, and projected fuel balances through May 31, 2029. For the June 30th update, the Company shall update only its natural gas price forecast and retain all other assumptions as included in the prior December update. Any additional reporting requirements identified through the RTP fuel credit methodology review referenced in Ordering Paragraph 6 shall be incorporated as directed by the Commission
12.
ORDERED FURTHER that, in conjunction with the ongoing level of analysis necessary to review fuel cost recovery issues prior to the filing of the FCR-28 case, the Company shall pay for any reasonably necessary specialized assistance to Commission Staff in an amount not to exceed $125,000 annually. The amounts paid by the Company under this paragraph shall be deemed a necessary cost of providing service, and the Company shall be entitled to recover the full amount of any such costs charged to the utility through the fuel clause.
13.
ORDERED FURTHER, that due to the complexity of this matter and the short statutory deadline for the issuance of an order, the Commission issues this order as a short order.  The Commission will promptly issue a more detailed order in this matter further explaining its decisions, findings and conclusions.
14.
ORDERED FURTHER, that a motion for reconsideration, rehearing, or oral argument or any other motion shall not stay the effective date of this Order, unless otherwise ordered by the Commission.
15.
ORDERED FURTHER, that jurisdiction over these matters is expressly retained for the purpose of entering such further Order or Orders as this Commission may deem just and proper.

The above by action of the Commission in Administrative Session on the __th day of May 2026.
