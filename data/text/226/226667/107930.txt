                    POST-HEARING BRIEF OF GEORGIA INTERFAITH
                     POWER & LIGHT AND SOUTHFACE INSTITUTE


          Georgia Interfaith Power & Light and Southface Institute (GIPL and Southface)

appreciate the compromises found within the proposed Stipulation reached between Staff and

Georgia Power but request the Commission deny Georgia Power’s ability to profit off major

storms.

          GIPL and Southface have two recommendations at this time. First, the Commission

should deny Georgia Power’s full rate of return as applied to under-recovered storm costs by

embracing either no rate of return or a debt-only rate of return. If the Commission chooses to

allow Georgia Power to earn its full rate of return, GIPL and Southface urge the Commission to

revisit that decision in the next base rate case in 2028.

          Second, GIPL and Southface ask that the Commission clarify the Stipulation’s provision

raising the threshold for “major storms” to $500,000 with the understanding that only costs in

excess of $500,000 are deferred to the storm damage reserve. This would reduce the amount of

costs accruing additional financing charges, thereby reducing bill impacts for customers.

I.      GIPL and Southface urge the Commission to deny Georgia Power’s ability to profit
        off the under-recovered storm costs.

        This proceeding follows the Commission’s approval of the Stipulation to Extend the

Alternative Rate Plan (ARP) where the Commission agreed to a docket to allow Georgia Power

to recover storm damage costs incurred through December 31, 2025. Georgia Power filed its

initial application in February, asking to increase rates not only for storm costs incurred before

2026 but also for future estimated storm costs. Georgia Power also proposed to spread the

recovery of the collective major storm costs over four years and earn its full rate of return on

those costs during that four-year period. In other words, Georgia Power wishes to earn a profit on

costs spent in the wake of major storms. GIPL and Southface ask that the Commission eliminate

the return, or “financing,” component of the revenue requirement on which the rate increase here

is based.

        More specifically, as part of Georgia Power’s initial application and the Errata filed in

April, it requested to earn its full rate of return at the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)

on the under-recovered storm cost balance when calculating the required revenue and rate

increases. Table 1 below shows the significant impact these financing costs have on the initial

revenue increase request, prior to the Stipulation. While these numbers are not updated to reflect

the Stipulation, they are nevertheless useful for demonstrating the cost billpayers incur for major

storms under each revenue scenario. Georgia Power asked billpayers to pay an additional

$47.8M for its return, which increased the annual revenue requirement by $63.8M after tax-

II.      GIPL and Southface support increasing the “major storm” threshold from $50,000
         to $500,000 and ask the Commission to clarify that only costs above the threshold
         are deferred, not the entire amount.

         Georgia Power’s $50,000 threshold to classify a storm as a “major storm” has “been in

existence as long as [Witness Berrigan is] aware of.” 14 When a storm is classified as a major

storm, Georgia Power has been deferring the incremental costs associated with the storm into the

storm damage reserve where it earns its full rate of return. 15 GIPL and Southface support

increasing the threshold from $50,000 to $500,000, though a higher threshold is likely needed for

a utility the size of Georgia Power, as discussed in Staff’s direct testimony, and we recommend

revisiting the threshold in the 2028 base rate case. 16

         However, if a storm exceeds the proposed $500,000 threshold, it is unclear whether the

Stipulation means only costs above the threshold get deferred to the storm damage reserve, or

whether all the costs – above and below – are deferred to the reserve. For example, during the

hearing, Georgia Power did not clearly answer how incremental costs of a storm above the

proposed $500,000 threshold would be treated. 17 The witnesses did not state whether a storm

with incremental costs of $800,000 would have only the $300,000 in excess of the threshold

deferred to the storm damage reserve or if all $800,000 would be deferred. 18 GIPL and Southface

support increasing the threshold to $500,000 but ask the Commission to clarify that only

incremental costs above the threshold are deferred to ensure that Georgia Power is not

unreasonably profiting off major storms.
