JURISDICTION AND AUTHORITY
Georgia Power Company (hereinafter “Georgia Power” or “the Company”) is a public electric utility serving retail customers within the State of Georgia. Pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-20, the Public Service Commission “shall have general supervision of all…. electric light and power companies…” Such general supervision includes “the authority to examine the affairs of all companies under its supervision and to keep informed as to their general condition,” as well as the “authority, whenever it deems advisable, to prescribe, establish, and order a uniform system of accounts to be used by…companies over which it has jurisdiction.” O.C.G.A. §§ 46-2-20 (e) and (f). Also, pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-23(a), “The commission shall have the exclusive power to determine what are just and reasonable rates and charges to be made by any person, firm, or corporation subject to its jurisdiction.”
BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
This matter comes before the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) to consider Georgia Power Company’s Storm Damage Recovery Application, pursuant to the July 31, 2025 “Order Granting Joint Petition of Georgia Power Company and The Public Interest Advocacy Staff and Approval Of The Stipulation To Extend The Alternative Rate Plan” entered by the Commission in the above-captioned docket 44280.  In relevant part, the Order stated:
The Company will be allowed to recover actual reasonable and prudent storm damage cost incurred through December 31, 2025, that exceeds the amount of the annual storm damage accrual approved in the Order Adopting Settlement Agreement as Modified. The Company will file for the recovery of under-recovered storm damage cost no sooner than February 1, 2026, and no later than July 1, 2026, with new rates effective the 1st of the month following 90 days after the request for recovery. The Company’s filing will include at a minimum pre-filed direct testimony and the documentation supporting the request for changes in the storm damage accrual as well as the proposed period over which to allow recovery of the under-recovered storm damage balance. The Commission shall determine the rates, the period over which under-recovered storm costs will be recovered, and any other issues the Commission deems necessary to address the limited issue of storm damage cost recovery. The rate increase shall be applied to each traditional base rate on an equal percentage basis. The energy, demand, and basic service charge components shall all be adjusted equally; provided that the Company shall not apply any increase to the basic service charge for the domestic and small business rate groups. The Commission shall issue a final decision within 90 days of the Company’s filing.
On February 21, 2026, the Commission entered a Procedural and Scheduling Order (“PSO”) designating the Company’s application for storm damage cost recover as a “contested case” under O.C.G.A. § 50-13-13. The PSO set deadlines for the filing of petitions for leave to intervene, pre-filed testimony, discovery requests and responses, and set a hearing date of May 13-14, 2026, for both direct and rebuttal testimony.
On February 27, 2026, Georgia Power filed its “Storm Damage Recovery Application,” including minimum filing requirements (“MFRs”) in this docket. The Application asserted the existence of a $912 million under-recovered storm damage regulatory asset balance. The primary cause of the under-recovered balance was attributed by the Company to Hurricane Helene, which struck Georgia in September 2024. According to the Company, Helene caused more than $780 million in costs to repair damage to Company property and restore normal electrical service to the Company’s customers. 
In support of its application, the Company filed the testimony of a panel of three senior employees: Cleve Fann, Senior Vice President, Region External Affairs, Josh J. Stallings, Vice President of Distribution Strategy and Support, and Matthew Berrigan, Assistant Comptroller. The Company’s Application sought to increase the amount accrued to its storm damage regulatory asset account from $31.4 million to $329.2 million in order to amortize the under-recovered balance over four years beginning June 1, 2026, and also increase its reserves for future storm events. To achieve these goals, combined with carrying costs, the Company calculated a yearly revenue deficiency of $360 million. However, the Company also proposed an adjustment to this amount by amortizing certain “Production Tax Credits” (or “PTCs”) in the amount of approximately $65 million per year, thus lowering the revenue deficiency to $270 million. The Company proposed recovering this deficiency by increasing base rates on an equal percentage basis, resulting in an increase in monthly bills of $4.42 for a typical residential customer using 1,000 kWh.   
On April 17, 2026, the Commission’s Public Interest Advocacy Staff as designated under the PSO (“hereinafter “Staff”), filed the testimony of Tom Newsome, PSC Director of Utility Finance, Lane Kollen, Vice President and Principal of J. Kennedy and Associates, Inc., and Randy Futral, Director of Consulting with J Kennedy and Associates, Inc. Staff’s witnesses argued in their testimony that Georgia Power significantly overstated its storm damage costs in the regulatory asset account and that any under-recovered balance should be recovered over a longer, levelized amortization period, resulting in essentially no increase to base rates. Staff’s witnesses also generally criticized Georgia Power’s storm damage response policies and accounting methods and recommended various changes to the Company’s response to future storm events.
Pursuant to the PSO, Georgia Association of Manufacturers, Georgia Interfaith Power & Light, Southface Institute, Georgia Watch, and the Metropolitan Atlantic Regional Transit Authority (“MARTA”) and Resource Supply Management filed timely petitions for leave to intervene, which were subsequently granted without object by the Commission. On May 4, 2026, Georgia WAND Education Fund Inc. filed an untimely Application for Leave to Intervene. Its Application did not assert good cause for the late filing.
On April 30, 2026, Georgia Power Company filed rebuttal testimony by witnesses Cleve Fann, Josh Stallings, and Matthew Berrigan responding to Staff’s pre-filed direct testimony. The Company also filed testimony by expert witness Derek Hasbrouck in response to Staff’s testimony as to the Company’s storm response procedures.
On May 12, 2026, the PIA Staff filed with the Commission a Stipulation entered into by Staff and the Company, resolving all issues between Staff and the Company with respect to the Company’s Application.  On May 13, 2026, the Company filed an updated Stipulation, a copy of which is attached hereto as “Exhibit A” (hereinafter referred to as “the Stipulation”), in which intervenor Georgia Association of Manufacturers joined as a signing party. Resource Supply Management (“RSM”) and the Metropolitan Atlanta Rapid Authority (“MARTA”) subsequently joined as a Stipulating Party on May 27, 2026, and May 28, 2026, respectively.
On May 13, 2026, the Commission held a hearing on the Company’s Application. All parties entered appearances, with the exception of RSM who was not present. Georgia Wand also was not present at the hearing and never requested leave to Intervene late and never provided good cause.
Georgia Power employees Fann, Stallings, and Berrigan testified on behalf of the Company and were subject to questions by the Commission and cross examination. The witnesses testified as to Georgia Power Company’s response efforts to Hurricane Helene in September 2024 and the Stipulation. The witnesses also testified that the proposed Stipulation was a fair and reasonable resolution of the issues in this proceeding.
PIA Staff witnesses, Newsome, Kollen, and Futral also testified and were subject to questions from the Commission and cross examination. Staff witness Newsome testified on behalf of the Panel that adoption of the Stipulation would be in the public interest, that the Stipulation was a fair and reasonable compromise resolving all issues in the proceeding, and the rate changes resulting from the Company’s application as modified by the Stipulation were just and reasonable.
Georgia Association of Manufacturers witness Jeffrey Pollock also testified in support of the Stipulation. 
Expert witness Justin Barnes testified on behalf of Intervenors Georgia Interfaith Power and Light and Southface Institute. Mr. Barnes testified in support of reducing carrying costs on Geogia Power Company’s under-recovered storm damage asset balance to 0% and was subject to cross examination and questions from the Commissioners. On cross examination, Mr. Barnes testified that he had reviewed the Stipulation and opined that, while the stipulation was not “perfect,” it was an improvement over the Company’s Application as filed.
Witness Daniel Hecht testified on behalf of Intervenor MARTA. On cross examination, Mr. Hecht testified that he had not reviewed the Stipulation and had no opinion as to whether the Stipulation was in the public interest or was a fair and reasonable compromise of any issues in this case.
Georgia Power Company also presented the rebuttal testimony of Cleve Fann, Josh Stallings, Matthew Berrigan, and expert witness Derek Hasbrouck, who further testified regarding Georgia Power’s response to Hurricane Helene, including the Company’s Emergency Response Plan.
On May 28, 2026, the Commission Advisory Staff recommended adoption of the Stipulation without modification and the Commission subsequently approved the Advisory Staff recommendation and adopted the proposed Stipulation in this matter. Due to the complexity of this matter and the short time before the new rates are scheduled go into effect, the Commission issues this order as a short order.  The Commission will promptly issue a more detailed order in this matter further explaining its decisions, findings and conclusions
FINDINGS OF FACT AND CONCLUSIONS OF LAW
The Stipulation
As recommended by Advisory Staff, the Commission finds and concludes that the proposed Stipulation is a reasonable resolution of the issues in this case and that adoption is in the public interest. The Commission finds that the testimony and evidence received by the Commission fully supports the agreement reached by parties to this proceeding. The Commission acknowledges that alternative findings can be made.  However, the Commission further finds that the resulting storm damage recovery rates and the agreements among the parties on the ancillary issues addressed by the Stipulation are just and reasonable resolutions of said issues.
ORDERING PARAGRAPHS
WHEREFORE IT IS ORDERED, Georgia Power’s Storm Damage Recovery Application is granted as modified by the Stipulation.
ORDERED FURTHER, within 90 days of the date of this Order Adopting the Stipulation, the Company and Commission Staff shall hold an initial meeting to discuss the Company's implementation of industry best practices for storm response. The Company and Staff shall hold at least two meetings prior to the filing of a storm damage cost recovery and accounting best practices report to be made to the Commission on or before June 30, 2027. To the extent deemed necessary, the Commission Staff and the Company may use the information garnered from this collaborative process to inform future recommendations regarding storm response best practices. Such recommendations, if made, may be made either jointly by Commission Staff and the Company, or separately, and will be considered in the Company’s next base rate case.
ORDERED FURTHER, the threshold for deferral of cost to storm damage reserve will be cost in excess of $500,000 for each “major storm” and “other emergency situation” as defined in the Company’s Storm Accounting Guidelines. The Company shall review this threshold in its next base rate case.
ORDERED FURTHER, within 90 days of the date of this Order Adopting the Stipulation, the Company and Commission Staff shall meet to review and potentially modify record keeping for documenting storm damage costs and procedures for incremental costs and capitalization.
ORDERED FURTHER, the Company shall file with the Commission updated rates and tariffs consistent with this Order and the Stipulation.
ORDERED FURTHER, all findings, conclusions, and decisions contained within the preceding sections of this Order are hereby adopted as findings of fact, conclusions of law, and decisions of regulatory policy of this Commission.
ORDERED FURTHER, that jurisdiction over this matter is expressly retained for the purpose of entering such further order or orders as this Commission may deem just and proper.
ORDERED FURTHER, any motion for reconsideration, rehearing, or oral argument shall not stay the effectiveness of this Order unless expressly ordered by the Commission.
