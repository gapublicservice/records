18              circumstances, such as those represented by relatively infrequent, though sometimes highly

20              important to first appreciate the fact that ratepayers directly bear significant costs

21              associated with storms, including but by no means limited to, electricity service

 1        disruptions. The ratepayers that will pay for storm restoration expenses are the same

 2        ratepayers that went without power for days or even more than a week in the case of

 3        Hurricane Helene, which for some likely resulted in direct monetary costs for things like

 4        spoiled food and lost wages. This is to say nothing of those that experienced significant

5         property damage, injuries, or even lost loved ones. To be clear, I am not saying that the

6         Company is at fault for those service disruptions or other impacts. That said, ratepayers

7         bear immediate, direct, and sometimes severe impacts from storms in a way that the

 8        Company’s shareholders do not, so ratepayers bear a disproportionate risk in the immediate

 9        period following a storm event.

10               Amortizing the recovery of expenses associated with storm recovery efforts over

11        an extended period moderates the rate impacts of storm costs, spreading the “secondary”

12        effects of storm damage over time and mitigating the magnitude of rate increases that

13        would be associated with a shorter recovery period. However, including a return on the

14        unrecovered balance of an amortized expense undeniably increases total ratepayer costs in

15        the interest of generating additional income for the benefit of shareholders. There is no

16        equitable sharing of risks under these circumstances. Shareholders are made whole and

17        ratepayers foot the bill for doing so, despite the fact that those same ratepayers

18        disproportionately shoulder the risks of storm-caused service disruptions themselves.

 3   A.       No. The Company’s overall realized return on a system-wide basis, and how that compares

 4            to its authorized return, is a product of numerous factors that may affect individual

 5            components of the Company’s costs and revenues in different ways and directions. For

 7            returns for individual functional components would differ. The distribution component

 8            might show a return 1% above the authorized return while the transmission component

 9            shows a return 1% below the authorized return. But neither result by itself means that the

10            Company is under- or over-earning since excesses for one portion can offset deficiencies

11            in another. A reasonable opportunity to earn the authorized return does not mean that the

12            Company is entitled to a guaranteed return on every individual cost component.
