COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before March 19, 2025.

Environmental Compliance

In its 2022 IRP, the Company proposed to permanently retire Bowen Units 1 - 2 no later than December 31, 2028. In the October 31, 2021 letter entitled “Plant Bowen – NPDES Permit No. GA0001449 Notice of Planned Participation Bowen Units 1 and 2,” the 2020 ELG compliance path was a complete cessation of combustion of coal via plant retirement for Units 1 and 2.  In a December 20, 2024 letter entitled “NPDES Permit No. GA0001449 – Plant Bowen ELG Progress Report,” the Company stated the following:
“The PSC decision in the 2022 IRP to reassess the timing of decertification of Plant Bowen Units 1-2 in the 2025 IRP does not impact the FGDW compliance strategy for Plant Bowen, as the FGDW system is a common environmental control for all four generating units.”
While the compliance strategy for the Bowen units might involve a common environmental control, there could be additional costs to extend the 2020 ELG compliance to Units 1 - 2. Please provide 2020 ELG capital cost estimates for the additional plant and equipment required to extend the Bowen Units 3 - 4 compliance strategy to cover Units 1 - 2.
Please provide estimates of operating and maintenance costs for the ELG compliance strategy divided between Units 1 - 2 and Units 3 - 4.
In the 2025 IRP Unit Retirement Study, study results tables such as those appearing in Appendix A and Appendix B show Plant Bowen Units 1 – 4, as opposed to only Plant Bowen Units 1- 2. Did the Company separate Bowen Units 1 - 2 from Units 3 - 4 for any of the analyses? If so, please provide summary tables like those shown in Appendix A and Appendix B for the Bowen separated units 1-2 and 3-4.


The 2024 ECS and the 2025 ECS both state that the 2025 IRP will make formal recommendations for the continued operation of Plant Bowen Units 1 - 2, as well as Plant Sherer Unit 3. 
Has the Company started the physical installation and expansion of the systems and equipment needed at Bowen Units 1-2 and Scherer Unit 3 in order for those units to comply with 2020 ELG requirements? The 2025 IRP Main filing states on p. 86-87 that the Plant Bowen ELG requirements will be completed by December 31, 2025. What work is being performed, above and beyond that needed for Bowen Units 3 and 4, and is needed only to bring Bowen Units 1 and 2 into compliance with the 2020 ELG rule?
Is the Company treating Bowen Units1-2 and Scherer Unit 3 upgrade costs as if they were approved as part of the 2023 IRP Update Final Order, or are these costs being treated as incremental and factored into the Company’s analysis in the 2025 IRP leading to the recommendation concerning the continued operation of these units?

The Unit Retirement Study uses a natural gas fired resource as the alternative resource for all the units studied. 
Did the Company analyze the use of a carbon-free solar, solar plus storage, or wind resources with existing transmission resources to replace one of the Plant Bowen or Scherer units, for example operating the unit until the end of 2028 and bringing the carbon-free unit online on 1/1/2029? If yes, please provide the results of this analysis.
Did the Company analyze the use of a carbon-free solar, solar plus storage, or wind resources with new transmission resources to replace one of the Plant Bowen or Scherer units, for example operating the unit until the end of 2028 and bringing the carbon-free unit online on 1/1/2029? If yes, please provide the results of this analysis.
If not, please explain why this alternative was not examined, particularly for Plant Bowen given the potentially high costs for firm gas transmission (FT) for partially or completely repowering the plant with natural gas.

 Regarding ash handling costs, please explain how ash costs are included in the analysis of plant closure dates for Plant Bowen and Plant Scherer and in the resource portfolio analyses that include these two plants. Do the plant closure date and resource portfolio analyses include estimates of (1) ongoing capital expenditures, (2) ongoing O&M expenses for handling and storing coal combustion residuals, and (3) the costs of future closure of ash handling facilities?

As outlined in Section 2.2 of the IRP Resource Mix Study appendix, the Company created scenarios around GHG pressures including regulations, pressures in various forms and limits on the level of allowable aggregate emissions. Please provide a table(s) summarizing the total GHG emissions over the 2025 IRP forecast period for each of the nine scenarios (i.e., those scenarios summarized in tables and graphics, such as those shown in Figure 14 of the IRP Resource Mix Study).

Using the total revenue requirements per resource scenario and the total quantities of GHG emissions generated by each resource plan, the Company could calculate a cost per metric ton of carbon that the system would not emit compared to some base. (The moderate gas/low carbon pressure scenario could be a base.) Such information could be useful for comparisons to estimates of the social cost of carbon (SCC) such as those used in other states (e.g., Washington) or for comparison to some of the proposed carbon taxes noted in the Company’s 2025 IRP. Given that the Company’s development of resource scenarios was predicated in large part on GHG constraints, can the Company provide estimates of the cost of GHG reductions achieved by each of the resource scenarios relative to the scenario the Company would consider to be the case that would be selected in the absence of GHG constraints? If yes, please provide the estimated cost per metric ton of GHG reductions.


In addition to GHG, each resource scenario has greater or lesser levels of regulated pollutants, such as particulates, sulfur dioxide, and nitrogen oxide. Please provide a table or tables that summarize by resource scenario the total emissions of regulated pollutants SO2, NOx, PM10, and mercury over the IRP analysis period.

In the IRP, Appendix H, the Company lists possible Research Activities. 
Strategic Aspiration #1 is developing a sustainable future. The following are questions related to this aspiration. 
The Company’s numbers in the Technology Screening appendix show that nuclear power has significantly higher capital cost than other technologies. Has the company considered how inverter-based technology could serve the functions that nuclear provides at the same or lower cost?  Is the company performing the research and development needed to bring inverter-based resources to a state where they might be as effective as rotating generators in regulating a power system?
Given the currently existing EPA GHG regulations, investigating hydrogen and oxy-combustion technologies also seems potentially cost-effective compared to nuclear. Drawing on the results presented in the 2025 IRP Appendix Technology Screening and Application Standards and based on the representative load factors included in that Appendix, please provide a representative rolled-in levelized cost per MWh for use in comparing the technologies listed in the Aspiration #1 including oxy-combustion technologies, CCS technologies in conjunction with CC and/or coal generation, nuclear, and inverter-based renewable resources. Include in the list, the same rolled-in levelized cost for the fuel cells included in technology screening and identified as potentially useful in increasing resiliency, but from all outward appearances, not selected for application in the IRP process.
Aspiration #1 indicates that work will continue to study CO2 storage. In the Resource Mix Study of the 2025 IRP, the Company placed a limit on carbon storage in Georgia and for the total system (presumedly the Southern Power Company system). Please explain how the Company determined the appropriate limit for the carbon storage capacity available in Georgia. To the extent this limit was based on preliminary drilling test results, please summarize the drilling work and how the drilling test results were extrapolated from a small set of drilling sites to the entire state of Georgia.
Aspiration #2 highlights the need to improve resiliency among other goals. Regarding the second bullet under Aspiration #2,the paragraph dedicates entire sentences to EMP/geomagnetic disturbances and to cyber-attacks.  There is a sentence split regarding “major threats and extreme events” between “both man-made and natural domains.” The presumption is that “natural domains” refers to the risks posed by extreme weather events such as the extreme flood events seen in western North Carolina in 2024.  Please explain the Company’s current efforts to identify and mitigate the risks posed by extreme natural events to the generating facilities, coal ash ponds, and to other assets such as transmission that were studied in the 2025 IRP.

In reviewing the 2025 IRP Unit Retirement Study, it appears from Appendix B, page 20, the Company provided costs for firm natural gas transportation (FT) to Plant Bowen for 40% co-fire and for the gas conversion. 
Is the FT cost an upgrade cost to increase natural gas pipeline capacity?
Please provide the basis for and specific components of the FT costs. 
Given that the IRP Resource Mix Study imposes limits on firm transportation for all new gas resources and applies such a high firm transportation cost for the Bowen Plant, please describe more fully why the Company believes the FT investment will ensure sufficient natural gas will be available to co-fire the plant 40% of the time. Can the Company guarantee that the Company will achieve the GHG reductions that should be available from co-firing?

In reviewing the 2025 IRP Unit Retirement Study, Appendix B shows that the most cost-effective option with Scherer Unit 3 for meeting the ELG and GHG requirements is CCS.  Please comment on whether the Company is considering implementing a CCS installation to keep the Scherer units operating beyond 2032, in compliance with the GHG regulation.
In reading the Unit Retirement Study, it appears the estimated 45Q benefit that makes CCS cost competitive has been reduced by 25% to account for risk. 
Please identify the risks that the Company accounted for with this 25% reduction.
Please explain how the 25% risk adjustment was determined.
There is a statement that the risk adjustment reflects “the potential need to transfer the credits.” What would cause the credits to need to be transferred and to what would the Company transfer the 45Q tax credits?
If the 45Q credit remains available and if the Company succeeded in installing and operating the CCS, the actual level of the 45Q credit would presumedly be higher than what is shown on the table in Appendix B. Please provide the estimated 45Q tax credit before the Company makes the risk adjustment.

Did Georgia Power consider economic reasons, such as fuel-price volatility, access to capital, litigation risks, and other factors related to cost-effectiveness, when deciding to update its retirement plans for its coal-fired plants and extend its operations? 

Please explain how Georgia Power is addressing the larger transition away from coal generation by electric utilities and its impact on coal production and supply transportation, specifically when choosing the natural gas co-firing compliance pathway for Plant Bowen and Plant Scherer. 


Is Georgia Power considering the health impacts on its customer base when extending the operations of these coal-fired units? If so, please explain.

Technical Appendix Volume 1 – 8 Environmental Compliance Strategy, includes Figure 2, which summarizes the “ten-year outlook for ECS projects for operational generating units.” Figure 2 only includes “boiler studies” to comply with the 111 GHG Rule. Is there additional information on measures and actions that will be implemented to comply with the rule once these studies are finished?
Please refer to the Company’s Environmental Compliance Strategy, page 19.

Please describe the capital projects that are required, and the estimated costs associated with them, for retrofits at the Bowen and Scherer coal units between now and 2029 to comply with the Supplemental ELG Rule.

The Company explains that “the Company’s scrubber wastewater treatment strategy for Plants Bowen and Scherer provides the flexibility to include those units in the treatment systems that are currently under construction if Plant Bowen Units 1-2 and Plant Scherer Unit 3 continue to operate beyond 2028.” Please explain whether continuing to operate Plant Bowen Units 1-2 and Plant Scherer Unit 3 beyond 2028 will or will not add to the costs associated with complying with the Supplemental ELG Rule. If yes, please describe the capital projects that are required and the costs associated with them. If not, please explain why not.
