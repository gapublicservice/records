                                FERMATA ENERGY
                       APPLICATION FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE


       COMES NOW, Fermata Energy, and hereby files this Application for Leave to Intervene

(“Application”) in the above-referenced docket pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59, Georgia Public

Service Commission (“Commission”) Rule 515-2-1-.06, and the Commission’s Procedural and

Scheduling Order (“PSO”) issued on December 28, 2025. In support hereof, Fermata Energy

respectfully states as follows:

       On January 31, 2025, Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power”) filed its 2025 Integrated

Resource Plan (“IRP”). This Application is timely filed pursuant to the Commission’s Procedural

and Scheduling Order, which states the following requirements:

       (a) Any person or party, on whom a statute does not confer an unconditional right
           to intervene, must file an application for leave to intervene within 30 days
           following the first published notice of the proceeding…

       (b) In addition to the requirements prescribed by O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59 for
           applications for leave to intervene, the application must (1) identify other
           intervening parties or intervening party applications whose interest is similar to
           that of the applicant, along with an explanation of why the identified intervening
           party or intervening party applicant will not adequately represent the applicant’s
           interest; and (2) state the applicant’s present intention to submit direct testimony
           and by whom and on what subject. The requirements identified herein shall
           constitute a continuing obligation of the applicant or intervening party. Each

                application for leave to intervene shall also contain a valid email address for the
                intervening party.1

           Fermata Energy is a leading provider of vehicle-to-grid (V2G) and vehicle-to-everything

(V2X) solutions, specializing in bidirectional electric vehicle (EV) charging technology that

enables EVs to support grid reliability and resilience while creating economic value for customers.

Fermata Energy’s optimization platform manages the charging and discharging of EVs in response

to real-time price signals, demand charges, and grid conditions—allowing customers to participate

in demand response, peak shaving, and other grid services.

           Fermata Energy has a substantial interest in Georgia Power’s 2025 IRP because the plan

directly impacts the role of distributed energy resources (DERs), including V2G technology, in

Georgia’s energy future. The 2025 IRP includes an Electric Transportation Vehicle-to-Everything

(“V2X”) Pilot focused on school buses (Chapter 10). This pilot is a critical first step but must be

designed, evaluated, and scaled effectively to maximize benefits to ratepayers and school districts.

The pilot should demonstrate grid benefits, including demand charge reduction, peak load shaving,

and local grid resiliency.

           No other intervening party adequately represents Fermata Energy’s specific interest in the

integration of bidirectional EV charging and V2G-enabled programs in Georgia Power’s IRP.

While other stakeholders may focus on DERs broadly, Fermata Energy’s expertise in V2G

technology and direct engagement with utilities in designing grid-interactive EV programs make

it uniquely positioned to contribute valuable insights to this proceeding.

       Fermata Energy’s participation will not unduly delay this proceeding or hinder the rights

of other parties. Rather, its intervention will contribute to a more comprehensive evaluation of the

proposed V2X electric school bus pilot.

       Pursuant to the Commission’s Procedural and Scheduling Order, Fermata Energy has not

yet finalized plans for direct testimony but expect to explore Georgia Power’s proposed V2X pilot

program. In the event that Fermata Energy submits testimony, the Company will be represented

by expert witness Steve Letendre, PhD, Senior Director of Regulatory Affairs with Fermata

Energy, a leading authority on EV-grid integration, distributed energy resource planning, and

energy market economics. Broadly speaking, potential testimony of Fermata Energy’s expert

witnesses will address the use of V2X technology to leverage the latent energy storage capability

of EVs to provide cost-effective grid flexibility.
