Residential Programs:

General Program Information
Potential Modification of Certified Programs 
The program design, delivery and implementation plans may be modified by Georgia Power and/or Southern Company during the three-year period (2026-2028) as needed to respond to unanticipated market barriers, implementation challenges, innovations, advances in technology, expansions in the market’s contractor capacity, program process evaluation findings, or other factors to improve the performance of the programs or otherwise achieve the stated goals. Georgia Power will evaluate potential strategy changes with the same cost-effectiveness criteria as used to design the programs, with notification or approval by Commission Staff, as appropriate.

Final program delivery mechanisms will be developed jointly by Georgia Power and its selected program implementer(s). 
Evaluation, Measure & Verification of Certified Programs
Georgia Power or Southern Company will contract with independent, third-party evaluators through a Request for Proposal (“RFP”) process to conduct comprehensive program Evaluation, Measure & Verification (“EM&V”). The EM&V will include the following: 
Market evaluations assessing customer and contractor awareness, providing insights into the success rate of program marketing, and outlining outreach and partnership efforts; 
Customer and contractor satisfaction measurements; 
Process evaluations, assessing the program design and implementation, determining effectiveness of administrative processes, reviewing program partner activities, and making recommendations on any identified modifications based on observations, customer feedback, and national best practices; and
Impact evaluations to determine the energy impacts produced by the program, and the costs of acquiring those impacts. Actual energy and demand savings occurring at the customer site will be calculated or verified (barring no health or safety issues that increase risk to the employees of the Company or its representative or customers) to determine a program realization rate, and cost-effectiveness as defined by industry standards. Free-ridership and participant spillover will also be calculated as part of the impact evaluations.
General Awareness and Marketing for Certified Programs
Georgia Power’s general awareness marketing strategies will promote the value, resources, and programs available for energy efficiency and will include cross-marketing of various resources and programs as well as collaboration with external partners to cross-promote and leverage other energy efficiency and/or demand response initiatives. The goal of general awareness is to increase both residential and commercial customers’ recall and knowledge of the benefits of energy efficiency and/or demand response initiatives, programs and resources.

In addition to the general awareness marketing efforts for DSM programs, each program will develop a customized, program specific go to market strategy and outreach plan focused on overcoming program market barriers to maximize equitable market penetration of energy-efficient measures and enhance program participation. Specific marketing and outreach strategies will be outlined during final program implementation processes with selected program implementer(s).

All reasonable and cost-effective channels will be used to market DSM programs and to build general awareness directly to customers. These channels may include, but are not limited to, the following materials and activities, all of which have proven to be cost-effective ways of attracting participants in other utility-sponsored programs:
Customer emails;
Digital advertising;
Customer newsletters;
Program brochures/fliers/fact sheets/case studies;
Social media marketing;
Customer bill inserts;
Customer on-bill message;
Targeted direct-mail campaigns and promotions;
Radio and television advertisements;
Print ads;
Georgia Power owned, self-service channels including online customer care; 
Communication through demand response devices and applicable manufacturers’ app;
Cooperative outreach and engagement through Trade Allies and participating program contractors, manufacturers, distributors, wholesalers, retailers, and other industry partner promotions; 
Point of purchase displays and messaging; 
One-on-one facilitation; and 
Trade shows, events, and outreach.

Program Performance Metrics
Program performance and progress towards achieving established goals will be tracked and communicated on a recurring basis. Program performance indicators will be developed to monitor program progress toward annual goals. These indicators and activities may include:
Type and number of measures installed;
Number of unique customers as applicable per program implementation approach;  
Achieved energy and/or demand savings; 
Total program costs (incentive and non-incentive spend) versus program budget comparisons; and
Status of progress towards achieving program goals. 

Program Administrative Procedure
Georgia Power or its representative(s) will provide administrative procedures including but not limited to the following major categories (as applicable): 
Customer call center and/or care support;
Participant inquiries and assistance;
Program eligibility validation;
Application review, processing, and approvals;
Invoice and incentive fulfillment processes;
Rebate intake;
Site visits (barring no health or safety issues that increase risk to the employees of the Company or its representative or customers); 
Technical resources; 
Quality control processes;
Management of participating channels; and
Program metrics tracking. 
Program administrative procedure requirements will be outlined during final program implementation processes with selected program implementer(s) based on specific program needs.

Residential Home Energy Improvement Program (“HEIP”)
1.1 Program Summary
Georgia Power’s HEIP promotes lasting energy efficiency improvements in new and existing homes and provides financial incentives for implementing eligible energy efficiency measures. The program provides customers, remodelers, builders, developers, and property owners with multiple participation options including a comprehensive, whole-house approach and an individual improvement(s) option to appeal to a wide range of residential customers. In addition to the financial incentives offered, the program includes customer education, program awareness campaigns, and contractor mentoring and training.

The goals of Georgia Power’s HEIP include:
Achieving energy and demand savings in new and existing homes and multifamily units by helping customers lower their energy usage; 
Raising the awareness of energy efficiency practices and services among Georgia Power’s residential customers, builders, remodelers, property owners, and local contractors to ensure the sustainability of the program’s energy efficiency efforts; and
Encouraging the use of ENERGY STAR or other similarly certified equipment and other energy efficiency measures and tactics.
1.2 Program Participant Eligibility & Incentives
The program is available to Georgia Power residential customers in both single-family and multifamily buildings. Eligibility for customers receiving Georgia Power’s HEIP rebates will be verified based on improvement documentation and the Georgia Power account or meter number provided on the rebate application, or other unique identifier for point of purchase validation options as applicable and available.

Georgia Power’s HEIP offers two participation options for customers: 
Whole-House Approach – consisting of the installation of a combination of energy saving measures that target whole-home energy reduction with rebates based on the overall reduction in the home’s electrical energy consumption; and
Individual Improvement(s) Approach – including the installation or improvement of one or more individual energy saving measures with rebates provided per installed measure. 

For customers participating in the Whole-House Approach, a diagnostic home energy assessment must be completed by a participating program contractor. Any energy saving measures that demonstrate an improvement in energy efficiency and contribute to an overall reduction in the home’s electrical energy consumption are eligible. Reduction in whole-home electric energy consumption is determined by a comparison of the simulated energy consumption of the home before and after the retrofit of the home by qualified modeling software approved by Georgia Power or its representative. 

For customers participating in the Individual Improvement(s) Approach, they can choose from the following:
Work with a program contractor for energy savings measures requiring performance testing;
Perform “do it yourself” installation of some measures; or 
Work with a licensed contractor for some measures. 
Determination of installation requirements will be made during final program design. For all eligible measures installed, the program implementation contractor will conduct verification inspections on a sample of participating homes, either virtually or on-site. 

The incentive amounts for the Whole-House and/or Individual Improvement(s) Approach may be periodically adjusted to reflect current market conditions or changes in equipment costs or program economics to encourage participation during designated time periods or to improve participation in particular geographical areas, while maintaining the overall cost-effectiveness of the program. Incentives may be paid to the residential customer, the participating contractor, or the builder, as needed to effectively meet program savings goals.

1.3 Program Implementation 
A key aspect of Georgia Power’s HEIP is for the customer to achieve energy and demand savings. Program implementation plans will include program delivery strategies to overcome market barriers, enable ease of participation and meet program goals.
1.4 Program Customer Participation Process 
Customers participating in Georgia Power’s HEIP will have two participation options, including the following:

The Whole-House Approach
Customer selects a participating program contractor, who will provide a comprehensive home energy assessment to evaluate the baseline conditions, identify potential energy savings opportunities, and calculate the electricity savings potential for these improvements. 
Customer decides on the energy saving measures to implement.
Contractor completes the project and works with the customer to complete the program application form and required documentation to Georgia Power or its representative.
The Individual Improvement(s) Approach
Customers can install some measures themselves, while other measures require an industry qualified contractor or a participating program contractor for installation.

Residential Energy Assistance for Savings and Efficiency Program (“EASE”)
1.1 Program Summary
Georgia Power’s EASE program promotes energy efficiency improvements in existing income-qualified and moderate-income single-family homes, manufactured homes, as well as multifamily properties. The program targets and provides direct energy efficient improvements to income-qualified and moderate-income households that are historically under-represented in energy efficiency program participation. The program also provides members of the community the opportunity to donate funds and assist neighbors in need of home energy efficiency upgrades or improvements. These donations will be additive to the funding provided through Georgia Power as part of the DSM tariff. Where possible, Georgia Power may work with community partners and local nonprofits to identify, assess, and implement projects within some of the state’s most energy burdened neighborhoods. In addition to the energy efficiency improvements offered, the program includes customer education and awareness campaigns, as well as contractor partnerships, mentoring and training that can increase market capacity overall. New this cycle is the inclusion of moderate-income customers and measures for manufactured homes to reach more customers. 

The goals of Georgia Power’s EASE program include:
Achieving energy and demand savings in existing income-qualified and moderate income homes; 
Alleviating affordability concerns and improve comfort in homes across multiple building types through energy efficiency improvements;
Raising awareness of energy efficiency practices and services among Georgia Power’s customers and local contractors to ensure the sustainability of the program’s energy efficiency efforts;
Encouraging the development and expansion of a statewide contractor network; 
Achieving direct financial savings for the homeowner or the tenant in rental homes;
Increasing customer engagement with Georgia Power; 
Increasing overall customer satisfaction with services provided by Georgia Power; and
Providing members of the community with a donation platform to assist customers in need of energy efficiency upgrades within their home.
1.2 Program Participant Eligibility & Incentives
The program is available to income-qualified and moderate-income Georgia Power residential customers statewide residing in single-family detached homes, manufactured homes, and multifamily properties. 

Income level eligibility will be developed during implementation, based on market best practices and to enable coordination with other energy efficiency programs, philanthropic groups, and/or governmental programs. It also may be updated to reflect market conditions or program economics to ensure equitable participation.

Single-family, manufactured homes or multifamily properties that are privately-owned or publicly-owned may be considered for inclusion in the program. 

The EASE program may offer energy saving measures, as listed in Appendix H, for single-family, manufactured homes and multifamily properties. Georgia Power will provide value up to $5,000 per home for energy efficiency improvements.

1.3 Program Implementation 
A key aspect of Georgia Power’s EASE program is to alleviate energy burden and increase comfort in homes of income-qualified and moderate-income customers. Program implementation plans will include program delivery strategies to overcome market barriers, enable ease of participation, and meet program goals.
1.4 Program Customer and Contractor Participation Process 
Customers seeking to participate in the program will complete an application through Georgia Power or through potential statewide resources that perform income-qualified assistance. Georgia Power’s program team will then determine eligibility. Once eligibility is confirmed, the EASE program team will assess the needs of the individual property.

Contractors will be recruited to register and participate in Georgia Power’s EASE program. The program will establish requirements for contractor(s) certifications and eligibility, proof of sufficient liability insurance, and list of project qualifications and/or references. Contractors will complete an application form and after review and approval by program staff, will be a program contractor.

Once enrolled in the program, contractor(s) may then complete energy efficiency improvements. Upon the completion of the job and submission of the documentation, contractors will be paid for services rendered for the job.

Residential Demand Response Program (“DR”)
1.1 Program Summary
The Residential Demand Response (“Res DR”) program promotes shifting electricity usage during peak demand periods. This could include hot summer days, cold winter days, or whenever there is a system capacity need. Georgia Power, or its representative, can manage the electric load from participants' homes by automatically adjusting thermostat or other connected devices. It can also incentivize customers to take action at specific times of day. Demand response programs, in general, help minimize the use of higher cost peaking plants during periods of high system loads. The Res DR program provides education and financial incentives for customers with a qualifying existing smart thermostat and/or other connected devices that are willing to reduce energy during periods of peak demand through demand response events called by Georgia Power. In addition to the incentives offered, the program includes customer education and awareness messaging.

The goals of Georgia Power’s Res DR program include:
Achieving peak demand reduction (kW) in homes during specific times of day;
Raising awareness by cross promoting energy efficiency practices and services among Georgia Power’s customers to ensure the sustainability of the program’s energy efficiency efforts; and
Increasing customer engagement with Georgia Power and increasing overall customer satisfaction with services provided by Georgia Power.
1.2 Program Participant Eligibility & Incentives
The program is available to Georgia Power residential customers in both single-family and multifamily homes. Eligibility for customers receiving an incentive will be verified based on customer data provided on the application. Georgia Power residential customers occupying single-family or multifamily homes with an individual electric meter are eligible to participate. 

Georgia Power’s Res DR program offers: 
New customers with an installed connected device (Bring Your Own Device (“BYOD”) or other enrollment option) will receive a one-time incentive to enroll in the program;
Existing enrolled customers will receive an incentive to continue program participation.

Additional enrollment options and incentives may be offered if deemed necessary by the Company and its program implementer.
1.3 Program Implementation 
Georgia Power’s Res DR program works with customers to encourage enrollment or action so that their electric connected device(s) can be called in demand response events. Program implementation plans will include program delivery strategies to overcome market barriers, enable ease of participation and meet program goals.
1.4 Program Customer Participation Process 
Customer participation has a BYOD path or the option of purchasing and enrolling a qualifying connected device. BYOD customers need to complete a program application form and provide the necessary network information for their connected device(s) to establish a secure connection with Georgia Power’s Res DR program. Customers in Georgia Power service areas purchasing qualifying connected devices may receive demand response program enrollment information through their connected device manufacturer app. They can also enroll through the Georgia Power website. Georgia Power, the implementer, and connected device manufacturers may send marketing communications, using propensity models, when possible, to identify customers with eligible electric load. 

When Georgia Power experiences high peak demand and anticipates the need for additional capacity, a demand response event may be initiated. Customers may opt not to participate in a single event. Customers may also notify Georgia Power if they no longer wish to participate in the program for any event and will be subsequently unenrolled from the program.

Residential Behavioral Program
1.1 Program Summary
Georgia Power’s Residential Behavioral program is designed to encourage customer engagement with home energy management and energy efficiency to reduce energy consumption. The program provides customer-specific information that allows them to compare their energy use for the month, as well as the past year, in all or some cases to the consumption of a peer group of similar homes and ones that are considered energy-efficient. Participating customers receive a paper and/or electronic Home Electric Report (“HER”) multiple times a year with a summary of their energy consumption data and consumption over the same time period by their peers. The reports also include seasonal and household-appropriate energy savings tips, as well as information on energy efficiency programs. 

The goals of Georgia Power’s Residential Behavioral program include:
Generating cost-effective energy savings by increasing customer awareness of household energy use and encouraging customers to adopt energy-efficient behaviors;
Educating customers about energy savings benefits and promoting other energy efficiency program options; 
Increasing customer engagement with Georgia Power, as well as increasing overall customer satisfaction with services provided by Georgia Power; and
Identifying income-qualified customers with a dedicated focus on outreach and relevant energy saving information. 
1.2 Program Participant Eligibility & Incentives
Georgia Power residential customers occupying a qualifying home type with an individual electric meter, and who have at least thirteen months of electricity consumption history, are eligible to be selected to participate in the Behavioral program. A portion of eligible customers are placed into a control group to satisfy EM&V requirements. Customers assigned to the control group are not eligible to participate in the Behavioral program. Customers enrolled in FlatBill are also not eligible. 

Income level eligibility criteria for any income-qualified or moderate-income waves will be developed in implementation based on market best practices and to be consistent across other residential energy efficiency programs. It also may be updated to reflect market conditions or program economics to ensure equitable participation.

The Residential Behavioral program does not offer financial incentives for the installation of specific energy efficiency measures in the home, but rather provides participants with a free HER summarizing their energy consumption data and consumption over the same time period as compared to their peers. The HER is also used to promote awareness of other Georgia Power energy efficiency programs and offerings. 
1.3 Program Implementation 
A key aspect of Georgia Power’s Residential Behavioral program is offering customers periodic reports to compare their household energy consumption with peers and to influence and change their behaviors to become more energy efficient. Program implementation plans will include program delivery strategies to overcome market barriers, enable ease of participation and meet program goals.
1.4 Program Customer Participation Process 
The Residential Behavioral program is an opt-out program in which Georgia Power, or its representative(s) will identify a population of eligible customers to receive periodic HERs and provide these reports at regular intervals throughout the year, unless the customer opts out. Customers may notify Georgia Power if they no longer wish to receive a paper HER, electronic HER, or both, and will be subsequently removed from the program.

Commercial Custom Program

1.1 Program Summary
Georgia Power’s Commercial Custom program provides a platform for comprehensive energy efficiency projects in existing and new facilities that go beyond discrete measures and common, measure-level efficiency practices. The Custom program provides incentives for efficiency improvements not included in other Georgia Power commercial program offerings. All program incentives are based on the calculated energy savings achieved for each project. The program does not define a specific list of eligible measures and bases participation on verifiable energy savings resulting from measures or system improvements implemented. Due to the complexity and variety of measures that could potentially be included, Georgia Power’s Commercial Custom program requires the applicant to submit calculations using industry-accepted methods for determining energy savings and appropriate baselines. These savings could be derived from capital improvements in equipment or from other building upgrades such as retro commissioning (“RCx”).

The goals of Georgia Power’s Commercial Custom program include:
Increasing customer acceptance and use of energy-efficient technologies and practices;
Encouraging and supporting comprehensive energy efficiency projects that go beyond discrete measures or common efficiency practices; and
Obtaining verifiable, cost-effective, and long-term electrical energy and demand savings. 

1.2 Program Participant Eligibility & Incentives

To participate in the Custom program, a customer must be served by Georgia Power and must install qualifying measures or conduct system or equipment upgrades in a commercial-class facility on a Georgia Power commercial tariff. Participation can be within existing or new construction buildings. Incentives are available for both advanced lighting and non-lighting measures that result in energy savings. Additionally, incentives are available for existing buildings that conduct upgraded processes that identify low-cost or no cost measures, including control adjustments, improvements to building performance, and reducing operating costs. All measures will require pre-installation and post-installation approval and will be reviewed using industry standard EM&V. Eligible measures consist of energy-efficient technologies that result in lasting energy savings. To provide program participants maximum flexibility in identifying potential projects, the program does not explicitly specify eligible measures or RCx processes. Instead, participants may propose the inclusion of measures that:
Produce measurable and verifiable energy and demand savings; and
Exceed government and industry minimum standards (project will be evaluated and incentives will be paid on a case-by-case basis only on the energy savings above and beyond current code or industry standards for energy efficiency). 

Measures that are not eligible for the program incentive include those that:
Rely on changes solely in customer behavior;
Achieve savings through rightsizing the HVAC equipment without an equipment efficiency upgrade; 
Involve only relocating existing equipment or processes from one facility to another;
Are required by local, state, or federal law; building or other codes; or are standard industry practice;
Produce a reduction in the use of electricity through substitution of another energy source;
Merely terminate existing processes, facilities, or operations;
Involve plug loads, except when exception is granted for emerging technologies or automation processes; or
Are eligible for an incentive through any other energy efficiency program offered by Georgia Power. 

Incentives will consist of a one-time payment based on estimated annual energy savings attained from the first year of operation. Savings baselines will be based on current code and/or industry standards for replacement and new construction measures. Georgia Power, or its representative, will reserve the right to exercise discretion and adjust baselines for retrofit projects affecting measures significantly before the end of their useful life or for measures where codes and/or current industry practices are not applicable.

The incentive amount may be periodically adjusted to reflect current market conditions, changes in equipment costs or program economics, to encourage participation during particular time periods, or to improve participation in particular geographical areas, while maintaining the overall cost-effectiveness of the program. 

1.3 Program Implementation 
A key aspect of Georgia Power’s Custom program is increasing customer awareness of the program, the participation process, as well as promoting qualifying energy-efficient products and measures. Program implementation plans will include program delivery strategies to overcome market barriers, enable ease of participation and meet program goals.
1.4 Program Customer Participation Process

The goals of the Prescriptive program include:
Increasing awareness and customer demand for high efficiency, energy saving equipment and technologies; and

Traditional Rebates; 
Distributor Rebates; or 
Retail Instant Rebates.

Program Plan Development Summary
Originating from the 2010 IRP Order and as reaffirmed in the 2013, 2016, 2019, and 2022 IRP Final Orders, the Commission approved the Nine Step DSM Planning Process (renamed the “DSM Program Planning Approach”) that guided the development of the Company’s 2025 IRP and DSM Certification plans. In addition, the Company met with the Demand-Side Management Working Group (“DSMWG”) eight times during 2023 and 2024 and engaged with the DSMWG to attempt to reach agreement on the development of DSM programs filed in the 2025 IRP and this DSM Application. Additionally, the Company met with DSMWG subcommittees in February 2024 to discuss their ideas for the DSM Proposed Case and later received program details for modeling of a DSM Advocacy Case (“Advocacy Case”) proposed by certain members of the DSMWG. Finally, the Company shared data with the DSMWG in preparation for, and leading up to, the 2025 IRP filing and this DSM Application.

DSM Program Cost-Effectiveness
Program cost-effectiveness calculations are estimated and outlined in Appendix H Program Design Metrics. In addition, Appendix E contains the summary of the cost-effectiveness tests for DSM programs the Company has filed. These appendices are in the 2025 DSM Program Planner Summary in Docket No. 56003. Even though avoided energy costs have increased since the 2022 IRP which has a positive impact on energy efficiency economics, both Total Resource Cost (“TRC”) and Rate Impact Measure (“RIM”) test results have declined primarily due to the substantial amount of resources needed to achieve high energy savings resulting from the Vogtle Prudence Order.  This trend in declining economics continues to raise concerns for the Company as it strives to balance the economic benefits that DSM programs provide to participating customers with the rate impacts to all residential and commercial customers, whether they participate in the programs or not. The Company plans to monitor program costs and economics through 2028 and will be prepared to modify programs if significant upward pressure on rates continues.
The Company is seeking a waiver of the TRC requirement within Commission Rule 515-3-4-.04(4)(a)(3) for the inclusion of the Residential Home Energy Improvement, Energy Assistance for Savings and Efficiency, and HopeWorks programs and Commercial Custom program due to the value these programs provide to customers, and the need for these programs to achieve the targeted energy savings outlined in the Vogtle Prudence Order. Each of these programs has high customer satisfaction and market potential. However, the programs do not reflect positive TRC results for the 2025 IRP cycle due to the significant resources needed to achieve the large energy savings target agreed to in the Vogtle Prudence Order. 
DSM Program Milestones
Upon Commission approval of this DSM Application, the Company will finalize its plans to implement the DSM programs for 2026 during the last several months of 2025. This will include planning for the continuation of and making modifications to some existing programs and beginning start-up activities and changes in program implementation, such as infrastructure refinement and contracting with third-party vendors. No sooner than six months after program implementation, the Company will begin process evaluations to review the effectiveness of key program elements. No sooner than twelve months after program implementation, the Company will begin impact evaluation studies. These studies will attempt to verify projected demand and energy savings, as well as market effects, such as free-ridership and participant spillover. It is important to recognize that estimates of market effects from these studies are relevant to program participation at the time the estimates are made and are not necessarily indicative of market effects for future program participation. The Company recommends the continuation of implementing evaluation results and applying deemed savings to the new program cycle beginning in January 2029 to be consistent with the Company’s three-year IRP and DSM planning cycles.
REGULATORY COST RECOVERY OF DSM PROGRAMS
The Company requests approval of the costs for certified DSM programs and other DSM activities as summarized in Appendix C-1 and C-2. The Company also requests approval of an Additional Sum amount for all certified DSM programs with energy savings in the Residential and Commercial portfolios, as summarized in Appendix F. The Company proposes that all approved and certified DSM program costs and other DSM activities, as well as an Additional Sum amount for all certified DSM programs, be collected through the existing Residential and Commercial DSM tariffs. These updated tariffs will be filed as part of the Company’s 2025 base rate case for implementation with any approved change of rates on January 1,2026. Consistent with past precedent, the Company will provide an annual update to the Commission Staff, including projected program costs for the following year, and the tariffs will be adjusted subsequently as the Commission deems appropriate.
The Company proposes to amend the calculation of the Additional Sum to provide more stability in the metric used to determine Additional Sum and certainty in the amount of incentive that the Company receives for implementing DSM programs, as allowed by state law. The proposed change in methodology better aligns the Additional Sum with the energy savings achieved from the customer classes eligible to participate in the Company’s DSM programs. In addition, the simplified approach will streamline reporting and provide more clarity associated with the annual DSM tariff true-up process. 
Under the revised calculation, the Additional Sum will be collected at $0.04 per kWh saved applied to net energy savings. All certified DSM programs with energy savings will be included in the Additional Sum calculation.  Any authorized Additional Sum will be specific to the corresponding customer class and will be collected through the Residential and Commercial DSM tariffs. The Additional Sum included in the DSM tariffs will apply the $0.04 per kWh saved to the total net energy savings estimated in the Company’s 2025 Proposed Case. The Company supports the annual true-up of the DSM tariffs based on reported energy savings and actual revenues collected.
