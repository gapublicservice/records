Large load customers account for much of the increase to the load forecast. For a detailed list of the large load economic development pipeline assumed in the load forecast used in the 2025 IRP, including location, announced load ramp, and whether the project is inside or outside Georgia Power Territory, please refer to Georgia Power’s Q2 2024 Large Load Economic Development Report filed on August 16, 2024. Due to the size and differing levels of progress of each project, the Company continues to utilize the Load Realization Model (“LRM”) developed for the 2023 IRP Update. This model uses a probabilistic approach to evaluate the range and likelihood of future potential outcomes of load growth from new committed and prospective large load customers. The model accounts for the size and various progress stages of individual projects in Georgia Power’s large load economic development pipeline. The LRM assesses the risk associated with announced loads being realized by assigning lower likelihoods than to committed customers. As such, all projects are included in the load forecast but at different levels of materialization.

A plant is licensed to operate within a specified power and core flow domain. Maintaining proper core flow is essential as it ensures effective cooling of the reactor. 
Maximum Extended Load Line Limit Plus (“MELLLA+”) is an approved method used in a boiling water reactor (“BWR”) power plant to safely increase the allowed range of water flow through the reactor core. By giving plant operators a larger range of licensed core flow, MELLLA+ allows more operational flexibility to manage core flow effectively and can improve plant capacity. MELLLA+ is increasingly important for BWRs with EPU. EPU reduces the allowed core flow range and MELLLA+ restores the allowed range of core flow.
MELLLA+ involves upgrading the Power Range Neutron Monitor system and updating the power-to-flow map. The Power Range Neutron Monitor is used to monitor the neutron flux – the measure of intensity of neutron radiation – in the reactor core, which is crucial for ensuring the safe and efficient operation of the reactor.
