COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before March 21, 2025. 


DEFINITIONS

As may be used in this document:  
1.“The Company,” “Georgia Power” or “The Utility” means Georgia Power Company and its present and former officers, employees, agents, representatives, directors, and all other persons acting or purporting to act on behalf of said Company.
The term “you” and “your” refer to “the Company.”
The term “person” means any natural person, corporation, corporate division, partnership, other unincorporated association, trust, government agency, or entity.
The term “document” or “documentation” shall have the broadest possible meaning under applicable law.  “Document” or “documentation” means every writing or record of every type and description that is in the possession, custody or control of the Company including, but not limited to, correspondence, memoranda, e-mails, drafts, workpapers, summaries, stenographic or handwritten notes, studies, notices, publications, books, pamphlets, reports, surveys, minutes or statistical compilations, computer and other electronic records or tapes or printouts, including, but not limited to, electronic mail files; and copies of such writing or records containing any commentary or notation whatsoever that does not appear in the original.  The term “document” or “documentation” further includes, by way of illustration and not limitation, schedules, progress schedules, time logs, drawings, computer disks, chart projections, time tables, summaries of other documents, minutes, surveys, work sheets, drawings, comparisons, evaluations, laboratory and testing reports, telephone call records, personal diaries, calendars, personal notebooks, personal reading files, transcripts, witness statements and indices.
The term “referring or relating to” means consisting of, containing, mentioning, suggesting, reflecting, concerning, regarding, summarizing, analyzing, discussing, involving, dealing with, emanating from, directed at, pertaining to in any way, or in any way logically or factually connected or associated with the matter discussed.
“And” and “or” as used herein shall be construed both conjunctively and disjunctively and each shall include the other whenever such construction will serve to bring within the scope of these discovery requests any information that would not otherwise be brought within their scope.
The singular as used herein shall include the plural and the masculine gender shall include the feminine and the neuter.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a person other than a natural person means to state:  the full name of the person and any names under which it conducts business; the present or last known address of the person; and, the present or last known telephone number of the person.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a document means to provide with respect to each document requested to be identified by these discovery requests a description of the document that is sufficient for purposes of a request to produce or a subpoena duces tecum, including the following:

Refer to Docket No. 44280, and STF-WG-2-26 Attachment TRADE SECRET.  This attachment provided 10 years’ of information about sub-transmission-level equipment outages, including line identification, dates and times, causes, equipment types, and other information, as well as customer interruptions (CI) and customer minutes of interruption (CMI) associated with each equipment outage (if any).  Using this attachment as a template, please provide the same information for calendar year 2024.

Refer to Docket No. 44280, and STF-WG-11-16 Attachment TRADE SECRET.  This attachment provides information on distribution service interruptions in 2022 and 2023, including event identifiers, feeders, dates, root causes, cause types, and other information, as well as CI and CMI associated with each service interruption.  Using this attachment as a template, please provide the same information for calendar year 2024.
 
Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document and the 2025 IRP Renewable Cost Benefit framework.  PIA Staff notes that Table 1 on page 3 indicates that Renewable Integration costs are included in the framework.
Presumably, the Company records Renewable Integration costs with a journal entry as capacity and energy are purchased from renewable and renewable plus storage facilities. For each cost, provide the sample journal entries the Company employs to incur such costs, as well as the offsetting accounts (such as plant maintenance or fuel costs).

Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, page 77, which states “Georgia Power, the Commission, and renewable energy stakeholders in Georgia have created a vibrant, sustainable renewable energy market that continues to benefit all Georgians. This success can be measured not only by the number of projects and renewable capacity on the System, but also by the value created for customers through the Company’s robust renewable planning and competitive procurement processes.”
Provide the Company’s coincident peak load in MW, along with the date and time, observed in 2021 and 2024.
Provide the Company’s energy sales (in MWh) in 2021 and 2024 by type of customer, including i) residential; ii) commercial; iii) industrial; and iv) institutional/other (co-operatively or municipally-owned utilities, as examples). 
Provide the Company’s marginal cost per MW of added generation capacity in its 2025 IRP as proposed and provide a citation to this figure.
 
Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, page 102, which states “The largest procurement of energy from customer-sited resources comes from residential solar installations, which are compensated at prices above the value these resources deliver.”  Provide workpapers supporting this statement, including the details of how customers are being compensated and the details behind the valuation of those resources.
 
Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, page 102, which states “Notably, only approximately 17% of customer-sited solar generation participating in a Georgia Power program include a BESS.”  Provide (or estimate) the capacity of these customer-sited solar generation with storage facilities by rate class (residential, commercial, and industrial) and by customer program as of December 31, 2024 (or other date convenient to the Company given the referenced statement).  

Page 105 of the 2025 IRP Main Document states, regarding CCSP enhancements, “Compensation for participating customers will be based on the energy and capacity value the systems are projected to deliver.”  Confirm that “Compensation” refers to the compensation levels described in the customer-directed and company-directed descriptions for the solar plus Storage Pilot Program on pages 104-105.  If this cannot be confirmed, explain how compensation will be determined for CCSP enhancements, and explain why this compensation is different than under the Solar Plus Storage Pilot Program.

Page 105 of the 2025 IRP Main Document states, regarding CCSP enhancements, “The capacity value created by the dispatchable storage systems will benefit all Georgia Power customers”.  Provide any workpapers indicating the dispatchable storage system costs per MW under the CCSP enhancements will be less than the Company’s system-wide costs per MW.  If dispatchable storage system costs per MW under the CCSP enhancements will be more than the Company’s system-wide marginal costs per MW added, please explain how dispatchable storage systems under this program “will benefit all Georgia Power customers.”

In regard to the Solar Plus Storage Pilot Program, the description of the Company-Directed program states the "Eligible customer generators will enter a ten-year contract to allow the BESS to be dispatched by Georgia Power. This contract will be tied to the premises and is intended to be conveyed to future customers in the same location in order to support the long-term nature of the agreement term." (2025 IRP Main Document, page 104).
How does the Company propose the contract be tied to the premises?
Does the Company have any experience managing these types of contracts?  If so, please describe relevant examples
Has the Company investigated whether this type of contractual arrangement would create an impediment if and when a participant goes to sell their home?

Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, pages 106-107, which describe four relatively new tariffs for DER sited at commercial customer premises, including RAS-1, DRC-1, DCL-1, and DCO-1.  
Provide a count of customers participating in each tariff as of December 31, 2024.  For each customer participating in any tariff provide i) the customer’s native load; ii) the capacity of the DER; iii) the capital cost added to the rate base for DER owned by the Company.
Provide an anonymized list of customers with which the Company is currently in discussions for each tariff.  For each such anonymized customer listed please provide i) the customer’s native load; and ii) the capacity (or estimated capacity) of the DER being discussed; and iii) the capital cost estimated to be added to rate base for DER to be owned by the Company (as applicable). 
 
Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, page 107, which describes the Large Customer Owned Resiliency Program. 
It appears to PIA Staff that the Company’s DPEC-4 tariff/demand response credit program already provides “economic value for firm load reductions while accelerating the timeframe in which capacity can be recognized”.  Given the Company’s DPEC-4 tariff/credit program, please explain why this proposed program is necessary.
Refer to the statement “Like all the Company’s DER programs, non-participating customers benefit through the shared savings model where capacity is being procured at a discount relative to generic capacity costs.”  Provide the workpapers indicating (or estimating) i) the price at which capacity is likely to be procured under the program; ii) generic capacity costs and iii) a sample calculation using current capacity values.  
 
Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, page 108, which states “The Company is currently administering a residential pilot with both passive and active managed charging participation options for customers.”  Please provide relevant internal documents regarding this pilot, including:
A description of the pilot and its design, including a list of all learning objectives and specific results to be measured.
A description of the pilot’s costs, presentations provided to Company management to secure spending approval, and Company management and/or Commission approval of the pilot.
Current status of the pilot, including passive participants, active participants, expected pilot completion, and anticipated pilot results reporting date.
 
Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, page 108, which describes a “six month pilot with Ford Pro involve(ing) more than 200 F-150 Lighting trucks across Southern Company’s fleet of company vehicles”.  Please provide relevant internal documents regarding this pilot, including:
A description of the pilot and its design, including a list of all learning objectives and specific results to be measured.
A description of the pilot’s costs, presentations provided to Company management to secure spending approval, and Company management and/or Commission approval of the pilot.
Current status of the pilot, including expected pilot completion and anticipated pilot results reporting date.

Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, pages 108-109, which describes a “Vehicle-to-Everything” pilot with up to 10 chargers installed with public school systems.  Please provide relevant internal documents regarding this pilot, including:
A description of the pilot and its design, including a list of all learning objectives and specific results to be measured.
Presentations provided to Company management to secure spending approval, and Company management and/or Commission approval of the pilot.
Current status of the pilot, including expected pilot completion and anticipated pilot results reporting date.

Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, pages 108-109, which describes a “Vehicle-to-Everything” pilot with up to 10 chargers installed with public school systems.
Are the 10 chargers to be installed intended to be bi-directional?
Will pilot funds be used to purchase public school vehicles?  If so, how many vehicles, what type(s), and how much is expected to be spent?

Refer to the 2025 IRP Main Document, page 109, regarding the descriptions of the Company’s efforts to deploy a DERMS.  Refer also to the Company’s 2025 IRP Renewable Cost-Benefit Framework.
Provide a description of the capabilities and benefits of each of the systems referred to on page 109, including but not limited to DERMS, ADMS, EMS, PPC, and other “existing and upcoming systems”.
Provide the DER gateway requirements and the Company’s anticipated implementation timelines, including any applications anticipated by the Company to update its DER interconnection standards. Specifically, include a description of the circumstances in which the Company expects to control customer-owned DER by customer DER program/DER facility capacity.  
Refer to the list of systems and descriptions provided in response to subpart (a) above. For each, provide the anticipated dollar amount of rate base additions required for deployment, along with the depreciation period the Company will use for each.
Refer generally to the list of systems and descriptions provided in response to subpart (a) above.  Please explain how Georgia Power accounts for the benefits and costs of these systems in the Company’s 2025 IRP Renewable Cost Benefit framework.
