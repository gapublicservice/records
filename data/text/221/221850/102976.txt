STF-DEA-1-13

Question:

Refer to the document titled “B2025 Load and Energy Forecast PUBLIC DISCLOSURE,” in Technical Appendix Volume 1, p. 27. The Company refers to an “efficiency variable derived from the long-term energy forecast model.” Please describe how this variable is calculated and justify why it is appropriate to include the result of a long-term energy model as an input to the short-term energy model.  

Response:

The efficiency variable is an index that represents the weighted average end-use efficiency from the long-term energy model, LoadMap. The variable is the sum product of the average stock efficiency and the usage share of each end-use. The B2025 efficiency variables were developed based on the B2024 LoadMap model.

It is appropriate to consider efficiency in the short-term energy model because efficiency improvement is one of the usage drivers. Since S&P Global, our economic forecast provider, does not provide a similar series in the economic forecast, the Company developed its efficiency variables to ensure it is not omitting any important variables during the model development process.

STF-DEA-1-15

Question:

Refer to the document titled “B2025 Load and Energy Forecast PUBLIC DISCLOSURE,” in Technical Appendix Volume 1, p. 32. Please describe the “IPGA” variable and justify the inclusion of this Industrial Production Index. 

Response:

An Industrial Production Index (“IPI”) measures the real output of the manufacturing, mining, and utilities sectors in an economy. It is a key economic indicator used to gauge the level of industrial activity and production over time. The index is typically expressed relative to a base year, which is set to 100. For example, an index value of 103 indicates that industrial production in an economy has increased by 3% compared to the base year.
The IPI for the United States is published by the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System. There is no official IPI at the state level. However, S&P Global, the Company’s provider of historical economic data and forecasts, produces one for each state based on the U.S. index. “IPGA” is the IPI provided by S&P Global for the State of Georgia. The Georgia IPI is preferred because it is calculated based on the state’s industrial mix, rather than the national average. The expectation is that the IPGA should thus be a better indicator of industrial activity in the state, which is why it is included in the forecast model for Industrial sales.

STF-DEA-1-21

Question:

Refer to the document titled “B2025 Load and Energy Forecast PUBLIC DISCLOSURE,” in Technical Appendix Volume 1, p. 35. Please describe the “estimate of the monthly historical pattern” used to distribute annual residential customer forecasts. If appropriate, provide this distribution in spreadsheet format with any assumptions clearly stated and all formulas intact.

Response:

The Residential customer forecast model is an annual change model, using the annual change in customers as the dependent variable in the model. Since the output of the model is the change in customers over the last 12 months, it needs to be converted into monthly values so it can be used in the monthly use per customer models. To do this, the Company develops ratios for allocating the annual change in customers to a monthly change. The calculation of these ratios utilizes actual customer data from the most recent five complete years (2019-2023 for Budget 2025). Please refer to STF-DEA-1-21 Attachment TRADE SECRET for the data and calculations.

STF-DEA-1-22

Question:

Refer to the document titled “B2025 Load and Energy Forecast PUBLIC DISCLOSURE,” in Technical Appendix Volume 1, p. 36, please explain how the exclusion of Unmetered Devices allows the Company to more accurately measure the “true growth of typical commercial customers…”

Response:

From 2014 to 2023, the monthly average use per customer of Unmetered Device is just 6% of the average use per customer for the total Commercial class (511 kWh vs 8,593 kWh). During the same period, the number of Unmetered Device accounts has grown 58% vs 9% for the total Commercial class. By excluding Unmetered Device accounts from the Commercial class customers, the Company was able to better capture the growth trend of Commercial customers.

STF-DEA-1-33

Question:

Refer to the document titled “B2025 Load and Energy Forecast PUBLIC DISCLOSURE,” in Technical Appendix Volume 1, p. 38. Please describe the term “unbilled sales,” and why these occur. 

Response:

Unbilled sales refer to the electricity that has been delivered to customers but has not yet been billed in a month. The Company bills its customers across the month in 21 different Bill Groups. Calendar energy sales are the basis of the Company’s actual financial record. Both billed and unbilled sales are utilized to calculate calendar sales, where unbilled represents sales in the portion of the month that has not yet been billed but must be estimated. 

The forecast is estimated on a billing basis, and unbilled energy is estimated as to mimic how actual sales data are ultimately reported on the calendar month basis. Calendar sales = Billed sales + current month Unbilled sales – previous month Unbilled sales.

STF-DEA-1-35
Question:
Refer to the document titled “B2025 Load and Energy Forecast PUBLIC DISCLOSURE,” in Technical Appendix Volume 1, p. 40. Please explain why the LoadMAP model is initialized to a base year of 2016. Explain the conditions under which the initialization year would change.
Response:
The base year of 2016 was chosen in 2020 for the then upcoming 2021 Technical and Achievable Potential Study. 2016 was chosen because there was a Company specific 2016 residential saturation survey to help determine appliance ownership. The U.S. Energy Information Administration’s (“EIA”) 2020 Annual Energy Outlook was used to supplement the Company’s end-use consumption and saturation estimates. Typically, the Company will choose a base year a few years prior to the time the rebase is performed to track and calibrate the performance of the model and assumptions. A base year change is multifaceted and resource intensive. Conditions to change the base year may include deteriorating model performance, changing markets, or regulatory requests.

STF-DEA-1-9

Question:

Refer to the document titled “B2025 Load and Energy Forecast PUBLIC DISCLOSURE,” in Technical Appendix Volume 1, p. 16, Attachment 2.0-1. Please explain how the Company calculates its “Territorial Supply (GWh)”. 


Response:

Retail energy is energy at the customer level, which is referred to as Level D. Territorial Supply is measured at the generator level, which the Company refers to as Level A. The difference between Level A and Level D is line losses that occur between the generator and the delivery to the customer’s meter. To calculate Territorial Supply, Retail sales are multiplied by the estimates of loss factors.
