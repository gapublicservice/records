                SOUTHERN RENEWABLE ENERGY ASSOCIATION
                    APPLICATION FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE

​          COMES NOW, the Southern Renewable Energy Association (“SREA”), and hereby files

this Application for Leave to Intervene (“Application”) in the above-referenced docket pursuant

to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59, Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) Rule 515-2-1-.06,

and the Commission’s Procedural and Scheduling Order (“PSO”) issued on December 28, 2025.

In support hereof, SREA respectfully states as follows:

           On January 31, 2025, Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power”) filed its 2025

Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”). This Application is timely filed pursuant to the Commission’s

Procedural and Scheduling Order, which states the following requirements:

           (a)​ Any person or party, on whom a statute does not confer an unconditional right
                to intervene, must file an application for leave to intervene within 30 days
                following the first published notice of the proceeding…

           (b)​In addition to the requirements prescribed by O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59 for
               applications for leave to intervene, the application must (1) identify other
               intervening parties or intervening party applications whose interest is similar
               to that of the applicant, along with an explanation of why the identified
               intervening party or intervening party applicant will not adequately represent
               the applicant’s interest; and (2) state the applicant’s present intention to submit
               direct testimony and by whom and on what subject. The requirements
               identified herein shall constitute a continuing obligation of the applicant or
               intervening party. Each application for leave to intervene shall also contain a
               valid email address for the intervening party.1

    PSO at 7.

​      SREA is a non-profit industry-led initiative that works to promote the responsible

development and use of utility-scale wind energy, energy storage, solar energy and transmission

throughout the South, including in Alabama, Arkansas, Georgia, Kentucky, Louisiana,

Mississippi, and Tennessee. ​SREA members have an interest in the proceeding because they

include businesses that develop, own, and operate facilities that generate and produce energy.

SREA’s members also include customers that desire to have more access to clean and renewable

sources of energy. A major focus of SREA’s regulatory work includes participating in IRP

proceedings and stakeholder processes through the South, including in Georgia. SREA has an

interest in ensuring that renewable energy, storage, and transmission solutions are given fair and

factually based considering in the IRP assumptions, scenarios, and methodologies used by public

utilities, including Georgia Power, and that customers are provided with ample opportunities to

access the benefits of these resources through public utility programs. As a result, SREA has

substantial interests in the outcome of this proceeding and desires to intervene in order to

advance and protect those interests.

​      In compliance with the first of the Application Requirements adopted by the Commission

in the PSO, there are currently no other intervening parties with similar interests to SREA. Still,

SREA anticipates that our specific interests in the development of utility-scale solar energy, wind

energy, energy storage, hybrid resources, and transmission are not and will not be adequately

represented by the other pending intervenors in this Docket, as further explained in the following

paragraph regarding SREA’s plans for testimony. Further, SREA’s participation in this matter will

not unduly delay these proceedings or hinder the rights of other parties.

​      Pursuant to the second Application Requirement set forth in the PSO, SREA states its

present intention to submit direct testimony by expert witness Alex Palomino, Senior Consultant


                                                 2

with Energy Strategies, LLC. Broadly speaking, the testimony of SREA’s expert witnesses will

address subjects including generation capacity expansion, transmission, procurement of

resources, and customer programs. SREA’s testimony will provide comprehensive review and

testimony will also present the results of independent capacity expansion modeling conducted by

with a focus on renewable energy and storage solutions.

COMES NOW, the Southern Renewable Energy Association (“SREA”), and hereby files this Application for Leave to Intervene (“Application”) in the above-referenced docket pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59, Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) Rule 515-2-1-.06, and the Commission’s Procedural and Scheduling Order (“PSO”) issued on December 28, 2025.  In support hereof, SREA respectfully states as follows:
On January 31, 2025, Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power”) filed its 2025 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”). This Application is timely filed pursuant to the Commission’s Procedural and Scheduling Order, which states the following requirements: 
Any person or party, on whom a statute does not confer an unconditional right to intervene, must file an application for leave to intervene within 30 days following the first published notice of the proceeding…

In addition to the requirements prescribed by O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59 for applications for leave to intervene, the application must (1) identify other intervening parties or intervening party applications whose interest is similar to that of the applicant, along with an explanation of why the identified intervening party or intervening party applicant will not adequately represent the applicant’s interest; and (2) state the applicant’s present intention to submit direct testimony and by whom and on what subject.  The requirements identified herein shall constitute a continuing obligation of the applicant or intervening party.  Each application for leave to intervene shall also contain a valid email address for the intervening party.

SREA is a non-profit industry-led initiative that works to promote the responsible development and use of utility-scale wind energy, energy storage, solar energy and transmission throughout the South, including in Alabama, Arkansas, Georgia, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, and Tennessee. ​SREA members have an interest in the proceeding because they include businesses that develop, own, and operate facilities that generate and produce energy.  SREA’s members also include customers that desire to have more access to clean and renewable sources of energy.  A major focus of SREA’s regulatory work includes participating in IRP proceedings and stakeholder processes through the South, including in Georgia.  SREA has an interest in ensuring that renewable energy, storage, and transmission solutions are given fair and factually based considering in the IRP assumptions, scenarios, and methodologies used by public utilities, including Georgia Power, and that customers are provided with ample opportunities to access the benefits of these resources through public utility programs. As a result, SREA has substantial interests in the outcome of this proceeding and desires to intervene in order to advance and protect those interests. 
In compliance with the first of the Application Requirements adopted by the Commission in the PSO, there are currently no other intervening parties with similar interests to SREA. Still, SREA anticipates that our specific interests in the development of utility-scale solar energy, wind energy, energy storage, hybrid resources, and transmission are not and will not be adequately represented by the other pending intervenors in this Docket, as further explained in the following paragraph regarding SREA’s plans for testimony. Further, SREA’s participation in this matter will not unduly delay these proceedings or hinder the rights of other parties. 
Pursuant to the second Application Requirement set forth in the PSO, SREA states its present intention to submit direct testimony by expert witness Alex Palomino, Senior Consultant with Energy Strategies, LLC. Broadly speaking, the testimony of SREA’s expert witnesses will address subjects including generation capacity expansion, transmission, procurement of resources, and customer programs. SREA’s testimony will provide comprehensive review and analysis of the IRP assumptions, scenarios, and methodologies used by Georgia Power. The testimony will also present the results of independent capacity expansion modeling conducted by SREA’s expert witnesses and identify opportunities for improvement in Georgia Power’s IRP, with a focus on renewable energy and storage solutions.
