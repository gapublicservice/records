                              PETITION TO INTERVENE
                       OF CLEAN ENERGY BUYERS ASSOCIATION

       The Clean Energy Buyers Association (CEBA), by its undersigned attorneys Keyes & Fox

LLP, pursuant to the provisions of O.C.G.A. §§ 50-13-14, 46-2-59, Commission Utility Rule 515-

2-1-.06, and the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (Commission) Procedural and Scheduling

Order (PSO), hereby respectfully petitions the Commission for leave to intervene and participate

in this docket as a party. In support of this petition, CEBA states as follows:

       1.      CEBA is a business trade association that activates a community of energy buyers

and partners to advance low-cost, reliable, carbon emissions-free global electricity systems.

CEBA’s more than 400 members represent more than $20 trillion in market capital and include

institutional energy customers of every type and size – corporate and industrial companies,

universities, and cities, as well as project developers and service providers. CEBA’s membership

includes one-fifth of the Fortune 500 companies and some of the largest buyers of clean energy

that conduct business operations across the United States and in Georgia, including customers of


                                                  1

Georgia Power Company (Georgia Power or the Company). CEBA’s corporate and industrial

members include companies across a variety of sectors including information technology, data

centers, auto manufacturing, clean energy manufacturing, heavy industry, food and beverage

manufacturers, financial institutions, restaurants, hotels, retail chains, and more. CEBA’s members

have ambitious clean energy commitments, and many of these members now consider, if not

prioritize, their ability to access clean energy when determining where to locate new facilities and

which existing facilities to expand.

          2.      In this proceeding, Georgia Power requests that the Commission approve its 2025

Integrated Resource Plan (IRP). Of particular interest to CEBA, Georgia Power requests that the

Commission approve its request to extend the operating life of several coal-fired power plants, its

proposal to modify its request for proposals (RFP) process to procure energy from new renewable

energy resources to meet the needs of subscribing customers, its proposed changes to the Clean

and Renewable Energy Subscription (CARES) program, and its transmission plan, among other

requests.1

          3.      As a business association comprised of large energy customers, including many

Georgia Power customers, the Commission’s approval, denial, or modification of Georgia Power’s

proposals in its 2025 IRP will directly impact CEBA members’ property and business interests.2

Specifically, CEBA members often purchase clean energy directly when possible and work to

ensure that their electricity supply is as clean as possible in states where options to purchase clean

energy directly are more limited. In Georgia, CEBA members’ ability to achieve their clean energy

goals is largely tied to the emissions profile of their utility’s resource mix and availability of

customer clean energy programs offered by the utility. As a result, the ability of CEBA members

that are Georgia Power customers to achieve their clean energy goals is dependent on the

Commission approving a resource mix for Georgia Power’s 2025 IRP that is as clean as possible

while ensuring least-cost principles, economic development goals, and reliable service

requirements are achieved. CEBA members can only protect their business interests in purchasing

electricity generated by clean energy resources through participation in this docket.

       4.      The cost of the resources Georgia Power procures as a result of the Commission’s

approval, denial, or modification of the 2025 IRP will be recovered through rates from Georgia

Power’s ratepayers. As ratepayers, CEBA members have property and business interests in

ensuring that the resources Georgia Power procures to meet its capacity needs are cost-effective.

       5.      As a party to Georgia Power’s 2023 IRP Update, CEBA signed a Letter Agreement

with Georgia Power in which CEBA and Georgia Power agreed to meet to discuss the development

of a carbon-free energy customer program. In the Letter Agreement, Georgia Power committed to

propose this program in the 2025 IRP. Georgia Power has proposed certain modifications to the

CARES program in the 2025 IRP, including a Customer Identified Resource option. CEBA has a

vested interest in ensuring that the carbon-free energy customer program the Commission approves

in this proceeding is consistent with Georgia Power’s commitment to CEBA in the Letter

Agreement and meets the needs of large clean energy customers, including CEBA members.

       6.      Further, CEBA has identified transmission planning and development as a major

potential impediment to customers’ ability to access reliable, clean energy to meet their clean

energy commitment. Simply put, substantial, cost-effective transmission development will hasten

economic development in the state from these businesses, while limited transmission will limit it.

CEBA has an interest in ensuring that the transmission plan the Commission approves in the 2025


                                                 3

IRP will lead to timely, cost-effective investments in new transmission projects and upgrades in

Georgia.

           7.      For these reasons, the outcome of this docket will directly impact CEBA’s

members, their property interests, and their business operations in Georgia.

           8.      CEBA respectfully states that no other entity can represent CEBA’s interests in this

proceeding. The nature of CEBA’s interests and the manner in which the outcome of this

proceeding will affect those interests are specific to CEBA’s mission to advance low-cost, reliable,

carbon emissions-free global electricity systems. Further, as a membership association of

businesses focused on purchasing cost-competitive, reliable clean energy, the interests of CEBA

and CEBA members are distinct from other large energy customers or customer groups that may

seek to intervene in this docket. Given these unique interests, CEBA has not identified any other

intervenors or prospective intervenors whose interests are similar to CEBA’s interests. Because

CEBA’s interests arise directly from Georgia Power’s proposals in this docket, granting CEBA’s

intervention will not unduly delay the proceedings or prejudice the rights of other parties.3

           9.      CEBA presently intends to submit direct testimony in this proceeding. Brent

Alderfer, Board member of New Energy Economics, and Priya Barua, Senior Director of Market

and Policy Innovation at CEBA, will sponsor direct testimony addressing Georgia Power’s

proposals that purport to implement Georgia Power’s commitments in the Letter Agreement

reached between CEBA and Georgia Power in the 2023 IRP Update, the implications of

customers’ clean energy commitments for Georgia Power’s capacity need, and related issues.

Additionally, Ted Thomas, Founding Partner of Energize Strategies, will sponsor direct testimony

addressing Georgia Power’s transmission plan, and related issues.
