COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before April 21, 2025. 


DEFINITIONS

As may be used in this document:  
1.“The Company,” “Georgia Power” or “The Utility” means Georgia Power Company and its present and former officers, employees, agents, representatives, directors, and all other persons acting or purporting to act on behalf of said Company.
The term “you” and “your” refer to “the Company.”
The term “person” means any natural person, corporation, corporate division, partnership, other unincorporated association, trust, government agency, or entity.
The term “document” or “documentation” shall have the broadest possible meaning under applicable law.  “Document” or “documentation” means every writing or record of every type and description that is in the possession, custody or control of the Company including, but not limited to, correspondence, memoranda, e-mails, drafts, workpapers, summaries, stenographic or handwritten notes, studies, notices, publications, books, pamphlets, reports, surveys, minutes or statistical compilations, computer and other electronic records or tapes or printouts, including, but not limited to, electronic mail files; and copies of such writing or records containing any commentary or notation whatsoever that does not appear in the original.  The term “document” or “documentation” further includes, by way of illustration and not limitation, schedules, progress schedules, time logs, drawings, computer disks, chart projections, time tables, summaries of other documents, minutes, surveys, work sheets, drawings, comparisons, evaluations, laboratory and testing reports, telephone call records, personal diaries, calendars, personal notebooks, personal reading files, transcripts, witness statements and indices.
The term “referring or relating to” means consisting of, containing, mentioning, suggesting, reflecting, concerning, regarding, summarizing, analyzing, discussing, involving, dealing with, emanating from, directed at, pertaining to in any way, or in any way logically or factually connected or associated with the matter discussed.
“And” and “or” as used herein shall be construed both conjunctively and disjunctively and each shall include the other whenever such construction will serve to bring within the scope of these discovery requests any information that would not otherwise be brought within their scope.
The singular as used herein shall include the plural and the masculine gender shall include the feminine and the neuter.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a person other than a natural person means to state:  the full name of the person and any names under which it conducts business; the present or last known address of the person; and, the present or last known telephone number of the person.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a document means to provide with respect to each document requested to be identified by these discovery requests a description of the document that is sufficient for purposes of a request to produce or a subpoena duces tecum, including the following:

a summary of the contents of the document;
the name and last known address of each person who presently has possession, custody or control of the document;
if any such document was, but is no longer, in your possession,  custody or control or is no longer in existence, state whether it: (1) is missing or lost; (2) has been destroyed; or (3) has been transferred voluntarily or involuntarily, and if so, state the circumstances surrounding the authorization for each such disposition and the date of such disposition.
“Identify”, “identifying” or identity” when used in reference to a communication should be read to include information regarding the date of the communication, whether the communication was written or oral, the identity of all parties and witnesses to the communication, the substance of what was said and/or transpired and, if written, the identity of the document(s) containing or referring to the communication.

PUBLIC DISCLOSURE

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-1-10. Please provide a narrative description of how the Company evaluates the level of model specification in its econometric models and avoids over-specification. As part of your response, please ensure to explain any methods the Company uses to evaluate the level of model specification, such as testing the coefficient of determination through the addition and removal of certain variables. 

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-1-12. Please respond to the following questions.
Please provide a narrative explanation why it is appropriate to assume autocorrelation in the Residential Use per Customer model. 
Please provide a narrative explanation of how Residential Use per Customer in billing day  ‘n’ is correlated with Residential Use per Customer in billing day ‘n-1.’
Please provide a narrative explanation of how the autoregressive term accounts for variation in residential usage that is otherwise unaccounted for by other variables in the Residential Use per Customer model.

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-1-14. Please respond to the following questions.
Please provide a narrative explanation why it is appropriate to assume autocorrelation in the Commercial Use per Customer model. 
Please provide a narrative explanation of how Commercial Use per Customer in month ‘n’ is correlated with Commercial Use per Customer in month ‘n-1.’
Please provide a narrative explanation of how the autoregressive term accounts for variation in commercial usage that is otherwise unaccounted for by other variables in the Commercial Use per Customer model.

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-1-16. Please respond to the following questions.
Please provide a narrative explanation why it is appropriate to assume autocorrelation in the MARTA sales model. 
Please provide a narrative explanation of how MARTA sales in month ‘n’ are correlated with MARTA sales in month ‘n-1.’
Please provide a narrative explanation of how the autoregressive term accounts for variation in MARTA sales that are otherwise unaccounted for by other variables in the MARTA sales model.
Please explain why the Company selected lag terms of 3 and 6 for each autoregressive term.

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-1-24. Has the Company observed any large Commercial customers end their service in the years 2020-2024 inclusive? If so, please provide the total number of customers, an estimate of their aggregate peak load demand, and an estimate of their aggregate annual energy requirements. Please explain what portion of these customers and associated demand were comprised of customers switching to alternative supply options.

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-1-26. Has the Company observed any large Industrial customers end their service in the years 2020-2024 inclusive? If so, please provide the total number of customers, an estimate of their aggregate peak load demand, and an estimate of their aggregate annual energy requirements. Please explain what portion of these customers and associated demand were comprised of customers switching to alternative supply options.

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-1-31. For each residual plot, provide the underlying data for the fitted, actual, and residual lines.


Refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-1-32, regarding the statement that “Change model MAPEs are not representative of the final customer count projection due to first differenced modeling approach. After first difference to level conversion, and annual frequency to monthly frequency conversion, final MAPEs are in line with other models.” Please response to the following questions:
Please clarify the time period for the MAPE values reported in the response. 
Please define the first differencing modeling approach.
Please demonstrate how the Company adjusts the change model MAPEs to their final reported forms.

Referring to the Company’s peak model, please provide summary statistics for each hourly regression model including the coefficient, statistical significance, model R2, Mean Absolute Percentage Error, and any other relevant summary statistics.

Referring to the Company’s peak model, please provide the model specification (variable selection) for each model.

Referring to the Company’s peak model, please explain how large load forecasts are incorporated into the peak forecast model on a class-by class basis. Please provide a quantitative demonstration in spreadsheet format with all formulas and references intact.

Referring to the Company’s peak load forecast in the 2025 IRP, for the period 2010 to 2024, provide forecasted and actual peak demand for Georgia Power and indicate the forecast vintage for each forecasted value. Include forecasted values from each IRP or IRP Update as separate line items.


Referring to the Company’s seasonal peak load forecast as provided in the document “Figures 3.4B-3.4D – Loads.xlsx,” please provide as separate line items for both summer and winter seasons the a) the total peak load forecast, b) the organic peak load forecast, and c) the large load peak load forecast for the entire forecast period (2025-2059). Please provide your response in spreadsheet format with capacity values (MW) for each line item request above.

Referring to the Company’s peak load forecast model, conducted in xxxxx, please demonstrate how the Company adjusts its calibrated long-term energy forecast to a peak demand forecast. Provide this demonstration for a representative year (2030) on a monthly basis. Include in your response both a narrative description of this calibration process as well as a quantitative spreadsheet demonstration of how the monthly energy forecast is adjusted to produce the peak demand forecast. Ensure the quantitative demonstration retains all formulas and references.

Referring to the Company’s xxxxxxxx long term energy sales model, please provide detailed workpapers, on a class-by-class basis, demonstrating how the Company’s short-term energy forecasts were calibrated to the long-term energy forecasts in the 2025 IRP. As part of your response, include both a narrative description of the process as well as a quantitative demonstration in spreadsheet format with all formulas and references in tact. Please ensure that the workpapers show the initial difference between short and long term on an annual basis prior to any calibration.

Please use xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx xxxxxxxxxx long term energy sales model as reference variables for this demonstration. 


Referring to the Company’s LoadMAP long term energy sales model, please respond to following questions:
Please explain why the base year for the model is xxxx.
At what point does the Company expect to update the base year in its long-term energy sales forecast?
Please describe the verification process the Company conducts when reviewing base-year data. 
Please demonstrate how the Company verified the model data for base-year xxxx. Provide any documentation demonstrating how the Company reviewed this data and ultimately determined that the data was acceptable for inclusion in the long term energy sales forecast.
Please list and discuss what metrics are used to determine that xxxx is still the appropriate base year to use for the residential forecasting and calibration activities as opposed to moving to the 2023 update. 
Please describe the variance threshold that must be met for each metric that factors into the Company’s decision of whether or not to update the base year.

Referring to the Company’s xxxxxxxx long term energy sales model, per the Technical Session discussion that occurred on March 6, 2025 where the Company demonstrated how the xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx was held flat in real terms (i.e., Data Set 6 of the model), please discuss the Company’s view on the impact, for each customer class, of large load customer growth and the related potential electricity price increases that may occur and could stifle the energy consumption and peak demand growth rates forecasted currently. Does the Company plan to adjust the price elasticity factor based on its forecasted large customer load growth?

Referring to the Company’s xxxxxxxxxx long term energy sales model, please provide the comparison of the long-term model saturation projections with the initial 2022/2023 saturation survey performed by AEG after which the Company concluded that the model is tracking closely as stated during the March 6, 2025 technical session.

Referring to the Company’s short-term energy sales model, please explain why the Company selected a xxxxxx horizon as the appropriate calibration period. Provide any support documentation that justifies this decision. 

Referring to the Company’s short-term energy sales model, has the Company or its forecasting consultant extended the short-term energy model for any class beyond the xxxxx interval for which it is used to calibrate the long-term model?  If yes, please provide a comparison for all years by class. Please provide associated workpapers with formulae intact.  If no, please explain why the Company has not performed this comparison.

Referring to the B2025 Load and Energy Forecast PUBLIC DISCLOSURE document, p. 125, please provide the EPRI study, including any associated data, relied upon to produce the forecast for electric vehicle adoption and projected consumption and all associated workpapers utilized by GPC to produce the EV forecast.

Please explain how the Company expects large load customer growth might impact the EV growth forecast provided in the 2025 IRP. Specifically, please discuss the Company’s view on the impact of large load customer growth and the related potential electricity price increases that may occur and could stifle forecasted EV adoption rates that may not materialize due to a higher cost of EV ownership. Does the Company’s EV forecast take EV ownership costs into account? If not, why not?

Please provide the template(s) for the Request for Service and/or Letter of Agreement forms that large load customers must complete to be considered a committed Georgia Power load. Please discuss if the Company allows amendments to these forms and if the Company has received any amended agreements from large load customers. If so, please provide how many customers have amended the agreements and list and describe the amendments that have been allowed.
