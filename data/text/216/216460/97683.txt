COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before December 11, 2023. 

DEFINITIONS

As may be used in this document:  
1.“The Company,” “Georgia Power” or “The Utility” means Georgia Power Company and its present and former officers, employees, agents, representatives, directors, and all other persons acting or purporting to act on behalf of said Company.
The term “you” and “your” refer to “the Company.”
The term “person” means any natural person, corporation, corporate division, partnership, other unincorporated association, trust, government agency, or entity.
The term “document” or “documentation” shall have the broadest possible meaning under applicable law.  “Document” or “documentation” means every writing or record of every type and description that is in the possession, custody or control of the Company including, but not limited to, correspondence, memoranda, e-mails, drafts, workpapers, summaries, stenographic or handwritten notes, studies, notices, publications, books, pamphlets, reports, surveys, minutes or statistical compilations, computer and other electronic records or tapes or printouts, including, but not limited to, electronic mail files; and copies of such writing or records containing any commentary or notation whatsoever that does not appear in the original.  The term “document” or “documentation” further includes, by way of illustration and not limitation, schedules, progress schedules, time logs, drawings, computer disks, chart projections, time tables, summaries of other documents, minutes, surveys, work sheets, drawings, comparisons, evaluations, laboratory and testing reports, telephone call records, personal diaries, calendars, personal notebooks, personal reading files, transcripts, witness statements and indices.
The term “referring or relating to” means consisting of, containing, mentioning, suggesting, reflecting, concerning, regarding, summarizing, analyzing, discussing, involving, dealing with, emanating from, directed at, pertaining to in any way, or in any way logically or factually connected or associated with the matter discussed.
“And” and “or” as used herein shall be construed both conjunctively and disjunctively and each shall include the other whenever such construction will serve to bring within the scope of these discovery requests any information that would not otherwise be brought within their scope.
The singular as used herein shall include the plural and the masculine gender shall include the feminine and the neuter.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a person other than a natural person means to state:  the full name of the person and any names under which it conducts business; the present or last known address of the person; and, the present or last known telephone number of the person.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a document means to provide with respect to each document requested to be identified by these discovery requests a description of the document that is sufficient for purposes of a request to produce or a subpoena duces tecum, including the following:

the name and last known address of each person who presently has possession, custody or control of the document;

PUBLIC DISCLOSURE

In the Main Document, page (“p”) 14, Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or “Company”) estimates that its Distributed Energy Resource (“DER”) Customer Program Pilot will provide approximately 150 MW of capacity in the capacity plan by the end of 2027. Please provide the estimated breakdown of customer generators for this program (size per installation, generator type, fuel type). Please provide all Georgia Power communications with interested customers for the DER Customer Program Pilot. 

Please refer to the IRP Update Main document p. 22 regarding the DER Colocation Program.
Describe which commercial and industrial customers would qualify for the program. 
Please describe in detail how the Company will select customers for the program.
When would the proposed tariff for this program be available?
Explain how “resiliency benefits” will be quantified. 
Please describe in detail how the price paid by the customer for the resiliency benefits provided by the DER will be determined.
Describe if there has been any recent outreach to potential customers. If there is interest in the program, please provide the number of customers with interest in the program, the size of each customer’s load, and any feedback that the Company has received from these customers. 

Please refer to the IRP Update Main document p. 22 regarding the DER Customer Owned Program.
Describe which commercial and industrial customers would qualify for the program.
When would the proposed tariff for this program be available?
Will only newly installed DER technologies be eligible? If so, why? If not, will there be a device age limit to which a customer’s DER will be eligible?
Describe if there has been any current outreach to potential customers. If there is interest in the program, please provide the number of customers with interest in the program, the size of each customer’s load, and any feedback that the Company has received from these customers. 
Will the customer still have independent control (the customer can override) over the DER equipment?
Describe Georgia Power’s proposed calculation for the credit that will be received by the customer for the capacity and energy value.
Please provide an example of how a participating customer’s credit will be calculated.

Please refer to the IRP Update Main document p. 23 regarding the Curtailable Load Program.
Describe which commercial and industrial customers would qualify for the program.
When would the proposed tariff for this program be available?
Please describe what amount of time Georgia Power would consider a “long-term commitment” from qualifying commercial and industrial customers for this program.
Explain how and when the Curtailable Load Program will be dispatched.
Explain how longer-term contracts, when compared to DPEC, will ensure resource availability.
Explain the results of the DPEC program during Winter Storm Elliot. Specifically, provide how many customers were notified to reduce their load and how many participating customers reduced their load and the amount of the load reduction. 
Describe how the value of the load reduction will be calculated. 
Please provide an example of how this value will be calculated.
Describe the “greater benefits” that the Curtailable Load Program will provide to the system and how the benefits will be valued.
Please provide all differences between the proposed Curtailable Load Program and the DPEC program.
Describe if there has been any current outreach to potential customers. If there is interest in the program, please provide the number of customers with interest in the program, the size of each customer’s load, and any feedback that the Company has received from these customers. 


Please explain why the Proposed DER and Demand Response Programs were not considered in Technical Appendix 9: Economic Analysis of Capacity Resources.

Please explain how and when the Proposed DER and Demand Response Programs will be dispatched.

In the Main Document, p. 14, Georgia Power is assuming that projects from the DG Renewable RFPs provide a 5% winter capacity equivalence.  What technology is Georgia Power assuming for this calculation (fixed vs. tracking percentage)?

In the Main Document, p. 14, Georgia Power is assuming the same winter capacity equivalence for projects from the DG Renewable RFPs as projects from the Utility Scale Renewable RFPs. Please explain why both (DG and Utility Scale) resources are assumed to have the same winter capacity equivalence. Were batteries assumed to be procured in the upcoming CARES RFP and factored into the capacity value? If not, please explain why. Please explain why the utility scale resources that can be paired with energy storage are not assumed to have higher capacity equivalence.

How did Georgia Power consider potential funding opportunities from the Inflation Reduction Act (“IRA”) that would increase Company spending on infrastructure and/or generation assets for the IRP Update filing?  Please describe how the IRA’s Domestic Content Bonus Credit and Energy Community Bonus Credit were considered in resource selection.

How did Georgia Power consider potential funding opportunities from the IRA that would increase customer spending on behind the meter generation assets and battery storage in the IRP Update filing? 

How did Georgia Power consider potential funding opportunities from the IRA that would increase customer spending on electric transportation readiness and infrastructure in the IRP Update filing?

How did Georgia Power attempt to maximize funding opportunities available from the IRA in the 2023 IRP Update?  How were the different funding sources used to determine where spending would result in the most cost-effective solution for Georgia Power’s projected capacity shortfall?

Please refer to the 2023 IRP Update Main Document p. 1, please provide the calculation for the Company’s load growth projection of 6,600 megawatts (“MW”). 

Please refer to the 2023 IRP Update Main Document, Figure 6 on p. 19, please provide all assumptions, for each Year, that were used in determining the amount of Additional Owned Capacity to Maintain 70% Ownership Minimum MW.

Please refer to the IRP Update Main document p. 20 and Technical Appendix 5 regarding the proposed 200 MW of BESS co-located with 200 MW of new solar facility.
How and when did the Company first become aware of this project? 
Did the Company consider other solar projects and locations? If so, what other projects were considered by the Company prior to the decision to move forward with the proposed project? 
Was the proposed project ever considered by the Company as a standalone solar facility? If not, why? 
Explain why the Company is proposing a solar plus battery project versus a standalone solar facility.
Why was the proposed facility location selected by the Company to include as part of the IRP Update.  Please describe in detail why this project was selected.
Explain how available transmission capacity could create opportunities for grid charging of the BESS.
Provide all workpapers and analysis to support the proposed facility size and design including battery type and tracking type.
Provide the current “Purchase Agreement” and all other current documents between Georgia Power and the Seller.
Describe in detail what “xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx.
Provide all documentation supporting the Company’s environmental due diligence of the project.
Provide the lease agreement for the property for the solar and BESS facilities.
Provide all workpapers and documentation for the interconnection of this facility.
Does the estimated overnight cost include all interconnection costs?
Describe the usage of the proposed BESS. Will the number of cycles and state of charge be limited on a daily, monthly or yearly basis? 

Please refer to the IRP Update Main document p. 20 regarding the 178 MW of BESS to be added to existing solar facilities at Robins and Moody Air Force Bases.
Please describe the decision-making process for selecting the solar facilities at Robins and Moody Air Force Bases as the location of the proposed storage devices. Provide all analysis conducted by the Company, or on its behalf, that supports these locations compared to locating BESS on other Company owned facilities at other military bases.
Please describe the process that the Company will have to go through to add BESS to current military projects.  Did the Company have to receive approval from the military for these additions? If so, were there other conditions required or agreed upon to locate the projects at Robins and Moody Air Force Bases? If so, please provide a complete list of such conditions.
Please explain the current 10% winter planning capacity attributed to the Robins and Moody Air Force Base solar sites when other solar resources assume a 5% winter capacity equivalence as found in the footnotes on p. 14.
Georgia Power states on page 20 that the “Company is also pursuing additional BESS opportunities at facilities that may have transmission capacity available as early as the winter of 2026/2027”. Specifically describe in detail how the Company is pursuing these opportunities. Is the Company only considering sites with Company owned facilities? Please describe any potential BESS locations that the Company has already identified and the status of those sites.
