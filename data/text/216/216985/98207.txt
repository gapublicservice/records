STF-PIA-8-2 

Question:
Provide a breakdown of the number of data centers currently within the Company’s portfolio, as of November 1, 2023, and those projected in the 2023 IRP Update. Please identify: 
The number, if available, of Hyperscalers.
The number of Network and Internet exchange service providers
The number involved with data mining and crypto currency.

Response:
The Company currently has 62 data centers in its portfolio. The type of data center (i.e., hyperscaler, network and internet exchange service providers, data mining and cryptocurrency) is not available for these existing customers. 
Of the 51 large load customers included in the 2023 IRP Update forecast, 33 were data centers. These 33 data centers included 21 hyperscalers, 10 network and internet exchange service providers, and 2 data mining and cryptocurrency customers. 
As of December 15, 2023, there were an additional 29 data center projects identified since the Company’s 2023 IRP Update forecast was finalized in September, including 4 hyperscalers, 24 network and internet exchange service providers, and 1 data mining and cryptocurrency. 
The Company does not have a list of data centers in the pipeline specific to November 1, 2023.

Question:
Has the Company coordinated with any Data Center customers to understand how the customer will coordinate rooftop PV, diesel generator backup, hydrogen, and BESS to serve as a resource during periods of peak demand?


Response:
As with any new large load customers interested in selecting Georgia Power to provide their electric service, the Company discusses with potential customers the various programs available for Distributed Energy Resource (“DER”) assets that can be used as a system resource. In addition, the Company often discusses the benefits and eligibility criteria for potential new large load customers to participate in one or more of Georgia Power’s customer renewable programs, such as the Clean and Renewable Energy Subscription (“CARES”) Program, the Flex Renewable Energy Credit (“REC”) Program, the Retail REC Retirement (“R3”) Program, or other renewable program solutions as outlined on Georgia Power’s website. For transmission planning purposes, the Company must consider the peak demand of the future load in its analysis.

STF-PIA-8-5

Question:
Do the data centers on Georgia Power’s system, or expected to be on Georgia Power’s system going forward, have demand flexibility to provide support services, including load management, during periods of peak demand? If so, what demand flexibility do the data centers have and what is the duration of these systems? If multiple systems are in place will they run simultaneously or can they operate independent of each other?  


Response:
The Company cannot speak for all data centers but believes a sizable portion of its prospective data center customers will install backup generation for resiliency during local outages. However, these backup generation resources are likely to be certified and permitted for emergency use only restricting them from running for system benefit. As such, the Company has existing distributed energy resources (“DER”) and demand response (“DR”) programs, and is proposing additional programs, which will incent these customers to install cleaner resources certified and permitted for non-emergency use and enable greater reliability benefits through participation in these programs.

STF-PIA-8-8

Question:
Does the Company have access to, or any knowledge of, the proposed data center power-use-effectiveness (PUE) rating and what margin of error has gone into Transmission Planning in these areas to assure adequate loading will be available?  Has the Company assessed proposed PUE ratings when negotiating with data center clients outside of Georgia Power’s immediate territory? 


Response:
Yes, the Company has access to power-use-effectiveness (“PUE”) ratings of potential large load data center customers and assesses the PUE ratings for potential customers inside and outside of Georgia Power’s service territory. The PUE ratings vary by potential data center customer but are typically between 1.4 and 1.6. The PUE rating ensures enough transmission and distribution infrastructure is provided to cover the potential customer’s projected utility load and not just its projected critical server load. The Company analyzes the impact of serving large loads in compliance with NERC Transmission Planning Reliability standards, which is consistent with the planning processes outlined in Technical Appendix Volume 3 of Georgia Power’s 2022 Integrated Resource Plan (Docket No. 44160). For transmission planning purposes, the Company must consider the peak demand of the future load. Analyses are based on the proposed load ramp rates provided by the potential customer, which incorporate the PUE ratings, and then re-evaluated annually through processes noted in Technical Appendix Volume 3.
