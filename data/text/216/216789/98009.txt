As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-DEA-2-1 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided information used in the Company’s load forecast sensitivity models. All of such information (the “Information”) constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains competitively sensitive details on the Company’s forecast methodology for determining the impacts of new large loads. Public dissemination of the Information would allow Georgia Power’s competitors, suppliers, and customers access to the Company’s anticipated load strategies and objectives, thereby bestowing insight into the Company’s strategic initiatives and forecasting capabilities. Competitors would obtain an unfair advantage because they are not required to reveal similar information and can structure their offers for competitive choice opportunities based on the Information rather than their own costs, capacity, abilities, strategies, or analysis.    

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.

Question:

Refer to file titled “Informal Data Request 11 Attachment A TS.”
It appears all projects, even those with certainty less than 100% appear to reach full load during the forecast horizon. Please explain how the certainty figures (column E on Tab External Additions) should be interpreted. Is the certainty value referring to the likelihood the load interconnects?

 If not, what is the correct interpretation? 
 If yes, why do these loads continue to grow to full capacity post 2027? 
How are the certainty figures (column E on Tab External Additions) determined?

Are all projects listed in this sheet assumed to be completed eventually? 

How does the certainty figure impact the future load beyond 2027?

It appears certainty values are only applied to 2027. How does the certainty value, and the subsequent growth rates of external loads affect load in years post 2027?

Please provide the expected peak energy requirements by year of all the external loads listed in this sheet. Please provide the expected peak energy requirements in an excel spreadsheet that clearly shows how the likelihood of the load coming online is factored into the peak energy requirements.

Response:

The certainty figures in Column E on the External Additions tab assess the likelihood that a customer will be served by Georgia Power (as compared to another electric service provider). The year-by-year announced load in columns H to AI is the announced load provided by the customer. The Company then adjusted the announced load down according to the assessed certainty, load realization, project completion, and timing risks. These elements of risk are evaluated via Monte Carlo simulation. 

These risks, and a description of the methodology and assumptions are provided in Section 1.5 of the Load and Energy Forecast document in the 2023 IRP Update Technical Appendix.         

Please refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-2-1(a). The Company relied on informed judgment within the Company to develop key assumptions for the load sensitivity models.   

No. The Company assumes a REDACTED probability that projects will not be completed. For example, projects may fail due to unfavorable economic conditions or financial problems. This risk is evaluated as part of the Monte Carlo simulation model that the Company uses to create and evaluate a range of possible loads to serve.

As discussed in part (a) above, the certainty figure evaluates the likelihood of a new large load customer being served by Georgia Power. It is a binary outcome that affects all years of future loads. In the Company’s modeling of large customers, 100,000 combinations are drawn to estimate the range of load outcomes. When certainty is evaluated, it is expected that the project will be served by the Company in a number of combinations driven by the certainty percentage. For example, if a project has a certainty of 25%, then when a simulation is run with 100,000 draws it means that the project will be served by Georgia Power in about 25,000 of the 100,000 cases when evaluating for certainty.

The year-by-year announced loads and growth in columns H to AI are provided directly to Georgia Power by the individual customers behind each project. How that certainty impacts the expected load is discussed in part (d) above.

Certainty is applied to all years of future load as discussed in part (d) above.

The peak and energy requirements are calculated at a portfolio level. The certainty in column E of the file “Informal Data Request 11 Attachment A TS” is only one of the inputs needed for the load modeling. The likelihood and uncertainties are factored in via the methodology described in Section 1.5 of the recently filed Load and Energy Forecast document in the 2023 IRP Update Technical Appendix (a Monte Carlo simulation model). 

The process to determine the peak requirements starts with the Monte Carlo simulation model. The output of this model gives the equivalent average customer load in MWs to serve each year. These MWs are then converted into hourly energy requirements that ultimately are used to determine the peak demand of the portfolio.

Question:
Relative to Customer Choice loads, are these customers served under standard rates? If yes, what current tariff is used to serve these loads? 

Response:
Customer choice loads are served under one of Georgia Power’s standard rates for which they qualify that have been approved by the Georgia Public Service Commission. If the customer does not qualify for a marginal-based tariff, the customer choice load will be served under a non-marginal or embedded tariff such as Power & Light or Time of Use. STF-DEA-2-9

Question:
Relative to Customer Choice loads, are any investments to serve such customers a cost charged to that customer or are other customers responsible for system improvements to serve a new customer choice customer? 

Response:
Investments to serve customer choice loads are assigned to each project and included in the customer choice offer. There are two types of costs that may be included in customer choice offers: local and upstream. Local costs are assigned directly to the customer choice project and may include both transmission and distribution investments where the customer choice project receives sole benefit. Upstream costs are allocated to each customer choice project based upon that project’s marginal use of the system over the life of the project. Upstream costs include investments that provide system benefit to other customers in addition to the customer choice project. The Company prepares each customer choice offer to fully recover both local and allocated upstream costs required to serve the customer choice load over the life of the project.
