BEFORE THE GEORGIA PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION

GEORGIA POWER COMPANY
DOCKET NO. 55378

Data Request No. STF-DEA-4-1

BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-DEA-4-1 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided engineering data and cost estimate information. All of such information (the “Information”) constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains information on worst case engineering outputs and schedules around when and how much improvements may cost. Publicly disclosing these costs would allow bidders in future solicitations to tailor their proposals and potentially set an artificial floor on bidding, which would harm customers by not allowing the Company to conduct a proper solicitation and obtain the best cost estimates for future resources, equipment, or consulting work. Lastly, the Company’s competitors are not required to reveal or publish similar information and to require the Company to do so would place it at an economic disadvantage.

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.

Question:
Did Georgia Power conduct any probability analysis or consider the likelihood of all future loads being online and operating at 100% of their requested MWs simultaneously, particularly during summer peak conditions? Submit any historical data from similar load customers that support this type of load profile assessment.


Response:
No, Georgia Power did not conduct any probability analysis or consider the likelihood of all future loads being online and operating at 100% of requested MWs simultaneously during summer peak conditions.  

The Company does not track individual customer peak loads; therefore, the requested data does not exist.

For Transmission Planning analyses, large load customers are initially studied using the peak load ramp rates provided by the customer. Once the customer is online, the customer is analyzed in the annual transmission planning processes based on peak load levels provided through the load forecasting process. Please refer to the Transmission Planning study processes outlined in Technical Appendix Volume 3 of the 2022 Georgia Power Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) (Docket No. 44160).

STF-DEA-4-7

Question:
For the load projections included in the November 27, 2023 presentation, the Georgia PSC requests copies of all relevant contracts signed, particularly those related to data centers and AI load centers.

Response:
Please see TS STF-DEA-4-7 Attachments A through N. The following provides a description of the types of contracts executed by customers and referenced in TS STF-DEA-4-7 Attachments A through N. 

Request for Electric Service (“RFS”): An agreement a prospective customer completes and submits to Georgia Power when there is a choice of electric service provider, and they have chosen to be served by the Company.

Contract for Electric Service (“CE”): An agreement between Georgia Power and a customer that further outlines the terms and conditions under which electric service will be provided. 

Customer Baseline Load (“CBL”) Agreement: An agreement for customers on Real Time Pricing (“RTP”) that defines a customer’s baseline usage level. The CBL is mutually agreed upon by the customer and Georgia Power and defines the portion of load that will be billed on an embedded tariff. 

Meter Totalization Terms and Conditions: An agreement that allows for the combination or totalization of readings from multiple electric meters at one premises into one for billing purposes. 

Engineering Letter Agreement to Proceed: Document signed by a captive load customer (10 MW or greater) that acknowledges Georgia Power will begin procuring necessary equipment to electrically serve the project.
