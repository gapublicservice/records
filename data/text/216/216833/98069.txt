STF-JKA-3-12

Question:
Regarding the Company’s Battery modeling Aurora:
Please explain what ELCC study the Company is relying on in modeling the reliable capacity for new BESS projects as proposed (Robins, Moody, REDACTED) and what reliable capacity/capacity contribution is assumed. Provide a copy of any study that was conducted.
Please explain what ELCC study the Company is relying on modeling generic BESS in the Mix Study and what reliable capacity/capacity contribution is assumed.  Provide a copy of any study that was conducted.
Please explain if the ELCC studies relied on consider degradation, what degradation is assumed, and how that impacts the reliable capacity/capacity contribution as modeled.

Response:
The Company is using the BESS ELCC values referenced in part (b) and STF-JKA-2-11 or 100% for the aggregate facility consisting of both the solar and BESS components. The capacity contribution of Robins and Moody are adjusted to account for the point of interconnection (“POI”) limits associated with transmission system limitations. This adjustment subtracts the POI limit by the existing solar resource’s current capacity value to ensure that the combined facility (existing solar and incremental BESS) capacity value does not exceed the POI limit. Therefore, the Robins and Moody proposed BESS resources are only assigned the incremental capacity value, but the overall aggregate facility (consisting of both the existing solar and incremental BESS components) equates to 100% ELCC. Please see STF-JKA-1-4 Attachment for these calculations, which are on rows 54 and below on the Robins and Moody tabs. Since REDACTED is a new incremental resource for both the solar and BESS additions, the paired resource will receive a capacity credit of 200 MW.

The workpaper detailing the development of the generic BESS ELCC values used in the Resource Mix Study was provided with the response to STF-JKA-2-11. The first 3 GW of 4-hour BESS penetration are expected to have average capacity values of 100% in both the winter and summer seasons.

The ELCC studies do not consider degradation. The Company’s economic analysis assumes augmentation costs for BESS resources. These maintenance capital investments preserve the BESS nominal capacity, mitigating degradation.

Question:
It appears the Company’s modeling in Aurora for the generic “CT with SCR” resource is different than the modeling for Yates 8 – 10.

For each input assumption that is modeled differently in Aurora for those resources, please compare and contrast and explain the modeling difference.

Response:

The assumptions for the generic CT with SCR resource and Plant Yates Units 8-10 are based off the same generic B2024 Technology Application Standards (“TAS”). However, there are a few differences in the way the generic CT with SCR and Yates CTs were modeled in the economic analysis.

Fuel Source: 
Generic CT with SCR – oil primary, operates on oil year-round. 

Yates 8-10 CTs – dual fuel, with oil operation in January, February, and December and natural gas operation in all other months. Please see STF-JKA-2-22(c) for more information on this assumption.

Heat Rate: 
Generic CT with SCR – modeled with seasonal heat rates based on oil operation. The winter oil heat rate was REDACTED Btu/kWh, spring oil heat rate was REDACTED Btu/kWh, and summer oil heat rate was REDACTED Btu/kWh.

Yates 8-10 CTs – modeled with seasonal heat rates based on mode of operation. The winter oil heat rate was REDACTED Btu/kWh, spring natural gas heat rate was REDACTED Btu/kWh, and summer natural gas heat rate was REDACTED Btu/kWh. The expected winter oil heat is REDACTED Btu/kWh, consistent with the generic CT with SCR. The higher winter oil heat rate used for Yates CTs was based on preliminary engineering analysis. Subsequent analysis revealed that REDACTED Btu/kWh is the expected value at this time. This does not impact the economic analysis results as Plant Yates Units 8-10 are not expected to provide any energy value when dispatching on oil.

Minimum Capacity: 
Both the generic CT with SCR and Yates CTs were modeled with a 50% minimum capacity assumption. The minimum capacity in Aurora can be input in the resource table as well as the heat rate definition table. The generic CT with SCR was entered in the resource table, and the Yates CTs were entered in the heat rate definition table.

Fuel Prices: 
Generic CT with SCR – modeled with a generic oil price year-round.

Yates 8-10 CTs – modeled with site specific fuel pricing using Plant Yates Units 6-7 for delivered natural gas and Plant Wilson for cost to operate on oil as a proxy.

Maintenance Rate: 
Generic CT with SCR – modeled with a 3.84% annual rate. This equates to two weeks of planned outages per year. 

Plant Yates Units 8-10 – modeled with a monthly 9% maintenance rate, input as a forced outage, in March, April, May, October, and November. This equates to two weeks of planned outages per year.

As stated in the “Minimum Capacity” section of part (a) of this response, both the generic CT with SCR and Plant Yates Units 8-10 were modeled with a 50% minimum capacity assumption. The minimum capacity in Aurora can be input in the resource table as well as the heat rate definition table. The generic CT with SCR was entered in the resource table, and the Plant Yates Units 8-10 were entered in the heat rate definition table.

Given the challenges facing the pipeline infrastructure industry, the Company applied a limit on new combined cycles (“CCs”) in the model. The Company assumed natural gas firm transportation (“FT”) used for existing units would be available to use for new CCs as the existing units either retired or power purchase agreement (“PPA”) expired. Beyond this FT, based on recent industry experience and its own judgement, the Company assumed an additional REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED REDACTED. Please note that new CCs were allowed to be selected beginning in 2030. While this constraint may limit new CCs selection in some years, overall, there is enough FT between existing FT becoming available and the new FT to add approximately REDACTED by 2050.

In the 2022 IRP, there wasn’t a limit applied to new CCs in the expansion plan; however, the Company reviewed each plan and compared against the existing FT that was projected to become available due to unit retirements and PPA expirations to ensure the CC build was reasonable. Question:
Regarding the setup of the Resource Mix study Aurora runs.
Why did the Company decide to leave out from modeling the resources the Company proposes to acquire to satisfy short term requirements?
When the Company conducts IRPs, does it always leave out of the Resource Mix study proposed resources from the modeling? The MPC PPA was the only proposed resource allowed to be selected in the Resource Mix Study. The other proposed resources will be included in future modeling, if approved in this docket.

For the 2023 IRP Update, the generic CT and battery resources serve as appropriate proxies for many of the proposed resources as it relates to the impact to avoided energy costs.

STF-JKA-3-15

Question:
Please see the last bullet on page 13 of PD Resource Mix Study.docx, which concerns capacity needs in 2027.  
Please try to explain in more detail why this had to be done, as the Company’s explanation requires clarification.
How can the Company model a purchase agreement with Mississippi Power for just one year. What happens in the remaining years.
If the Mississippi Power PPA is acquired, how would the modeling of the Mississippi Power resources be different than they are modeled today. Please explain. 
In any modeling performed for the IRP, did the Company model the Mississippi Power PPA per the proposed new contract terms? If so, please identify the inputs used to model the Mississippi Power PPA contract terms.

Response:
As stated in response to STF-JKA-2-2, the Company included the Mississippi Power PPA (“MPC PPA”) as an option in the Resource Mix Study. The capacity need for the Company is 1,875 MW in Winter 2026/2027. While the Company did not have to offer the MPC PPA, given the magnitude of the capacity need and to demonstrate the extraordinary value of the MPC PPA, it was offered as a selection option to help reduce the capacity need if economic to do so. As shown in the Resource Mix Study, it was an economic option and helped reduce the capacity need to be met with other resource options.  
The Resource Mix Study is used to determine a roadmap for capacity resource additions once a capacity need has been identified. Because the System does not have a capacity need until Winter 2026/2027, the MPC PPA is modeled to reflect it being selected at the time of the capacity need. While this won’t capture the full 5-year cost of the MPC PPA, it will capture the market rate of capacity in the years 2027 and 2028 to allow generic capacity resource options to compete alongside it. 
With the execution of the MPA PPA, the resource ledgers for Mississippi Power and Georgia Power will be appropriately adjusted to reflect the capacity that is purchased from Mississippi Power.
