COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before January 8, 2024.

Transmission Planning and Data Centers

Has the Company evaluated the use of Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) to provide benefits to the transmission system and to provide capacity during peak demand periods? If so, please provide the studies, evaluation results, conclusions, and recommendations of the Company’s evaluation(s).

Provide a breakdown of the number of data centers currently within the Company’s portfolio, as of November 1, 2023, and those projected in the 2023 IRP Update. Please identify: 
The number, if available, of Hyperscalers.
The number of Network and Internet exchange service providers
The number involved with data mining and crypto currency.

What lines/circuits are projected to be overloaded with the introduction of new Data Centers? How is the Company addressing this issue and has the Company projected any future data center, industrial, or residential increases on load in their Transmission Planning upgrades?

Has the Company coordinated with any Data Center customers to understand how the customer will coordinate rooftop PV, diesel generator backup, hydrogen, and BESS to serve as a resource during periods of peak demand?

Do the data centers on Georgia Power’s system, or expected to be on Georgia Power’s system going forward, have demand flexibility to provide support services, including load management, during periods of peak demand? If so, what demand flexibility do the data centers have and what is the duration of these systems? If multiple systems are in place will they run simultaneously or can they operate independent of each other?  

Has the Company investigated grid and transmission stability of the large load data centers and reconnection times due to facility protection equipment, and how to mitigate risk that could impact a wider range of customers? 
Provide studies or analysis if available?
If no studies or analysis have been conducted, please provide reasoning for this? 
Provide data on the frequency that outages have occurred and are directly linked to load consumption from data centers. 

Has the Company compared the forecasted load of existing data center customers to the actual load of these customers to better understand the accuracy of load ramp forecasts for data centers? If so, please provide the study, results, and conclusions from the analysis. 

Does the Company have access to, or any knowledge of, the proposed data center power-use-effectiveness (PUE) rating and what margin of error has gone into Transmission Planning in these areas to assure adequate loading will be available? 
Has the Company assessed proposed PUE ratings when negotiating with data center clients outside of Georgia Power’s immediate territory?
