COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before December 22, 2023. 

DEFINITIONS

As may be used in this document:  
1.“The Company,” “Georgia Power” or “The Utility” means Georgia Power Company and its present and former officers, employees, agents, representatives, directors, and all other persons acting or purporting to act on behalf of said Company.
The term “you” and “your” refer to “the Company.”
The term “person” means any natural person, corporation, corporate division, partnership, other unincorporated association, trust, government agency, or entity.
The term “document” or “documentation” shall have the broadest possible meaning under applicable law.  “Document” or “documentation” means every writing or record of every type and description that is in the possession, custody or control of the Company including, but not limited to, correspondence, memoranda, e-mails, drafts, workpapers, summaries, stenographic or handwritten notes, studies, notices, publications, books, pamphlets, reports, surveys, minutes or statistical compilations, computer and other electronic records or tapes or printouts, including, but not limited to, electronic mail files; and copies of such writing or records containing any commentary or notation whatsoever that does not appear in the original.  The term “document” or “documentation” further includes, by way of illustration and not limitation, schedules, progress schedules, time logs, drawings, computer disks, chart projections, time tables, summaries of other documents, minutes, surveys, work sheets, drawings, comparisons, evaluations, laboratory and testing reports, telephone call records, personal diaries, calendars, personal notebooks, personal reading files, transcripts, witness statements and indices.
The term “referring or relating to” means consisting of, containing, mentioning, suggesting, reflecting, concerning, regarding, summarizing, analyzing, discussing, involving, dealing with, emanating from, directed at, pertaining to in any way, or in any way logically or factually connected or associated with the matter discussed.
“And” and “or” as used herein shall be construed both conjunctively and disjunctively and each shall include the other whenever such construction will serve to bring within the scope of these discovery requests any information that would not otherwise be brought within their scope.
The singular as used herein shall include the plural and the masculine gender shall include the feminine and the neuter.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a person other than a natural person means to state:  the full name of the person and any names under which it conducts business; the present or last known address of the person; and, the present or last known telephone number of the person.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a document means to provide with respect to each document requested to be identified by these discovery requests a description of the document that is sufficient for purposes of a request to produce or a subpoena duces tecum, including the following:
the type of document (e.g., letter, memorandum, etc.);
the date of the document; the title or label of the document;
the Bates number or other identifier used to number the document for use in  litigation; the identity of the originator;
the identity of each person to whom it was sent;
the identity of each person to whom a copy or copies were sent;
a summary of the contents of the document;
the name and last known address of each person who presently has possession, custody or control of the document;
if any such document was, but is no longer, in your possession,  custody or control or is no longer in existence, state whether it: (1) is missing or lost; (2) has been destroyed; or (3) has been transferred voluntarily or involuntarily, and if so, state the circumstances surrounding the authorization for each such disposition and the date of such disposition.
“Identify”, “identifying” or identity” when used in reference to a communication should be read to include information regarding the date of the communication, whether the communication was written or oral, the identity of all parties and witnesses to the communication, the substance of what was said and/or transpired and, if written, the identity of the document(s) containing or referring to the communication.

Referring to Informal Data Request 11, Attachment A, External Additions Tab
For each of lines 3 through 38, what is the source of the Certainty provided in Column E?  For each line item please provide a description of the approach and identify for those less than 100% when in the process of providing service that the certainty will increase to 100%.
For each of lines 3 through 38, what is the source of the Load provided in Column F and the Load Factor in Column G?  
How did Georgia Power develop the growth in demand shown in each line of the External Additions? For example, did Georgia Power assume an average growth for each, or has each potential load addition provided the forecasted demand, or did Georgia Power use some other method?
Is GPC using the Certainty provided in Column E of the Informal Data Request 11 Attachment A external Additions Tab to generate Peak Demand Need summarized in the Attachment STF-244-12-a or is a 100% expectation utilized or some other approach?  Please explain the rationale behind the source of the projection.

Refer to the file titled Attachment STF-244-12-a. 
Please provide the spreadsheet that builds up by customer segment plus the new loads to result in the Peak Demand shown in line 6. Provide the spreadsheet with all formulas and references intact..  
Are all the New Load Additions assumed to be 100% probable in the GPC Base Load vs Capability Tab or is there some probability of completion assumed in the Peak Demand for such loads?  
Please provide sufficient detail to understand GPC’s derivation of the new planning peak with regard to the new load additions and whether the residential, commercial and other customer segments also differ from the 2022 IRP.
Relative to the GPC Base Load vs Capability, is the only change in the table associated with Peak Demand in row 6 or has GPC included other modifications?  If additional modifications are made, please identify each and describe the changes and why they are necessary.
Please provide sufficient detail to understand how the addition of large external or internal loads contributes to peak requirement? How is the likelihood of a load coming online factored into the peak energy requirement?

Refer to the file titled “External Load Sorted.” 
Has Georgia Power assumed that all 11,241 MW shown in Cell C48 will be included in the 2023 IRP update?  
It appears that some of the loads shown have not submitted an RFS. Where are all these projects in the process of commitment to taking services from Georgia Power?
Please clarify the contents of each column with column headers and provide a note for the column header if necessary. 
For Column I, answer the following:

Does “RFS” refer to a “Request for Service?”
Is an RFS required for all “outside the primary territory” loads that may be served?  
Please provide a narrative description of the RFS process.
Please identify the point at which GPC determines that a new load needs to be included in the load forecast used in the IRP.  

Refer to file titled “Informal Data Request 11 Attachment A TS”
It appears all projects, even those with certainty less than 100% appear to reach full load during the forecast horizon. Please explain how the certainty figures (column E on Tab External Additions) should be interpreted. Is the certainty value referring to the likelihood the load interconnects? 

If not, what is the correct interpretation?
If yes, why do these loads continue to grow to full capacity post 2027?

How are the certainty figures (column E on Tab External Additions) determined?
Are all projects listed in this sheet assumed to be completed eventually?
How does the certainty figure impact the future load beyond 2027?
Please provide context and the methodology used to determine the differing growth rates used for various loads, especially those with certainty less than 100%.
It appears certainty values are only applied to 2027. How does the certainty value, and the subsequent growth rates of external loads affect load in years post 2027?
Please provide the expected peak energy requirements by year of all the external loads listed in this sheet. Please provide the expected peak energy requirements in an excel spreadsheet that clearly shows how the likelihood of the load coming online is factored into the peak energy requirements.


What has GPCs experience with large loads historically, particularly with respect to large loads with multiple potential sites and their resulting selection of GPC over other sites?  For example, GPC experiences about 50% of the projected loads becoming a reality?  Has GPC experienced significant delays in completion dates for such projects?

How did GPC project new large loads in the 2022 IRP?  Are any of the loads identified in the 2023 update included in the 2022 IRP?  If yes please explain.

Relative to Customer Choice additions, what commitments are required of Customer Choice customers if Georgia Power agrees to serve?  

Relative to Customer Choice loads, are these customers served under standard rates? If yes, what current tariff is used to serve these loads?

Relative to Customer Choice loads, are any investments to serve such customers a cost charged to that customer or are other customers responsible for system improvements to serve a new customer choice customer?
