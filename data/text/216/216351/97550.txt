STF-WG-10-29

Question:

In projects that have the phrase ‘… by the end of the plan’ in the justification, is this referring to 2025? 2031? If 2031, explain what equipment or other factors are triggering such a lead time.

Response:

The phrase “by the end of the plan” refers to the end date of the Grid Investment Plan as presented in the 2019 Base Rate Case, which is 2031. As mentioned in the Company’s response to STF-WG-9-3 subpart (c), the Company intends to extend the Grid Investment Plan beyond 2031 to comply with the Commission’s Order in the 2022 Base Rate Case to reduce the overall plan spending by 40% during the current 3-year Alternate Rate Plan period. Although the Company intends to extend the plan, 2031 remains the benchmark for the project justifications included in STF-WG-9-3 Attachments B and C. 
The significant number of transmission assets approaching or exceeding their Company expected life by 2031 underscores the need for transmission investment through the Grid Investment Plan.  This investment is essential due to the heightened risk of failure as assets age beyond their expected life. Further, investment is necessary to prevent transmission system reliability deterioration across the system. Having an increased number of assets nearing or past expected life also presents both operational and supply chain challenges should a failure occur, as the lead time for equipment has extended significantly in recent years (e.g., 3-5 years for substation transformers and breakers). The Grid Investment Plan provides a structured, programmatic approach to replacing aging transmission assets in a way that accounts for these considerations.

GEORGIA POWER COMPANY
Docket No. 44280
Georgia Power Company’s 2022 Rate Case
Staff Data Request No. STF-WG-10


STF-WG-10-27

Question:

Refer to the Company’s response to STF-WG-10-26 above. For each equipment outage indicated in this response, identify any failure to the backup supply serving the load during the indicated equipment outage. In a MS Excel spreadsheet, please provide a list of any outages that occurred on the backup supply capacity. For each such outage of the backup supply, please provide the date, the transmission substation or transmission line ID, the duration of the outage of the backup supply, and the primary and any secondary causes recorded, along with any field notes. For those subsequent transmission line and substation equipment outages resulting in a service interruption for customers, please also include a count of customers interrupted (CI) and the duration in minutes of the service interruption (CMI). Please use the same format employed in the response to STF-WG-10-4.

Response:

The Company interprets “backup supply” as equipment that will carry the load in the event of a failure on the transmission system. The transmission system is dynamic in nature, where sources could change depending on the system configuration at any point in time. Transmission equipment does not generally have a specified “backup supply,” as network power flows automatically reroute to all other electrical paths in the transmission network if transmission equipment is out of service. In transmission operations planning and in real-time operations, the Company assesses N-1 and other contingencies to identify transmission constraints and adjusts the power system, as needed, to maintain reliable operations consistent with the NERC reliability standards. Keeping in mind that the “backup supply” that would serve as a redundant power source in the event of a failure changes dynamically, it is not possible to provide a list of outages that occurred on the “backup supply capacity,” It is important to note that each occurrence of an outage on major transmission equipment, such as transformers that are approaching or beyond the Company’s expected life, reduces the reliability of the system and often cancels planned/ongoing work on other portions of the system, which has negative impacts to cost and reliability.
