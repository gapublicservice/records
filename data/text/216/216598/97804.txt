COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before January 4, 2024. 

DEFINITIONS

As may be used in this document:  
1.“The Company,” “Georgia Power” or “The Utility” means Georgia Power Company and its present and former officers, employees, agents, representatives, directors, and all other persons acting or purporting to act on behalf of said Company.
The term “you” and “your” refer to “the Company.”
The term “person” means any natural person, corporation, corporate division, partnership, other unincorporated association, trust, government agency, or entity.
The term “document” or “documentation” shall have the broadest possible meaning under applicable law.  “Document” or “documentation” means every writing or record of every type and description that is in the possession, custody or control of the Company including, but not limited to, correspondence, memoranda, e-mails, drafts, workpapers, summaries, stenographic or handwritten notes, studies, notices, publications, books, pamphlets, reports, surveys, minutes or statistical compilations, computer and other electronic records or tapes or printouts, including, but not limited to, electronic mail files; and copies of such writing or records containing any commentary or notation whatsoever that does not appear in the original.  The term “document” or “documentation” further includes, by way of illustration and not limitation, schedules, progress schedules, time logs, drawings, computer disks, chart projections, time tables, summaries of other documents, minutes, surveys, work sheets, drawings, comparisons, evaluations, laboratory and testing reports, telephone call records, personal diaries, calendars, personal notebooks, personal reading files, transcripts, witness statements and indices.
The term “referring or relating to” means consisting of, containing, mentioning, suggesting, reflecting, concerning, regarding, summarizing, analyzing, discussing, involving, dealing with, emanating from, directed at, pertaining to in any way, or in any way logically or factually connected or associated with the matter discussed.
“And” and “or” as used herein shall be construed both conjunctively and disjunctively and each shall include the other whenever such construction will serve to bring within the scope of these discovery requests any information that would not otherwise be brought within their scope.
The singular as used herein shall include the plural and the masculine gender shall include the feminine and the neuter.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a person other than a natural person means to state:  the full name of the person and any names under which it conducts business; the present or last known address of the person; and, the present or last known telephone number of the person.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a document means to provide with respect to each document requested to be identified by these discovery requests a description of the document that is sufficient for purposes of a request to produce or a subpoena duces tecum, including the following:
the type of document (e.g., letter, memorandum, etc.);
the date of the document; the title or label of the document;
the Bates number or other identifier used to number the document for use in  litigation; the identity of the originator;
the identity of each person to whom it was sent;
the identity of each person to whom a copy or copies were sent;
a summary of the contents of the document;
the name and last known address of each person who presently has possession, custody or control of the document;
if any such document was, but is no longer, in your possession,  custody or control or is no longer in existence, state whether it: (1) is missing or lost; (2) has been destroyed; or (3) has been transferred voluntarily or involuntarily, and if so, state the circumstances surrounding the authorization for each such disposition and the date of such disposition.
“Identify”, “identifying” or identity” when used in reference to a communication should be read to include information regarding the date of the communication, whether the communication was written or oral, the identity of all parties and witnesses to the communication, the substance of what was said and/or transpired and, if written, the identity of the document(s) containing or referring to the communication.

For the proposed transmission upgrades identified in the screening analysis, please identify the case or cases (including whether the seasonal case was at normal dispatch or Area Max generation) that led to each transmission need.

In reference to the upgrades proposed in the results presented on November 27, 2023, please provide a detailed assessment of alternative solutions evaluated for their cost-effectiveness compared to the proposed upgrades or reconductoring. Include feasibility studies, comparative cost analysis, and potential benefits of each alternative.

With respect to the findings presented on November 27, 2023, was there an analysis on the feasibility and impact of bundling or reconductoring existing circuits for accommodating higher thermal ratings? Please provide the detailed findings and rationale for the chosen approach.

Did Georgia Power consider constructing additional 345 kV substations and transformers as an alternative means of energy provision from the bulk transmission system, as per the scenarios discussed on November 27, 2023? Provide all relevant studies, evaluations, and conclusions.

Please submit any analysis conducted by Georgia Power on higher future load scenarios, specifically in the context of the presentations and projections made on November 27, 2023, including system impact assessments and possible alternatives studied.

In relation to the increased load projections presented on November 27, 2023, has Georgia Power identified additional transmission capital investments required for accommodating load additions outside its current geographic area? Provide detailed plans and justifications.

For the load projections included in the November 27, 2023 presentation, the Georgia PSC requests copies of all relevant contracts signed, particularly those related to data centers and AI load centers.

In reference to the preliminary results presented on November 27, 2023, provide detailed N-0, N-1, N-1-G, and other relevant power flow results, including data showing the MVA flow of overloaded transmission elements pre- and post-contingency. 

In the context of the preliminary results shared on November 27, 2023, please elaborate on whether Georgia Power evaluated the potential of localized battery storage solutions to mitigate peak loads. Were customers initiating new load requests required or incentivized to install peak-shaping battery storage or self-generating clean energy systems? Provide any studies or models that detail the impact of such localized solutions on the proposed load.

Did Georgia Power assess and incorporate the clean energy requirements or preferences of each new load customer into the planning process? Please provide the data and correspondence that pertain to each new customer's explicit clean energy requirements or requests, as well as any clean energy options that were offered or negotiated.

Was the application of dynamic line ratings considered as an alternative to permanent line upgrades, thereby allowing for a temporary thermal rating increase under certain contingencies? Detail any analysis carried out in this regard, including the technical and economic feasibility of utilizing dynamic line ratings within the existing infrastructure.

Regarding the upgrades presented on November 27, 2023, how did Georgia Power consider the load profile of the future load in their analysis? Was there a specific focus on facilities with operational flexibility for short activation periods, as opposed to their 'base' load levels? Please provide the load profile analysis and any assumptions regarding operational flexibility.

Did Georgia Power conduct any probability analysis or consider the likelihood of all future loads being online and operating at 100% of their requested MWs simultaneously, particularly during summer peak conditions? Submit any historical data from similar load customers that support this type of load profile assessment.

In designing transmission solutions for the proposed new load, did Georgia Power consider and align with Southern Company's internal targets regarding clean energy? Please provide documentation that outlines how transmission solutions were designed to support these clean energy goals.


In the preliminary results shared on November 27, 2023, did Georgia Power consider the role of transmission-level battery storage being online or charging during the studied scenarios? Supply the analysis or models that include transmission-level battery storage as a factor in maintaining system reliability and managing peak loads.

For the screening analysis, were any transmission upgrades from the separate “North-South” transmission study included, either in the base case or as a sensitivity?
