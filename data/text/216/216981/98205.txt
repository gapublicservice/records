BEFORE THE GEORGIA PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION

GEORGIA POWER COMPANY
DOCKET NO. 55378

Data Request No. STF-PIA-7-1

BASIS FOR THE ASSERTION THAT THE 
INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-PIA-7-1 (“Response”).  In the Response, the Company has provided data supporting the economic analysis of projects proposed in the 2023 IRP Update including an acquisition with sensitive pricing and confidential details (the “Information”).  All of such information constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use.  Specifically, the Information includes competitively sensitive details specific to the resources under contract, proposed to be acquired or developed. If the Information were made public, competitors, bidders, and suppliers could use the Information to unfairly manipulate the request for proposals process and competitive market to structure future bids and set an artificial price floor to arbitrarily increase prices to the detriment of the Company and its customers. Public dissemination of the Information would undermine Georgia Power’s ability to negotiate the best price and contract terms and could harm the Company’s ability to secure the best cost bids and resources for the benefit of customers. In addition, the terms of the agreements themselves provides for the confidentiality and protection of the Information. Compromising the confidentiality of such Information could harm Georgia Power in current or future negotiations, as counterparties may use the Information as a bargaining tool in negotiations or fear compelled disclosure of key contractual terms. Lastly, the Company’s competitors are not required to reveal or publish similar information.

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy.  Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information.  Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis.  Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.  


STF-PIA-7-1

Question:
Please identify all assumptions, costs and benefits in the 2023 IRP Update related to federal, state and local tax incentives, grants, loan subsidies or other benefits. 
Please explain and quantify the impact of each on load forecast.  
Please explain and quantify the impact of each on transmission needs and investments.
Please explain and quantify the impact on the generation resources (Company owned and PPAs) the Company has identified to meet new load growth. 


Response:
The Company does not explicitly include any assumptions, costs, and benefits related to federal, state and local tax incentives, grants, loan subsidies, or other benefits in the load forecast.
Transmission planning does not consider any assumptions, costs and benefits related to federal, state, and local tax incentives, grants, loan subsidies or other benefits in its analyses on transmission needs and investments. The Company analyzes the impact of firm and future firm generation and load in compliance with NERC Transmission Planning Reliability standards, which is consistent with the planning processes outlined in Technical Appendix Volume 3 of the 2022 Georgia Power Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) (Docket No. 44160).
For the generation resources contemplated, the Company has assumed and modeled tax obligations from federal, state, and local taxing authorities in the form of property and income taxes among others. The Company also assumed and modeled applicable tax incentives for qualifying Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”) and solar facilities.   The Company modeled both production tax credits (“PTC”) and investment tax credits (“ITC”) for eligible facilities in its 2023 IRP Update. Please see STF-JKA-2-19 subpart (f) for more information on these assumptions. 
Additionally, for the REDACTED REDACTED, a local county property tax abatement has been negotiated by the developer. Please see the Company’s response to STF-JKA-2-28 for additional information. 
The Company will continue pursuing opportunities to enhance benefits related to federal, state, and local tax incentives, grants, loan subsidies or other benefits as those opportunities are identified and vetted.

INFORMATION SUBMITTED IS A TRADE SECRET

As part of Georgia Power Company’s 2023 Integrated Resource Plan Update filed in Docket No. 55378 (“2023 IRP Update”), Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) submits to the Georgia Public Service Commission its response to STF-PIA-7-20 (“Response”). In the Response, the Company has provided detailed information and data regarding the negotiations and payments for energy and capacity pursuant to a power purchase agreement with a third party. All of such information (the “Information”) constitutes trade secret information of Southern Company, Georgia Power, and its affiliates and is therefore protected from public disclosure under Commission Rule 515-3-1-.11.

 The Information derives economic value from not being generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use. Specifically, the Information contains competitively sensitive details on the costs to obtain energy from a third party through purchase. If the Information were made public, competitors, bidders, and suppliers could use the Information to unfairly manipulate the request for proposals process and competitive market to structure future bids and set an artificial price floor to arbitrarily increase prices to the detriment of the Company and its customers. Public dissemination of the Information would undermine Georgia Power’s ability to negotiate the best price and contract terms and could harm the Company’s ability to secure the best cost bids and resources for the benefit of customers. In addition, the terms of the power purchase agreements themselves provides for the confidentiality and protection of the Information. Compromising the confidentiality of such Information could harm Georgia Power in future PPA negotiations, as counterparties may fear compelled disclosure of key contractual terms. Lastly, the Company’s competitors are not required to reveal or publish similar information.

The Information is subject to substantial procedures to maintain its secrecy. Only select Georgia Power and Southern Company Services personnel are granted access to the Information. Those personnel receive access only on a “need to know” basis. Parties outside the Company who have been granted access to the Information, if any, have been required to sign confidentiality agreements with respect to the Information.
