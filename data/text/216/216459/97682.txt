COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before December 21, 2023. 

DEFINITIONS

As may be used in this document:  
1.“The Company,” “Georgia Power” or “The Utility” means Georgia Power Company and its present and former officers, employees, agents, representatives, directors, and all other persons acting or purporting to act on behalf of said Company.
The term “you” and “your” refer to “the Company.”
The term “person” means any natural person, corporation, corporate division, partnership, other unincorporated association, trust, government agency, or entity.
The term “document” or “documentation” shall have the broadest possible meaning under applicable law.  “Document” or “documentation” means every writing or record of every type and description that is in the possession, custody or control of the Company including, but not limited to, correspondence, memoranda, e-mails, drafts, workpapers, summaries, stenographic or handwritten notes, studies, notices, publications, books, pamphlets, reports, surveys, minutes or statistical compilations, computer and other electronic records or tapes or printouts, including, but not limited to, electronic mail files; and copies of such writing or records containing any commentary or notation whatsoever that does not appear in the original.  The term “document” or “documentation” further includes, by way of illustration and not limitation, schedules, progress schedules, time logs, drawings, computer disks, chart projections, time tables, summaries of other documents, minutes, surveys, work sheets, drawings, comparisons, evaluations, laboratory and testing reports, telephone call records, personal diaries, calendars, personal notebooks, personal reading files, transcripts, witness statements and indices.
The term “referring or relating to” means consisting of, containing, mentioning, suggesting, reflecting, concerning, regarding, summarizing, analyzing, discussing, involving, dealing with, emanating from, directed at, pertaining to in any way, or in any way logically or factually connected or associated with the matter discussed.
“And” and “or” as used herein shall be construed both conjunctively and disjunctively and each shall include the other whenever such construction will serve to bring within the scope of these discovery requests any information that would not otherwise be brought within their scope.
The singular as used herein shall include the plural and the masculine gender shall include the feminine and the neuter.

The company currently reports on its website that there are 2,366,818 residential customers and indicates in the 2023 IRP update that it has plans to include an additional 25,000 customers in its Thermostat Control program to reduce peak; with this addition the Company will have 50,000 customers in the program for 2024 and remains at that level.  
Do you agree that this is approximately 2.1% of all residential customers?  
How many customers in total could be offered this program?  
Why is the Company not proposing to increase as much as possible in the short run to address growth in peak since, as indicated in Attachment C of the Load Forecast Update,  the program now passes the TRC test?

How is the cost of electricity incorporated into the energy and peak forecasts by customer class? 

How does price of elasticity get addressed in the various load forecasting models utilized by Georgia Power?

Please provide monthly historical average electricity prices by customer class from 1990 through YTD 2023 in spreadsheet format.

Please provide anticipated price increases as a result of the expected January 1, 2024 approved increase and other base and fuel increases over the coming years to reflect the inclusion of Vogtle Unit 4 in rate base going forward.

Provide a forecast of upcoming price increases as a result of the inclusion of Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 into rate base as well as the new load acquisitions necessary to serve major new load growth.

Please provide a process map showing how each of the load forecasting steps relates and how the overall energy and peak forecasts develop including identifying the models utilized.

Please provide documentation for the LoadMap model including a description of the key parameters in the modeling and model interactions, and sources of historic information and projected information.


Please provide each regression equation utilized to complete the short-term energy forecast and show the alternative models not selected with rationale for rejection. For each equation/model, provide summary statistics (mean, median, min, max, std. deviation, variance, R2, etc).

Please provide a backcast for each of the equations utilized in the load forecast in the previous question, including a comparison of each backcast to actuals for each year of the backcast. Essentially, provide an in-sample analysis of each equation.

Please provide all the economic parameters utilized in the models used by Georgia Power to complete its forecast including historic values since 2000 and projected values for the forecast period. 

Please provide since 2000, a matrix showing the error of each energy and peak forecast by sector.  This matrix should include the error of the 2000 forecast for each year (2001, 2002, 2003, etc.) through the end of the period of the forecast and for each forecast since 2000.  Please provide the difference (positive or negative) and the percentage error of each.

Please provide documentation for the Peak Metrix Model including a description of the key parameters in the modeling, sources of historical information and projected information.  Please describe how the energy forecasts are used for the peak projections.

Please provide documentation for the @Risk model utilized and describe how the quarterly load is ultimately calculated.

For the @Risk tool, please develop peak projections for each scenario below:
Assume all the low values occur for the three parameters modeled, and show the MW forecast and the probability of forecast expectation.
Assume all the mid values for the three parameters modeled, and show the MW forecast and the probability of forecast expectation.
Assume all the high values for the three parameters modeled, and show the MW forecast and the probability of forecast expectation.


Please identify whether the shift between the short and long-term models requires external adjustments and if yes, identify what approach is used and provide the before and after adjustment data.

Please provide a written summary of the structure of your load forecast model, explaining the following:
The construction/structure of the model and any structural assumptions made in the model (random drift, fixed effects, transformations of variables, etc.) 
The key drivers for load growth in the non-customer choice portion of load.
The time period of the data underlying the model and the extent to which they vary.

Refer to the 2023 IRP Update, Page 1 of the Update Load and Energy Forecast, Section 1.0 Executive Summary Overview, stating that Large load projects where Georgia Power was selected in 2022 represented almost 2,200 MW.  For each customer that makes up the 2,200 MW please identify the customer, customer’s load (MW) and whether the customer is located inside Georgia Power service territory or outside service territory.

Please provide the Large load projects where Georgia Power was selected in 2023 to date For each customer that selected Georgia Power please identify the customer’s load (MW) and whether the customer is located inside Georgia Power service territory or outside service territory.

For each new large customer in the Company’s response to STF-DEA-1-2 please identify whether the customer is located inside Georgia Power service territory or outside service territory.
