STF-PIA-13-15

Question: 

The following questions refer to the contractual arrangements between the Purchaser Furnished Equipment, or Southern Company Services (SCS), Equipment providers (Mitsubishi Power Americas, Vogt Power International, Hitachi, and Siemens), and the Construction Contractor (Black & Veatch Construction Inc).
a. Please provide a narrative explanation of these contracting relationships relative to SCS’s legal responsibilities as Purchaser.
b. Please provide a narrative explanation for engaging in Equipment Services Agreements as opposed to contracting under an EPC agreement that encompasses Procurement.
c. Please indicate when equipment ownership interests transfer between the three organizations, i.e. Equipment providers, SCS, Construction Contractor.
d. Please explain the benefits and risks associated with SCS performing Procurement for the Major Equipment Procurement.

Response:

The Purchaser holds separate contracts for major equipment providers: Mitsubishi Power Americas (“MPWA”) for the combustion turbines, steam turbines, and associated generators; and Vogt Power International for heat recovery steam generators. The Purchaser holds an engineering, procurement, and construction (“EPC”) agreement with Black and Veatch Construction, Inc. (“BVCI”) for the purchase of balance of plant equipment, design, and construction. The Purchaser will work with the parties to execute the contracts.

The Company issued separate inquiries for equipment and EPC services to facilitate independent selection of each. This approach allowed the Company to evaluate each original equipment manufacturer (“OEM”) and each EPC contractor that submitted proposals in response to the inquiries and to select the preferred providers for their respective services based on the merits of their proposal.

For MPWA, title to Equipment and parts of the Work furnished under the Contract will pass to the Company as “Purchaser” upon receipt by Contractor of the payment for delivery of Equipment by Contractor. For Vogt, Contractor warrants that title to Equipment, materials, and other components and parts of the Work furnished under the Contract will pass to Purchaser either upon delivery to the Work Site, or upon receipt of payment by Contractor, whichever occurs first. For BVCI, title to all Work, materials, and Equipment shall pass to Purchaser upon the earlier of (i) payment for such Work, materials, and Equipment or (ii) delivery to the Plant Site of such Work, materials, and Equipment.

Southern Company Services (“SCS”) is able to leverage its relationships and bulk purchasing power with OEMs to provide more favorable terms than if purchases are made by Georgia Power alone. The Company does not foresee any risk with SCS procuring major equipment as opposed to Georgia Power.

Question: 

Please provide a listing of risks that are intended to be covered by the Vendors with which Company has Agreements in place. The purpose of the question is to understand the risks for which the Company will not be liable under any circumstances during the Project.

Response:

The negotiated engineering, procurement, and construction (“EPC”) and equipment procurement frameworks reflect shared risk allocations that balance responsibilities between the Company and its vendors in a manner consistent with industry practice for combined cycle generation projects. Risks are allocated to the party best positioned to manage, control, or mitigate the identified risk (e.g., force majeure, unforeseen subsurface conditions, and certain supply chain disruptions). This approach ensured the contracts were competitively priced—as a contract under which the Company has no liability for common construction or procurement risks would be prohibitively expensive and not in the best interest of customers.
 
Each party remains responsible for its own performance, representations, and commitments, and the Company retains responsibility for its defined contractual scope and coordination obligations. Except for vendor contract breaches, there is no circumstance under which the Company has no liability.

===== DKT 56298 & 56310 STF-PIA-13 PD/STF-PIA-13-19.docx =====
STF-PIA-13-19

Question: 

Please refer to Exhibit C Purchaser Provided Permits 1) Prevention of significant deterioration (PSD) Nonattainment New Source Review (NSR) air construction permit.

a. When were the applications for each site Wansley, Bowen, and McIntosh submitted to the Georgia Environmental Protection Division (EPD)?
b. When does the Company expect to receive these permits?
c. What construction activities are permitted prior to receiving these permits?
d. In the Level 1 schedules provided for each site how has the air permitting duration been incorporated? Has any float been included in the activity for obtaining the permits in the event that one or more permit applications is/are challenged by an intervener?
Response:

The application for Wansley was submitted on June 9, 2025; the application for Bowen was submitted on March 5, 2025; and the application for McIntosh will be submitted before November 2025.

The Company has submitted, or intends to submit, the applications into the Georgia Environmental Protection Division’ (“EPD”) Air Protection Branch Expedited Permitting Program. EPD’s goal for these types of applications is to complete review within 183 days. The draft permit for Bowen was issued on September 10, 2025.
Under the Clean Air Act and its regulations, a permit applicant requiring a prevention of significant deterioration (“PSD”) permit may not “begin actual construction” prior to receiving the permit. “Begin actual construction” is defined as “initiation of physical onsite construction activities on an emissions unit which are of a permanent nature.” This means that any onsite physical activities that are not on an emissions unit can proceed in advance of receiving the permit, even if those activities are permanent in nature. The activities identified in the Level One schedules as occurring in advance of full mobilization (Bowen and Wansley) or awards for deep foundations, concrete, or underground contracts (McIntosh) can be conducted in advance of receipt of the permit because they do not constitute construction activities “on an emissions unit.”
Approximately 9-12 months of float is included in schedules, given the application dates and anticipated timeframe for EPD to complete expedited reviews.
