===== DKT 56298 & 56310 STF-PIA-18 PD/STF-PIA-18-4.docx =====
STF-PIA-18-4
Please refer to Company’s response STF-PIA-9-25 ATTACHMENT TRADE SECRET.
Question: 
One account has negative Base Revenue. Please provide an explanation and calculation of this negative value.

Response:
The referenced account is on a fixed price rate offering for a defined time period, which holds the customer accountable to an agreed upon total price, inclusive of fuel and base rate components. As actual fuel prices have fluctuated beyond the assumed fuel prices used in the customer’s contract, the Company allocates the true cost of fuel to fuel revenues, leaving a negative base revenue remaining.

===== DKT 56298 & 56310 STF-PIA-18 PD/STF-PIA-18-5 PUBLIC DISCLOSURE.docx =====
STF-PIA-18-5
Please refer to Company’s response STF-PIA-9-25 ATTACHMENT TRADE SECRET.
Question: 
REDACTED Data Center has a very high fuel unit revenue ($ / MWh) compared to the other accounts. Please provide an explanation.

Response:
Many of the customers in the subset identified in STF-PIA-9-25 Attachment TRADE SECRET take service under Georgia Power’s Real Time Pricing (“RTP”) Tariff. Total fuel costs for these customers are recovered through RTP and Customer Baseline Load (“CBL”) mechanisms. The average fuel unit revenue can vary from customer to customer because RTP prices capture the true cost of fuel. Fuel costs can fluctuate from hour to hour, and usage at varying fuel costs can differ for each customer. These factors can drive the average fuel unit revenue to differ among customers. Additionally, fuel charged to a customer’s CBL is reflective of the then-current Commission-approved Fuel Cost Recovery (“FCR”) rate. Differences in the contract CBL requirements can also drive a difference in the realized average fuel unit revenue among customers.
