Question: 

Threshold and Termination Rights: What was the rationale for including a 10% threshold and a seller termination right in the All-Source RFP PPA? Was a similar mechanism considered for the Supplemental Resources PPA?

Response:

The All-Source Request for Proposals (“RFP”) power purchase agreements (“PPAs) include a “Threshold Amount” as a mechanism for Georgia Power and Seller to share risk of Change of Law Capital Expenditures, which is consistent with prior Commission-approved capacity RFPs.  Please see the NextEra Energy (“NEER”) battery energy storage (“BESS”) PPAs Section 17.1 (Change of Law) and Tenaska PPA Section 17.2 (Change of Law) where “Threshold Amount” and Seller termination right mechanisms are also included in the Supplemental Resources PPAs.

Question: 

Impact on Capacity Pricing: How does the All-Source RFP PPA’s Change of Law provision affect the Annual Capacity Price? Why is there no similar pricing adjustment mechanism in the Supplemental Resources PPA?

Response:

The Supplemental Resources PPAs include the same mechanism for adjusting the annual capacity prices for Excess Change of Law Capital Expenditures as the All-Source RFP PPAs. Under this framework, GPC and Seller must, acting in a commercially reasonable manner, agree to an annual fixed charge rate that will be applied to adjust the annual capacity price.

STF-PIA-14-3

Question: 

Collateral Form Requirements: Why does the All-Source RFP PPA require at least 50% of the performance security to be in the form of a letter of credit or cash if the guarantor is not investment grade? Was this requirement considered or applied in the Supplemental Resources PPA?

Response:
Unlike cash or a cash-equivalent instrument like a letter of credit, a guaranty is only as good as the guarantor’s willingness and ability to pay the Seller’s default liabilities. As such, to post 100% of performance security in the form of a guaranty, the guarantor must have an investment grade or issuer rating that is slightly higher than the investment grade rating required for Creditworthiness. 
Please see the “Eligible Collateral” definition in the Supplemental Resources power purchase agreements (“PPAs”), which includes the same guarantor requirements as the All-Source Request for Proposals (“RFP”) PPAs.

Question: 

Structure and Rationale: Please explain the rationale for using a tiered performance security structure in the All-Source RFP PPA versus the fixed or customized structure in the Supplemental Resources PPA.

Response:

Like the performance security required for All-Source Request for Proposals (“RFP”) power purchase agreements (“PPAs”), the Supplemental Resources PPAs include a tiered performance security structure that sets different amounts based on the stage of project development and applicable Annual Period. The tiered performance security structure is appropriate because Georgia Power’s exposure to damages varies based on the term length, project development stage, and remaining term life.

Question: 

Negotiation Flexibility: Was the performance security structure in the Supplemental Resources PPA the result of bilateral negotiation? If so, why was a more standardized approach used in the All-Source RFP PPA? Change of Law

Response:

 The performance security structure outlined in the Supplemental Resources power purchase agreements (“PPAs”) was not the result of bilateral negotiations. Rather, the performance security requirements in the Tenaska PPA mirror those that would have applied to the Tenaska facility under the All-Source Request for Proposals (“RFP”). Similarly, the NextEra Energy (“NEER”) battery energy storage system (“BESS”) PPAs adopt a tiered performance security structure, consistent with the All-Source RFP PPAs. However, the required amounts over the term are lower than those stipulated under the All-Source RFP. These amounts were determined unilaterally by the Company and were not subject to negotiation.
