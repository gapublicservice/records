STF-PIA-15-4

Question: 
With reference to STF-PIA-8-7, part (c):
Please explain in detail the requirements that a prospective customer must meet to execute a Request for Service.
Is there any financial commitment associated with executing a Request for Service? If yes, please explain what the commitment is. If no, please explain why not.
Please explain in detail the requirements that a prospective customer must meet to execute a Contract for Electric Service.
Is there any financial commitment associated with executing a Contract for Electric Service? If yes, please explain what the commitment is. If no, please explain why not.

Response:
a.  To execute a Request for Service (“RFS”) Large Load customers under the revised rules and regulations provide the following information:
signed non-disclosure agreement (“NDA”)
site control status
zoning status 
required project intake documentation  
initial performance security and associated documentation

Additionally, Georgia Power will have performed work associated with engineering and planning studies relating to their in-service date and associated load ramps. 

b.   Yes. Large Load customers post performance security upon executing an RFS. When the customer later signs a Contract for Electric Service (“CES”), the performance security held under the RFS is replaced by the performance security terms as part of the CES.

Prior to executing a CES, Large Load customers under the revised rules and regulations must complete the RFS and associated requirements identified in subpart (a), when required, and must also provide an increased performance security amount associated with the CES. 

Yes. In addition to other terms and conditions included in the CES, Large Load customers under the revised rules and regulations are responsible for each of the following financial obligations upon execution of the CES:
contractual minimum bills over the term of the contract
Electric service charges 
ongoing performance security requirements 
termination payments 
any excess facilities charges or other charges or fees as defined in the CES

STF-PIA-15-5

Question: 
Regarding the capital costs of the proposed Company-owned battery storage and paired solar-storage projects listed in Tables 1.3.1 and 1.3.2 of the All-Source RFP Certification Filing, as well as the Wadley BESS supplemental project: 
Has GPC completed or commissioned any studies analyzing the potential impact of trade tariffs on the cost and timeline of constructing the proposed facilities? If so, please provide all such studies, including supporting workpapers with formulas intact. 
For each proposed project, please explain whether GPC has already locked in a purchase price for necessary project equipment.

Response:
The Company is actively monitoring and managing impacts from tariffs for the proposed projects. The latest analysis completed on tariff impacts for BESS projects is provided in STF-PIA-15-5 Attachment A TRADE SECRET and supporting calculations are provided in STF-PIA-15-5 Attachment B TRADE SECRET.   
Certainty regarding tariff impacts will only be achievable once these components are imported into the United States, which is when the then applicable tariffs will be applied to the equipment. In August, a U.S. Court of Appeals determined that tariffs imposed under President Trump through the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (“IEEPA”) were unlawful, which are the primary tariffs impacting BESS today. See V.O.S. Selections, Inc. v. Trump, 149 F.4th 1312 (2025).  
For the Wansley, South Hall, Wadley, Yates 320 MW, Yates 250 MW, and Bowen I projects, Engineering Procurement Construction (“EPC”) agreements and Sale and Purchase Agreements (“SPA”) for the battery storage equipment have been executed. These agreements lock in a purchase price for major project equipment, with the limitations outlined in the Company’s response to STF-PIA-4-25 subpart (a.). For Thomson, Bowen II, and Hammond II, EPC agreements have been executed, which set equipment pricing subject to the same limitations outlined in the Company’s response to STF-PIA-4-25 subpart (a.); the battery SPAs will be signed at a later date. The self-build McIntosh project, scheduled for a 2030 commercial operation date (“COD”), will lock in equipment pricing post certification. For the Laurens and Mitchell BESS + Solar projects, the SPA for the battery equipment has been executed and the EPC agreements will be executed following certification, which will lock in the price for other major equipment.

STF-PIA-15-8

Question: 
Please refer to the “Main” tab of the excel attachment to the Q2 2025 Large Load Economic Development Report. Does Georgia Power anticipate that peak load from the prospective customers shown on this tab will be different in the summer and winter? If so, please provide equivalent versions of this dataset (projected peak load by customer and year) for the summer and winter.

Response:
Yes. Georgia Power’s load forecast produces both summer and winter peaks, and the Company applies commercial (data‑center) and industrial load shapes so that the seasonal differences are reflected when developing aggregate class and segment peak demands. This is done within the aggregate forecasting process rather than by maintaining customer‑level summer/winter peak files. Accordingly, the requested customer‑level summer and winter datasets do not exist. (See Technical Appendix, section 1.5.2.3 for details on external peak adjustments for large loads)
