STF-ASR-1-10

Question:

For each Plant, provide the Company's coal inventory policies and procedures and target amounts that applied for 2023.

Response:

The Company’s coal inventory target is set in accordance with the Commission’s Final Order issued in FCR-22 (Docket No. 33302). Actual inventories are not static, and it is common for actual inventory levels to fluctuate above and below targets. The purpose of inventory targets is to reasonably assure that Georgia Power can meet its obligation to provide electricity even during events of supply disruption, whether mine or rail-related, and during periods when coal burn exceeds projections. To this end, the Company purchases coal and plans for deliveries based on maintaining its inventory within the inventory targets and takes prudent actions to return plant inventories to target when they deviate. However, it is important to note that although the Company plans its procurement with a target of an overall average of 40-50 days of inventory across all plants over the course of a year, monthly targets for a particular plant may be set outside the range. Please see STF-ASR-1-10 Attachment A for coal inventory policies and procedures. Also, refer to STF-ASR-1-10 Attachment B for 2023 monthly targets.

The Company’s vegetation management plan is as follows:
Transmission Herbicide – Work plan consists of one-third system miles to be sprayed each year.

Re-clearing – Work plan is based on approximately one-sixth of system miles.

Yard Tree Removal - Scheduled based on the forester’s assessment of the condition of lines within their territories, with respect to planted incompatible species on the Right-of-Way. Distribution Herbicide – Work plan is based on a two-year cycle where approximately half of the system miles are sprayed each year.

Distribution Tree Trimming – Work plan determined by an assessment of vegetation conditions, outage history, and criticality of circuits.  Maintenance intervals typically range from 24-60 months.  On average, one quarter of the system is maintained each year.
Distribution Right-of-Way Improvement – New easements are acquired for circuit segments based on an assessment of vegetation conditions, criticality of circuit, and segment performance.  Approximately 200-400 miles of worst performing distribution right-of-ways are improved each year.

Question:

Prepaid pension asset.  Is any amount for Southern Company Services' net pension asset included in the Company's retail jurisdictional rate base for 2023, by line item, on the Company's 2023 ASR filing Schedule 2, page 1 of 2?  If not, explain fully why not.   If so, identify how much by FERC account.  If exact amounts are not available, provide the Company's best estimates and show in detail how such estimates were calculated.

Response:

No, the Company does not include any of Southern Company Services’ (SCS) net pension asset in retail rate base. The capitalized portion of the pension costs, which are billed to the Company by SCS on a monthly basis, are included in rate base. The billed amounts are imported into the Company’s general ledger and distributed among the various FERC accounts included in the Company’s 2023 ASR filing Section 2, page 1 of 2.

The estimates of the pension cost billed to the Company are not easily determined. This is because pension amounts billed by SCS to the Company, and subsequently distributed among the various FERC accounts in the Company’s general ledger, are calculated by the use of allocations and cost pools. Some of the allocations used in the process utilize dynamic allocation rates, which use direct labor dollars as the allocator. Therefore, there is not a consistent allocation rate that can be applied to recalculate exact amounts billed to the Company because the direct labor billed to the Company changes each month. Also, during the initial SCS billing process, the pension dollars are accumulated in a cost pool and comingled with other benefits before being allocated to the Company’s direct labor charges.  Because the pension costs are not allocated as individual dollars, are co-mingled with other benefits cost, and are incorporated into direct labor charges, it would be extremely difficult to estimate the amount of pension costs billed to the Company.

12.0 Obsolete Materials

Obsolete materials are items that have lost their intrinsic value and must be removed for
disposition. Items identified and verified as obsolete materials MUST be removed from stock
within 90 days of identification and adjusted from inventory on the company’s books.


Identification and Approval
Obsolete materials cannot be identified and approved by the same employee. Plant obsolete
material may be identified by the system owner (engineer). T & D obsolete material may be
identified by a staff engineer. Plant management and T & D management must have
documentation of appropriate review of obsolete material.


Items become obsolete in one of two ways:
1. Technology
Changes in technology render an item unusable.
• Parts support an asset that is no longer used.
• The part is no longer used when it has been replaced by a different part.


2. Shelf-Life Expirations
• Items lose quality over time.
• Items are deemed obsolete if they exceed established life standards.


A report of slow-moving material should be reviewed annually. It is the responsibility of Supply
Chain Management (Transmission, Distribution, Substation) and system owners (Plants) to
insure the evaluation every item on the report as soon as practicable. System upgrades and
changeouts should include a review of potential stranded material. Guidelines for plant
obsolescence review and material disposition are detailed in the Southern Company Generation
Playbook Chapter XIII.


For items stocked at the MDC, the MDC will notify region locations of pending obsolete
material items. The locations will be allowed four weeks to return any items to the MDC. After
the four week period, the region supervisor will work with the storekeeper to insure appropriate
disposition of obsolete items.

STF-ASR-1-70

Question:

For each temporary difference identified in response to the previous question, indicate whether the Company believes the related ADIT is protected or unprotected.  If the Company believes that the ADIT is protected, provide all citations to the IRC, Regulations, PLRs, and all other authoritative sources that address whether the ADIT is protected or unprotected.


Response:

ADIT accounts are either “protected” and subject to the Internal Revenue Code (IRC) normalization requirements, or “unprotected” and not subject to normalization requirements. Protected accounts are those that are the result of book-tax depreciation timing differences.

To be entitled to accelerated methods of depreciation for tax purposes, the Company must use a normalization method of accounting as required by IRC sections 168(f)(2) and 168(i)(9). Specifically, protected ADITs result from the excess of tax depreciation deductions allowable under IRC section 168 (Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System and Accelerated Cost Recovery System), and depreciation deductions that would be allowable under IRC section 167(l).  Unprotected ADITs, which include all ADITs other than protected (regardless of whether they are property-related such as basis differences), are not addressed in the IRC nor the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act.  

As such, in the Company’s response to STF-ASR-1-69, the Company classified excess accelerated depreciation ADITs in FERC accounts 281 and 282 as protected, and all other regulated temporary items as unprotected and not subject to IRS normalization requirements.

As previously stated, the IRC requires the Company to use normalization as a prerequisite to claim accelerated depreciation. If these tax benefits are not normalized in the ratemaking process, the utility loses the right to claim the benefits. In addition, section 13001(d)(1) of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act states that a normalization method of accounting shall not be treated as being used with respect to any public utility property for purposes of section 167 or 168 of the IRCif the taxpayer, in computing its cost of service for ratemaking purposes and reflecting operating results in its regulated books of account, reduces the excess tax reserve more rapidly or to a greater extent than such reserve would be reduced under the average rate assumption method.

The Company excluded settlements of matters involving condemnation of private property for use as a utility easement as they do not constitute lawsuits filed against the Company. The expenses associated with the payments are accrued in the period when the amount is probable and reasonably estimable, which may include periods prior to 2023. Any portion of the settlement amounts expected to be recovered through the insurer are reserved and excluded from expenses. Expense accruals are trued up with final settlement payments including actual insurance recoveries.
  
In general, compromise settlements of lawsuits are “above the line” expenses because such costs are normal operating expenses of the regulated business. In 2012, FERC required the Company to classify expenses resulting from a compromise settlement of employment lawsuits “below the line.”

NRC Fixed Fees:
The NRC calculates the annual fixed fee by dividing the recoverable budgeted costs equally among the licensed power reactors (currently 94 operating reactors).

NRC Variable Fees:
