                                         INTRODUCTION

         Georgia Power Company surprised its captive customers by filing an “Update” to its

Integrated Resource Plan just over a year after the resolution of the 2022 IRP proceedings. Flipping

the IRP process on its head, Georgia Power requested the Commission find “exceptions” to the

Request for Proposal (“RFP”) rules for a fossil fuel-heavy suite of hand-picked resources. The

competitive RFP process is required by Commission Rules and protects customers by ensuring

they only have to pay for the most affordable resources. In its update, and still today, Georgia

Power has not analyzed the impact its requests to build and buy additional resources will have on

residential and small business electric bills.

         Georgia Power Company and Public Interest Advocacy Staff have now presented this

Commission with a Stipulation intended to resolve all issues in this docket. The approval of


                                                 1

Georgia Power’s requests in the Stipulation is unprecedented, unnecessary, and unfair to

residential and small business customers.

        Georgia Power’s own data reveals that its energy needs can be met by competitively

purchasing clean, reliable energy. The businesses flocking to Georgia and causing load growth

want this as well. But Georgia Power chose an overly aggressive load forecast and asked this

Commission to cast aside its consumer protections so Georgia Power can recruit more business,

largely outside of its territory. If costs are distributed as they typically are in a 2025 Rate Case,

residential and small business bills will unjustly have to subsidize these new large customers,

primarily data centers.

        It is the public policy of the State of Georgia to “suppress monopolies and to encourage

competition.” 1 The Stipulation would allow for significant departures from procedures that have

been put in place to uphold this long-standing public policy. Thus, GIPL urges this Commission

to substantially modify the Stipulation.

                              SUMMARY OF RECOMMENDATIONS

        The Commission has an obligation to ensure that Georgia Power is providing affordable

and reliable electric service to its customers and an opportunity to mitigate customer bill increases.

To do so, GIPL respectfully requests that the Commission:

        1. Adopt the language in Attachment 1 to protect the billpayers who did nothing to
           cause this request from paying for the costs of it.

        2. Deny the Yates CTs as unnecessary at this time. Or, in the alternative, order that
           any approved Yates CTs must be competitively procured through an expedited
           All-Source RFP, and in doing so, do not enforce the 70% ownership policy from
           2001, but rather order Georgia Power to file a new proposed ownership
           percentage and supporting evidence in the 2025 IRP.

                                           DISCUSSION

       If the Commission approves the Stipulation as it stands, it will have failed to meet its

statutory obligations in part because Georgia Power’s updated IRP has not adequately forecasted

its load growth, nor has it properly considered the costs that its requests will have on its customers.

       I.      Approving Georgia Power’s requests would be both procedurally and
               substantively unprecedented.

       Approving Georgia Power’s requests, as they stand in the Stipulation, would require the

Commission to make highly unusual if not entirely unprecedented decisions, including:

       1. Approving a novel process of “spend first, ask for approval later,” by giving Georgia
          Power authorization to hand-pick and spend billpayer money on unapproved, long-
          term resources;

       2. Ordering billpayers to pay for Georgia Power’s business expansion plans—without a
          thorough rate case proceeding and without requiring Georgia Power to demonstrate
          that it has made less profit than allowed under its profit-band;

       3. Finding that a 26% reserve margin is the appropriate target reserve margin, despite
          overwhelming evidence from Staff and intervenor experts that this outdated number
          is uneconomic and unwarranted to ensure reliability;

       4. Allowing Georgia Power to build new fossil fuel power plant units without the
          precautions or protections of a competitive bidding process;

       5. Approving generating units based on a finding that Georgia Power must own 70% of
          its capacity without any reliable evidence to support this ownership structure;

       6. Ordering billpayers to pay an additional profit (“additional sum”) to Georgia Power
          for power it purchased without first shopping for the best prices;

       7. Ordering billpayers to pay an additional sum for a “capacity resource” that lacks firm
          transmission;

         III.     Plant Yates Units 8-10 should not be approved.

         The most troubling circumvention of consumer protections presented in the Stipulation is

the approval of the Yates CTs. First, granting Georgia Power’s request would require the

Commission to make a finding that a 70% capacity ownership policy still makes sense and is in

the public interest in 2024, when that is very clearly not the case. Second, Georgia Power has

presented no reliable evidence that the Yates CTs are cost competitive. Lastly, however, if the

Yates CTs are approved, the Commission has options at its disposal to protect billpayers in the

absence of an RFP.

          A. No exception to the RFP Rule applies, and the Commission lacks discretion to
             approve the Yates CTs.
         Georgia Power’s request to skip the required competitive process is neither legal nor in the

public interest. Commission rules are clear that an RFP is required for “each block of required new

supply-side resources identified in the IRP.” 18 Because the RFP process is the key safeguard to

ensure Georgia Power’s captive customers only have to pay for the best-cost resources, it can be

bypassed “only” in limited circumstances. 19 Georgia Power has failed to prove that any exception

to the RFP Rule applies to the Yates Units 8-10. 20 Instead, Georgia Power relies on a discretionary

regulation which says the Commission “may suspend these rules and provide guidance” when the

ownership policy may be triggered. 21

                                         INTRODUCTION

         Georgia Power Company surprised its captive customers by filing an “Update” to its

Integrated Resource Plan just over a year after the resolution of the 2022 IRP proceedings. Flipping

the IRP process on its head, Georgia Power requested the Commission find “exceptions” to the

Request for Proposal (“RFP”) rules for a fossil fuel-heavy suite of hand-picked resources. The

competitive RFP process is required by Commission Rules and protects customers by ensuring

they only have to pay for the most affordable resources. In its update, and still today, Georgia

Power has not analyzed the impact its requests to build and buy additional resources will have on

residential and small business electric bills.

         Georgia Power Company and Public Interest Advocacy Staff have now presented this

Commission with a Stipulation intended to resolve all issues in this docket. The approval of


                                                 1

Georgia Power’s requests in the Stipulation is unprecedented, unnecessary, and unfair to

residential and small business customers.

        Georgia Power’s own data reveals that its energy needs can be met by competitively

purchasing clean, reliable energy. The businesses flocking to Georgia and causing load growth

want this as well. But Georgia Power chose an overly aggressive load forecast and asked this

Commission to cast aside its consumer protections so Georgia Power can recruit more business,

largely outside of its territory. If costs are distributed as they typically are in a 2025 Rate Case,

residential and small business bills will unjustly have to subsidize these new large customers,

primarily data centers.

been put in place to uphold this long-standing public policy. Thus, GIPL urges this Commission

to substantially modify the Stipulation.
