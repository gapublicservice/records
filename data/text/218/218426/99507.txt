Make Ready Program

Georgia Power’s Make Ready Program is designed to reduce cost barriers for commercial and industrial
customers seeking to install electric vehicle chargers, thus helping to address the growing demands for
electric transportation (“ET”) solutions. The program allows Georgia Power to support its customers’
adoption of electric vehicle charging solutions by allowing the Company to design, own, install, and
maintain electric vehicle charging infrastructure from behind the customer’s meter, up to, but not
including the electric vehicle charger. The program requires participating customers to install at least six
(6) Level 2 Chargers (“L2”) or one (1) Direct Current Fast Charger (“DCFC"). Chargers can be dual port if at
least six parking spaces are dedicated to ET charging.

During the first half of 2023, Georgia Power worked with Commission Public Interest Advocacy Staff
(“Staff”) to update the Make Ready Program based on new program requirements that were established
in the Company’s 2022 rate case. The program was relaunched in May 2023 when these updates were
finalized. At the beginning of 2023, there were more than 100 projects from the 2019 rate case cycle still
under construction and almost 400 customers waiting to participate in the program, pending the
outcome of the 2022 rate case. During the first quarter of 2023, Georgia Power reviewed waitlisted
projects to determine whether they met program requirements established by the 2022 rate case. Only
projects meeting all requirements of the 2022 rate case were eligible to participate in the program,
resulting in the cancellation of 237 applications.

In 2023, Georgia Power saw a significant decrease in the number of Make Ready Program applications as
compared to prior years. In 2021 and 2022, the multi- family segment was the most active of the
customer segments, making up approximately 30% of the applications submitted. Due to the new 2022
rate case requirement related to public facing projects, few multi-family properties met program
requirements. The retail segment is now the most active customer segment, accounting for 42% of
applications submitted. Overall, Georgia Power received 162 applications in 2023 compared to 468 in
2022. These applications all move at various speeds through each stage of the process. Much depends
on how far along customers are in becoming charging station owners and operators before they apply
for the program and how long it takes for their internal approval processes. The number of submitted
applications each year will always differ from the number of projects in construction and the number of
completed installs due to varying project timelines. No application is rejected or denied unless they do
not meet the program's qualifications, at which point it is cancelled.

Installation of chargers and infrastructure is a detailed and comprehensive process and like most
construction projects take time. Make Ready construction timelines depend on several factors, including
supply chain delays for the utility equipment needed to complete installations. For example, it can take
up to 12 months of lead time when ordering transformers. Switchgear has also presented some lead
time challenges. In 2023, the Company closed out 108 Make Ready projects and has carried over an
additional 78 projects under construction and expected to be completed in 2024.

In 2023, The Company booked $13,727,554 for 214 Make Ready projects. This amount includes projects
for which construction commenced prior to 2023, in addition 108 projects that were closed out in 2023,
and 78 projects that remained under construction at the end 2023. The 2023 Make Ready Capital Spend
& Charger Counts by Project chart, on pages 5-10, is included showing capital spend by location. In total,
these projects support adding 1,924 electric vehicle chargers (including a mix of L2 and DCFC) averaging
about nine chargers per location, with a cost of approximately $7,000 per charger. The projects closed
out in 2023 supported the addition of 970 chargers.

Community Charging Program

Georgia Power’s Community Charging Program is designed to provide a limited number of public-facing
fast chargers to support electric vehicle charging needs in underserved areas. In 2023, Georgia Power
implemented the program based on the new requirements established for the program in the 2022 rate
case. Consistent with these requirements, the Company submitted eleven (11) locations for approval in
the spring starting the 60-day right of first refusal process. Nine (9) locations were approved for
installation.

Georgia Power worked with property owners to obtain the rights to build and operate chargers at the
nine (9) sites, and construction on all sites began in the fall of 2023. In order to improve the customer
experience, all new community charging sites include only DCFC.

Georgia Power also performed site upgrades at existing Community Charging Program locations, which
provided an increase in the kW output of chargers and improved the driver experience. The fourteen
(14) sites upgraded included one (1) Tritium 50 kW DCFC and one (1) ChargePoint CT4020-HD-GW L2.
Due to reliability concerns of Tritium units, the Company upgraded to a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-
CHD, which is a 62.5 kW DCFC. This allowed the Company to place all Community Charging chargers on
the ChargePoint network, which provides network oversight efficiencies as well.

The Community Charging location at 4404 North Shallowford Road in Dunwoody was also upgraded with
new technology. The site consisted of a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC and a ChargePoint
CT4020-HD-GW L2. The upgrade added the EXPP-PL202x1BC-6A1S1-6A1S1 (Express Plus 2000) DCFC and
is now capable of dispensing 400 kW output to a single vehicle or split power to charge two cars
simultaneously. As the Company evaluates this charger technology, it may be considered for future
program installations.

In 2023, Georgia Power booked $3,217,023 as capital expenses attributed to thirty-three (33) different
projects, of which, $2,906,417 was for on new site construction and upgrades to the fourteen (14) sites
referenced above. The remainder was booked as carry-over billing from prior year projects, which was
necessary because construction and billing timelines do not always coincide within the same calendar
year. The net revenue associated with Community Charging stations was $986,748.

Finally, in 2023, Georgia Power transitioned to a kWh billing model from time-based billing.

ET Rebate Programs

Georgia Power’s ET Rebate Programs are designed to help reduce cost barriers to customers seeking to
adopt ET technologies. These programs are designed for both residential and business customers and
include program specific requirements.

The total rebate budget authorized in the 2022 rate case was $6,700,000, which translates to
approximately $2,250,000 per year. Each program is budgeted separately, as the additional rebate dollars
from the rate case were allocated as a Make Ready rebate.

The residential program saw significant participation, with the instant rebate offered on the Georgia
Power Marketplace being well received. The residential rebate budget was depleted by the end of July
and formally paused on August 31st. This pause allowed for time to evaluate how to fulfill all rebate
requests from customers. The Company elected to use a portion of the commercial rebate budget to
satisfy the requests from customers and fulfill these residential requests in December.

The Residential Builders Rebate, which provides a rebate to builders who install electric vehicle plugs
was not heavily used in 2023. The Business Charger Rebate was also not heavily utilized by customers, as
the Make Ready Program proved more beneficial to qualifying business customers.

The former Make Ready Rebate has been combined with the Business Charger Rebate to create just one
business rebate program now called the Business Charger Plus Rebate Program and is available to
customers that are not able to participate in the Make Ready Program. The Business Charger Plus Rebate
has two Tiers. Tier 1 replaces the former business charger rebate and Tier 2 is intended to cover more of
the infrastructure costs similar to the Make Ready Program. The Tier 2 rebate was designed to cover
approximately 20-25% of the cost of a typical Make Ready Program project. The design is based on a
dollar per kW model to ensure it passes the Rate Impact Measure (“RIM”) test. Moving forward, the old
business rebate and Make Ready Rebate, which was piloted in December to test processes. This program
will be the Business Charger Plus rebate and available to business customers as a single program.

Make Ready Program 
Georgia Power’s Make Ready Program is designed to reduce cost barriers for commercial and industrial customers seeking to install electric vehicle chargers, thus helping to address the growing demands for electric transportation (“ET”) solutions. The program allows Georgia Power to support its customers’ adoption of electric vehicle charging solutions by allowing the Company to design, own, install, and maintain electric vehicle charging infrastructure from behind the customer’s meter, up to, but not including the electric vehicle charger. The program requires participating customers to install at least six (6) Level 2 Chargers (“L2”) or one (1) Direct Current Fast Charger (“DCFC"). Chargers can be dual port if at least six parking spaces are dedicated to ET charging. 
During the first half of 2023, Georgia Power worked with Commission Public Interest Advocacy Staff (“Staff”) to update the Make Ready Program based on new program requirements that were established in the Company’s 2022 rate case. The program was relaunched in May 2023 when these updates were finalized. At the beginning of 2023, there were more than 100 projects from the 2019 rate case cycle still under construction and almost 400 customers waiting to participate in the program, pending the outcome of the 2022 rate case. During the first quarter of 2023, Georgia Power reviewed waitlisted projects to determine whether they met program requirements established by the 2022 rate case. Only projects meeting all requirements of the 2022 rate case were eligible to participate in the program, resulting in the cancellation of 237 applications. 
In 2023, Georgia Power saw a significant decrease in the number of Make Ready Program applications as compared to prior years. In 2021 and 2022, the multi- family segment was the most active of the customer segments, making up approximately 30% of the applications submitted. Due to the new 2022 rate case requirement related to public facing projects, few multi-family properties met program requirements. The retail segment is now the most active customer segment, accounting for 42% of applications submitted. Overall, Georgia Power received 162 applications in 2023 compared to 468 in 2022. These applications all move at various speeds through each stage of the process. Much depends on how far along customers are in becoming charging station owners and operators before they apply for the program and how long it takes for their internal approval processes. The number of submitted applications each year will always differ from the number of projects in construction and the number of completed installs due to varying project timelines. No application is rejected or denied unless they do not meet the program's qualifications, at which point it is cancelled.  
Installation of chargers and infrastructure is a detailed and comprehensive process and like most construction projects take time. Make Ready construction timelines depend on several factors, including supply chain delays for the utility equipment needed to complete installations. For example, it can take up to 12 months of lead time when ordering transformers. Switchgear has also presented some lead time challenges. In 2023, the Company closed out 108 Make Ready projects and has carried over an additional 78 projects under construction and expected to be completed in 2024. 
In 2023, The Company booked $13,727,554 for 214 Make Ready projects. This amount includes projects for which construction commenced prior to 2023, in addition 108 projects that were closed out in 2023, and 78 projects that remained under construction at the end 2023. The 2023 Make Ready Capital Spend & Charger Counts by Project chart, on pages 5-10, is included showing capital spend by location. In total, these projects support adding 1,924 electric vehicle chargers (including a mix of L2 and DCFC) averaging about nine chargers per location, with a cost of approximately $7,000 per charger. The projects closed out in 2023 supported the addition of 970 chargers.

Community Charging Program 
Georgia Power’s Community Charging Program is designed to provide a limited number of public-facing fast chargers to support electric vehicle charging needs in underserved areas. In 2023, Georgia Power implemented the program based on the new requirements established for the program in the 2022 rate case. Consistent with these requirements, the Company submitted eleven (11) locations for approval in the spring starting the 60-day right of first refusal process. Nine (9) locations were approved for installation. 
Georgia Power worked with property owners to obtain the rights to build and operate chargers at the nine (9) sites, and construction on all sites began in the fall of 2023. In order to improve the customer experience, all new community charging sites include only DCFC. 
Georgia Power also performed site upgrades at existing Community Charging Program locations, which provided an increase in the kW output of chargers and improved the driver experience. The fourteen (14) sites upgraded included one (1) Tritium 50 kW DCFC and one (1) ChargePoint CT4020-HD-GW L2. Due to reliability concerns of Tritium units, the Company upgraded to a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD, which is a 62.5 kW DCFC. This allowed the Company to place all Community Charging chargers on the ChargePoint network, which provides network oversight efficiencies as well. 
The Community Charging location at 4404 North Shallowford Road in Dunwoody was also upgraded with new technology. The site consisted of a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC and a ChargePoint CT4020-HD-GW L2. The upgrade added the EXPP-PL202x1BC-6A1S1-6A1S1 (Express Plus 2000) DCFC and is now capable of dispensing 400 kW output to a single vehicle or split power to charge two cars simultaneously. As the Company evaluates this charger technology, it may be considered for future program installations.
In 2023, Georgia Power booked $3,217,023 as capital expenses attributed to thirty-three (33) different projects, of which, $2,906,417 was for on new site construction and upgrades to the fourteen (14) sites referenced above. The remainder was booked as carry-over billing from prior year projects, which was necessary because construction and billing timelines do not always coincide within the same calendar year. The net revenue associated with Community Charging stations was $986,748.
Finally, in 2023, Georgia Power transitioned to a kWh billing model from time-based billing.

ET Rebate Programs 
Georgia Power’s ET Rebate Programs are designed to help reduce cost barriers to customers seeking to adopt ET technologies. These programs are designed for both residential and business customers and include program specific requirements. 
The total rebate budget authorized in the 2022 rate case was $6,700,000, which translates to approximately $2,250,000 per year. Each program is budgeted separately, as the additional rebate dollars from the rate case were allocated as a Make Ready rebate. 
The residential program saw significant participation, with the instant rebate offered on the Georgia Power Marketplace being well received. The residential rebate budget was depleted by the end of July and formally paused on August 31st. This pause allowed for time to evaluate how to fulfill all rebate requests from customers. The Company elected to use a portion of the commercial rebate budget to satisfy the requests from customers and fulfill these residential requests in December.
The Residential Builders Rebate, which provides a rebate to builders who install electric vehicle plugs was not heavily used in 2023. The Business Charger Rebate was also not heavily utilized by customers, as the Make Ready Program proved more beneficial to qualifying business customers. 
The former Make Ready Rebate has been combined with the Business Charger Rebate to create just one business rebate program now called the Business Charger Plus Rebate Program and is available to customers that are not able to participate in the Make Ready Program. The Business Charger Plus Rebate has two Tiers. Tier 1 replaces the former business charger rebate and Tier 2 is intended to cover more of the infrastructure costs similar to the Make Ready Program. The Tier 2 rebate was designed to cover approximately 20-25% of the cost of a typical Make Ready Program project. The design is based on a dollar per kW model to ensure it passes the Rate Impact Measure (“RIM”) test. Moving forward, the old business rebate and Make Ready Rebate, which was piloted in December to test processes. This program will be the Business Charger Plus rebate and available to business customers as a single program.

Make Ready Program

Georgia Power’s Make Ready Program is designed to reduce cost barriers for commercial and industrial
customers seeking to install electric vehicle chargers, thus helping to address the growing demands for
electric transportation (“ET”) solutions. The program allows Georgia Power to support its customers’
adoption of electric vehicle charging solutions by allowing the Company to design, own, install, and
maintain electric vehicle charging infrastructure from behind the customer’s meter, up to, but not
including the electric vehicle charger. The program requires participating customers to install at least six
(6) Level 2 Chargers (“L2”) or one (1) Direct Current Fast Charger (“DCFC"). Chargers can be dual port if at
least six parking spaces are dedicated to ET charging.

During the first half of 2023, Georgia Power worked with Commission Public Interest Advocacy Staff
(“Staff”) to update the Make Ready Program based on new program requirements that were established
in the Company’s 2022 rate case. The program was relaunched in May 2023 when these updates were
finalized. At the beginning of 2023, there were more than 100 projects from the 2019 rate case cycle still
under construction and almost 400 customers waiting to participate in the program, pending the
outcome of the 2022 rate case. During the first quarter of 2023, Georgia Power reviewed waitlisted
projects to determine whether they met program requirements established by the 2022 rate case. Only
projects meeting all requirements of the 2022 rate case were eligible to participate in the program,
resulting in the cancellation of 237 applications.

In 2023, Georgia Power saw a significant decrease in the number of Make Ready Program applications as
compared to prior years. In 2021 and 2022, the multi- family segment was the most active of the
customer segments, making up approximately 30% of the applications submitted. Due to the new 2022
rate case requirement related to public facing projects, few multi-family properties met program
requirements. The retail segment is now the most active customer segment, accounting for 42% of
applications submitted. Overall, Georgia Power received 162 applications in 2023 compared to 468 in
2022. These applications all move at various speeds through each stage of the process. Much depends
on how far along customers are in becoming charging station owners and operators before they apply
for the program and how long it takes for their internal approval processes. The number of submitted
applications each year will always differ from the number of projects in construction and the number of
completed installs due to varying project timelines. No application is rejected or denied unless they do
not meet the program's qualifications, at which point it is cancelled.

Installation of chargers and infrastructure is a detailed and comprehensive process and like most
construction projects take time. Make Ready construction timelines depend on several factors, including
supply chain delays for the utility equipment needed to complete installations. For example, it can take
up to 12 months of lead time when ordering transformers. Switchgear has also presented some lead
time challenges. In 2023, the Company closed out 108 Make Ready projects and has carried over an
additional 78 projects under construction and expected to be completed in 2024.

In 2023, The Company booked $13,727,554 for 214 Make Ready projects. This amount includes projects
for which construction commenced prior to 2023, in addition 108 projects that were closed out in 2023,
and 78 projects that remained under construction at the end 2023. The 2023 Make Ready Capital Spend
& Charger Counts by Project chart, on pages 5-10, is included showing capital spend by location. In total,
these projects support adding 1,924 electric vehicle chargers (including a mix of L2 and DCFC) averaging
about nine chargers per location, with a cost of approximately $7,000 per charger. The projects closed
out in 2023 supported the addition of 970 chargers.

Community Charging Program

Georgia Power’s Community Charging Program is designed to provide a limited number of public-facing
fast chargers to support electric vehicle charging needs in underserved areas. In 2023, Georgia Power
implemented the program based on the new requirements established for the program in the 2022 rate
case. Consistent with these requirements, the Company submitted eleven (11) locations for approval in
the spring starting the 60-day right of first refusal process. Nine (9) locations were approved for
installation.

Georgia Power worked with property owners to obtain the rights to build and operate chargers at the
nine (9) sites, and construction on all sites began in the fall of 2023. In order to improve the customer
experience, all new community charging sites include only DCFC.

Georgia Power also performed site upgrades at existing Community Charging Program locations, which
provided an increase in the kW output of chargers and improved the driver experience. The fourteen
(14) sites upgraded included one (1) Tritium 50 kW DCFC and one (1) ChargePoint CT4020-HD-GW L2.
Due to reliability concerns of Tritium units, the Company upgraded to a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-
CHD, which is a 62.5 kW DCFC. This allowed the Company to place all Community Charging chargers on
the ChargePoint network, which provides network oversight efficiencies as well.

The Community Charging location at 4404 North Shallowford Road in Dunwoody was also upgraded with
new technology. The site consisted of a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC and a ChargePoint
CT4020-HD-GW L2. The upgrade added the EXPP-PL202x1BC-6A1S1-6A1S1 (Express Plus 2000) DCFC and
is now capable of dispensing 400 kW output to a single vehicle or split power to charge two cars
simultaneously. As the Company evaluates this charger technology, it may be considered for future
program installations.

In 2023, Georgia Power booked $3,217,023 as capital expenses attributed to thirty-three (33) different
projects, of which, $2,906,417 was for on new site construction and upgrades to the fourteen (14) sites
referenced above. The remainder was booked as carry-over billing from prior year projects, which was
necessary because construction and billing timelines do not always coincide within the same calendar
year. The net revenue associated with Community Charging stations was $986,748.

Finally, in 2023, Georgia Power transitioned to a kWh billing model from time-based billing.

ET Rebate Programs

Georgia Power’s ET Rebate Programs are designed to help reduce cost barriers to customers seeking to
adopt ET technologies. These programs are designed for both residential and business customers and
include program specific requirements.

The total rebate budget authorized in the 2022 rate case was $6,700,000, which translates to
approximately $2,250,000 per year. Each program is budgeted separately, as the additional rebate dollars
from the rate case were allocated as a Make Ready rebate.

The residential program saw significant participation, with the instant rebate offered on the Georgia
Power Marketplace being well received. The residential rebate budget was depleted by the end of July
and formally paused on August 31st. This pause allowed for time to evaluate how to fulfill all rebate
requests from customers. The Company elected to use a portion of the commercial rebate budget to
satisfy the requests from customers and fulfill these residential requests in December.

The Residential Builders Rebate, which provides a rebate to builders who install electric vehicle plugs
was not heavily used in 2023. The Business Charger Rebate was also not heavily utilized by customers, as
the Make Ready Program proved more beneficial to qualifying business customers.

The former Make Ready Rebate has been combined with the Business Charger Rebate to create just one
business rebate program now called the Business Charger Plus Rebate Program and is available to
customers that are not able to participate in the Make Ready Program. The Business Charger Plus Rebate
has two Tiers. Tier 1 replaces the former business charger rebate and Tier 2 is intended to cover more of
the infrastructure costs similar to the Make Ready Program. The Tier 2 rebate was designed to cover
approximately 20-25% of the cost of a typical Make Ready Program project. The design is based on a
dollar per kW model to ensure it passes the Rate Impact Measure (“RIM”) test. Moving forward, the old
business rebate and Make Ready Rebate, which was piloted in December to test processes. This program
will be the Business Charger Plus rebate and available to business customers as a single program.
