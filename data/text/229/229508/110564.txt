COMES NOW the Staff of the Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) and, pursuant to the authority vested in it by the Commission pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 46-2-57, herein propounds the following interrogatories and requests for production of documents (collectively, “data requests”), to be answered under oath by designated representatives or agents of Georgia Power Company.  Staff requests that a complete set of the responses and supporting documents be filed with the Commission’s Executive Secretary in the manner set forth in Utility Rule 515-2-.04(4).  Staff requests that an original and five (5) copies be filed with the Executive Secretary of the Commission.  Accompanied therewith shall be an electronic version of the filing, which shall be made on a 3 ½ inch diskette or a CD ROM containing an electronic version of its filing in Microsoft Word® for text documents or Excel® for spreadsheets.  As contemplated by law, responses to these Data Requests are expected from Georgia Power Company on or before December 18, 2026. 


DEFINITIONS

As may be used in this document:  
1.“The Company,” “Georgia Power” or “The Utility” means Georgia Power Company and its present and former officers, employees, agents, representatives, directors, and all other persons acting or purporting to act on behalf of said Company.
The term “you” and “your” refer to “the Company.”
The term “person” means any natural person, corporation, corporate division, partnership, other unincorporated association, trust, government agency, or entity.
The term “document” or “documentation” shall have the broadest possible meaning under applicable law.  “Document” or “documentation” means every writing or record of every type and description that is in the possession, custody or control of the Company including, but not limited to, correspondence, memoranda, e-mails, drafts, workpapers, summaries, stenographic or handwritten notes, studies, notices, publications, books, pamphlets, reports, surveys, minutes or statistical compilations, computer and other electronic records or tapes or printouts, including, but not limited to, electronic mail files; and copies of such writing or records containing any commentary or notation whatsoever that does not appear in the original.  The term “document” or “documentation” further includes, by way of illustration and not limitation, schedules, progress schedules, time logs, drawings, computer disks, chart projections, time tables, summaries of other documents, minutes, surveys, work sheets, drawings, comparisons, evaluations, laboratory and testing reports, telephone call records, personal diaries, calendars, personal notebooks, personal reading files, transcripts, witness statements and indices.
The term “referring or relating to” means consisting of, containing, mentioning, suggesting, reflecting, concerning, regarding, summarizing, analyzing, discussing, involving, dealing with, emanating from, directed at, pertaining to in any way, or in any way logically or factually connected or associated with the matter discussed.
“And” and “or” as used herein shall be construed both conjunctively and disjunctively and each shall include the other whenever such construction will serve to bring within the scope of these discovery requests any information that would not otherwise be brought within their scope.
The singular as used herein shall include the plural and the masculine gender shall include the feminine and the neuter.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a person other than a natural person means to state:  the full name of the person and any names under which it conducts business; the present or last known address of the person; and, the present or last known telephone number of the person.
“Identify” or “identifying” or “identification” when used in reference to a document means to provide with respect to each document requested to be identified by these discovery requests a description of the document that is sufficient for purposes of a request to produce or a subpoena duces tecum, including the following:

Referring to STF-8-2 project 20019. Why was Georgia Power Company work moved to TEAMS Number 19248? Was 19248 a new project or an existing one? Additionally, it was noticed that the two projects are listed in different zones (ID 19248 is Zone 214 & ID 20019 is Zone 215), what is the reason for that discrepancy?

The scope of many projects (IDs 18679, 20018, 17075, 18960, and 20067) appear to have changed substantially but retained their project number. Who determines the need for a project number change? Please describe the criteria and reasons for project number changes.

Referring to STF-DEA-8-8, is project ID 19248 active or cancelled? Please explain the project status, what has changed with the project and whether any ITS member is still engaged with the project?

Refer to STF-DEA-8-11.a where the phrase “project scope may evolve and planning studies progress and system needs change”. Does this language carry the same meaning as the language in STF-DEA-8-12, where “nature and scope” is used?


Refer to STF-DEA-8-13. In the 2022 TYP, most of the projects were listed as upgrades or minor replacements. In the 2025 TYP, most of the same projects were listed as rebuilds at significantly higher cost. Based on the data provided, please answer the following questions:
How did the Company determine that these projects needed to be rebuilt rather than simply upgraded or undergo minor replacements?
Why is an upgrade no longer a viable solution, as it was in the 2022 TYP? Provide the Company’s written analysis / justification for changing scope from upgrade / minor replacement to rebuilds.
What study methods did the Company use to make these determinations?
Did the Company perform contingency and reliability assessments (including thermal overload violations), congestion analyses, maintenance outage evaluations, and cost estimates for the best options, or apply other transmission power system testing criteria? If so, please provide the results in a chart, compare them, and state the conclusions
If no such study used was listed provide all material used to make the determination to alter projects from upgrade to rebuilt.
Does the selected option address all project needs in the study area, meet NERC reliability criteria, represent the least-cost option, and constitute the only feasible solution? If it is the lowest cost option, please identify the alternative methods the Company analyzed before making its final decision.
Did the Company request approval to rebuild any of the new projects listed in the 2025 IRP? If so, please identify the projects, identify where in the Company’s 2025 IRP filing these projects’ scope were changed to rebuilds and describe the outcome of each request.
Refer to STF-DEA-8-10. In its responses, Georgia Power pointed to the transmission planning process methodologies in the 2025 IRP Technical Appendix Volume 3. That describes the general basis for how needs are identified It does not provide the specific load and generation needs that led to each 500kV project. For each project listed below, provide:
The individual needs the project was identified to address, including the specific overloads or reliability issues it resolved and the load growth or generation it creates capacity for.
The reason 500kV was selected for the project rather than other voltage levels, such as 345kV or 230kV, including any alternatives that were considered.

Refer to STF-DEA-8-9 and STF-DEA-8-15. In these responses, 27 of 255 projects are at least partially attributable to load growth and 8 of 255 projects are at least partially attributable to generation interconnection.
Please explain the criteria for being attributable to load or generation drivers. Is there a certain threshold that must be met to be designated as attributable?
What are the remaining projects from the 2025 TYP attributable to?
Refer to STF-DEA-8-8. Georgia Power was asked to identify the cancellation drivers for the 2022 and 2025 TYP. In response to this request, a table was provided for cancellation drivers for each project. Most of these cancellation drivers are listed as “NERC TPL Compliance Drivers changed”, the name of the project itself (i.e. Georgia Transmission Corporation (GTC) project), and “operating guide was used in lieu of project”. Please break down these cancellation drivers more granularly on what caused them to get cancelled using the same projects from the table.
