STF-20-1Regarding the 2027 Annual ECON-1 Rider Model, the “1. Inputs” tab, how was the $0.08 increase for the R-1 category determined? What is driving this increase?

RESPONSE:


The $0.08 increase in the R-1 rate is primarily driven by approximately $28 million of additional ECON-1 investment reflected in the 2027 rider, which increases the rate by approximately $0.05, and the impact of the overcollection existing at the end of 2025, which accounts for approximately $0.03 of the increase. The overcollection at the end of 2025 reduced the amount required to be collected from customers through the 2026 rate. Because that reduction was reflected in the 2026 rate and does not recur in 2027, it contributes approximately $0.03 to the year-over-year rate increase.
Other changes, including the full-year revenue requirement associated with ECON-1 investment placed in service during 2026, customer count, the state income tax rate, and the cost of capital, had a comparatively minor impact on the rate change.

STF-20-7Please answer the following regarding the 2027 ECON-1 Rev Req & Rate Model tab 4:

The Company added categories to Plant in Service for Distribution Land Rights and Distribution Measure/Reg Station Equip in the Q1 2026 ECON-1 Report. Regarding the annual model, please provide how these categories (in Column A) have values in the months of April 2025 – December 2025, if these categories did not exist until 2026.
Please provide why, for the months of April 2025 – December 2025, Large PI Distribution Mains (Line 13) in the Annual Rider Model have different values than what is provided in the Q2 2026 (Docket 41559, Document 227623) filed on August 14, 2026.

RESPONSE:

The Company generally budgets ECON-1 projects in FERC Account 376, Distribution Mains, because the primary scope and majority of the anticipated costs relate to main installation and replacement work.  ECON-1 projects may also include associated facilities, such as distribution land rights and the distribution measure/reg station equipment categories, when those facilities are affected by installation and/or replacement.

The related additions were included in the ECON-1 model when placed in service and were initially reflected in FERC Account 376. Accordingly, incorporating the additional FERC categories did not add previously omitted investment to the model, change the total additions, or result in duplicate recovery; it provided a more detailed classification of additions already reflected in the model.

Because ECON-1 projects are budgeted and managed at the overall project level, presenting the additions by the more detailed asset classifications required a review of the completed asset records after the related facilities were placed in service. The Company performed that review to confirm that the applicable costs were appropriately assigned and that the model classifications were consistent with the underlying Plant Accounting records. Following that validation, the additional FERC categories were incorporated into the Q1 2026 ECON-1 model.

The Annual Rider Model incorporated the re-allocation of the applicable dollars for the months of April – December 2025 from Large PI Distribution Mains to Distribution Land Rights and Distribution Measuring/Reg Stations. The Q3 2026 ECON-1 model incorporates this update as well. The total plant in service by month is the same.

STF-20-9 In the 2027 Annual Rider Model, on the Cost of Capital tab, Line 62 states “*Cost of debt as reported in the Q2, 2026 QFR GRAM Filing; 2027 GRAM Update filing will be based on these amounts.” Why was this information pulled from the quarterly GRAM and not the 2026 Annual GRAM filing? 

RESPONSE:

The GRAM Tariff (Section 26.5) requires that the cost of capital used to set GRAM rates reflect the actual cost of debt as of June 30. Because the GRAM Annual Rate Filing is due by July 1, it initially uses the first-quarter cost of debt, and the subsequent GRAM Update filing then updates the cost of debt to the second quarter (June 30) amounts. Consistent with this process, the GRAM Annual Rate Filing submitted on July 1, 2026, reflected the first-quarter 2026 cost of debt, and the forthcoming GRAM Update filing will reflect the second-quarter 2026 cost of debt. The second-quarter 2026 amounts will, therefore, serve as the basis for the cost of capital to be included in the GRAM Update filing for the 2027 Rate Effective Period. Under the ECON-1 Tariff, the Return on Capital is the overall rate of return set by the Commission for the applicable rate effective period in the Company's most recent ratemaking proceeding, which, for 2027, is the GRAM proceeding. Accordingly, the 2027 ECON-1 Rider Model uses the second-quarter 2026 cost of debt so that it aligns with the 2027 cost of capital to be included in the GRAM Update filing. This approach is consistent with the Tariff and with prior ECON-1 filings.
