$3,049,350 (through October of 2026) 

IT spending supports the broader technology environment and business capabilities needed to operate the company, including enterprise systems, infrastructure, applications, end-user technology, and other technology-enabled business initiatives. Cybersecurity spending is more specifically risk-driven and is focused on protecting those IT and OT environments, customer and company data, and the reliable delivery of energy and water from evolving cyber threats. Liberty's Cybersecurity Program is a coordinated, multi-year transformation program organized around capabilities to govern, identify, protect, detect, respond to, and recover from cyber threats. Its investments are intended to measurably improve cybersecurity maturity and reduce risk through areas such as identity and access management, network protection, data security, security monitoring, vulnerability management, incident response, resiliency, governance, and third-party risk management. In this sense, IT generally provides and enables the technology capabilities the business uses, while Cybersecurity establishes and strengthens the safeguards, controls, monitoring, and resilience needed to ensure those technologies and the information they process can be used securely and reliably. The two areas are closely integrated, and some projects may involve shared technology or infrastructure, but Cybersecurity investments are distinguished by their primary purpose of reducing cyber risk and strengthening security and resilience.

When comparing WP 2-7-2 in the 2027 GRAM to the 2026 GRAM filing, the December 2022 Additions, October 2023 Additions, and the March 2024 Additions are not the same in both. Please explain why those amounts changed from one year to the other.
RESPONSE

The December 2022, October 2023, and March 2024 additions reflected in WP 2-7-2 were updated in the 2027 GRAM to incorporate the applicable Section 174/174A tax basis adjustments associated with qualifying software costs.  

Section 174 of the Internal Revenue Code requires certain research and experimental expenditures, including qualifying software development costs, to be capitalized and amortized for federal income tax purposes rather than immediately deducted.  

As part of Liberty's updated tax review, qualifying software costs were identified for the 2022 through 2024 tax years. Because the applicable assets had already been placed in service and depreciation had commenced, Liberty updated the applicable historical addition periods using the qualifying assets net tax values as of year-end 2024.  

Accordingly, the differences between the 2026 and 2027 GRAM filings reflect updated federal tax treatment of qualifying software costs and do not represent changes to the underlying book additions
