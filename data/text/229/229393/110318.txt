Re:Georgia Power Company’s Update on Commissioner Hubbard’s Deposit and Itemized Bill Motions – Docket No. 44280.

Dear Commissioners:
At the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (“Commission”) October 1, 2026, Energy Committee meeting, Commissioner Peter Hubbard made two motions at the close of the meeting. The first motion, related to residential billing, would require Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) to provide itemized residential bills as the default billing format for all residential customers. The second motion, related to utility deposit requirements, would require the Company to offer residential customers the option to pay any assessed security deposit in twelve equal monthly installments. These deposit measures were introduced to assist customers who desire to switch from the Prepay and Pay-by-Day Programs to the traditional post pay residential rate under Residential Service Schedule “R-31” (“Rate R”). Pursuant to Energy Chair Stan Wise’s direction, parties were given 30 days to consider the matters raised in the motion and to provide the Commission with additional information. 
Georgia Power hereby respectfully files this letter to provide the Commission with additional information regarding the issues raised in the two motions and to update the Commission on actions taken by the Company to address the concerns raised by the motions.
 
Bill Redesign.

Georgia Power does not oppose having more information on the bill. Indeed, as was discussed at the October 1, 2026, Energy Committee meeting, the Company provides additional bill itemization when requested by the customer. In this regard, we share the desire to have a bill that best meets the needs of our customers and provides clear and transparent information.
However, it is important to distinguish between providing individual customers with more bill details, which the Company currently provides, with a complete redesign of the bill that will be utilized by all customers. While the former can currently be achieved relatively quickly, the latter requires significant planning and coordination. Whenever the Company undertakes to redesign the retail customer bill, the Company does so by utilizing best practices. 
The first step in any bill redesign is coordination and planning. In order to extract efficiencies and cost savings, some elements of Georgia Power billing are common across Southern Company’s retail operating companies and must be coordinated among the three operating companies. In addition to working with our retail system operating companies, Georgia Power also directly coordinates outreach to customers in focus groups and through surveys to better understand how the bill can be improved from the customer perspective and designed in a manner that provides the information that meets customers’ wants and needs. We also determine what educational tools or materials are needed to prepare customers for pending changes to their bills to avoid any confusion and to prepare them for the change. Critical to these customer interactions is ensuring that customer service representatives are proactively trained to respond to questions prior to the release of a new bill redesign. These collective actions are just a few examples of the necessary steps to ensure that any bill redesign is accepted by customers, and importantly, avoids unnecessary customer confusion or anxiety while providing the intended benefits.  
The Company is not opposed to redesigning the bill to provide more information and would like to work with the Commission in this effort. We are currently in the early stages of considering a bill redesign and happy to work with the Commission on a reasonable timeline to consider and implement any bill changes.  
Improving eligible customer experience for migrating from Prepay and Pay-by-Day to Rate R. 
The Company shares the intent of Commissioner Hubbard’s motion to ensure that customers are permitted to be served under any tariff rates for which they are eligible. This includes customers under the Prepay and Pay-by-Day Programs that may wish to migrate back to the post-pay Rate R. Commissioner Hubbard’s motion targets relaxing certain deposit requirements to achieve this end. This includes allowing deposits to be paid over twelve installments and providing deposit alternatives like letters of credit, guarantees, and automatic bank draft enrollment. 
As this Commission is aware, requiring deposits based upon a customer’s credit and payment history is a protective measure for all customers. The purpose of charging a deposit is to limit exposure to bad debt, because higher levels of bad debt increase rates for all customers. That is why bad debt is a component of a rate case and changes in deposits are typically considered in that forum. Deposits by their nature are intended to protect customers from rate increases driven by uncollectible accounts. Importantly, deposits are not intended to unduly penalize or prevent a customer from accessing tariff rates applicable to that customer. Moreover, as the Commission Rules require, the deposits are returned to the customer, with interest, after 24 months of on-time payments.
Currently, Commission Rules allow the Company to charge a customer up to two and half times the estimated charge for service at a premises for the ensuing twelve months.  However, as explained at the October 1, 2026, Energy Committee meeting, the Company currently caps residential deposits at $200. This $200 cap for residential deposits generally applies, with certain exceptions related to bankruptcy and instances where the customer has left the Company with an unpaid balance for previous accounts. In addition, the Company also allows residential customers to pay the deposit in two installment payments.
The Prepay and Pay-by Day Programs are intended to give customers an option to pay in advance of service, thereby eliminating the need for any deposit requirements. In that regard, Prepay and Pay-by-Day already serves as an alternative for customers who desire not to pay a deposit or are unable to pay a deposit. As was discussed in the 2019 rate case, and subsequently, the Prepay and Pay-by-Day Programs can give a customer more control over managing their energy usage and paying at more frequent intervals rather than just monthly. Georgia Power realizes that the Prepay and Pay-by-Day Programs are not best for every customer, but these programs do have the benefit of no deposit requirement and can be helpful when a customer may have previously been in arrears and would have difficulty staying on the standard residential rate.
The Company understands that customers who are on Prepay and Pay-by-Day may desire to migrate back to Rate R. One impediment to that migration can be a deposit requirement that exceeds the $200 cap generally applied to residential customers. In some instances, customers on Prepay and Pay-By-Day who previously left the Company with an arrears balance have not been eligible for the $200 deposit cap. Due to the arrears balance, these customers were considered a credit risk and could be subject to the deposit levels otherwise allowed by the relevant Commission Rule. Following Commissioner Hubbard’s motion, the Company has been actively evaluating ways to lessen the impact of a deposit on Prepay customers who (1) have established a consistent payment history (i.e.,12 months of on time payments and no disconnection), and (2) no longer have an arrears balance.  The Company is proposing to relieve the deposit requirements for Prepay customers wishing to migrate to Rate R that meet these two requirements. The Company believes this approach would address the stated concern in Commissioner Hubbard’s motion in a manner that is still reasonably protective of existing customers.  
As it pertains to alternative forms of deposit satisfaction, the Company is not opposed to exploring options that are feasible and protective of both the customer utilizing the option as well as all other customers. As discussed at the October 1, 2026, Energy Committee meeting, the Company believes it is important to closely study options such as letters of credit and third-party guarantees to ensure that vulnerable customers are not taken advantage of when availing themselves of these options. Moreover, alternatives like letters of credit are unlikely to be a readily available option for this purpose.  Ultimately, adding additional deposit alternatives may also lead to higher implementation costs, which would need to be evaluated prior to any deployment. 
Conclusion
In closing, Georgia Power appreciates the opportunity to provide its input on the issues raised by Commissioner Hubbard’s motions. We hope that our letter provides additional information as well as a path for the Commission and the Company to work together on these issues. Georgia Power’s Prepay and Pay-by-Day programs, deposit structure, and bill design are fundamental aspects of the Company’s business and retail residential offerings that require the time and the close engagement with the Commission, Staff, and customers to ensure that any changes are made in a manner that will be in the best interest of all customers. We hope our letter sets forth a pathway for the Company to work with the Commission on these efforts.
