RESPONSE

WP 4-1A includes a sizeable number of customers with adjustments so Liberty chose to combine those adjustments for simplicity in the schedule. As described in the response to STF 31-1, including the residential customer class provides greater accuracy and transparency to the mechanism.  

The values shown on WP 4-1-A (cell range B8:O9) represent adjustments tied to specific residential development growth projects listed on WP 2-1-2 Cap Spend. These adjustments address situations where the Company identified atypical growth in the residential housing sector that is not fully captured by usage and customer counts adjustments calculated on WP 4-1-1 Cust Growth.

By combining the residential customer usage amounts from WP 4-1-A and WP 4-1-1, the Company adjusts the historical test year billing units to reflect the expected level during the rate-effective period.

The Company has not linked calculations on WP 4-1-A in the past associated with specific individual customer adjustments but is willing to incorporate additional support work papers in the GRAM filing upon mutual agreement to do so, such that the values on WP 4-1-A can be better explored.

The Company does not agree. For the revenue month of June 2026, the billing cycles for 39,000 bills were only 27 days due to the meter reading cycles. This resulted in customer charges being prorated and only 90% of a full month’s customer charge revenue being booked, which makes our HTY ending customer bill counts lower than actual customers. This produces an unrealistic customer growth calculation for Residential and Commercial customers in WP 4-1-1 Cust Grth. To adjust the historical period to reflect the full number of customers active, the HTY amounts are being pro forma adjusted to reflect the actual number of bills in our forecasted test period. This is being done by multiplying the historic level of customers by the proration shortage amount of 10%, which removes the impact of the short reading cycle on GRAM adjustment calculations and also increases HTY revenues to the appropriate levels.

Please explain where in the Quarterly reports these additional contract rates can be traced. Why are these rates not included with the Special Contract since the new contract started in October 2025?
RESPONSE

The special contract rates established prior to Liberty's acquisition of Peach State Gas were based on contract negotiations. To calculate updated October 2025 rates, Liberty used the June 2025 approved volumetric 860 tariff rates, then applied a 50% discount. The contract customer charge prior to October 2025 was developed based on a revenue requirement for investment prior to the acquisition. An update to the net book value and depreciation schedule was used to calculate the October 2025 rates. These rates do not appear in the quarterly reports, but the Company will start to include them with the Q4 2026 filing. With regards to the rates being included, the period for the filing is July 2025 – June 2026, so there are three months where the new contract rates were not in place.
