Q.DR. BEPPLER, MR. EVANS, AND MR. LOONEY, DID YOU PRESENT DIRECT TESTIMONY ON BEHALF OF GEORGIA POWER IN THIS PROCEEDING?
A.Yes. 
Q.WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF THE PANEL’S REBUTTAL TESTIMONY?
A.Our rebuttal testimony responds to the testimony of the Public Interest Advocacy Staff (“PIA Staff”) and Georgia Interfaith Power and Light, Park Avenue Baptist Church, Temple Sinai, and Unitarian Universalist Church of Savannah (collectively, “GIPL”) related to the review of Georgia Power’s Real-Time Pricing (“RTP”) Revenue Credit and Allocation Methodology in Docket No. 57171. Our testimony focuses on several key issues without attempting to address every issue or recommendation raised in PIA Staff or Intervenor testimony. The fact that the Company may not respond to a particular position of PIA Staff or an Intervenor should not be construed as acceptance of such position, particularly given that many of those positions were acknowledged as outside the scope of this docket.
Q.PLEASE SUMMARIZE YOUR REBUTTAL TESTIMONY.
A.Our rebuttal testimony addresses certain PIA Staff and GIPL recommendations regarding the RTP Revenue Credit and Allocation Methodology. Specifically, we address PIA Staff’s three overarching guidelines, which serve as the basis for their recommended modifications to the RTP fuel credit methodology. We explain how these three guidelines, which are introduced for the first time in this proceeding as a policy recommendation, are not supported by cost allocation and ratemaking principles and should be given no weight by the Georgia Public Service Commission (the “Commission”). 
Next, our rebuttal testimony addresses the fundamental difference between PIA Staff’s and the Company’s views on treatment of firm transportation (“FT”) costs, which are fixed fuel costs. We explain that FT costs are established and fixed years in advance of operation and do not vary based on how much energy is produced. Therefore, notwithstanding the fact that FT costs are recovered as fuel costs, they are not marginal costs and should not be allocated to RTP customers on an energy basis. Our testimony also clarifies that FT is not part of the approved revenue requirement reflected in base rates and, as such, there is no possible double recovery since the Company is not charging customers for the same costs twice.   
Regarding non-dispatchable power purchase agreements (“PPAs”), we emphasize our support, alongside GIPL, for Option 1, which preserves the integrity of RTP as a marginal cost-based rate by excluding both the costs and output of non-dispatchable PPAs from the RTP fuel credit calculation. Our testimony then responds to PIA Staff’s recommended reporting requirements and modifications to the Commission’s minimum filing requirements (“MFRs”), many of which are duplicative and unnecessary. Finally, we conclude by briefly reiterating that testimony that veers into cost allocation, rate design, and larger changes to RTP components should be reviewed in the context of the Company’s next base rate case and is outside the scope of this proceeding as directed by the Commission. 
Q.HOW IS YOUR TESTIMONY ORGANIZED?
A.Our rebuttal testimony is organized as follows:
Section II addresses the guiding principles cited by PIA Staff in support of their recommended changes to the RTP Fuel Credit Methodology; 
Section III discusses the procurement, recovery, and proposed allocation of FT; 
Section IV responds to the additional cost categories that PIA Staff and Intervenors recommend be included in the RTP fuel credit calculation; 
Section V responds to the recommended changes to the reporting obligations and MFRs; 
Section VI addresses PIA Staff and Intervenor recommendations and allegations outside the scope of this proceeding; and 
Section VII concludes our rebuttal testimony.
II.RTP FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY
Q.PIA STAFF TESTIFIES THAT IT USED THREE GUIDELINES IN EVALUATING THE COMPANY’S RTP FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY. HOW DO YOU RESPOND? 
A.As explained in our direct testimony, the RTP fuel credit is based on marginal replacement fuel costs as the RTP rate reflects the marginal cost of serving incremental load. It necessarily follows that this does not incorporate certain fixed costs (including fixed fuel costs) that do not vary with changes in demand and, as a result, are not considered marginal costs. PIA Staff’s guidelines are new policy recommendations for the Commission’s consideration, and are not based on or consistent with cost-allocation best practices, previous Commission policy decisions, or any widely accepted principles, by their own admission. 
Q. ARE THE THREE GUIDELINES PIA STAFF USED IN ITS EVALUATION APPROVED BY THE COMMISSION OR REQUIRED BY LAW?
A.No. PIA Staff devised these guidelines post hoc to support its recommended changes to the RTP fuel credit methodology. PIA Staff testified during the hearing that this was the first opportunity to present these guidelines, despite the RTP fuel credit methodology being in place for 16 years and numerous fuel cases having transpired in the interim. PIA Staff’s guidelines are not found in any statute, regulation, or Commission Order, and have not been the basis for prior recommendations before the Commission. 
Q.HOW SHOULD THE COMMISSION CONSIDER PIA STAFF’S GUIDELINES?
A.The Commission should give PIA Staff’s guidelines no weight as an authority and justification for proposed modifications to the RTP fuel credit methodology. 
Q.DOES THE COMPANY HAVE ANY OVERARCHING CONCERNS WITH PIA STAFF’S RECOMMENDATIONS?
A.Yes. PIA Staff’s recommendations are not consistent with cost-causal allocation methodologies. They fail to consider that costs should be recovered based on and in proportion to the extent uniquely situated customers drive those expenditures. In seeking “consistency” across customers, they fail to acknowledge that all customers are not identical. Instead, PIA Staff allows the recovery mechanism to drive the cost allocation when rate design should follow from cost allocation as opposed to informing it. Applying PIA Staff’s guidelines broadly would have substantial and dangerous consequences beyond the fuel docket.   
PIA Staff’s recommendations endeavor to treat RTP load more like non-RTP load in how costs and revenues are allocated. However, the RTP rate is by design a marginal cost-based rate that is not intended to recover all embedded costs. This does not mean that RTP customers do not pay their fair share of system costs or cost to serve. Rather, the pricing for RTP is dynamic and allows participating customers to take on price risk to better manage how and when they use energy. Eligible commercial and industrial customers can respond to price signals and modify their usage on an hourly basis. RTP customers and non-RTP customers are not similarly situated and do not take service on similar rates by design. Efforts to make RTP cost and revenue allocations more like those for non-RTP customers would undermine the effectiveness and competitive value of RTP as a marginal cost-based rate. 
Adopting cost allocation guidelines untethered from either industry cost-allocation practices or the manner in which costs are allocated to customers today would set a dangerous policy precedent. The consequences could extend well beyond fuel cost recovery (“FCR”), materially affecting the Company’s allocation of costs and the prices ultimately paid by all customers. 
III.FIRM TRANSPORTATION
Procurement
Q.HOW DOES THE COMPANY PROCURE NATURAL GAS FT?
A.SCS, on behalf of Georgia Power, procures FT to support the reliable delivery of fuel to its gas generating fleet. These FT contracts are often executed for multiple years, and though the Company does not have an obligation to use procured FT at a specific plant, it does identify the FT needs to provide fuel assurance to units as part of the resource planning and generation procurement processes. The Company may also acquire monthly or seasonal FT to support seasonal generating fleet needs and least-cost operations. In all cases, the contract costs are based on the Company’s reserved FT capacity and do not vary with usage.
Q.HOW IS THE NEED FOR FT DETERMINED?
A.The Company determines how much FT to procure through its resource planning and generation procurement processes. Ultimately, as resource decisions are made through the Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) and the supporting RFPs, the Company enters into FT contracts to ensure that pipeline capacity is available to deliver fuel to natural gas-fired facilities. For natural gas combined cycle facilities, the amount of FT is intended to support full-load operation in all hours across the year. This allows for reliable operation of the system and economic dispatch. While FT costs can change over time, this is a response to changes in the resource plan and not a direct response to changes in energy usage. As such, FT is not dissimilar to other fixed resource costs.
Q.SHOULD FT BE CONSIDERED A VARIABLE COST?
A.No. In any traditional use of the term as it applies to utility cost allocation, FT is a fixed cost for the ability to deliver fuel. Importantly, the costs are established based on reserved pipeline capacity to ensure deliverability of gas and not based on the volume of fuel actually delivered as that is captured in the fuel commodity consumed. The Company makes long-term commitments to secure pipeline capacity based on the needs of the generating fleet. These contracts often span multiple years, and the terms do not vary day-to-day or even month-to-month. In other words, FT costs are established and fixed years in advance of operation and do not vary based on how much energy is produced by a facility. 
Q.DO GEORGIA POWER’S FT COSTS VARY AT ALL BASED ON ENERGY USAGE?
A.No. In the Company’s resource planning and generation procurement processes, the load forecast is used to establish a capacity need, the capacity need is used to justify resource additions, and resource additions can require additional FT. While this process does establish a relationship between peak demand and FT costs, this same relationship exists for all fixed costs associated with new generation resources. This relationship certainly does not suggest that FT is a variable cost or that an energy allocation, as PIA Staff and GIPL recommend, is most appropriate for cost recovery of FT expense.
Q.WHY WOULD MONTH-TO-MONTH VARIATIONS BE OBSERVED IN THE COMPANY’S FT COST?
A.At a portfolio level, the Company’s total FT cost may vary month-to-month, but this variation is based on the resource mix, not changes in demand. While the Company procures FT across all months for combined cycle facilities, the same is not always the case for simple cycle combustion turbine (“CT”) facilities. The decision to procure FT for CT facilities considers the economic availability of FT and the availability of other fuel options such as onsite fuel oil storage. This consideration often results in short-term summer-only FT procurement for CT facilities. Even in these circumstances, the FT is known and fixed over the terms of the contracts. 
Q.DOES THE REMARKETING OF FT SUGGEST THAT FT HAS A VARIABLE COST COMPONENT AS SUGGESTED BY GIPL WITNESS WILSON? 
A.Not at all. As previously discussed, FT procurement decisions are often made years in advance and are dictated by what is available in the market. As a result, FT contracts will not perfectly match the timing of the Company’s deployment of generation resources. The Company takes advantage of remarketing opportunities to align the available FT with the resource needs as resource decisions are finalized and construction schedules materialize. This is a cost-optimization exercise available in the market and not a direct response to any changes in demand or energy use. This cost optimization is solely to the benefit of customers, as any proceeds from remarketing efforts are returned to customers through the fuel clause. Similarly, the Company may remarket generation capacity, but this does not change the allocation of generation capacity costs. 
Recovery
Q.HOW DOES THE COMPANY RECOVER ITS FT COSTS?
A.FT costs are recovered through the FCR rate. 
Q.GIVEN THAT FT IS NOT RECOVERED IN BASE RATES, IS IT POSSIBLE FOR THE COMPANY TO “DOUBLE RECOVER” FT COSTS?
A.No. PIA Staff and GIPL both erroneously allege that the Company may be double recovering FT costs while, at the same time, claiming those costs may not be recovered at all. These are inherently contradictory positions, and neither is true. The Company would only be double recovering if it was charging customers for FT costs in both fuel and base rates; however, FT costs are charged to fuel and are not included in base revenue requirements. To be recovered in base rates, a cost must be included in the Commission-approved base revenue requirements. As PIA Staff admitted at the evidentiary hearing, FT costs are not included in the Company’s revenue requirements, which is what sets base rates. Therefore, there is no possibility of double recovery. 
That said, just because the full cost of FT in fuel is not directly offset by RTP fuel revenue does not mean that RTP customers are not effectively paying their share of fixed costs with the portion of RTP revenue that is allocated to base. To be clear, RTP revenue allocated to base for fixed costs (like FT) through capital substitution or other mechanisms indicates that such RTP revenue is reducing other costs recovered through base rates and effectively lowering the retail cost of service for customers in a base rate case. 
Q.DOES THE RTP RATE STRUCTURE PROVIDE ADEQUATE REVENUE TO COVER THE COST OF FT?
A.Yes, sufficient revenues are included in the rate structure to recover fixed costs, including FT, through the capital substitution component. As GIPL witness Wilson states, “Capital substitution is a core component of the peaker method and represents the contribution from lambda to the cost of more efficient generation . . . at some periods the system lambda recovers more revenues than are credited to cover fuel costs. This surplus is needed to cover the higher cost of generation that operates more frequently than peakers.” FT is precisely one of the fixed costs associated with baseload or intermediate generation that help enable those resources to deliver lower energy costs. As described previously, FT is a fixed cost for pipeline capacity that allows a facility such as a combined cycle to participate in economic dispatch. The costs associated with FT are considered during the resource planning process and the selection of the most economic resource portfolio. 
When the Company makes resource decisions, it accounts for the total cost of the candidate resources including all fixed costs such as FT. A combined cycle resource is more expensive than a peaking resource due to a combination of additional capital equipment, higher fixed operations and maintenance expenses, and the requirement for year-round FT. If the benefit of the efficiency provided by a combined cycle does not overcome these additional costs, then the Company would instead add a peaking resource. This tradeoff between higher fixed cost and higher efficiency is exactly what capital substitution reconciles. Although the word “capital” is used in the terminology, which costs are capitalized is a matter of regulatory accounting and does not impact the application of Peaker Theory to the recovery of fixed costs.
Allocation
Q.WHAT HAS PIA STAFF RECOMMENDED FOR THE ALLOCATION OF NEW AND EXISTING FT?
A.PIA Staff now recommends that the Company recover new and existing FT on a megawatt-hour (“MWh”) or energy basis. That recommendation cannot be reconciled with PIA Staff’s own FCR-27 testimony filed just five months ago, in which it allocated FT using a 1-CP allocator, a demand-based method. PIA Staff has since stated the prior testimony was just illustrative, but they had the information available to provide an energy-based illustration, which undermines this claim. GIPL’s position has proven equally unstable: it recommended a 4-CP allocator, also demand-based, in its testimony one month ago, yet appeared to abandon that position at the evidentiary hearing in favor of an energy basis. In other words, until very recently, both parties treated FT as a demand-related cost, which is precisely the Company’s position. PIA Staff’s sole justification for its energy allocator is that the Company recovers FT costs from non-RTP load on an energy basis in fuel rates. That reasoning allows the existing pricing structure to dictate cost allocation, rather than first determining cost causation and then designing rates to recover those costs. The fact that the FCR price is expressed as a ¢/kWh charge does not, by itself, make the underlying costs energy related. If that logic were adopted more broadly, cost studies would become circular and irrelevant in Commission proceedings, with existing rate design dictating cost allocation rather than cost allocation informing rate design.
Such abrupt changes by both PIA Staff and GIPL over such a short period are telling. Positions that shift from month to month are not grounded in cost causation and offer no reliable foundation for an allocation methodology. If PIA Staff and GIPL do not consistently support their own recommendations, the Commission should not adopt them either.
Q.WHY HAS GEORGIA POWER PROPOSED TO MOVE REVENUES ASSOCIATED WITH NEW FT COSTS DRIVEN BY LARGE LOAD GROWTH TO FUEL? 
A.In light of concerns raised by PIA Staff and other parties in the FCR proceeding and at the technical conference, the Company proposes to distinguish new FT (for any such new FT driven primarily by large load growth) from existing FT (which is not driven by large load growth). As we testified at the direct hearing, there is an opportunity to identify resources and related FT costs that have been procured since the start of the 2023 IRP Update as specifically “new FT” costs. The Company proposes to move revenues from base to fuel for further assurance and transparency that new FT costs attributable to large load customers are fully recovered from those customers in the FCR clause and do not impact non-large load customers. 
Q.WHY IS AN ENERGY ALLOCATION NOT APPROPRIATE FOR FT?
A.In contrast to PIA Staff, the Company is theoretically opposed to allocating a fixed cost (i.e., FT among others in this case) to a marginal cost-based rate on an energy basis when the cost does not vary or fluctuate based on energy usage. As such, energy is not an appropriate allocation factor for FT because FT is fixed, driven by peak demand, and should be treated similarly to other large infrastructure expenses (generation, transmission, distribution) and allocated on a demand basis. FT is procured in advance at a fixed cost and should not be confused with or compared to the fuel commodity use, which does vary based on usage. FT costs capture the fixed cost for the capacity reservation, which should not be allocated based on an energy basis. 
Q.IS A DEMAND-BASED ALLOCATION OF NEW FT, AS GEORGIA POWER HAS RECOMMENDED, CONSISTENT WITH RECENT COMMISSION ACTIVITY?
 A.Yes. The Commission has implemented industry-leading measures to protect existing customers from any cost shifting associated with adding new large load customers. Among other considerations, these measures protect existing customers if proposed large load projects fail to materialize as projected. The Company’s proposed demand-based allocation for new FT ensures that the RTP revenues booked to fuel will cover the large load causation of the FT cost independent of project materialization since the allocation will be fixed based on the planning assumptions that drove the need. PIA Staff’s recommended energy-based allocation for new FT does not provide this level of customer protection and is inconsistent with the Commission’s approach to customer protection.
IV.ADDITIONAL COST CATEGORIES
Q.DID YOUR DIRECT TESTIMONY ADDRESS THE MAJOR COST CATEGORIES IDENTIFIED BY PIA STAFF OR OTHER INTERVENORS? 
A.Yes. The Company considered PIA Staff and other Intervenor recommendations, which led to the Company’s proposed modifications for new FT and storage costs, as discussed above, and to non-dispatchable PPAs. Through these recommendations, and according to PIA Staff’s own testimony, the Company has proposed solutions to address “[t]he two most significant cost items excluded from the RTP Fuel Credit Methodology calculation,” which account for 98% of the cost impacts.
As it pertains to non-dispatchable PPAs, the Company presented two options: 
Option 1 - exclude both the costs and the output (MWh) from non-dispatchable PPAs in the RTP fuel credit calculation. 
Option 2 - include both the costs and the output (MWh) from non-dispatchable PPAs in the RTP fuel credit calculation. 
The Company and GIPL recommend the Commission approve Option 1. GAM and PIA Staff agree that Option 1 will increase the amount of RTP revenues credited to fuel.
Q.WHY SHOULD THE COMMISSION APPROVE OPTION 1?
A.Option 1 preserves the integrity of RTP as a marginal cost-based rate and is consistent with the foundational principle that the RTP fuel credit should be derived from marginal replacement fuel costs, consistent with marginal cost recovery theory. PIA Staff’s preferred option (Option 2) would be a departure from the marginal fuel cost-based approach approved by the Commission and in place since 2010.
Q.IN WHAT WAYS IS OPTION 1 BEST ALIGNED WITH A MARGINAL COST-BASED RATE LIKE RTP?
A.There are several ways in which Option 1 is best aligned with a marginal cost-based rate. As noted by GIPL, “incremental RTP load should be assigned the cost of resources that may be dispatched in response to price-sensitive demand.” This clearly excludes non-dispatchable PPAs. Similarly, when RTP load does respond to the price signal, the non-dispatchable PPA cost is unable to be avoided by that response; thus, it should not be included in the credit given for demand response or the cost for taking service. Lastly, the benefits of non-dispatchable PPAs, which deliver energy at a fixed price, accrue to FCR customers by reducing average fuel costs when the fixed price is below the marginal fuel cost. RTP load pays the marginal fuel cost and does not directly benefit from the fixed price feature of the non-dispatchable PPA.
Q.HOW DOES THE COMPANY RESPOND TO THE ADDITIONAL CATEGORIES OF COSTS THAT PIA STAFF AND INTERVENORS RECOMMEND BE RECOVERED THROUGH THE FUEL CREDIT CALCULATION?
A.The Company maintains its position advocated for in our direct testimony. In its Table 1, PIA Staff presents a list of cost categories that it recommends be included in the RTP fuel credit based on its three guidelines, which are policy recommendations. As we have testified, there are cost allocation and RTP rate design principles that run counter to PIA Staff’s policy recommendations. As such, the Commission should decline to adopt PIA Staff’s recommendations presented in Table 1 and instead preserve the integrity and structure of the RTP rate design, as proposed by the Company in its direct testimony. 
V.MONTHLY REPORTING REQUIREMENTS AND MFR MODIFICATIONS
Q.PLEASE BRIEFLY DESCRIBE PIA STAFF’S RTP AND FT REPORTING RECOMMENDATIONS.
A.PIA Staff recommends the Company’s monthly fuel report filed in Docket No. 3382 include a new separate line item in the Retail Fuel Revenues section showing RTP sales (in MWh) and RTP revenues, which should be accompanied by a corresponding separate report to show the calculation of RTP revenues credited to fuel revenues, starting no later than January 1, 2027. PIA Staff also recommends Georgia Power provide monthly fuel reporting on FT and natural gas storage costs incurred, new contracts signed, and updated status on the Company’s efforts to remarket excess gas capacity.
Q.HOW DOES GEORGIA POWER RESPOND?
A.The Company does not believe that PIA Staff’s recommended monthly reporting and MFR modifications are needed and, in some instances, they are not feasible in the requested timeframe. First, as noted by PIA Staff in its testimony, the Company already files monthly FCR Over/Under Reports in Docket No. 3382. These reports contain details on the actual expenses, revenues, MWh sales, and regulatory adjustments. PIA Staff now recommends adding RTP sales and revenues to this monthly report, which only increases the administrative burden on the Company to process and report on a significant amount of data in a short amount of time after month end and close of the billing cycle. This information is also already available to PIA Staff upon request, which PIA Staff’s testimony illustrates, noting that the requested monthly FT reporting information should be provided “consistent with the Company’s response to STF-JKA-3-6.” As such, it is not necessary to implement additional reporting requirements on the Company.
Q.DOES PIA STAFF MAKE ADDITIONAL RECOMMENDATIONS WITH REGARD TO THE COMMISSION’S MFRS?
A.Yes. PIA Staff proposes modifications to the historic and projected MFRs in future FCR proceedings requesting Georgia Power report on costs currently excluded from the RTP fuel credit consistent with the Company’s response to STF-JKA-3-6 in this docket, and a modified historic MFR requiring the Company provide support for its marginal pricing and coal inventory management decisions.
Q.PLEASE RESPOND.
A.PIA Staff’s recommended modifications to the historic and projected MFRs are not only unnecessary but also duplicative. To start, PIA Staff recommends “continued monitoring and additional reporting related to the omitted cost categories” in future FCR proceedings. This recommendation ignores the fact that the Company already provides a significant portion of this requested information through the current MFRs. For example, historic and projected hedging gains/losses are already provided in multiple MFRs such as MFRH-2/MFRH-10 and MFRP-1/MFRP-6.1. In addition, the Company does not project certain information in its fuel budget such as rail car shortfall/liquidated damages costs or gains and losses on sale of natural gas, among other items. Where such information is available but not filed through one of the Company’s MFRs, PIA Staff is free to request such information through the discovery process, as it has demonstrated it is capable of doing. Accordingly, the Commission should decline to adopt PIA Staff’s recommended MFR modifications in this proceeding. 
VI.RECOMMENDATIONS OUTSIDE THE SCOPE OF THIS PROCEEDING
Q.SHOULD THE COMMISSION CONSIDER RECOMMENDATIONS OUTSIDE THE SCOPE OF THIS PROCEEDING IN ITS DECISION ON PROPOSED MODIFICATIONS TO THE RTP FUEL CREDIT METHODOLOGY?
A.No. The Stipulating Parties, confirmed by the Commission’s Final Order in Docket No. 56765 and the Procedural and Scheduling Order in this case, were clear as to the scope of the Commission’s continued review of the RTP Fuel Credit Methodology. The current proceeding is appropriately limited to the accounting treatment of the RTP customer revenues between base and fuel. Intervenor testimony that strays into cost allocation, rate design, and changes to the RTP components are changes the Commission can and should review in the context of the Company’s next base rate case. 
VII.CONCLUSION
Q.DOES THIS CONCLUDE YOUR REBUTTAL TESTIMONY? 
A.Yes.
