STF-JKA-5-7

Question: 

Refer to Georgia Power’s Response to STF-JKA-2-4(f) in Docket No. 57171, [1-CP]
Please provide the costs in each category by month covering historic period FCR-26, FCR_27, and projected period FCR-27.  Identify which categories have increased in cost between FCR-26 and FCR-27, and by how much.
Please describe how procurement decisions are made for each of the cost categories. For example, when and how are decisions made to procure rail car leases or incremental FT?  Does the Company rely on projected load and projected unit dispatch when determining coal take minimums?
For each procurement decision, identify what load forecast was or would be the basis at the time of decision.
Provide each such existing load forecast in spreadsheet format, separating our components of load growth split by customer class, and further split by RTP, organic data center, and large load demand.
To the extent not provided in the answer to the prior question, provide all load forecasts filed with the Commission beginning with the 2022 IRP, with the same components broken out as in the above question.

Response:

Please see STF-JKA-5-7 Attachment A for FCR-26 historic period costs, FCR-27 historic period costs, and FCR-27 projected period costs with the increasing cost categories identified. Please note, Income Qualified Discount (“IQD”) expenses prior to October 2020 are not available due to system limitations, and Corporate Overhead and Labor expenses prior to January 2022 are not available due to Enterprise Foundations conversion of accounting systems. In addition, the Company does not budget for natural gas (gains)/losses on resales, coal quality penalties and premiums, or rail car shortfall/liquidated damages costs.

For each of the applicable cost categories, please see responses below for details on procurement process:
Natural gas firm transportation – firm transportation (“FT”) is contracted to support the addition of potential combined cycles (Company-owned or PPAs) to support needs in capacity RFPs and to replace expiring contracts. The related volumes are based on the Company’s Fuel Policy to support the reliable operation of the resources to meet system demand. The timing of such purchases is dependent upon the availability of new FT from the pipeline companies, whichmay not always perfectly align with schedule of RFPs to address the needs of the system. The Company optimizes the coordination of both processes and works with pipeline companies to best align these processes.
Natural gas storage – natural gas storage is contracted on a system basis to make sure there is enough storage to support system reliability and daily pipeline balancing requirements. For combined cycles with an FT path that does not access gas storage, fuel oil backup replaces the reliability component of gas storage.
Natural gas hedging gains/losses – not applicable.
Natural gas gains/losses on resales – not applicable.
Coal quality penalties and premiums – not applicable. 
Coal rail car leases – Coal railcar lease decisions are based on projected load forecast and coal delivery requirements, including projected unit dispatch, existing fuel and transportation commitments, plant inventory targets, railroad cycle times, fleet capacity, lease expirations, and an appropriate level of spare cars needed to support reliable operations. As railcar leases approach expiration, the Company reassesses its fleet needs and evaluates available alternatives, including renewal, replacement, partial renewal, or fleet reduction, while periodically market-testing expiring leases through ongoing market participation and discussions with qualified lessors. Lease proposals are evaluated on their overall value, considering factors such as lease rates, term, escalation provisions, equipment availability and condition, maintenance responsibilities, return requirements, and operational flexibility. The selected lease structure reflects the best overall balance of total cost, reliability, and flexibility based on the forecasts, market information, and operating conditions available at the time of the decision, ensuring the Company can maintain a reliable fuel supply to meet its generation needs.
Coal rail car shortfall/liquidated damages costs – not applicable.
Start-up costs for Purchased Power Agreements (PPAs) – applicable per each PPA contract term.
Energy costs associated with non-dispatchable PPAs (Solar, Wind, Biomass, and Qualifying Facilities) – decisions to procure the resources are made as part of Commission-approved RFPs or under PURPA but not on a daily basis as these resources provide energy to the Company based on their performance and are not part of dispatch decisions. 
FCR over/under-balance carrying costs – not applicable.
Income Qualified Discount (IQD) - not applicable.
Non-subscription renewables program expenses – not applicable.
Corporate overhead and labor - not applicable.

As described above in the response to subpart (b), many of the cost categories are not based on a particular load forecast. Please see below for the load forecast used to support procurement of FT.
Natural gas firm transportation – the FT for the combined cycles certified in the 2025 Certification Order for the 2029-2031 All-Source Capacity RFP and Supplemental Resources in Docket Nos. 56298 and 56310 was primarily supported by load forecast in the 2023 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) Update in Docket No. 55378. The response to PIA-3-39 filed in Docket No. 56298 (the 2029-2031 All-Source RFP Certification) discusses the relevant contracts and notes the execution date. The decision was also supported by the many large load sensitivities the Company was evaluating during the large load increases in 2023 as was discussed during the Commission’s 2023 IRP Update proceedings as well as the Budget 2026 load forecast as filed in the 2029-2031 All-Source Resource Certification proceeding. Please see STF-JKA Attachment B for the 2023 IRP Update load forecast (Docket No. 55378).
