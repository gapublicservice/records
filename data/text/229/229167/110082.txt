GEORGIA PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION STAFF’S RESPONSE TO RESPONDENT
                VOLTAGRID LLC’S MOTION TO DISMISS

       Comes now, Georgia Public Service Commission Staff (hereinafter “Staff”), through their

undersigned counsel of record and make the following response to Respondent Voltagrid LLC’s

September 16, 2026 Motion to Dismiss. Pursuant to the August 24, 2026 “Assignment to Hearing

Officer” by the Georgia Public Service Commission (hereinafter “the Commission”) and the

Hearing Officer’s “Notice of Hearing and Scheduling Order,” resolution of the issues raised in

Respondent’s Motion should be deferred until after the hearing ordered by the Commission to take

place on December 9, 2026.

                                       BACKGROUND

       The Petitioners, Sustainable Newton, a Georgia non-profit organization, Sara and Randall

Vinson, and Theodosia and William Wade, filed a “Petition for Expedited Declaratory Judgment

and Enforcement against Voltagrid” with the Commission on July 13, 2026. In their petition, the

Petitioners allege generally that Respondent Voltagrid is constructing a 90-megawatt power plant

in Covington, Georgia with the intent to provide electrical service to an adjacent data center to be

owned an operated by a separate business entity commonly referred to as “Serverfarm.” The

Petitioners contend that the provision of such electrical service violates the Georgia Territorial Act,

O.C.G.A. § 46-3-1 et seq., under which the right to provide electrical service is assigned to utility

companies according to territorial boundaries established by the Commission. See Pet. For Exp.

Decl. Judg. And Enforcement at ¶¶ 23-25. The Petitioners also claim that the provision of electrical

service to Serverfarm would violate the Georgia Cogeneration and Distribution Act of 2001,

O.C.G.A. § 46-3-51 et seq. For relief, the Petitioners request that the Commission “(a) Determine

and declare whether Voltagrid, LLC is planning to construct and operate a power plant to furnish

retail electric service in violation of the Territorial Act,” and “(b) Order that Voltagrid, LLC cease

and desist any operations in violation of the Territorial Act or Cogeneration Act.” Pet. For Exp.

Decl. Judg. And Enforcement at 12.

        On August 24, 2026, the Commission assigned the Petition to Hearing Officer, Chris

Collado, ordering, “The hearing officer will issue a Procedural and Scheduling Order setting the

matter down for hearing on the issues set forth in the Petition and on whether Respondent is

providing service that is subject to the jurisdiction of the Commission and, if so, what if any action

the Commission should take….” (emphasis added). Accordingly, the same day, August 24, 2026,

Hearing Officer Chris Collado issued a “Notice of Hearing and Scheduling Order” setting the

matter down for a hearing on December 9, 2026. The Hearing Officer also ordered that the

following issues be addressed at the hearing: “(1) Whether the Commission has jurisdiction over

Voltagrid as an electric supplier” under O.C.G.A. § 46-3-3(3), (2) Whether Petitioners are

“interested parties” entitled to initiate their Petition, and (3) Whether Voltagrid’s planned

construction and operation of the proposed generating facility, and delivery of the resulting energy

to the data center Serverfarm violates O.C.G.A. §§ 46-3-1 through 15 (the “Territorial Act”) and/or

§§ 46-3-50 though 56 (the “Cogeneration Act”).”

        On September 16, 2026, Voltagrid filed a “Motion to Dismiss.” The Motion is not clear as

to whether Voltagrid is seeking merely to remove the Petitioners from the proceedings in this

docket, or whether Voltagrid is seeking the full resolution of the issues identified by the Hearing

Officer in the Notice of Hearing and Scheduling Order. In its Brief in Support of the Motion to

Dismiss, Voltagrid challenges whether Petitioners are “interested parties” under the Territorial Act,

such that they have standing to petition the Commission for relief under the Territorial Act. Brief

in Support of Mot. to Dismiss, at 8-14. In its Brief, Voltagrid also argues at length that its provision

of services to Serverfarm is not in violation of the Territorial Act, stating, “Because Voltagrid does

not furnish service to the public, the Commission has no jurisdiction over it, and it is not an ‘electric

supplier’ for the purposes of the Territorial Act.” Brief in Support of Mot. to Dismiss, at 17.

                                            DISCUSSION

        The Hearing Officer has already scheduled a hearing in this matter for December 9, 2026,

as to all of the issues argued in Voltagrid’s Motion to Dismiss. The Commission has ordered the

Hearing Officer to determine whether Volatagrid is “providing service that is subject to the

jurisdiction of the Commission” regardless of whether the Petitioners are “interested parties” under

the Territorial Act or otherwise have standing under the Territorial and Cogeneration Acts, and

Commission Rules, to ask the Commission for the relief requested in the Petition. Dismissing the

Petition or the Petitioners from this docket before the scheduled hearing will not serve the

Commission’s objective of timely and efficiently resolving the issue of whether Voltagrid’s

Covington project falls under the Commission’s jurisdiction or is subject to the Territorial Act.

                                        CONCLUSION

       Wherefore, Staff requests that the Hearing Officer reserve making a decision as to

Voltagrid’s Motion to Dismiss until after the December 9, 2026 hearing in this matter.
