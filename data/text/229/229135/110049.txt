                 RESPONSE IN OPPOSITION TO MOTION TO STRIKE

       Georgia Interfaith Power & Light, Park Avenue Baptist Church, Temple Sinai, and

Unitarian Universalist Church of Savannah (together, Respondents), oppose Georgia Power’s

motion to strike as “irrelevant” and “outside the scope” portions of the pre-filed testimony of John

D. Wilson. Mr. Wilson speaks to issues raised by Georgia Power’s testimony and to the central

questions before the Commission: which fuel costs caused by RTP customers should be paid by

RTP customers, including large load data centers, and how would changes to the current approach

affect Georgia Power’s customers. Georgia Power asks the Commission to strike:

           •   Supporting rationales for Mr. Wilson’s recommended changes to the
               RTP fuel credit.
           •   Mr. Wilson’s response to Georgia Power’s own pre-filed testimony,
               including direct quotations of Georgia Power’s testimony.
           •   Recommended tools and next steps for implementation and evaluation
               of changes to the RTP fuel credit.

As explained below, the entirety of Mr. Wilson’s testimony falls well within the scope of this

proceeding and is relevant to the issues identified in the Procedural and Scheduling Order

(PSO). The Commission should deny Georgia Power’s motion.

                                             Standard of Review

        Georgia Power bears the burden to establish that the challenged testimony is irrelevant and

beyond the scope; it cannot carry this burden. Relevance is a broad concept and accordingly

“relevant evidence” is defined by state law as “evidence having any tendency to make the existence

of any fact that is of consequence to the determination of the action more probable or less probable

than it would be without the evidence.”1

        The Commission is not bound by strict technical rules of evidence and per state law “may

exercise such discretion as will facilitate its efforts to ascertain the facts bearing upon the right and

justice of the matters before it.”2 As the Commission itself has recognized, when “confronted with

objections to testimony or evidence, the Commission has always erred on the side of

admissibility.”3

        The PSO identifies the issues at hand in this proceeding: reviewing “the methodology by

which [Georgia Power] credits Real-Time Pricing (‘RTP’) Revenue to the FCR revenues and the

allocations [Georgia Power] is making under such methodology.”4 The Commission may make

modifications if appropriate. And “implementation of such modifications, if any, shall be credited

to fuel revenues as determined by the Commission, and any corresponding change to base revenues

shall be recorded in a regulatory asset or liability and addressed in the next base rate case.”5 Mr.

Wilson’s testimony makes recommendations for such modifications and discusses implementation

and ongoing considerations for the Commission to weigh.

                                               Argument

        Respondents do not dispute the scope of the proceeding, only what is relevant for the

Commission’s consideration. Georgia Power opened the door to much of the testimony it now

seeks to strike. Georgia Power presented testimony about the sufficiency of the revenue it is

collecting from customers on RTP rates and the impact that changes to the RTP fuel credit may

have in the base rate case: And now, Georgia Power asks the Commission to find testimony and

recommendations responding to its own conclusory assertions irrelevant.

        Other challenged aspects of Mr. Wilson’s testimony relate to policy considerations for the

Commission to weigh and explanations of the potential rate case impacts of recommended changes

to the RTP fuel credit. All of this testimony is “relevant” for the Commission to consider and

within the scope of this proceeding.

        As described below, there are several categories of statements that Georgia Power seeks

to strike. The first discusses nuances in Mr. Wilson’s recommendations for the RTP fuel credit

that are contingent upon an understanding of how Georgia Power calculates RTP pricing inputs.

The second category responds directly to Georgia Power’s own pre-filed direct testimony; in

fact, Georgia Power asks the Commission to strike direct quotations of its own pre-filed direct

testimony. The third relates to recommendations for tools that the Commission should consider

as it implements changes to the fuel credit.

   I.      Testimony relating to allocation of “fixed” fuel costs is relevant and within the
           scope.

        This proceeding is evaluating which fuel costs caused by RTP customers should be paid

by those customers under the RTP fuel credit. Yet Georgia Power seeks to strike portions of Mr.

Wilson’s testimony that provide support for part of his recommendation about the RTP fuel credit.

Georgia Power’s inputs to RTP price components and describes the impact that RTP pricing
