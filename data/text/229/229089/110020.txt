12   Q:   PLEASE DESCRIBE HOW SUSTAINABLE NEWTON OPERATES AS AN

13        ORGANIZATION.

14   A:   Sustainable Newton is a membership-based organization comprised of dues-paying

15        members. The organization is run entirely by volunteers. There are approximately 40-50

16        annual, dues-paying members. Sustainable Newton also regularly communicates by e-mail

17        with a few hundred individuals to raise awareness of the issues in their community.

18        Sustainable Newton has six board members. As a board, we meet six times a year, with all

19        members welcome at every meeting. Both board and non-board members collaborate on

20        various committees to advocate for and implement local environmental solutions through

21        partnerships with residents, businesses, and government officials.

 1        Sustainable Newton’s work happens largely in committees, which are the places where

 2        priorities of the organization are identified, and next steps for transforming concerns into

 3        positive action are developed. Currently, we have four working committees: (1) Clean

 4        Energy & Efficiency, (2) Waste Reduction & Diversion, (3) Clean Transportation, and (4)

 5        Environmental Hazards.

 6   Q:   PLEASE PROVIDE AN OVERVIEW OF EACH OF THE FOUR COMMITTEES.

 7        First, the Clean Energy & Efficiency Committee helps Newton County households,

 8        businesses, and local governments work towards cleaner, more affordable energy by

 9        expanding access to solar power, supporting home and building electrification, and

10        maximizing energy-efficiency opportunities. Sustainable Newton offers advice, coaching,

11        and resources to help residents and organizations take advantage of federal, state, and

12        private-sector incentives that reduce the cost of solar, electrification, and energy-saving

13        upgrades. Sustainable Newton is a champion of solar energy as a cleaner, more

14        affordable alternative to fossil fuels and an effective way to relieve the energy cost

15        burden faced by many households in our community. For example, during our Solarize

16        Newton-Morgan program, we signed up 16 households and installed 85 kW of solar

17        capacity. In addition, through this committee, Sustainable Newton partners with

18        Groundswell on their SOUL City of Covington & South Fulton County, Georgia program

19        in order to assist low-income households in the City of Covington apply for and receive

20        Home Energy Rebates administered by the Georgia Environmental Finance Authority

21        (GEFA). Corporate donations will be used to fill any cost gaps so that upgrades are zero-

22        cost for homeowners. The program focuses on insulation, weatherization, and high-

23        efficiency electric heat pumps to continue working towards energy efficiency.

 1   Second, the Waste Reduction & Diversion Committee focuses on promoting a zero-waste

 3   educational campaigns and community and municipal partnerships. Sustainable Newton

14   also advocate for improvements to community infrastructure for active transportation

15   mode like walking, biking, and e-mobility.

 3   Q:   PLEASE       SUMMARIZE          SUSTAINABLE          NEWTON’S          HARMS       FROM

 4        VOLTAGRID’S PLANNED OPERATIONS.

 5   A:   Sustainable Newton has several concerns about VoltaGrid. First, VoltaGrid is planning to

 6        operate a significant power plant in our community without any oversight from the Public

 7        Service Commission or elected officials in the City of Covington, which means that we

 8        do not have a publicly accountable body to whom we can raise our concerns, other than

 9        by bringing this Petition. Second, Sustainable Newton is very concerned about the harm

10        VoltaGrid’s powerplant will have on our members and on our natural environment.

11        Third, VoltaGrid is following different rules for energy providers that we understood

12        applied in Georgia, and if VoltaGrid’s behavior is illegal, it should be stopped and forced

13        to follow the rules.

18        members and followers across our broader communications channels about the potential

19        impacts so that they could engage both on their own and collectively through Sustainable

20        Newton. If there was any opportunity to get involved publicly, Sustainable Newton

21        would have engaged to ensure the interests and needs of our community are heard and

22        accounted for in the regulatory process.

 1   Q:   YOU MENTIONED THAT YOU ARE CONCERNED ABOUT THE HARM

 2        VOLTAGRID WILL HAVE ON YOUR MEMBERS AND THE NATURAL

 3        ENVIRONMENT. COULD YOU EXPLAIN THIS FURTHER?

 4   A:   Yes. Sustainable Newton and its members are concerned about a number of impacts.

 5        VoltaGrid is a power plant that is planning to operate in the midst of our community. Our

 6        members drive by its prominent site regularly, will breathe the pollution it emits, will

 7        hear the noise it will create, and are impacted by the localized and broader environmental

 8        impacts it will have. VoltaGrid’s plan for 24/7 operations will result in 24/7 noise, light,

 9        heat, and air pollution. VoltaGrid’s location is on an industrial block, but it is also in

10        close proximity to wildlife sanctuaries, neighborhoods, one of our primary rivers, and the

11        Newton County reservoir and water treatment plant. Hazelbrand Road, where VoltaGrid

12        is being built, connects multiple large residential neighborhoods in east Newton County

13        to downtown Covington. As a result, the community is not only concerned about

14        VoltaGrid’s impact on the natural environment and its obtrusive presence in the

15        neighborhood, but the community is also worried about how its light and noise pollution

16        may have downstream impacts on the wildlife and natural resources surrounding

17        VoltaGrid.

18

20        Environmental Law Center in submitting comments to the Environmental Protection

21        Division on the sufficiency of the air permit protections in place from air pollution.

22        Sustainable Newton wrote the Environmental Protection Division separately to express

23        our community’s deep concern about the cumulative impacts of the numerous industrial

12        ENERGY RULES THAN OTHER ENERGY PROVIDERS. CAN YOU EXPLAIN

13        THIS HARM FURTHER?

14   A:   Yes. We were surprised to learn of VoltaGrid’s proposed arrangements with its customer,

15        Serverfarm, because we believed Georgia’s energy laws prohibited those kinds of

16        arrangements. Widespread perception about what is and is not permissible under

17        Georgia’s energy laws has limited the kind of projects that we have been able to help

18        develop during our advocacy. VoltaGrid’s seeming failure to follow the same rules runs

19        contrary to our missions to promote clean energy and to ensure that our community is

20        protected from pollution and other hazards.

 1   Q:   HOW HAVE GEORGIA’S ENERGY LAWS IMPACTED SUSTAINABLE

 2        NEWTON’S SOLAR DEVELOPMENT?

 3   A:   In August 2022, Sustainable Newton submitted a Letter of Interest to develop a grant

 4        proposal for the Ray C. Anderson Foundation under the Drawdown Georgia Climate

 5        Solutions & Equity Grant program that would bring solar to low-income communities

 6        within the City of Covington. We wanted to select the Fowler Court apartments for this

 7        project, but we learned from the City of Covington that our proposed single solar project

 8        could not deliver energy to individually metered apartments. To serve the Fowler Court

 9        apartments, we explored options with the Covington Housing Authority to convert the

10        community to a single meter such that the grant could be used to build a solar microgrid

11        to serve all the apartments. However, we were informed that Georgia’s energy laws did

12        not allow a third, non-utility party to provide energy in that way, and we would not be

13        able to retain control or operate the solar panels for the different metered apartments.

14        Ultimately, Sustainable Newton was not invited by the funders to submit a full

15        application because of the barriers to apply the proceeds to the Fowler Court apartments,

16        due to Georgia’s energy laws.

17

18        A similar situation occurred when Meta granted Sustainable Newton $50,500 to install

19        rooftop solar for a local non-profit. During our 2023 search for a suitable non-profit

20        recipient, Sustainable Newton sought to have the funds go towards a community

21        nonprofit that would install a rooftop solar project to serve disadvantaged portions of the

22        community. We again faced similar challenges with Georgia energy laws that would not

23        allow us to build and operate a community solar microgrid without utility company


                           Maurice Carter, on behalf of Sustainable Newton
                          Georgia Public Service Commission, Docket No. 57243
                                                 Page 10

 1        involvement. So, as a result, we were limited in our options. We ultimately used the grant

 2        to fund a 21 kW rooftop solar system for McIntosh Trail Early Childhood Development

 3        Council. However, we would have preferred to have more options to leverage the funds

 4        into a larger, more impactful result.

 5

 6        VoltaGrid is attempting to operate behind the meter in a way that proliferates harmful

 7        fossil fuels in our community and in a way that we believe violates the law. If

 8        VoltaGrid’s operations are unlawful, we want them to be enforced. If VoltaGrid’s

 9        operations are ultimately determined to be lawful, Sustainable Newton would greatly

10        benefit from clarity in the law so that its own efforts to support clean energy projects are

11        not as constrained in the future.
