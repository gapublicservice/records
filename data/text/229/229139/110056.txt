STF-PIA-45-2

Question: 

Has the Company performed any analysis related to increasing or decreasing the 10% trigger? If so, please provide the analysis and results.

Response:

The Company analyzed the Budget Billing recalculation threshold beginning in April of 2026 to evaluate the impact of changing the 10% trigger and $5 minimum threshold on customer deferred balances. The analysis showed two key findings:
Lowering the percentage threshold increased recalculation frequency and lowered the average deferred balance. Raising the percentage threshold reduced recalculation frequency and increased the average deferred balance.
Raising the minimum dollar threshold generally reduced recalculation frequency, though it had less impact on deferred balances than the percentage threshold. 

The results show that reducing the percentage and increasing the minimum dollar threshold could lower deferred balances while maintaining a similar number of recalculations. The recalculation counts include both anniversary and threshold triggered recalculations. A summary of the average number of recalculations per year and the average deferred balance change for each combination of recalculation and dollar thresholds is below. The full analysis is provided in STF-PIA-45-2 Attachment.

STF-PIA-45-7

Question: 

Has Georgia Power compared customer bill volatility on budget bill vs other rate structures such as (R, REO, RD)? If so, please provide that information to the Commission.

Response:

Georgia Power has not conducted an analysis comparing customer bill volatility on Budget Billing. Budget Billing is designed to reduce fluctuations in customer bills by calculating the monthly payment using a rolling 12‑month average of charges rather than a single month's usage.
