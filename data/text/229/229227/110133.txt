Background and Overview
The Georgia Public Service Commission’s (“GPSC” or “Commission”) Order Adopting Settlement Agreement as Modified in Docket No. 44280, dated December 30, 2022, adopted a Settlement Agreement among (1) Georgia Power Company (“GPC” or “Company”), (2) Public Interest Advocacy Staff  (“Staff”), (3) City of Atlanta, (4) Commercial Group, (5) Georgia Industrial Group (“GIG”), (6) Georgia Association of Manufacturers (“GMA”), (7) Metropolitan Atlanta Transit Authority (“MARTA”), and (8) Kroger Company, which, among other terms, established a three-year Alternative Rate Plan ("ARP") with rates that became effective on January 1, 2023, pursuant to a three-year rate plan set to continue through December 31, 2025.  For Annual Surveillance Reporting (“ASR”) purposes, a provision of the Settlement Agreement established that beginning January 1, 2023, an earnings band between 9.50% and 11.90% for retail Return on Equity ("ROE") was established.  Specifically, a Settlement Agreement that was adopted by the Commission at Paragraphs 10 and 11 (pages 16-17) of the Commission’s Order stated that:
The structure of the earnings band was set aside from the Proposed Agreement for Commission decision as a policy issue.  Provision 9 provides for ASR purposes, beginning January 1, 2023, the earnings band will be set at __% to __% ROE, to be decided by the Commission, and the Company shall report earnings based on the actual historic cost of debt and capital structure described in Paragraph 2.  The Company will not file a general rate case unless its calendar year retail earnings are projected to be less than the bottom end of the band. (Agreement Provision 9)
At the December 20, 2022 Administrative session, Chair Pridemore offered a motion to set the earnings band from 9.5% to 11.9% ROE.  The motion passed 4-1.  The Commission finds this motion includes a just, reasonable and appropriate ROE earnings band that is in the public interest. 
The Stipulation stated that subsequent to finalization of Staff’s review of the respective ASR, any excess retail revenues above the top end of the earnings band will be shared, with seventy percent (70%) being applied to regulatory assets in the following priority: Accumulated CCR ARO; Retired Generating Plant; and Obsolete Inventory, ten percent (10%) being directly refunded to customers, allocated on a percentage basis to all customer groups including the base revenue contribution of RTP incremental usage, and the remaining twenty percent (20%) retained by the Company.
At the December 20, 2022 Administrative Session, Chair Pridemore offered a motion modifying the sharing mechanism offered by the Stipulation.  The motion stated that forty percent (40%) of earnings above the band would be applied to regulatory assets, forty percent (40%) would be directly refunded to customers and twenty percent (20%) would be retained by the Company.  The motion passed unanimously.  The Commission finds that this motion represents a just, reasonable and appropriate allocation of any excess retail revenues. 
Georgia Power Company submitted its 2025 ASR filing with a calculation of its earned Retail ROE of 11.34%. The Company’s ASR filing for calendar 2025 was filed pursuant to the Settlement Agreement as approved by the Commission in Docket No. 44280.  As shown on page 1 of Section 1 of the Company's 2025 ASR filing, Georgia Power Company reported a Retail ROE of 11.34% for the year ended December 31, 2025.
The Company indicated that its retail surveillance report for 2025 has been prepared in accordance with the ratemaking principles established by the Commission in Dockets 4007, 6292, 6739, 9355, 14000, 18300, 19758, 25060, 31958, 36989, 42516 and 44280.
Pursuant to the Settlement Agreement and the GPSC’s Order Adopting Settlement Agreement as Modified dated December 30, 2022, issued in Docket No. 44280, earnings above the upper limit of the 9.50% to 11.90% ROE earnings band are subject to sharing.  Specifically, earnings above the upper limit of the 9.50% to 11.90% ROE earnings band are subject to sharing, whereby 40% of earnings above the band would be applied to regulatory assets, 40% would be directly refunded to customers and the remaining 20% would be retained by the Company.  For the 12 months ending December 31, 2025, GPC determined that there were no earnings in excess of the 11.90% ROE earnings band.
The 11.34% earned ROE reported by GPC for 2025 is within the band, so there is no application of sharing of earnings for 2025.  Moreover, there are no additional adjustments to GPC’s reported 2025 earnings and operations that, if made, would result in GPC’s earnings for 2025 to be above 11.90%, the top end of the ROE earnings band.

The System Average Interruption Duration (“SAIDI”) measures the total amount of time that the average customer is without power over a given period, which is typically one year.  The System Average Interruption Frequency (“SAIFI”) measures the average number of times that a customer experiences a sustained power outage over a given period, which also one year typically.  The Customer Average Interruption Duration Index (“CAIDI”) measures the average time (in minutes or hours) it takes to restore power to a customer after an outage begins.
Staff requested that the Company provide its SAIDI, SAIFI and CAIDI information for calendar years 2023, 2024 and 2025.  GPC provided the requested information in its responses to STF-ASR-10-10, STF-ASR-10-11 and STF-ASR-10-13, which is summarized in the table below:

Higher SAIDI, SAIFI and CAIDI figures represent worse performance.  Lower reported figures represent an improvement in performance.  As shown in the above table, while the Company’s CAIDI performance was lower (i.e., reflects better performance) in 2025 than in 2023 and 2024.  The opposite is true as it relates to GPC’s SAIDI and SAIFI, which each show higher figures (i., e., worse performance) as both increased in 2025 compared to 2023 and 2024.  With regard to the increases in 2025 for the Company’s SAIDI and SAIFI metrics, in its responses to STF-ASR-10-10 and STF-ASR-10-11, the Company stated:
While the Company has observed improvements in underlying system performance, year-over-year SAIDI (and SAIFI) results can be influenced by differences in how major event days are identified and excluded under the IEEE 1366.2.5-Beta methodology.  In 2025, fewer major event days were excluded relative to prior years, resulting in a greater number of outage days being included in reported performance.  Additionally, 2025 experienced a higher number of moderate (“mid”) threat level days and fewer “low” threat days compared to 2023 and 2024, indicating more consistently adverse operating conditions.  As a result, more days with elevated outage activity were included in the calculation, which contributed to higher reported SAIDI (and SAIFI) values despite continued system improvements.  

The Company has further communicated to Staff that Staff’s assessment of the 2025 SAIDI and SAIFI results do not fully reflect the effects of the major event exclusion methodology used in reliability reporting. Georgia Power calculates these metrics using the publicly available IEEE 1366.2.5 Beta Method, which is designed to identify and exclude qualifying major event days from normal reliability performance reporting. Variations in storm activity and the treatment of major event days can have a meaningful impact on year-over-year results, making direct comparisons challenging. When viewed in the context of the number of severe and less severe weather impacts, the 2024 and 2025 metrics are more comparable than the reported values alone suggest and would provide a different perspective on underlying system reliability.

As discussed above, the Company’s actual distribution and transmission vegetation spending was substantially higher than the budgeted amounts for 2025, although these budget to actual variances are not as high as they were in the 2024 ASR.   In addition, the Company’s Distribution and Transmission related backlogs were zero as of January 1 and December 31, 2025.  These backlogs had been eliminated as of the end of 2024 and continued to be zero through December 31, 2025.  Staff recommend that GPC adhere to its commitment to continue to maintain its distribution and transmission vegetation management schedules to avoid amassing substantial distribution and/or transmission vegetation backlogs.  Staff recommend that the Company identify and report to Staff on ways in which the Company’s SAIDI and SAIFI performance can be improved, on or before April 30, 2027.

Uncertain Tax Positions
Staff inquired as to whether GPC had any uncertain tax positions as of January 1, 2025, or December 31, 2025.  In its response to STF-ASR-9-26, the Company stated that it recorded a $72 million uncertain tax position in December 2025 for Internal Revenue Code (“IRC”) Section 45U zero-emission nuclear power Production Tax Credits (“PTC”) generated for the 2025 tax year.  The Company plans to reverse the uncertain tax position upon IRS approval, which GPC expects to receive later in 2026.  In its response to STF-ASR-10-7(a), the Company stated that since the $72 million of nuclear PTCs was recorded in December 2025, it was not claimed on GPC’s 2024 federal income tax return.  The treatment for 45U nuclear PTCs generated in 2024 and 2025 were previously addressed in Docket No. 44280 when the Commission approved the Stipulation to Extend the Alternate Rate Plan (“ARP Extension Order”). The Company further updated the Commission in the Company’s Storm Damage Recovery Application in the same Docket regarding the pending approval of the 45U PTCs generated in 2025. As approved in the Commission’s Order Adopting Stipulation in Georgia Power Company’s Storm Damage Recovery Application in Docket 44280 (“Storm Damage Recovery Order”), the 45U PTCs generated in 2024 and 2025 were partially utilized to offset storm damage cost recovery.
The Company is currently in the process of preparing its 2025 federal income tax return, which it anticipates filing in the fall of 2026.  As to whether any portion of the $72 million nuclear PTC reduced federal income tax expense in 2025, the Company stated that it recorded an offsetting uncertain tax position that fully offset the estimated credit benefit, which resulted in a net zero impact to 2025 federal income tax expense.  The response to STF-ASR-10-7(c) states that the entries for the 2025 45U tax credit benefit and related uncertain tax position offset to zero net impact are reflected within the Federal Income Taxes Payable line item in Section 3, page 2 of the 2025 ASR.
Staff requested that GPC explain why it believes the $72 million of nuclear PTCs are considered uncertain.  In its response to STF-ASR-10-7(g), the Company stated:
Internal Revenue Code (IRC) Section 45U was enacted as part of the Inflation Reduction Act to provide an annual PTC for electricity generated from existing nuclear power plants that are not advanced nuclear facilities for the period from 2024 through 2032. However, the credit is subject to a phase-down through a “reduction amount,” which is calculated, in part, based on the “gross receipts” from electricity produced by the facility. As gross receipts increase, the amount of the credit decreases. At this time, the IRS has not issued a definitive interpretation of “gross receipts” for purposes of this calculation. 

The Company calculated gross receipts using a market-based methodology. This approach utilizes hourly electricity sales to unrelated third parties and the hourly system lambda (market price indicator) to determine gross receipts for electricity generated by the nuclear facilities operating in the hour. Until this methodology is reviewed and approved by the IRS CAP team, the 2025 45U nuclear PTCs will be considered uncertain. 
In its response to STF-ASR-10-7(e), GPC stated that the uncertain tax position was recorded because the estimated tax credits need to be approved by the IRS and that upon IRS approval, the uncertain tax position will be reversed.  The Company expects the IRS to approve the $72 million of nuclear PTCs by the end of 2026, following GPC’s receipt of an acceptance letter of the 2025 federal income tax return by the IRS Compliance Assurance Process (“CAP”) team.  In its response to STF-ASR-10-7(f), if the IRS approves the $72 million of nuclear PTCs, the Company will account for them in accordance with the Commission Order in GPC’s storm damage recovery application in Docket No. 44280 whereby GPC will amortize the nuclear PTCs ratably from June 2026 through May 2027.
As it relates to GPC’s 2024 federal income tax return, Staff asked the Company whether it filed either Schedule UTP to report uncertain tax positions or Form 3115 to report accounting changes.  In its response to STF-ASR-12-1(a), the Company stated:
The Company did not file Schedule UTP with its 2024 federal income tax return because the Company did not have any uncertain tax positions required to be disclosed on Schedule UTP. As part of Southern Company's participation in the Internal Revenue Service’s (“IRS”) Compliance Assurance Process (“CAP”), significant federal income tax issues are generally identified, reviewed, and resolved with the IRS on a real-time basis before the federal income tax return is filed. As a result, the Company did not have any unresolved federal income tax positions for the 2024 tax year at the time the 2024 tax return was filed (in the fall of 2025) that required disclosure on Schedule UTP.
The response to STF-ASR-12-1 also stated that the Company did not file Form 3115 with its 2024 federal income tax return, nor does the Company plan to file either Schedule UTP or Form 3115 with its 2025 federal income tax return as GPC expects to resolve any uncertain tax positions with the IRS during its CAP review which will occur prior to the Company filing its 2025 federal tax return in the fall of 2026. 
Recommendation
As noted above, none of the $72 million nuclear PTC reduced federal income tax expense in 2025 as the Company recorded an offsetting uncertain tax position that fully offset the estimated credit benefit, resulting in a net zero impact to 2025 federal income tax expense.    
The Company has recently notified Staff that the IRS will approve the $72 million of nuclear PTCs by the end of 2026.  Upon IRS approval, the Company states that it will account for the $72 million of nuclear PTCs in accordance with the Commission’s ARP Extension Order and Storm Damage Recovery Order.

For transactions lacking segment data, reconcilers reviewed other sources such as payables, payroll, and journal entries to determine the correct category. Through the review of these sources, any identifiable transactions were re-categorized and/or new categories were added as reflected in the 2024 ASR. Any unidentified transactions that could not be tied to specific projects, in the amount of $27 thousand, were written off to expense in March 2025.

Plant Vogtle Property Tax Over-Assessment
On July 15, 2026, it was reported by the WRDW news organization in Augusta, Georgia, that the Georgia Department of Revenue (“GDOR”) lowered the taxable value of the Company’s Plant Vogtle generating plant, located in Burke County, by approximately $2.8 billion.  On June 29, 2026, the GDOR notified the Burke County Tax Assessor that the county had overstated the Plant Vogtle’s taxable value by $7.7 billion in 2023, by $6.4 billion in 2024 and by $2.7 billion in 2025.  The WRDW news article indicated that GPC will not seek changes for prior years, but that the 2025 reduction in assessed value will impact the Company’s upcoming budget.  In addition, the WRDW news article included the following statement from GPC:
Georgia Power recently identified an error in our property tax filings with the Georgia Department of Revenue related to Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4.

Specifically, the Company found an error in the calculation that was made in the years surrounding the transition between pre-operation and operation of the new units, where the Company reported the value of assets in both ‘in service’ as well as ‘construction work in progress,’ resulting in an overstatement of assets reported in Burke County.

We will not seek a refund for past overpayments, but must correct this reporting moving forward.  We understand and apologize for the difficult position and inconvenience this has created for Burke County.

Georgia Power remains deeply committed to Burke County.  Beyond property taxes, our operations generate billions of dollars in local sales tax revenue, support hundreds of jobs, and provide significant investment in regional community initiatives.

Staff requested that GPC confirm whether the Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 taxable values were overstated by $7.7 billion in 2023, $6.4 billion in 2024 and $2.7 billion in 2025 (as stated in the WRDW new article).  In its response to STF-ASR-13-1(c), the Company stated:
The amounts reported in the cited news article are historical capital costs that were duplicated as both CWIP and Plant in Service costs for Plant Vogtle Units 3 & 4 for property tax valuation purposes.  While the general ledger balance was correct, the duplicate costs were included in the capital project balances used to determine taxable costs for the property tax calculation of Plant Vogtle Units 3 & 4.

Concerning the impact on property tax expense in each of those years, the Company stated that:
The Company is assessed as a single unit for all property in the state, and the GDOR has stated the unit valuations for tax years 2024 and 2025 are reasonable.  Therefore, the total 2024 and 2025 property tax returns (corresponding with taxable amounts as of December 31, 2023, and December 31, 2024, respectively) are final and will not be adjusted.
  
As noted in the Company’s statement in the above passage, GPC will not seek a refund for the past overpayment of property taxes.  The Company elaborated on this in its response to STF-ASR-13-1(f), whereby GPC stated that, based on conversations it had with the GDOR, there are no mechanisms for refunds of prior year property taxes for centrally assessed properties.  In addition, the Company stated that it is assessed as a single unit for all property in Georgia, and that the GDOR has determined that the 2024 and 2025 unit valuations are reasonable.  Consequently, no property tax assessment corrections have been made by the GDOR.  According to the response to STF-ASR-13-1(k), the Company has not received any direct communication from the Burke County Tax Assessor concerning the overstatements of Plant Vogtle’s taxable value for tax years 2023 through 2026.
In terms of the impact the 2025 reduction to Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 taxable value will have on 2025 and 2026 property tax expense, in its response to STF-ASR-13-1(h), the Company stated that property tax expense for 2024 and 2025 will not be impacted because, as noted above, no changes have been made by the GDOR.  
As for 2026, GPC stated that property tax is being recorded in accordance with the correct Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 taxable values.  In addition, the unit value has yet to be determined by the GDOR, which the Company anticipates will be established in late 2026.  Specifically, in response to STF-ASR-13-1(a), the Company stated:
The Georgia Department of Revenue (“GDOR”) has not yet issued its 2026 property tax assessment.  The Company filed its 2026 property tax return indicating a reduction in the taxable value for Burke County as compared to the 2025 return, and the GDOR communicated the upcoming change to Burke County.  The GDOR has not changed any prior year assessments. 

As it relates to the Company’s methodology for determining the fair market value of Plant Vogtle for property tax purposes, GPC’s response to STF-ASR-13-1(m), states as follows:
The Company is centrally assessed by the GDOR as a unit for all of the Company’s property located in Georgia.  In determining the fair market value of the Company’s property as a whole, the GDOR applies standard property tax appraisal methodologies, including the income approach, cost approach, and sales or market approach.  The GDOR has discretion to determine the weighting assigned to each valuation approach, the applicable capitalization rates, and other factors that may affect the final unit value.  Because there are typically no true sales of comparable properties, the cost and income approaches are generally the primary approaches used by both GDOR and the Company.

Counties do not value utility property.  It is handled at the state level by GDOR since it is centrally assessed.

With regard to whether the Company has had any correspondence with the Burke County Tax Assessor from 2022 through 2026 pertaining to Plant Vogtle’s valuation (i.e., notices of assessment, digest corrections, objection, appeals, etc.) the Company stated it is not aware of any direct correspondence, other than the annual assessment notices it receives from Burke County. 
In response to the Staff’s review of this issue, the Company has further stated that the Georgia Department of Revenue (GDOR) is aware of the matter and believes that 2024 and 2025 property tax assessments on a total Company basis were calculated using the correct overall unit value and that the property taxes assessed, billed, and paid were consistent with the GDOR’s final assessments. Because tax years 2024 and 2025 are closed, GPC cannot independently amend the returns or adjust property taxes owed absent a decision by the GDOR to reopen those tax years. 
Recommendation
Based on the foregoing, it appears that GPC will not be requesting any reimbursements for the Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 property taxes that were over-assessed for the years 2023, 2024 and 2025.  Staff would have concerns if GPC’s decision to not seek refunds of over-assessed Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 property taxes are based on attempts to maintain a good corporate image and to build and retain goodwill with Burke County instead of a reasoned assessment that no refunds of overpaid property taxes would be achievable if those were to be diligently pursued by the Company. In Staff’s view, the Company has a duty to ratepayers to not overpay property tax expense and to pursue refunds of over-assessed and over-paid property taxes, whenever those are identified, particularly where the amounts are material.  The Staff would have concerns if a decision to forego pursuing and obtaining refunds for the Vogtle property taxes that were over-assessed and overpaid was based on maintaining corporate goodwill, rather than on an assessment that over-assessed property taxes were not subject to refund.  To the extent the decision to not seek a refund of Vogtle property taxes was based upon goodwill, Staff would recommend that the property tax expense amounts that were overpaid based on the erroneous assessments be reclassified from FERC account 408.1, Taxes Other Than Income to a charitable donations account (i.e., a FERC 426 subaccount), where it would be reflected as a “below-the-line” expense.  To address Staff’s concerns, the Company has reiterated that the tax years are final and that GPC owes the property taxes based on the amount issued by the GDOR's assessments. The Company further explained that the property taxes paid by the Company do not represent an optional payment or charitable contribution but are a legally required payment. Staff is not recommending any adjustments due to the Plant Vogtle Property Tax Over-Assessment.
Conclusion
Staff finds that the Company’s earned ROE of 11.34% for 2025 is within the earnings band.  No adjustments not already made by the Company have been identified by Staff which would put GPC’s earnings for 2025 above 11.90%, the top end of the earnings band.  Staff have made the following recommendations as a result of their review of the 2025 ASR: 
Staff recommend that the Company continue to address land held in the PHFFU account by having quarterly meetings to re-evaluate the intended uses of the land and to determine if and when it will be used.  Staff recommend that the Company maintain documentation of those quarterly PHFFU reviews.  If it is determined that a parcel of land will not be used 15 years since its acquisition date, the Company will be required to present the specific plan for the property(s) in the respected IRP or base rate proceeding. The Commission will decide the matter in that proceeding.
Staff is not recommending an adjustment to the Vegetation Management expense included in the 2025 ASR.  The Company’s actual transmission and distribution vegetation spending was over the budgeted amounts for 2024.    In addition, both the Company’s Distribution and Transmission related backlogs were zero as of December 31, 2025.  Staff recommend that GPC adhere to its commitment to maintain its distribution and transmission vegetation management schedules in an efficient manner so as to avoid amassing substantial distribution and/or transmission vegetation backlogs in the future.
Staff recommend that the Company identify and report to Staff, actions that could be undertaken by the Company to improve its 2025 reported SAIDI and SAIFI performance. The Company does not object to reporting to Staff regarding actions to improve SAIDI and SAIFI performance. As the Company previously explained, the 2025 SAIDI and SAIFI results do not fully reflect the effects of the major event exclusion methodology used in reliability reporting. When viewed in the context of the number of severe and less severe weather impacts, the 2024 and 2025 metrics are more comparable than the reported values alone suggest and would provide a different perspective on underlying system reliability.
Staff recommend that the Company continue to monitor its regulatory asset deferral accounts to ensure that the accelerated amortization of regulatory assets is prioritized in the order as described in the Order Adopting Settlement Agreement as Modified in Docket No. 44280.
Staff is not recommending any adjustments to the 2025 ASR related to large load customers.  However, Staff will continue to evaluate each new large load contract to ensure that sufficient protections for existing residential ratepayers are functioning and remain in place.  Staff will also be monitoring GPC’s addition of large load customers, such as data centers, to its system, and the related incremental costs and revenues in terms of how those large customer additions are, or are not, fulfilling the expectation that such load growth will be putting downward pressure on residential rates.
The Company has recently notified Staff that the IRS will approve the 45U PTCs by the end of 2026.  Upon IRS approval, the Company states that it will account for the 45U PTCs in accordance with the Commission’s ARP Extension Order and Storm Damage Recovery Order.
During 2025, GPC recorded costs totaling $315.209 million that relate to ash pond closures and remediation totaling $294.292 million and recorded costs related to landfill closures and remediation totaling $20.917.  In addition, the Company recorded $6.2 million of capital costs (in CWIP, thus not included in retail rate base), $620,033 for cost of removal and $10.7 million in O&M costs in 2025 related to CCRs.  Staff is not recommending any adjustments at this time to the CCR ARO costs included in the 2025 ASR.  However, Staff will continue to monitor these costs.
With regard to PS&I costs included in the 2025 ASR rate base, the Company’s PS&I amounts increased by approximately 110% from 2024 to 2025 for which GPC provided explanations for the largest increases by PS&I category.  Staff is not recommending any adjustments to 2025 rate base for PS&I amounts and will continue to monitor and investigate significant fluctuations in PS&I amounts.
To the extent that GPC’s decisions to forego pursuing and obtaining refunds for the Vogtle property taxes that were over-assessed in years 2023, 2024 and 2025, were based on corporate goodwill building considerations rather than on conclusions that refunds of overpaid property taxes could not be legally pursued and achieved, Staff recommends that the property tax expense amounts that were overpaid based on the erroneous assessments be reclassified from FERC account 408.1, Taxes Other Than Income to a charitable donations account (i.e., a FERC 426 subaccount), where it would be reflected as a “below-the-line” expense.  Staff notes that this reclassification for 2025 ASR purposes would likely not result in increasing GPC’s earned ROE for 2025 above the 11.90% top end of the band.  This recommendation is being made to bring this issue to the Commission’s attention and to encourage the Company to pursue reductions in property tax expense where reasonably available. The Staff does note that Company has stated that the tax years are final and that Georgia Power owes the property taxes based on the amount issued by the GDOR's assessments. The Company further explained that the property taxes paid by the Company do not represent an optional payment or charitable contribution but are a legally required payment.
