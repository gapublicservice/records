RESPONDENT, VOLTAGRID, LLC’S MOTION FOR EXPEDITED RULING ON ITS MOTION TO DISMISS AND TO EXTEND DIRECT TESTIMONY DEADLINE

Comes now, VOLTAGRID, LLC (“VoltaGrid”), Respondent in the above-styled docket, and files this MOTION FOR EXPEDITED RULING ON ITS MOTION TO DISMISS AND TO EXTEND DIRECT TESTIMONY DEADLINE, showing the Commission as follows:
On September 16, 2026, VoltaGrid filed a Motion to Dismiss seeking dismissal of Petitioners’ Petition for Expedited Declaratory Judgment and Enforcement Against VoltaGrid on jurisdictional grounds, contending (among other things) that VoltaGrid does not provide a “service to the public” because it will serve one (and only one) user. VoltaGrid tendered the affidavit of Mr. Chris Atchley, Executive Vice President of VoltaGrid, to establish the limited scope of its contract services. Mr. Atchley testified that VoltaGrid has only one customer in Georgia, Serverfarm, and is not engaged in negotiations with or pursuing any other customer in the state.  VoltaGrid also filed a Motion to Stay all proceedings in this docket until the Motion to Dismiss is decided.
On September 28, 2026, the Hearing Officer denied VoltaGrid’s Motion, holding:
However, as recited above in the Assignment, the Hearing Officer in this case is charged with more than the adjudication of Petitioners requested relief. As stated in paragraph two of the Assignment, the Commission has ordered its own investigation into “whether Respondent is providing service or offering to provide service that is subject to the jurisdiction of the Commission and, if so, what if any action the Commission should take.” VoltaGrid’s Motion to Dismiss raises three threshold questions. Two of them, whether Petitioners are “interested parties” under O.C.G.A. § 46-3-13 and whether the Commission may enforce the Cogen Act, would not dispose of the question the Commission separately referred for hearing, even if resolved in VoltaGrid’s favor. The third, whether VoltaGrid’s arrangement is subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction under O.C.G.A. § 46-2-21(b)(5), is substantially the question the Commission itself directed the Hearing Officer to hear. Because the Assignment reserves the decision of that question to the Commission on a certified record, a stay pending the Hearing Officer’s ruling on the Motion to Dismiss would not produce a final determination and would delay development of the record the Commission has directed. For these reasons, VoltaGrid’s request for a stay is DENIED. 

The Hearing Officer’s rationale in denying a stay confirms that a single threshold question could dispose of this entire docket: whether VoltaGrid’s contractual arrangement with Serverfarm is subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction under O.C.G.A. § 46-2-21(b)(5) because it constitutes “service to the public.” This question can and should be answered on the existing record, after Petitioners have an opportunity to respond, and additional testimony and a merits hearing will not aid its resolution.
The defenses raised by VoltaGrid in its Motion to Dismiss should therefore be resolved first. Those defenses are fully briefed and supporting facts are contained in Mr. Atchley’s affidavit. VoltaGrid expects Petitioners to respond to the Motion to Dismiss by October 16, 2026, consistent with the 30-day response deadline that generally applies to motions. At that point, the jurisdictional and standing issues will be poised for decision, and their resolution will “facilitate the orderly, efficient and expeditious adjudication or other resolution of this matter.” As necessary, the Hearing Officer may hold a conference to decide this preliminary matter, as specifically authorized by the Notice of Hearing entered in this case. 
I.
JURISDICTION MUST BE DETERMINED BEFORE THIS PROCEEDING CAN CONTINUE
A decision on VoltaGrid’s Motion to Dismiss should be expedited in light of the Hearing Officer’s Stay Order. The Stay Order opines that whether VoltaGrid is subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction is a question to be developed at the hearing and decided on a certified record. That reverses the order the law requires: jurisdiction is a precondition for holding a contested case hearing in the first place. A tribunal without jurisdiction is “powerless to proceed to an adjudication.” Thus, “[j]urisdiction of a court … is a matter which should be decided preliminarily, at the outset.” 
As the Stay Order acknowledges, the Motion to Dismiss presents ripe legal questions that go to whether the Commission has jurisdiction over VoltaGrid and the subject matter of this case. Those legal questions must be resolved first. No further evidence is required to rule because no material facts are in dispute, as clarified by Mr. Atchley’s affidavit. Even so, to the extent the Hearing Officer believes there are factual disputes relevant to jurisdiction, they should be specifically identified, and a preliminary hearing held to “make relevant factual findings to decide the threshold issue.” 
This procedure is specifically contemplated by the Notice of Hearing. Expedited consideration of the Motion to Dismiss promotes an efficient disposition of the proceeding and eliminates the central question before the Commission — namely, whether VoltaGrid “furnish[es] service to the public” and is thus subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction under O.C.G.A. § 46-2-21(b)(5). Furthermore, expedited consideration is consistent with the Administrative Procedure Act because it will assist in identifying the matters asserted, the issues on which evidence and argument must be presented, and the written defenses and arguments that should guide the final hearing, if any issues remain. All facts needed to determine whether VoltaGrid’s contract services to Serverfarm are covered by § 46-2-21(b)(5), are in the record.  Therefore, VoltaGrid requests that the Hearing Officer rule on the pending Motion to Dismiss before October 22,, 2026, or as soon as practicable after receiving any response from Petitioners.


II.
THE DEADLINE FOR FILING DIRECT TESTIMONY SHOULD BE EXTENDED UNTIL A RULING ISSUES ON THE MOTION TO DISMISS
Consistent with the dispositive question identified in the Stay Order, VoltaGrid’s Motion to Dismiss narrows the scope of this proceeding to one issue: whether VoltaGrid is subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction by “furnishing service to the public.” Based on the authorities cited in VoltaGrid’s brief, the Hearing Officer has sufficient factual evidence regarding VoltaGrid’s contract services to determine whether they constitute “furnishing service to the public.” Therefore, the necessity of pre-filed testimony may be eliminated on all other issues. The deadline for pre-filed direct testimony is October 9, 2026. VoltaGrid requests that deadline be extended fourteen (14) calendar days to allow for the consideration of the Motion to Dismiss. 
Granting such an extension is within the authority and discretion of the Hearing Officer and will not unduly delay these proceedings nor prejudice Petitioners. The Hearing Officer possesses authority to regulate the course of this proceeding and establish an orderly schedule that affords reasonable notice, preserves the parties’ opportunity to be heard, and permits written defenses and arguments to be distinctly presented. Here, good cause exists for VoltaGrid’s request to extend the deadline for filing direct testimony. If the factual evidence presented in support of the Motion to Dismiss shows that VoltaGrid is not “furnishing service to the public” and the Motion is granted, the dispositive issue identified in the Stay Order will be resolved; the Commission will lack jurisdiction over VoltaGrid; and the issue (whether raised by the Commission’s own investigation or the Petition) will be resolved. If the deadline for direct testimony is not extended and VoltaGrid’s Motion to Dismiss is granted, all parties must prepare direct testimony which may never be presented or may prove irrelevant.  
Petitioners will not be prejudiced or otherwise harmed if an extension is granted. The proposed extension for filing direct testimony will apply to all parties equally. All parties will benefit from a ruling on VoltaGrid’s Motion to Dismiss, which (even if denied) will narrow the issues and provide guidance for the substance of the direct testimony. The final hearing will remain scheduled for December 9, 2026. 
III.
PETITIONERS CANNOT CONTINUE TO PARTICIPATE AS PARTIES IN THIS CASE IF THEY DO NOT HAVE STANDING

The Stay Order concludes that VoltaGrid’s challenge to Petitioners’ standing “would not dispose of the question the Commission separately referred for hearing, even if resolved in VoltaGrid’s favor.” Even accepting that conclusion, the Motion to Dismiss must be decided before any testimony is filed and before any hearing. That is because its outcome will determine who the parties are, which claims remain, and in turn which issues VoltaGrid must address by testimony and exhibits and to whom it must respond. 
Georgia law is clear that “[s]tanding is a jurisdictional prerequisite to a plaintiff’s right to sue”; without it, a tribunal “lacks subject matter jurisdiction to address the merits” of a claim. Accordingly, “standing must be determined at the time at which the plaintiff’s complaint is filed in order to place an actual case or controversy within the purview of the court.” Here, Petitioners are parties to this docket only because they filed the Petition. If they are not “interested parties” entitled to invoke O.C.G.A. § 46-3-13, the Petition must be dismissed at the outset, and Petitioners will no longer be parties. 
The Motion to Dismiss therefore carries significant consequences, both practically and legally. If it is granted, Petitioners may not file testimony, examine witnesses, or otherwise participate as parties.  Any such application should be decided on its own merits, including its timeliness and whether Petitioners’ interests are already represented, and VoltaGrid reserves the right to respond. Any order admitting Petitioners should also be conditioned to confine them to the question the Hearing Officer identified. The scope of this proceeding would narrow accordingly: Petitioners’ claims under the Territorial Act and the Cogeneration Act would fall away with the Petition, leaving only the jurisdictional question.
If the Motion to Dismiss is not decided first, Petitioners will file direct and rebuttal testimony on the full range of their claims, and VoltaGrid will be forced to answer, all before the Hearing Officer determines whether Petitioners had the right to raise those claims at all. A later ruling in VoltaGrid’s favor could not undo that. Deferring a decision on the Motion to Dismiss risks turning the gatekeeping requirement of O.C.G.A. § 46-3-13 into a virtual nullity: a person who lacks standing to bring a complaint cannot keep every right of a party.  For these reasons, the Hearing Officer should rule on the Motion to Dismiss before October 22,, 2026, or as soon as practicable after receiving any response from Petitioners.

CONCLUSION
For these reasons, VoltaGrid prays that:
 Its Motion for Expedited Ruling on its Motion to Dismiss and to Extend Direct Testimony Deadline be granted;
 The Hearing Officer rule on VoltaGrid’s pending Motion to Dismiss by October 22, 2026; 
The Hearing Officer extend the deadline for filing direct testimony until VoltaGrid’s Motion to Dismiss is resolved, but in no event to extend the hearing date of December 9, 2026, set in the Notice of Hearing and Scheduling Order; and
VoltaGrid have such other and further relief as the Hearing Officer deems proper.
