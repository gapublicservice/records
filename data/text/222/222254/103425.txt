===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-DEA-4 PD/STF-DEA-4-7.docx =====
STF-DEA-4-7
Question:
Please refer to the Direct Testimony of Jeffrey R. Grubb, J. Randy Hubbert, M. Brandon Looney, Michael B. Robinson, and Francisco Valle, Section VI: Transmission and Innovative Solutions, ‘A. Strategic Transmission’, Lines 6-26, pp.41/44. Please respond to the following questions:
Please explain if the $160 million of grant funding through the DOE’S GRIP program faces any at risks of cancellation or retraction under the new administration. 
Has the Company identified any additional grant or loan opportunities to support the proposed transmission expansions beyond the DOE’s GRIP program? If so, list all federal, state, or private grant programs and any federal or state loan programs being considered. 
What specific risks are associated with the $160 million DOE GRIP grant due to administrative or policy changes? Provide contingency plans in case funding is delayed or reduced.
What are the conditions tied to the award of the $160 million of grant funding through the DOE’s GRIP program?
Is the funding through the GEFA at risk due to changes in how federal funding is being processed by the United States government? 
What percentage of the identified transmission investments in the 2025 IRP is covered by federal or state funding, and what is the expected cost impact on ratepayers? Provide cost allocation details and projections. 
Response:
Under the new administration, negotiations with the Department of Energy (“DOE”) for the Grid Resilience and Innovation Partnerships (“GRIP”) grant funding have been paused, and the Company has been told the following from the DOE: “The DOE is currently conducting a comprehensive review of ongoing financial assistance activities, and we are awaiting further guidance. We will follow up once that guidance is available.”

Since the filing of the Company’s 2025 IRP, the Company has not pursued any additional funding programs beyond those listed in the filing.

The Company’s applications for funding listed in the 2025 IRP filing primarily include funding for projects that are already included in the Company’s transmission and distribution budgets. If the DOE’s GRIP program funding is delayed or reduced, the Company will continue to proceed with all necessary projects that were included within the scope of these funding applications without funding assistance from the DOE and will eliminate any portion of the Company’s GRIP application scope that is not necessary or already included in the Company’s planned budget. The Company will continue to work with the DOE and other applicable entities through negotiations within each of the programs mentioned in the 2025 IRP filing to maximize the amount of funding that can reduce the cost of planned transmission and distribution projects to customers.

The Company is working through the negotiations of the award contract with the DOE, therefore  the conditions have not yet been defined. 

The Company is working diligently with Georgia Environmental Finance Authority (“GEFA”) to provide any requested information. GEFA is working directly with the DOE regarding funding for the GEFA-directed grant program.

Currently none of the projects identified in the 2025 IRP are covered by federal or state funding. Once the funding opportunities have been secured, any associated funds will be reflected as a benefit to the overall cost of the project.
