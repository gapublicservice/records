Make Ready Program

Georgia Power’s Make Ready Program is designed to reduce cost barriers for business customers seeking
to install electric vehicle chargers that benefit the public good, thus helping to address the growing
demands for electric transportation (“ET”) solutions. The program allows Georgia Power to support its
customers’ adoption of electric vehicle charging solutions by allowing the Company to design, own,
install, and maintain electric vehicle charging infrastructure from behind the customer’s meter, up to, but
not including the electric vehicle charger. The program requires participating customers to install at least
six (6) Level 2 Chargers (“L2”) or one (1) Direct Current Fast Charger (“DCFC"). Chargers can be dual port
if at least six parking spaces are dedicated to ET charging.

In 2024, Georgia Power saw a steady number of Make Ready Program applications as compared to 2023.
The retail segment is now the most active customer segment, accounting for 35% of applications
submitted. Overall, Georgia Power received 175 applications in 2024 compared to 162 in 2023. These
applications all move at various speeds through each stage of the process. Much depends on how far
along customers are in becoming charging station owners and operators before they apply for the
program and how long it takes for their internal approval processes. The number of submitted
applications each year will always differ from the number of projects in construction and the number of
completed installs due to varying project timelines. No application is rejected or denied unless they do
not meet the program's qualifications, at which point it is cancelled.

Installation of chargers and infrastructure is a detailed and comprehensive process and, like most
construction projects, takes time. Make Ready construction timelines depend on several factors,
including, but not limited to, supply chain delays for the utility equipment needed to complete
installations. For example, it can take up to 12 months of lead time when ordering transformers.
Switchgear has also presented some lead time challenges. In 2024, the Company closed out 76 Make
Ready projects and has carried over an additional 61 projects under construction and expected to be
completed in 2025.

In 2024, The Company booked $8,253,829 for 153 Make Ready projects. This amount includes projects
for which construction commenced prior to 2024, in addition to 76 projects that were closed out in
2024, and 61 projects that remained under construction at the end 2024. The 2024 Make Ready Capital
Spend & Charger Counts by Project chart, on pages 4-7, is included showing capital spend by location. In
total, these projects support adding 1,128 electric vehicle chargers (including a mix of L2 and DCFC)
averaging about seven chargers per location, with a cost of approximately $7,300 per charger. The
projects closed out in 2024 supported the addition of 628 chargers.

Community Charging Program

Georgia Power’s Community Charging Program is designed to support electric vehicle charging needs in
underserved areas. In 2024, Georgia Power submitted eleven (11) locations for approval in the spring
starting the 60-day right of first refusal process (ROFR). Five locations received a ROFR. The company
filed to replace those five locations and those five along with the six from the first filing were approved
for installation.

Georgia Power worked with property owners to obtain the rights to build and operate chargers at ten
(10) sites, but we were unable to successfully locate a site host for one (1) site. Construction on the sites
began in the 3rd quarter of 2024. All new community charging sites include two DC Fast Chargers (DCFC)
and no level II chargers.

Georgia Power also performed site upgrades at existing Community Charging Program locations, which
provided an increase in the kW output of chargers and improved the driver experience. Two (2) sites
were upgraded.

The Community Charging location at 4045 Marietta Highway Canton GA 30114 was upgraded with new
technology. The site consisted of a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC and a ChargePoint
CT4020-HD-GW L2 prior to the upgrade. The upgrade added two CPE280-800-NA-1A3M2-3A1M2 DCFC
chargers and is now capable of dispensing 160 kW output to a single vehicle or split power to charge two
cars simultaneously. The CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC removed from the Canton site was used to
upgrade the community charging station located at 171 LakePoint Pkwy Cartersville GA 30121 from one
(1) ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD and (1) ChargePoint CT4020-HD-GW L2 to two (2) ChargePoint
CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC chargers and the station is now capable of dispensing 125 kW output to a
single vehicle or split power to charge two cars simultaneously. The Company continues to evaluate the
program and potential upgrade sites to improve the driver experience.

In 2024, Georgia Power booked $2,159,200.89 as capital expenses attributed to eleven (11) different
projects, of which, $1,749,244.12 was for on new site construction. The remainder was booked as carry-
over billing from prior year projects, which was necessary because construction and billing timelines do
not always coincide within the same calendar year. The net revenue associated with Community
Charging stations was $2,118,928.62 for 2024 which more than doubled 2023 revenue.

ET Rebate Programs

Georgia Power’s ET Rebate Programs are designed to help reduce cost barriers to customers seeking to
adopt ET technologies. These programs are designed for both residential and business customers and
include program specific requirements.

The total rebate budget authorized in the 2022 rate case was $6,700,000, which translates to
approximately $2,250,000 per year, this included additional rebate dollars that were allocated as the
Make Ready rebate, now called Business EV Charger Plus program.

The residential program rebate moved from $250 per charger to $150 in 2024. This change was due to
the overwhelming response in 2023 which caused a pause in the rebate program. The residential rebate
continued to see a steady increase in participation. A large portion of the increase can be contributed to
the introduction of the “Instant Rebate” offered on the Georgia Power Marketplace, which makes up for
almost 48% of the residential rebates. This instant rebate is immediately applied to the cost of the
charger at check out and no additional action for getting the rebate is required.

The Residential Builders Rebate, which provides a rebate to builders who install electric vehicle plugs
continues to be under utilized as many builders are either unaware or don’t take the time to provide the
necessary documentation.

The Business Charger Rebate had a lower-than-expected participation rate, this was mainly contributed
to the application portal not being available until the 2nd quarter, which is when the official roll-out took
place. The program also had a pre-approval requirement which we eventually waived for 2024
installations to try and drive-up participation.

With the overall budget underspend, we made additional program changes in addition to waiving the
pre-approval requirement, we also started allowing installers to submit applications on customers behalf
to speed up the process

We spent the fourth quarter looking for opportunities to drive participation in the 2025 rebate program
resulting in a simplification of the program by eliminating the different rebate Tiers and upping the
spending cap per project. These changes were implemented in January of 2025.

Make Ready Program 
Georgia Power’s Make Ready Program is designed to reduce cost barriers for business customers seeking to install electric vehicle chargers that benefit the public good, thus helping to address the growing demands for electric transportation (“ET”) solutions. The program allows Georgia Power to support its customers’ adoption of electric vehicle charging solutions by allowing the Company to design, own, install, and maintain electric vehicle charging infrastructure from behind the customer’s meter, up to, but not including the electric vehicle charger. The program requires participating customers to install at least six (6) Level 2 Chargers (“L2”) or one (1) Direct Current Fast Charger (“DCFC"). Chargers can be dual port if at least six parking spaces are dedicated to ET charging. 
In 2024, Georgia Power saw a steady number of Make Ready Program applications as compared to 2023. The retail segment is now the most active customer segment, accounting for 35% of applications submitted. Overall, Georgia Power received 175 applications in 2024 compared to 162 in 2023. These applications all move at various speeds through each stage of the process. Much depends on how far along customers are in becoming charging station owners and operators before they apply for the program and how long it takes for their internal approval processes. The number of submitted applications each year will always differ from the number of projects in construction and the number of completed installs due to varying project timelines. No application is rejected or denied unless they do not meet the program's qualifications, at which point it is cancelled.  
Installation of chargers and infrastructure is a detailed and comprehensive process and, like most construction projects, takes time. Make Ready construction timelines depend on several factors, including, but not limited to, supply chain delays for the utility equipment needed to complete installations. For example, it can take up to 12 months of lead time when ordering transformers. Switchgear has also presented some lead time challenges. In 2024, the Company closed out 76 Make Ready projects and has carried over an additional 61 projects under construction and expected to be completed in 2025. 
In 2024, The Company booked $8,253,829 for 153 Make Ready projects. This amount includes projects for which construction commenced prior to 2024, in addition to 76 projects that were closed out in 2024, and 61 projects that remained under construction at the end 2024. The 2024 Make Ready Capital Spend & Charger Counts by Project chart, on pages 4-7, is included showing capital spend by location. In total, these projects support adding 1,128 electric vehicle chargers (including a mix of L2 and DCFC) averaging about seven chargers per location, with a cost of approximately $7,300 per charger. The projects closed out in 2024 supported the addition of 628 chargers.

Community Charging Program 
Georgia Power’s Community Charging Program is designed to support electric vehicle charging needs in underserved areas. ET Rebate Programs 
Georgia Power’s ET Rebate Programs are designed to help reduce cost barriers to customers seeking to adopt ET technologies. These programs are designed for both residential and business customers and include program specific requirements. 
The total rebate budget authorized in the 2022 rate case was $6,700,000, which translates to approximately $2,250,000 per year, this included additional rebate dollars that were allocated as the Make Ready rebate, now called Business EV Charger Plus program. 
The residential program rebate moved from $250 per charger to $150 in 2024.  This change was due to the overwhelming response in 2023 which caused a pause in the rebate program.  The residential rebate continued to see a steady increase in participation. A large portion of the increase can be contributed to the introduction of the “Instant Rebate” offered on the Georgia Power Marketplace, which makes up for almost 48% of the residential rebates.  This instant rebate is immediately applied to the cost of the charger at check out and no additional action for getting the rebate is required.  
The Residential Builders Rebate, which provides a rebate to builders who install electric vehicle plugs continues to be under utilized as many builders are either unaware or don’t take the time to provide the necessary documentation.
The Business Charger Rebate had a lower-than-expected participation rate, this was mainly contributed to the application portal not being available until the 2nd quarter, which is when the official roll-out took place.  The program also had a pre-approval requirement which we eventually waived for 2024 installations to try and drive-up participation.  
With the overall budget underspend, we made additional program changes in addition to waiving the pre-approval requirement, we also started allowing installers to submit applications on customers behalf to speed up the process  
We spent the fourth quarter looking for opportunities to drive participation in the 2025 rebate program resulting in a simplification of the program by eliminating the different rebate Tiers and upping the spending cap per project. These changes were implemented in January of 2025.

Make Ready Program

Georgia Power’s Make Ready Program is designed to reduce cost barriers for business customers seeking
to install electric vehicle chargers that benefit the public good, thus helping to address the growing
demands for electric transportation (“ET”) solutions. The program allows Georgia Power to support its
customers’ adoption of electric vehicle charging solutions by allowing the Company to design, own,
install, and maintain electric vehicle charging infrastructure from behind the customer’s meter, up to, but
not including the electric vehicle charger. The program requires participating customers to install at least
six (6) Level 2 Chargers (“L2”) or one (1) Direct Current Fast Charger (“DCFC"). Chargers can be dual port
if at least six parking spaces are dedicated to ET charging.

In 2024, Georgia Power saw a steady number of Make Ready Program applications as compared to 2023.
The retail segment is now the most active customer segment, accounting for 35% of applications
submitted. Overall, Georgia Power received 175 applications in 2024 compared to 162 in 2023. These
applications all move at various speeds through each stage of the process. Much depends on how far
along customers are in becoming charging station owners and operators before they apply for the
program and how long it takes for their internal approval processes. The number of submitted
applications each year will always differ from the number of projects in construction and the number of
completed installs due to varying project timelines. No application is rejected or denied unless they do
not meet the program's qualifications, at which point it is cancelled.

Installation of chargers and infrastructure is a detailed and comprehensive process and, like most
construction projects, takes time. Make Ready construction timelines depend on several factors,
including, but not limited to, supply chain delays for the utility equipment needed to complete
installations. For example, it can take up to 12 months of lead time when ordering transformers.
Switchgear has also presented some lead time challenges. In 2024, the Company closed out 76 Make
Ready projects and has carried over an additional 61 projects under construction and expected to be
completed in 2025.

In 2024, The Company booked $8,253,829 for 153 Make Ready projects. This amount includes projects
for which construction commenced prior to 2024, in addition to 76 projects that were closed out in
2024, and 61 projects that remained under construction at the end 2024. The 2024 Make Ready Capital
Spend & Charger Counts by Project chart, on pages 4-7, is included showing capital spend by location. In
total, these projects support adding 1,128 electric vehicle chargers (including a mix of L2 and DCFC)
averaging about seven chargers per location, with a cost of approximately $7,300 per charger. The
projects closed out in 2024 supported the addition of 628 chargers.

Community Charging Program

Georgia Power’s Community Charging Program is designed to support electric vehicle charging needs in
underserved areas. In 2024, Georgia Power submitted eleven (11) locations for approval in the spring
starting the 60-day right of first refusal process (ROFR). Five locations received a ROFR. The company
filed to replace those five locations and those five along with the six from the first filing were approved
for installation.

Georgia Power worked with property owners to obtain the rights to build and operate chargers at ten
(10) sites, but we were unable to successfully locate a site host for one (1) site. Construction on the sites
began in the 3rd quarter of 2024. All new community charging sites include two DC Fast Chargers (DCFC)
and no level II chargers.

Georgia Power also performed site upgrades at existing Community Charging Program locations, which
provided an increase in the kW output of chargers and improved the driver experience. Two (2) sites
were upgraded.

The Community Charging location at 4045 Marietta Highway Canton GA 30114 was upgraded with new
technology. The site consisted of a ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC and a ChargePoint
CT4020-HD-GW L2 prior to the upgrade. The upgrade added two CPE280-800-NA-1A3M2-3A1M2 DCFC
chargers and is now capable of dispensing 160 kW output to a single vehicle or split power to charge two
cars simultaneously. The CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC removed from the Canton site was used to
upgrade the community charging station located at 171 LakePoint Pkwy Cartersville GA 30121 from one
(1) ChargePoint CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD and (1) ChargePoint CT4020-HD-GW L2 to two (2) ChargePoint
CPE250C-500-CCS1-CHD DCFC chargers and the station is now capable of dispensing 125 kW output to a
single vehicle or split power to charge two cars simultaneously. The Company continues to evaluate the
program and potential upgrade sites to improve the driver experience.

In 2024, Georgia Power booked $2,159,200.89 as capital expenses attributed to eleven (11) different
projects, of which, $1,749,244.12 was for on new site construction. The remainder was booked as carry-
over billing from prior year projects, which was necessary because construction and billing timelines do
not always coincide within the same calendar year. The net revenue associated with Community
Charging stations was $2,118,928.62 for 2024 which more than doubled 2023 revenue.

ET Rebate Programs

Georgia Power’s ET Rebate Programs are designed to help reduce cost barriers to customers seeking to
adopt ET technologies. These programs are designed for both residential and business customers and
include program specific requirements.

The total rebate budget authorized in the 2022 rate case was $6,700,000, which translates to
approximately $2,250,000 per year, this included additional rebate dollars that were allocated as the
Make Ready rebate, now called Business EV Charger Plus program.

The residential program rebate moved from $250 per charger to $150 in 2024. This change was due to
the overwhelming response in 2023 which caused a pause in the rebate program. The residential rebate
continued to see a steady increase in participation. A large portion of the increase can be contributed to
the introduction of the “Instant Rebate” offered on the Georgia Power Marketplace, which makes up for
almost 48% of the residential rebates. This instant rebate is immediately applied to the cost of the
charger at check out and no additional action for getting the rebate is required.

The Residential Builders Rebate, which provides a rebate to builders who install electric vehicle plugs
continues to be under utilized as many builders are either unaware or don’t take the time to provide the
necessary documentation.

The Business Charger Rebate had a lower-than-expected participation rate, this was mainly contributed
to the application portal not being available until the 2nd quarter, which is when the official roll-out took
place. The program also had a pre-approval requirement which we eventually waived for 2024
installations to try and drive-up participation.

With the overall budget underspend, we made additional program changes in addition to waiving the
pre-approval requirement, we also started allowing installers to submit applications on customers behalf
to speed up the process

We spent the fourth quarter looking for opportunities to drive participation in the 2025 rebate program
resulting in a simplification of the program by eliminating the different rebate Tiers and upping the
spending cap per project. These changes were implemented in January of 2025.
