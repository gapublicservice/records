   Line Location
    In selecting the routes for the location of the transmission lines, the Location Committee
    considers several factors, such as existing land uses in the geographic area, existing corridors,
    existing environmental conditions, engineering practices related to the construction and
    operation of the lines, and costs associated with the construction, operation, and maintenance of
    the line.
    Several alternative construction approaches were considered by Georgia Power, which were
    rejected for the following reasons:
    • Underground Construction Option – Several factors make overhead transmission construction
      more advantageous than underground transmission lines. For example:
         •    During an outage, restoring service for underground transmission facilities requires
              more time and resources to complete.
         •    Competition for limited utility space makes underground construction much more
              complex than overhead construction.
         •    There are direct bore engineering and operational challenges to crossing under
              railroads, highways, and streams.
         •    The cost of underground construction is substantially greater than the cost of overhead
              construction.
         •    Property owners will have more restrictions on their ability to use the company’s right-
              of-way when facilities are buried underground (e.g., no vegetation other than grass, no
              paving allowed unless pre-approved by Georgia Power, etc.).
         •    Engineering, procurement, and construction durations are extremely long for
              underground facilities.
    • Co-locating in the Existing Transmission Line Corridor – GA Power strives to use existing
      corridors whenever possible, but the current corridor was deemed unviable due to the existing
      utilities and the need for additional easement space. Although we co-located new facilities
      with existing ones for most of the project, further inspection revealed that existing gas and
      transmission lines occupied the easement. Expanding it to add another transmission line would
      displace several families from their homes.

    Line Location
     In selecting the routes for the location of the transmission lines, the Location Committee
     considers several factors, such as existing land uses in the geographic area, existing corridors,
     existing environmental conditions, engineering practices related to the construction and
     operation of the lines, and costs associated with the construction, operation, and maintenance of

     rejected for the following reasons:
     • Distribution Option – Distribution facilities are facilities with an operating voltage of less than
       46kV. The distribution facilities presently in a portion of the route are not considered a
       potential solution because distribution-grade electric lines are neither designed nor able to
       transmit transmission-grade operating voltage over long distances between substations. This
       transmission line is needed to transmit power across long distances and provide transmission
       voltage to electric substations in the area.

       more advantageous than underground transmission lines. For example:
           •   During an outage, restoring service for underground transmission facilities requires
               more time and resources to complete.

               complex than overhead construction.
           •   There are direct bore engineering and operational challenges to crossing under
               railroads, highways, and streams.
           •   The cost of underground construction is substantially greater than the cost of overhead
               construction.
           •   Property owners will have more restrictions on their ability to use the company’s right-
               of-way when facilities are buried underground (e.g., no vegetation other than grass, no

             NOTICE OF INTENT TO CONSTRUCT Gravelly Creek - Midway 115 kV Transmission Line


           As part of an ongoing effort to provide reliable electric service to its customers, Georgia Power
           Company (“Georgia Power”) intends to build an electric transmission line with a design operating
           voltage of 115 kilovolts (kV), known as the Gravelly Creek – Midway 115kV Transmission Line. The
           construction of this transmission line is needed to ensure safe and reliable electric service to commercial
           and residential customers in the surrounding area.

           Georgia Power will hold a public meeting regarding this project, as required by O.C.G.A. 22-3-
           160.1, at the following time and place:

           Date/Time:      January 16, 2025, from 6:00 PM to 7:30 PM
           Location:       Sassy Studios GA
                           1642 S Broad Street
                           Commerce, GA 30529

           The purpose of the aforementioned meeting is to provide public notice of Georgia Power’s intent to
           construct an electric transmission line for which the right of eminent domain may be exercised.

           The transmission line route begins at the existing East Maysville 115kV substation and runs east along
           Twin Mill Drive before turning south toward Maysville Road (Georgia Highway #98). The line route
           then turns east just before reaching the Norfolk Southern railroad tracks, running along the northern side
           of the railroad tracks for approximately 1,000 feet before aerially crossing both the railroad tracks and
           Hwy. #98 in a southeasterly direction. The line route continues east, along the south side of Hwy. #98
           until it reaches Hardman Orchard Road. Approximately 300 feet southeast of the road intersection, the
           line route crosses Hwy. #98, the railroad tracks, and Lam Hardman Road in a northeasterly direction. The
           line route then turns southeast, following along the northern and eastern side of the railroad tracks and
           Lam Hardman Road, crossing Interstate Highway #85. Approximately 900 feet after crossing Interstate
           Hwy. #85, the line route turns south near the intersection of the railroad tracks and Gordon Farm Road,
           and aerially crosses both the railroad tracks and Gordon Farm Road in a southern direction. The line route
           continues along the east side of Gordon Farm Road for approximately 250 feet before turning southwest,
           aerially crossing Gordon Farm Road and Hwy. #98, and continuing cross-country in a southwestern
           direction for approximately 750 feet. The line route then turns south, continuing cross-country to the
           proposed Gravelly Creek 115kV substation. The transmission line route is approximately 3.9 miles in
           length, all of which is located in Jackson County, Georgia.

           It is proposed that guyed and self-supporting, steel, concrete and/or hybrid pole design structures will be
           used along the route to support the transmission line. The acquisition of new easements will be
           determined by requirements from the company’s construction, operations, maintenance, and engineering
           groups. Where required, the company will need to obtain new easements with widths approximately 30’
           along roadside and 100’ cross-country for the required right of way.

           Several alternative construction approaches were considered by Georgia Power, which were rejected for
           the following reasons:

               •   Distribution Option – Distribution facilities are facilities with an operating voltage of less than
                   46 kV. The distribution facilities presently in this portion of Jackson County are not considered a
                   potential solution because this transmission line is needed to transmit power across long distances
                   and provide transmission voltage to electric substations in the area. Distribution solutions will not
                   allow for this to occur.
               •   Underground Construction Option – Several factors make overhead transmission construction
                   more advantageous than underground transmission construction. For example:
                       o During an outage, restoring service for underground transmission facilities requires
                           more time and resources to complete.

                       o   Competition for limited utility space makes underground construction more complex
                           than overhead construction.
                      o There are direct bore engineering and operational challenges to crossing under railroads,

Question:
Please describe any coordination the Company engages in with other ITS participants related to public concerns or responses to new transmission infrastructure in impacted communities.
Response:
It is important to note that each Integrated Transmission System (“ITS”) participant individually owns discrete transmission system facilities and equipment and is responsible for constructing their new transmission infrastructure. Provisions are made to authorize another ITS participant to perform work on the requester’s facilities on an as-needed basis.  

In instances where the Company is requested to perform work on another ITS participant’s transmission facilities, extensive discussion and correspondence take place to reach agreement on the project’s scope, design, and construction. These discussions and correspondence typically involve detailed meetings, technical evaluations, and thorough documentation to ensure all parties are aligned on the objectives, timelines, and responsibilities associated with the project.  When concerns arise regarding new transmission infrastructure in an affected community, similar comprehensive coordination and communication are conducted to make every effort to mitigate the impact of the new infrastructure on the community.
