C. Strategic Projects
                    Smart Valve Power Flow Control Device Installation at Eatonton Primary Substation
                                                    TEAMS 20466
                                           Project Need Date: June 1, 2025


I. Executive Summary
The Smart Valve Installation project addresses thermal constraints on the Branch – Oasis 230kV and Eatonton
Primary – Oasis 230kV lines associated with NERC TPL-001-5 planning event contingencies. The thermal
constraints begin in 2024 and increase until the East Walton 500/230kV Area project is placed in service in
2027.

Operating guides (switching operations) could not be utilized to resolve the thermal constraints beyond 2025
due to the severity of the constraints. Installation of Smart Valve devices on the Branch – Oasis 230kV and
Eatonton Primary – Oasis 230kV lines allows for optimized control of power flow on the lines under both
normal and contingency conditions thus increasing reliability and resiliency of the transmission system in the
area as shown in Figure 4.

Additionally, the installation of the Smart Valve non-wire alternative technology will provide valuable
operational information as the Company looks to deploy similar technologies to assist with transmission
system build out associated with Generation Fleet Transition (fossil retirement and increased renewables)
along with Customer Choice projects.

This solution is the result of joint planning efforts amongst the GA ITS Participants.

STF-PIA-14-12
Question:
What benefits and metrics does Georgia Power use to evaluate ATTs and NWAs? Has Georgia Power developed a standardized benefit-cost analysis framework for evaluating ATTs and NWAs alongside other transmission investment options? 
Response:
As described in Technical Appendix Volume 3, Section A.6b, of the Company’s 2025 Integrated Resource Plan, the Company evaluates alternative solutions during its transmission planning process, including the consideration of Alternative Transmission Technologies (“ATTs”) and Non-Wires Alternatives (“NWAs”). No, there is not a standardized benefit-cost analysis framework at this time. Consistent with the selection of any preferred solution during the transmission planning process, the selection of a solution including an ATT or NWA depends on many factors including, but not limited to, cost and time before the solution is needed for North American Electric Reliability Corporation (“NERC”) compliance. As such, these solutions can be deployed to meet a temporary or long-term transmission reliability need. The benefits, metrics, and analysis vary based on the specific project need and potential solutions available to address the reliability need in the required timeframe.

Question:
Given the rising costs and extended lead times for gas turbines (CTs as well as turbines that are part of CCs) compared to when Georgia Power initiated its modeling, has Georgia Power or Southern Company already reserved any equipment for potential needs identified in the IRP?  If yes, please specify how many, and please specify if any portion of the proposed turbine fleet has not yet been reserved. How were the resource cost assumptions used in the IRP developed in light of these changing cost and lead time conditions? For transformers, please answer the same questions.
Response:
The Company is an active participant in Georgia Power’s All-Source Capacity Request for Proposals (“RFP”) for 2029-2031 and cannot disclose specific RFP-related information in this docket.
The generic expansion modeling results presented in the Resource Mix Study do not represent resource commitments but instead provide an informative roadmap for long-term planning and to support analyses. The Resource Mix Study offers insights into the optimal mix of technologies, which indicates the types of resources that may be competitive through an RFP process. The procurement outcome, including equipment needs, will ultimately be determined based on the market responses, the Company’s evaluation process, economic decision analysis, and established procurement protocols. Additionally, Georgia Power’s established relationships with equipment manufacturers enable the Company to reserve long lead time equipment, such as transformers and breakers, ensuring that the Company can manage procurement timelines effectively despite rising costs and extended lead times.
The resource cost assumptions used in the Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) were based on the annual process of developing cost and performance metrics for technologies included in the Technology Application Standards. This process includes subject matter experts in Generation Development, Research and Development, Technical and Shared Services, Southern Nuclear, and Power Delivery. These experts engage directly with the market to get the best available information for cost and performance for all technologies considered in the IRP. This annual update process typically accounts for changes in costs, but the current market conditions have indicated a significant increase in costs for combustion turbines and combined cycles. As stated during the direct hearings, these increases in costs are being considered when evaluating new large load customer requests to ensure the appropriate costs are being assumed in those evaluations. The Company will continue to monitor market conditions and use the best available information in current evaluations and future annual planning processes.

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-14 PD/STF-PIA-14-6.docx =====

STF-PIA-14-6
Question:
As discussed in Technical Appendix Volume 1 “B2025 Load and Energy Forecast,” the company states it includes projects that have not yet selected the company as its service provider. Please describe how the company avoids double counting large loads that may be incorporated in load forecasts in other jurisdictions.  
Response:
Projects that have not selected the state of Georgia are considered to be looking at other jurisdictions. As such, the Company includes a fraction of the project’s requested load in the forecast based on the results of the Load Realization Model (“LRM”). Such load included in the forecast is meant to reflect the uncertainty of the project selecting an electricity provider that is not Georgia Power. While certain projects in the Company’s forecast may also be in forecasts for other jurisdictions, this does not change the fact that the Company could potentially end up serving these projects. As such, the Company includes such loads in its forecast at lower levels of materialization than projects who have selected the Company as its service provider. 

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-14 PD/STF-PIA-14-7.docx =====

STF-PIA-14-7
Question:
Please confirm how the company’s methodology for the large load forecast external adjustment aligns with requirements set in the Official Code of Georgia Annotated § 46-3A-2 or GA PSC Rules and Regulations Subject 515-3-4 Integrated Resource Planning. 
Response:
O.C.G.A. § 46-3A-2 requires the Company to file an Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”) every three years and provides for a public hearing before the Commission regarding the contents and adequacy of Georgia Power’s IRP. Following the hearing, the Commission is required to determine, in part, whether the Company’s forecast requirements are based on substantially accurate data and an adequate method of forecasting. 
The 2025 IRP supports the reasonableness and accuracy of the data, assumptions, and methods underlying the Company’s 20-year load and energy forecast. 
Commission Rule 515-3-4-.03 identifies detailed Energy and Demand Forecasting Requirements. The Commission Rules set forth a floor on the minimum requirements of the load and energy forecast in terms of the methodology, data, and determinants, as well as the appropriate sensitivity analysis for major assumptions. Consistent with prior IRPs, the Company’s forecast meets each of the requirements set forth in the Commission Rules.
The Company’s forecast accounts for organic growth associated with the residential, commercial, industrial, government, and MARTA classes, as well as for the impacts of demand-side management (“DSM”), behind the meter customer generation, electric vehicles, and large loads. Initially introduced in the Company’s 2023 IRP Update, in Docket No. 55378, the large load adjustment is an external adjustment applied to the organic forecast to account for the likelihood and volume of additional large customer load associated with Georgia’s extraordinary economic growth. This adjustment, which is developed using the probabilistic Load Realization Model, is necessary because it accounts for load that would not otherwise be captured in the extrapolation of historic, organic growth load.
The risk adjustment for large loads is appropriate and comparable to the other three external adjustments the Company has historically employed (DSM, behind the meter customer generation, and electric vehicles), which are consistent with the impact analysis required in Commission Rule 515-3-4-.03(c).
