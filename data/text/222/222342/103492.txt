In 2024, the Company recorded four additions to Plant Held for Future Use.

This property was not addressed in the Company’s 2022 IRP or 2023 IRP Update considering the timing of the purchase and not included in the Company’s 2025 IRP filing as the project is driven by station retirement. The property is in the unincorporated area of Elbert County and was acquired in June 2024. This property is currently expected to be in utility service in 2028. The East Canon Substation site will be used for a new distribution substation. This property was not addressed in the Company’s 2022 IRP or 2023 IRP Update considering the timing of the purchase but was addressed in the Company’s 2025 IRP in Volume 3, Section E. The Spring Valley Road Substation site will be used for a new distribution substation. This property was not addressed in the Company’s 2022 IRP or 2023 IRP Update considering the timing of the purchase but was addressed in the Company’s 2025 IRP in Volume 3, Section D. The property is in Athens-Clarke County and was acquired in October 2024.  This property is currently expected to be in utility service in 2026. The Balus Creek Substation site will be used for a new distribution substation. Question:

For each Plant, provide the Company's coal inventory policies and procedures and target amounts that applied for 2024.

Response:

The Company’s coal inventory target is set in accordance with the Commission’s Final Order issued in FCR-22 (Docket No. 33302). Actual inventories are not static, and it is common for actual inventory levels to fluctuate above and below targets. The purpose of inventory targets is to reasonably assure that Georgia Power can meet its obligation to provide electricity even during events of supply disruption, whether mine or rail-related, and during periods when coal burn exceeds projections. To this end, the Company purchases coal and plans for deliveries based on maintaining its inventory within the inventory targets and takes prudent actions to return plant inventories to target when they deviate. However, it is important to note that although the Company plans its procurement with a target of an overall average of 40-50 days of inventory across all plants over the course of a year, monthly targets for a particular plant may be set outside the range. Please see STF-ASR-5-10 Attachment A for coal inventory policies and procedures. Also, refer to STF-ASR-5-10 Attachment B for 2024 monthly targets.

===== DKT 44280 STF-ASR-5 PD/STF-ASR-5-56.docx =====
STF-ASR-5-56
Question:
Prepaid pension asset. Is any amount for Southern Company Services' net pension asset included in the Company's retail jurisdictional rate base for 2024, by line item, on the Company's 2024 ASR filing Schedule 2, page 1 of 2? If not, explain fully why not. If so, identify how much by FERC account. If exact amounts are not available, provide the Company's best estimates and show in detail how such estimates were calculated.
Response:
No, the Company does not include any of Southern Company Services’ (SCS) net pension asset in retail rate base. The capitalized portion of the pension costs, which are billed to the Company by SCS on a monthly basis, are included in rate base. The billed amounts are imported into the Company’s general ledger and distributed among the various FERC accounts included in the Company’s 2024 ASR filing Section 2, page 1 of 2.
 
The estimates of the pension cost billed to the Company are not easily determined. This is because pension amounts billed by SCS to the Company and subsequently distributed among the various FERC accounts in the Company’s general ledger are calculated by the use of allocations and cost pools. Some of the allocations used in the process utilize dynamic allocation rates, which use direct labor dollars as the allocator. Therefore, there is not a consistent allocation rate that can be applied to recalculate exact amounts billed to the Company because the direct labor billed to the Company changes each month. Also, during the initial SCS billing process, the pension dollars are accumulated in a cost pool and comingled with other benefits before being allocated to the Company’s direct labor charges.  Because the pension costs are not allocated as individual dollars, are comingled with other benefits cost, and are incorporated into direct labor charges, it would be extremely difficult to estimate the amount of pension costs billed to the Company.

12.0 Obsolete Materials
Obsolete materials are items that have lost their intrinsic value and must be removed for disposition. Items identified and verified as obsolete materials MUST be removed from stock within 90 days of identification and adjusted from inventory on the company’s books.
Identification and Approval
Obsolete materials cannot be identified and approved by the same employee. Plant obsolete material may be identified by the system owner (engineer). T & D obsolete material may be identified by a staff engineer. Plant management and T & D management must have documentation of appropriate review of obsolete material.
Items become obsolete in one of two ways:
1. Technology
Changes in technology render an item unusable.
• Parts support an asset that is no longer used.
• The part is no longer used when it has been replaced by a different part.

2. Shelf-Life Expirations
• Items lose quality over time.
• Items are deemed obsolete if they exceed established life standards.

A report of slow-moving material should be reviewed annually. It is the responsibility of Supply Chain Management (Transmission, Distribution, Substation) and system owners (Plants) to insure the evaluation every item on the report as soon as practicable. System upgrades and changeouts should include a review of potential stranded material. Guidelines for plant obsolescence review and material disposition are detailed in the Southern Company Generation Playbook.
For items stocked at the MDC, the MDC will notify region locations of pending obsolete material items. The locations will be allowed four weeks to return any items to the MDC. After the four week period, the region supervisor will work with the storekeeper to insure appropriate disposition of obsolete items.
Inventory adjustments entered in Maximo must include “obsolete” as the adjustment reason. Any other information necessary to explain the adjustment is also included in the “comments” field. Documentation must be retained locally for one year for audit purposes. Contact the appropriate financial analyst for appropriate obsolete materials project and task.
Fleet obsolete material adjustments must be approved by the System Administrator and the Fleet Financial Analyst.
Surplus Material
Upon conclusion of a project, surplus standard approved materials should immediately be taken into stock.
Materials which are non-standard, or which are known to be slow moving or obsolete, should not be placed in stock. The Supply Chain Management Supervisor should contact the Investment Recovery Manager (or designee) to determine the disposition of the materials.

===== DKT 44280 STF-ASR-5 PD/STF-ASR-5-72.docx =====
STF-ASR-5-72
Question:
For each temporary difference identified in response to the previous question, indicate whether the Company believes the related ADIT is protected or unprotected. If the Company believes that the ADIT is protected, provide all citations to the IRC, Regulations, PLRs, and all other authoritative sources that address whether the ADIT is protected or unprotected.
Response:
ADIT accounts are either “protected” and subject to the Internal Revenue Code (IRC) normalization requirements, or “unprotected” and not subject to normalization requirements. Protected accounts are those that are the result of book-tax depreciation timing differences.
To be entitled to accelerated methods of depreciation for tax purposes, the Company must use a normalization method of accounting as required by IRC sections 168(f)(2) and 168(i)(9). Specifically, protected ADITs result from the excess of tax depreciation deductions allowable under IRC section 168 (Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System and Accelerated Cost Recovery System), and depreciation deductions that would be allowable under IRC section 167(l). Unprotected ADITs, which include all ADITs other than protected (regardless of whether they are property-related such as basis differences), are not addressed in the IRC nor the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act. These regulations do not pertain to book-tax timing differences with respect to state income taxes.
As such, in the Company’s response to STF-ASR-5-71, the Company classified federal excess accelerated depreciation ADITs in FERC accounts 281 and 282 as protected and all other regulated temporary items as unprotected and not subject to IRS normalization requirements. All state excess deferred taxes resulting from changes in the Georgia income tax rate have been classified as unprotected since normalization requirements do not apply to the remeasurement of state deferred income taxes.  
As previously stated, the Internal Revenue Code requires the Company to use normalization as a prerequisite to claim accelerated depreciation. If these tax benefits are not normalized in the ratemaking process, the utility loses the right to claim the benefits. In addition, section 13001(d)(1) of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act states that a normalization method of accounting shall not be treated as being used with respect to any public utility property for purposes of section 167 or 168 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 if the taxpayer, in computing its cost of service for ratemaking purposes and reflecting operating results in its regulated books of account, reduces the excess tax reserve more rapidly or to a greater extent than such reserve would be reduced under the average rate assumption method.

The following is an overview of the process by which the fees are set and the factors impacting the variability of such fees.

Each year, Congress approves the NRC Budget. Once the budget is approved, the NRC goes through a rulemaking process to establish fixed fees and hourly rates. Approximately 90% of the NRC’s budget is recovered by setting fees for their various activities and licensees.

NRC Fixed Fees:
The NRC calculates the annual fixed fee by dividing the recoverable budgeted costs equally among the licensed power reactors (currently 94 operating reactors).

NRC Variable Fees:  
The NRC uses an hourly rate to assess fees for specific services provided by the NRC
Variable fees fluctuate from year to year, and are calculated using the hourly rate and the number of hours required for NRC activities performed such as: 
Inspections 
License amendment reviews
Operator license training
