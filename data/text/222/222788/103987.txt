                                                                                       Overview

                                   This reference document is intended to provide vital information in preparing for and executing the many processes and
                                   procedures involved in responding to major events such as storms that impact Power Delivery operations

                                   • Storm Center Activation: Georgia Power activates a Storm Center under appropriate circumstances to handle storm
                                     team transfers. Early evaluation at the Region level dictates the need for Area Supervision, with storm teams combined
                                     under one Area Supervisor based on storm location and intensity. Activation of the Storm Center is based on imminent
                                     storm damage potential or the need to coordinate large numbers of storm teams.

                                   • Safety and Response: The company prioritizes safety for both the public and its employees, followed by efficient power
                                     restoration. The company will proactively anticipate and react to major emergencies affecting customer service.
                                     Different emergencies require tailored responses, determined by the Storm Center or Storm Evaluation & Restoration
                                     Plan (SERP) on-call manager based on the type of storm and anticipated damage. To reduce customer outage time to a
                                     minimum, following major damage, the company moves sufficient line crew and tree crew help into a damaged area in
                                     the shortest possible time, then follows a logical plan to ensure that those crews are used in the most effective way to
                                     restore service. Decentralizing the operations to the smallest section, whether it is by staging areas, substations, or
                                     remote operating centers, will make for the most effective operational decisions. An early evaluation of the damage is
                                     also an essential element to a successful restoration.

                                                                                                                         Job Descriptions


                                   Power Delivery (PD) Storm Center Operations Manager
                                      Responsible for developing, updating, and executing the Company's Emergency Operating Procedures. This position also coordinates the overall restoration effort. With the assistance of the
                                      Power Delivery Storm Center staff, as well as assistance from other Company departments, the position has the ultimate responsibility of allocating manpower, materials, logistic support,
                                      staging areas, and equipment in a manner to efficiently restore service.
                                   Emergency Preparedness Manager
                                      Provides critical support to the Storm Center during major events and acts as a backup to the Storm Center Operations Manager as required. Leads the strategy for emergency preparedness
                                      planning, manages continuity planning, and oversees the implementation of prevention and recovery plans for major events statewide.

                                   Public Information Officer / Media Relations Coordinator
                                       Collects all available data from the Corporate Headquarters Storm Center and prepares statements to be released to all information sources (newspaper, television, radio, etc.). These releases,
                                       which may or may not be presented by Public Information Personnel, are to be coordinated with releases made in the PD-Areas. Ensures all releases are factual according to information
                                       furnished and are consistent throughout the Company.
                                   Communication Director
                                       Manages crisis communication strategy during storm events, ensuring timely and accurate information dissemination to the public, stakeholders, and internal teams. Develops long-term
                                       communication strategies to enhance the storm center's preparedness and response capabilities.
                                   Safety Director
                                       Responsible for the overall coordination of any safety related problems that may arise because of the storm. This position monitors the situations and coordinates activities with the Corporate
                                       Headquarters and PD-Area to ensure the wellbeing of employees.
                                   Supply Chain Emergency Director
                                       Responsible for evaluating the material needs for the emergency service restoration effort. This position acts as a coordinator between the Supply Chain Area Inventory Managers in the field
                                       who are working with the PD-Areas, and the Corporate Headquarters Storm Center.
                                   Georgia Emergency Management Agency (GEMA) Coordinator
                                      Serves as a liaison to the GEMA. The GEMA Coordinator reports to the GEMA as required when conditions threaten the transmission and distribution facilities of the company. General
                                      responsibilities include working with representatives from various agencies to minimize the negative impact caused by emergency situations. Examples of these agencies are the Department of
                                      Transportation, Public Safety, Forestry, the National Guard, the Public Service Commission, Red Cross, AT&T, REA, etc.

                                   Security Director
                                      Responsible for providing the necessary security to safeguard all personnel and equipment involved in the restoration process. All requests concerning additional security are arranged through
                                      this person.
                                   Vegetation Management Manager
                                      Responsible for ensuring adequate tree crew/ vegetation management staffing levels and use of appropriate equipment types for the scope of work. Procures contract crews and coordinates
                                      their mobilization to align the restoration plan

                                                                                                                         Job Descriptions


                             Technology Organization (TO) Coordinator
                                 Responsible for ensuring that all communication needs are met both in the Transmission Storm Center and at all Staging Areas. The TO Field Operations and Workplace Support groups assist those in the
                                 field by ensuring communication infrastructure is available. When crews are deployed to support both on- and off-system storm restoration events, TO sends representatives to support many of the
                                 digital communications systems at the staging areas. In many cases, a command trailer and satellite trailers are deployed with office and technology equipment to assist storm leaders with the necessary
                                 data access, whether it is connectivity back to the Southern Company infrastructure for application access or amplified cell phone coverage from weak or congested cell signals. In some instances,
                                 Southern Linc also partners during storm events to provide Linc coverage when other cell carrier signals are non-existent.
                             Southern LINC Coordinator
                                   Because the Southern LINC Radio System is the primary tool used by field forces during restoration events, the Southern LINC Wireless Coordinator is responsible for communicating LINC infrastructure
                                   damage, implementing a plan for infrastructure solutions and for providing the services needed by the Company Distribution. The Southern LINC Wireless Coordinator will utilize a LINC representative
                                   who will be integrated with the Emergency Operations Plan (“EOP”) Logistics group and will participate in SCS/IT Critical Situation calls to request assistance, provide status, etc.
                             Fleet Director
                                 Responsible for coordinating all activities relating to automotive and mechanized equipment needs. This includes advising and assisting non-resident crews in securing parts and equipment, maintaining
                                 and fueling of equipment, and acquiring the necessary mechanics and equipment.

                             Manpower Director

                                   Assists the Emergency Operation Plan (EOP) directors in evaluating available information to determine the extent and severity of the damage. All necessary actions required to deploy available company
                                   personnel is the responsibility of the Manpower Director. This position will contact unaffected PD-Areas to obtain help and transmit information concerning acquired help to the appropriate PD-Area
                                   Manpower Directors. This position also initiates actions for obtaining assistance from other utilities.

                             Damage Assessment Team (DAT) Director
                                 Responsible for the success of Storm Damage Assessment teams (DAT) statewide. This person implements the Statewide DAT Plan and manages the process. This person also coordinates out-of-state
                                 requests. Director is responsible for receiving all requests from affected Regions for DAT resources and assigning DAT resources from outside the affected Region state-wide
                             Transmission Director
                                 Responsible for interpretation of intelligence reports for the general direction and implementation of the restoration efforts for the transmission system (40 KV and above). All the necessary actions for
                                 restoration is the responsibility of the Transmission Director. The Transmission Director will communicate and coordinate activities and efforts with the EOP Director.

                                             Process: Dispatching Forces to Regions needing assistance

                                   Large or Small Unanticipated Storm
                                   1. Region conducts initial evaluation.
                                   1.1Use TCMS/Troublevision where available.
                                   2. Region contacts Company SERP On-Call Manager and informs him or her of status, or requests additional help. This shall always be
                                       done.
                                   3. Company SERP Director determines if Company Storm Center should be opened.
                                   4. If center opens then Company Storm Center will coordinate crew request.
                                   5. Provide crew information to Region seeking assistance by use of S.T.O.R.M. 2000, email, or STORMLITE computer system.
                                   6. Provide Crew Supervisor with:
                                       a. Name and address of Region requesting assistance
                                       b. Name, address, and phone number of receiving supervisor in Region requesting assistance
                                       c. Conditions of emergency, i.e., rain, snow, wind, lightning, flood, etc.
                                       d. Estimated duration of emergency
                                       e. Equipment needed
                                       f. Tools required
                                       g. Weather – present and forecast
                                   7. Dispatch and oversee Logistics for crew and equipment to destination.
                                   8. The Company SERP Director will notify top management, through proper channels, of assistance being provided and to whom.

              ACCOUNTING FOR STORM DAMAGE & OTHER EMERGENCIES


I. General
   The following procedure should be followed in the event of a major storm that damages Georgia Power’s
   Distribution, Transmission, or Generation systems, and other emergency situations described below.
   A major storm is defined as a storm where the cost to repair / restore the systems to normal operating
   conditions will exceed $50,000 of incremental costs including labor, materials and other directly related
   incremental costs. These procedures will be followed by all Georgia Power Company personnel involved
   in accounting for major storm costs. Questions regarding Major Storm Declarations should be directed
   to the Storm Center Operations Manager.
   Other emergency situations covered by this procedure include localized storms not meeting the
   requirements to be classified as a major storm; holding personnel over as a contingency for possible
   storm activity; load shed contingency actions in the event of major system problems; and other
   emergency situations determined by the Southern Company Security Council. For each of the situations
   described above, the costs associated with them should be charged to the applicable project (will be
   provided via storm declaration), as well as the business units organization code.
   Responsibility
   Overall responsibility for compliance with this procedure resides with the GPC Budget Manager.
   Compliance at the various organizational levels resides with the appropriate organization manager. The
   GPC Budget Manager will coordinate the process of accounting and reporting for major storms and
   other emergency situations including gathering needed data from the field, routing it through the
   appropriate accounting organizations, and reporting as directed.

II. Declaration

   Declaration of a major storm will be done by the Storm Center Operations Manager or his designee after
   the needed data is gathered to indicate that the conditions detailed below have been met. When the Storm
   Center Operations Manager has declared a major storm, the GPC Budget Manager or their designee will
   be notified to assign a Storm Number and appropriate Project numbers for storm accounting and
   reporting purposes. The GPC Budget Manager or their designee will also notify the Managers of the
   Storm Center, Resource Budgeting, Financial & Regulatory Reporting, and other personnel impacted by
   this procedure, of the major storm accounting Project numbers for distribution and use by field and
   support personnel.

III. Requirements to be declared a major storm:

   A. Damage to the plant, buildings, equipment, and accessories of the Distribution, Transmission,
   Generation systems and general plant equipment and facilities must exceed $50,000 in incremental costs
   to restore the system to normal operating conditions and resume electrical service to customers.

V. Allowable Charges to Major Storms:

      The following guidelines indicate what the appropriate expenses are for major storm work that can be
      charged to the major storm Project numbers. If the expenses do not meet these criteria, they must be
      charged to the appropriate functional account number with the appropriate manager’s approval.
      Expenses found to be in a Major Storm Project that are outside these guidelines will be moved to an
      appropriate O&M account for further review by the appropriate manager.

      A. Straight time labor for personnel directly involved in restoration efforts, as directed by the
         following guidelines:

         ▪   Employees who charge all or a portion of their time to capital E&S (FercSub 30802600), their
             work plan should not be overridden with the Base Storm Number for Straight Time Labor.

         ▪   For off-system storms ALL employees’ labor should be charged to the appropriate off-system
             storm Project number.

         ▪   Meter department employees should not override their work plans for straight time labor. Cost
             to remove and install meters is capitalized at the time meters are purchased.

         ▪   For all other personnel (Covered Labor, Customer Service, External Affairs, Sales, etc.)
             straight time labor should be charged to the appropriate Base Storm EWO.

         ▪   Managers/Supervisors shall ensure that all straight time storm hours are entered accurately into
             the timekeeping system using the appropriate Project/Task

         ▪   P&C Test Engineers who record their time as “Variable” should charge straight time to the
             Base Storm Project/Task.

      B. Approved overtime or excess straight time for personnel described above and directly involved
         in restoration activities:

         ▪   ALL personnel working overtime or excess straight time will be required to have their time
             entered by the timekeepers with the appropriate Reserve Storm Project/Task

       For GPC System Storm Events – Region and Network Underground storerooms
       o   Major Material items: Charge Major Material items to the Declared Storm Project with the
           local HQ organization department code. Any major material items charged out but not used
           during the Storm should be credited back to the Declared Storm Project.


       o   Minor Material items: Charge all Minor Material items to Project 10124331 & Task
           100.05 for Region and Lighting locations with the local HQ/Department. Network
           Underground crews should charge all minor materials for Network repair work to their

I. Reimbursement for forfeited personal expense costs/deposits

   Due to major storms or system emergencies, it may be necessary for management to require an
   employee to cancel plans where the employee has paid a non-refundable deposit. Employees seeking
   reimbursement must notify their supervisor of the expense at the time vacation is being cancelled
   and must include a summary justification with their expenses to indicate they were called in for
   storm restoration and had to cancel their vacation plans. They can enter this in the expense
   justification on the expense statement or add a statement with expense documentation that is scanned
   into Oracle. Such as:
   **This company expense is a reimbursement for employee’s personal expenses due to the
   employee being called in to work for storm restoration efforts.
   Off Southern Company system storm restoration is normally assigned to volunteers only so these
   expenses should not be charged to an off-system storm.
   Expense statement should be routed to manager and manager should forward to their GM for
   approval. The manager’s forwarding to the GM signifies the manager has reviewed and approves
   the reimbursement request.

J. Obtaining Cash Advance from P-Card

   Circumstances may dictate that several team members on a deployed storm restoration team are
   asked to obtain a cash advance using their Georgia Power P-Card. The steps required to accomplish
   this are listed below:


       ▪ Have management or the storm center send an email to rfholcom@southernco.com need
         to verify contact (Southern Accounts Payable) stating that the employee will be requesting
         a cash advance. The email should include the cardholder’s name and management’s
         approval for cash.
       ▪ Cash advances will be set up for $2,500
       ▪ Once the cash has been set up on the card, an email will be sent to the cardholder and
         management or storm center, if applicable
       ▪ If the card has already been activated and a pin number set up, the card will be valid for
         cash immediately.
       ▪ If the card has not been activated, the Cardholder will contact Wells Fargo at the Customer
         Service number on the back of the card. They will enter the card account number and then
         select options 2-1-2. The unique identifier requested will be the last 5 digits of the
         employee number. Once the security questions are answered, the cardholder can set up a
         4-digit pin. This needs to be something they will remember as neither AP nor Wells Fargo
         has access to this code. If they forget, it will require calling Wells Fargo again and setting
         it up again. Cash will be available immediately upon setting up the pin number.
       ▪ If the cardholder does not remember the pin number or the pin number has not yet been
         set up and they are unable to access a phone to reset their pin, the cardholder can go into
         the bank with proper ID and obtain cash.

VI. Field Responsibility


    Field personnel including engineering, support, supervision, and management are responsible for
    insuring compliance with this procedure by personnel assigned to their areas involved in restoration
    efforts.

    A. Ensure the correct Project/Tasks are used as directed.

    B. Ensure that only appropriate charges go to a Declared Storm Project.

    C. Ensure that the appropriate Plant Accounting procedures are followed in determining what items
        are to be capitalized and what items are to be expensed. In general, there are no special
        circumstances that would alter compliance to the Plant Accounting procedures. What is
        capitalized during normal operations is what is capitalized during storm restoration efforts.
        See Appendix I & II.


VII. GPC Budget Manger Responsibilities:

     A. The GPC Budget Manager will ensure the designated staff person coordinates the activities needed
        to complete the accounting for major storm activities by the field.

     B. The GPC Budget Manager or designated staff member will ensure that the appropriate Power
        Delivery personnel complete a Storm Distribution Work Order or General Work Order to move all
        applicable capital charges from the reserve and the performing business unit’s operation and
        maintenance accounts into the correct capital retirement units – See Appendix I & II. The GPC
        Budget Manager or designee will ensure that only appropriate Power Delivery storm related
        charges are accounted for regarding the Storm Reserve account.

     C. Invoices/Expense statements are to be processed using the following guidelines below:

       Southern Company Business Expense Guidelines

                       Distribution Storm Work Order Estimation Procedure:
When an "official" storm is declared by the company, there are several rules to follow in creating a
WO to account for labor and materials used. This is particularly important for property accounting for
statistical data and for transfer of dollars from O&M to capital.

A separate WO is required for each quarter’s storms unless a single storm WO is requested. The address
or description should describe the particular storms. There will only be one JETS WO per job for the
entire state. The corporate office – Distribution Systems & Standards will have the responsibility to
create this WO using a list of materials issued during the storm.

The foreman for these jobs must be "Major Storm”. The Type Customer must be "Storms (Official
Storms)".

The following is a list of the steps for creating the JETS estimate:

1) Get a MAXIMO listing of all materials charged to the storm. Exclude items inappropriately
    charged to the storm and O&M items.
2) Use Location GP999 to estimate the DWO and Org GPC2392. For the E&S rate, use the current
    E&S rate for the statewide average. This must be done each time a storm DWO is estimated as the
    rate changes each month. Specify it in JETS under Calculation Adjustments, EOH labor and EOH
    material. Check repair allowance on the Work Order screen.
3) Estimate the DWO using current funding project for the current storm. Get it from Martha Hale.
    The JETS estimate is used to calculate plant retirement units as well as amounts for the journal.
4) Estimate all labor as GPC labor.
5) Determine the JETS ID for each item left on the list from #1 above. Estimate a Remove and Install
    for each item. You will not be able to assign JETS ID’s for minor material.
6) It may be helpful to organize your data such that like items are all in a particular JETS location,
    with different locations for each grouping. For example, transformers of a particular voltage would
    be assigned to one location and transformers of a different voltage would be assigned to a different
    location.
7) A certain percentage of hardware, wire, and poles should be classified as “Congested” or
    “Inaccessible” for purposes of estimating the correct labor. 50% was used by agreement. Lights
    should not be classified as such.
8) Do not estimate any salvage.
9) Incidental maintenance should be zero or close to it.
10) Authorize the job and add the appropriate tax areas.
11) Construction Complete the DWO, but do not Field Close it.
12) Email a copy to of the estimate to the appropriate Financial Analyst responsible for Distribution
    Capital.
13) The Financial Analyst responsible for Distribution Capital will make the journal transfers using
    the Journal Name CSO-50360.
14) After the dollar transfers, DS&S will field close the job.

3.1.2 Emergency Purchase Requests
Emergency conditions can develop and should be handled appropriately as part of the purchasing

6.0 Reporting Materials Short or Damaged
The statute of limitation for filing short or damaged claims with a carrier is nine (9) months from
date of delivery. Therefore, it is important that employees responsible for receiving and checking
materials follow a definite procedure in reporting shortages or damages presumably caused by the
transportation company.

6.1 Inspection of Incoming Material
All individuals responsible for receiving materials must carefully inspect incoming shipments to:
• Verify that the material ordered is an exact match to the material that was delivered or that it
    is an acceptable substitute.
• Verify that the quantity received agrees with the quantity shown on the freight bill.
• Determine the condition of the container or packaging.
• Determine the condition of the equipment or material.

If the condition of the container or packaging indicates a possible shortage or damage, immediate
inspection of the material must be made in the presence of the carrier's driver. If at all possible, it
is advisable to make a brief inspection of all shipments in the presence of the carrier's driver.

When a short or damaged receipt is discovered upon delivery, the carrier's driver must be asked to
sign and date an exception statement on the freight bill. The exception statement should indicate
clearly the extent of shortage or damage. If the carrier's driver refuses to sign the exception
statement, the exception should still be noted and the notation "driver refused to sign" noted on the
freight bill.

6.2 Short or Damaged Claims
In all cases involving short or damaged goods,
• The receiving report must be completed, and the shortage or damage noted on the packing slip.
• The buyer/analyst is notified. The buyer/analyst will be responsible for contacting the vendor.

No repairs are to be made locally until authorization is received from the SCM Organization. If
the damage is repaired locally, the cost of the repair must be charged to the non-electric service
business assigned by the SCM Organization.

SCM serves as a liaison and coordinates handling matters relative to repairs, replacements, etc.
among the receiving location, carrier, and vendor.

6.2.1 Concealed Short or Damaged Claims
Concealed short or damaged goods are not detectable at the time of delivery and must be reported
to the carrier within fifteen (15) days from the date of delivery. When a concealed shortage or

• A request to the carrier's agent for an inspection should be confirmed by a letter. Under no
    circumstances should the carrier's inspection report be signed by anyone other than the carrier’s
    representative.

It is not necessary to request an inspection report from the Company’s current dedicated freight
carrier(s). However, a notation of shortage or damage must be shown on the Bill of Lading.

6.3 Interstate Commerce Commission Ruling
A ruling by the Interstate Commerce Commission regarding payment of concealed damage claims
states that it is not proper for the carrier to pay all or any part of a claim when the consignee takes
no exception at the time of delivery and no actual evidence of carrier liability can be shown.
However, carriers are required to conduct an investigation of each case and each instance of
concealed damage will be considered upon its own merit.

Thus, it becomes the responsibility of the individual signing for the merchandise or material to
check for any signs of damage and obtain a signed exception statement from the carrier's
representative at time of delivery to permit the Company to realize maximum recovery from the
loss or damage.

The information is then compared to previous storms and historical data is used from the Distribution Resource Management tracking application to determine resource needs. For post-storm response, the Company uses intelligence from field and regional resources including events, customer outages, and the causes of outages to determine the necessary resources.

===== DKT 44280 STF-PIA-29 PD/STF-PIA-29-7.docx =====
STF-PIA-29-7

Question: 

For large storm events such as Hurricane Helene, indicate if the Company has one or more target metrics or quantitative goals for outage restoration times that is factored directly or indirectly into the number and type of resources acquired and/or the various storm response logistics modeling tools.  If so, identify and describe each such target metric or quantitative goal and describe the manner in which it is used to determine the number and type of resources acquired, including, but not limited to, the various storm response logistics modeling tools.


Response:

The Company does not have specific metrics or quantitative goals for outage restoration times that factor into the number and type of resources needed for initial preparations of the storm response. Once the initial storm event has occurred and damage for various areas has been assessed by the Company, it assigns an outage event level from 1 through 4, which can be used as an input for resourcing decisions.  The outage event level also helps determine an Estimated Restoration Time (ERT) to track restoration times for customers. ERTs are developed and communicated for major storms using a cross-functional team as mentioned in the Company’s response to STF-PIA-29-1.

At the onset of significant outage events, it is difficult to accurately determine the extent of electrical system damage due to continuing extreme weather conditions, darkness, or incomplete damage assessment. The Company has established the four outage event level definitions to provide consistent guidance for restoration management and communication to its customers. These guidelines provide expectations around when customers should receive an ERT and how ERTs are managed. The Company does not wait for a complete damage assessment before exercising the event level guidelines; rather, an initial decision of storm level is made early in a major event prior to finishing a complete assessment of the electric system.

Question: 

Provide a copy of all internal or external reviews or critiques, including lessons learned, prepared by or for the Company or by a reviewing entity of the Company’s performance in its response to Hurricane Helene.

Following the Company’s response to Hurricane Helene, a cross functional team was brought together to review the Company’s storm response process. Develop a base camp playbook for leaders to utilize to successfully run a base camp.

Determine a process for assigning storm roles to every new and existing employee at the Company and providing necessary training for the newly assigned roles. 
Develop a process for hosting an annual Company-wide storm drill and include a scenario of a severe hurricane impacting the whole state, including Metro Atlanta, in the first drill.
Standardize the Company’s approach to local (county/city) communication during major storm events.
Evaluate and update the safety onboarding process for off-system personnel.
Develop a standardized process for damage assessment data collection and delivery. 
Refresh storm preparedness checklists included in all region Storm Emergency Response Plans.

Question: 

As it relates to each kind of goal or measure used by the Company to assess its effectiveness in the Hurricane Helene restoration:
Provide a listing and description of each goal or measure used by the Company to assess the effectiveness of its storm planning and response process.

Response:

There are no specific metrics or documented goals for Hurricane Helene restoration efforts; however, the primary aim during any storm event is to safely and efficiently restore electric service to all affected customers as quickly as possible. This includes prioritizing public safety, critical infrastructure (such as hospitals and emergency services), and effective communication with the public. The process involves assessing storm damage, mobilizing and deploying repair crews and resources, coordinating with local agencies, and providing timely updates to customers throughout the restoration process. As referenced in the Company’s response to STF-PIA-29-7, while there is no specific quantitative metric or goal concerning storm performance, the priority is to restore power to every customer promptly and provide Estimated Restoration Times (ERT) as part of the process.
