Question: 

How much investment tax credit did Georgia Power generate in 2024?  Identify the amounts in total and for each type of ITC.
For each type of ITC that the Company generated in 2024, show in detail how the Company accounted for that ITC.
In 2024 did the Company sell any ITCs?  If so, explain fully and show the amounts of the ITC sales and the related discount to face value related to each ITC sale.
As of January 1, 2024, did the Company have any deferred ITC?  If so, show the amounts by type and account.
As of December 31, 2024, did the Company have any deferred ITC?  If so, show the amounts by type and account. 
In 2024 did the Company amortize any ITCs?  If so, show the amounts of such ITC amortization by type and account. 
During 2024 were there any instances where the Company had the ability to exercise discretion as to whether to claim available federal income tax benefits as either ITC or some other alternative, such as, but not limited to Production Tax Credits (“PTC”)?  If so, explain fully.  Also, explain which types of tax benefits are being claimed, and how the Company decided which form of such tax benefits was more beneficial.  Identify and provide related analysis showing how the Company analyzed the alternatives for claiming such tax benefits. 

Response:
Please see STF-ASR-6-2 Attachment A. In 2024, Georgia Power generated the following investment tax credits (ITCs):

Approximately $25.6 million of estimated federal ITCs associated with the Renewable Energy investment tax credits (ITCs) on the Mossy Branch battery storage project. In 2024, the Company also accrued the sale, at a discount, of $0.945 on a dollar for a $1.4 million total discount, which was completed in 2025. The net tax benefit of ITCs on Mossy Branch after the sale was $24.2 million.
Approximately $6.1 million of estimated federal ITCs associated with the Renewable Energy ITCs on the Tugalo Unit 3 hydro modernization project. In 2024, the Company also accrued the sale, at a discount, of $0.945 on a dollar for a $0.3 million total discount, which was completed in 2025. The net tax benefit of ITCs on Tugalo Unit 3 after the sale was $5.8 million. The Company has since revised its 2024 estimate of $6.1 million to $5.6 million in 2025, which lowered the credits after the sale to $5.3 million.
Approximately $0.4 million of federal ITCs were generated in 2024 associated with the various solar facilities, partially offset by $0.2 million of federal ITCs recorded in 2024 as part of the 2023 tax return true-up process.
Approximately $0.2 million of federal ITCs associated with Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property generated in 2023, recorded in 2024 as part of the tax return true-up process.
Approximately $0.7 million of federal ITCs associated with Electric Vehicles generated in 2023, recorded in 2024 as part of the tax return true-up process.
Approximately $49.5 million of state ITCs associated with various facilities that qualify for the Georgia Manufacturer’s ITC, as provided in STF-ASR-6-2 Attachment B.

For the $24.2 million net tax benefit of federal ITCs for Mossy Branch, the ITCs were recognized to operating income as generated. ITCs on Mossy Branch qualified for normalization opt-out treatment as part of the Inflation Reduction Act and were amortized over a shorter period as compared to the asset lives. The ITCs were included in the 2022 Base Rate Case, Docket No. 44280, and were therefore excluded from being deferred per Stipulation Paragraph 14 of the 2022 Rate Case Settlement Agreement. 

For the $5.8 million net tax benefit of federal ITCs for Tugalo Unit 3 hydro modernization accrued in 2024, the ITCs were subject to IRS normalization rules, were recorded to FERC account 255 unamortized ITC liability, and were excluded from retail rate base.  The Company did not amortize any of these Tugalo Unit 3 ITCs in 2024. Upon transfer of the portion of ITCs in 2025, the Company is no longer subject to the IRS normalization requirements with respect to the ITCs transferred. As a result, the Company included the proceeds from the sale of unamortized ITC liability of $5.3 million, after the estimate revision, as a reduction to retail rate base in 2025.

For the $0.4 million of federal ITCs on various solar facilities, $0.2 million of federal ITCs associated with Federal Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property, and $0.7 million of federal ITCs associated with Electric Vehicles, the ITCs were subject to IRS normalization rules, were recorded to FERC account 255 unamortized ITC liability, and excluded from retail rate base. The Company did not amortize any of these solar ITCs in 2024. These credits will begin amortizing upon being utilized on the Company’s federal tax return in accordance with IRS normalization rules. In 2024, the Company amortized approximately $22 thousand of this Federal Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property ITCs true-up and $67 thousand of this Electric Vehicle ITCs true-up.

For the $49.5 million of state Manufacturer’s ITCs, the ITCs are not subject to IRS normalization rules and were recognized to operating income as generated in 2024.

In July 2024, the Company sold $12 million of the ITCs related to Tugalo Units 1 and 2 generated in 2023, at a discount of $0.935 on a dollar ($0.78 million total discount). Upon transfer of the portion of ITCs in 2024, the Company is no longer subject to the IRS normalization requirements with respect to the ITCs transferred. As a result, the Company included the proceeds from the sale of unamortized ITC liability of $11.2 million as a reduction to retail rate base in 2024. The Company did not amortize any of the credits in 2024, in accordance with Stipulation Paragraph 14 of the 2022 Rate Case Settlement Agreement. Yes, the Company had the ability to exercise discretion in 2024 as to whether to claim federal ITCs or PTCs on two new renewable energy projects: Tugalo Unit 3 Hydro Modernization and the McIntosh Solar Facility.  

For the Tugalo Unit 3 Modernization project, the Company determined that upon selling the ITCs and opting out of normalization, the net present value of the ITC amortization net of the sale discount, along with the reduction to retail rate base for any unamortized ITC, was more favorable than the net present value of the PTCs on the incremental efficiency and capacity increases at Tugalo Unit 3 claimed over a 10 year period.  Therefore, the Company recorded ITCs on Tugalo Unit 3 in 2024 and entered into an agreement with a third party to sell and transfer the ITCs.  The credits were sold in April 2025.  Please see STF-ASR-6-2 Attachment C for the related analysis.

For the McIntosh Solar Facility project, the Company recorded approximately $0.3 million of PTCs in the 2024 income tax provision.  The Company is still in the process of evaluating the best option and may claim or transfer ITCs instead of claiming PTCs on the McIntosh Solar Facility.  The 2024 federal tax return is expected to be filed in the third quarter of 2025.  If the Company chooses ITCs instead of PTCs, the Company will record a true-up to the 2024 tax provision as part of the tax return to provision true-up entries recorded in 2025.
