              AMENDED APPLICATION OF MICROSOFT CORPORATION
                   FOR LEAVE TO INTERVENE OUT OF TIME

       COMES       NOW,     Microsoft    Corporation       (“Microsoft”),   pursuant   to   O.C.G.A.

§§ 50-13-14 and 46-2-59 and Georgia Public Service Commission (“Commission”) Rule 515-2-

1.06, files this Amended Application for Leave to Intervene Out of Time (“Application”) in the

above-captioned proceeding. In support of this Application, Microsoft respectfully states the

following:

                                                 1.

       On January 28, 2025, the Commission issued a Procedural and Scheduling Order (“January

Order”) to direct certain procedures, set a hearing schedule and filing dates, and identify issues to

be addressed in the above-captioned proceedings. On January 31, 2025, Georgia Power Company

(“Georgia Power”) filed an application for approval of its 2025 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”),

as well as an application for approval of an amended Demand-Side Management Plan (“DSM”)

and certification and decertification of certain DSM programs in Docket Nos. 56002 and 56003,

respectively. Because Georgia Power’s application for certification or decertification must be

accompanied by its current IRP, the Commission found it appropriate to consider both dockets and

their schedules concurrently. In accordance with the January Order and Commission Rule 515-2-

1.04(3), Georgia Power provided first notice of the proceedings on February 13, 2025 (“First

Notice”).

                                                 2.

        Microsoft is a worldwide technology company that constructs, owns, and operates data

centers around the world. Because datacenters require a reliable and resilient supply of electric

power, Microsoft’s ability to procure reliable, sustainable, and cost-effective energy for its

datacenters is paramount. Microsoft is committed to industry-leading sustainability objectives,

and region-specific electricity rates and the resource mix of power supply portfolios are significant

considerations for Microsoft when evaluating possible sites for new facilities and expansions at

existing facilities.

                                                 3.

        As required by Part IV of the January Order and O.C.G.A. § 46-2-59, Microsoft submits

that the company has a substantial interest in these proceedings as a large consumer of Georgia

Power with particularized needs for electric service, and its interest in these proceedings cannot be

adequately represented by any other intervenor or party. Microsoft established its presence in

metropolitan Atlanta in 2007, and maintains corporate facilities in Atlantic Yards in West Midtown

Atlanta. In February 2021, Microsoft announced plans for a new datacenter region in Georgia,

and is currently building those datacenter facilities in Douglasville, Palmetto, and East Point.

Microsoft’s investment in these communities supports economic development in Fulton and

Douglas counties and Greater Atlanta. By the end of 2026, Microsoft projects employment of 225

full-time employees and contractors across all its operational datacenter facilities in the state. As

a customer of Georgia Power, the Commission’s approval, denial, or modification of Georgia

           Microsoft is not presently intending to submit any testimony in these proceedings.

                                                 5.

        Microsoft requests that the Commission grant its intervention out of time. The

Commission’s January Order directs interested entities to file an application to intervene within

30 days of publication of the First Notice. Georgia Power published the First Notice on February

13, 2025, thereby setting March 17, 2025 as the deadline for timely interventions. Microsoft

initially filed an Application for Leave to Intervene on March 24, 2025, which was seven days

after the deadline. Microsoft files this amended Application to submit that good cause exists to

grant its intervention out of time. As a customer of Georgia Power, Microsoft’s interests will be

affected by the outcome of this proceeding and its interests cannot be adequately represented by

any other party. Microsoft’s intervention will not unreasonably broaden the issues, burden the

record, or unreasonably delay or cause disruption to the proceedings, and no party will be harmed

or prejudiced by the grant of its intervention. Microsoft submits that it will accept the record

compiled to date.

                                                  6.

        Wherefore, for the foregoing reasons, Microsoft respectfully requests that the

Commission accept Microsoft’s Amended Application for Leave to Intervene and admit it as a

full party of record in these proceedings with all rights attendant thereto.
