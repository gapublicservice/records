COMMENTS OF
AMERICANS FOR AFFORDABLE CLEAN ENERGY
ON GEORGIA POWER COMPANY’S 2025 COMMUNITY CHARGING PLAN

Comes now, AMERCANS FOR AFFORDABLE CLEAN ENERGY (“AACE”), an Intervenor in the above styled docket and pursuant to the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (“Commission”) Order Adopting Settlement Agreement as Modified, files these Comments on Georgia Power Company’s (the “Company” or “Georgia Power”) 2025 Community Charging Plan (the “Plan”), showing the Commission, as follows:
AACE intervened in the above styled Docket to address issues related to electric vehicle (“EV”) charger deployment. AACE signed the Settlement Agreement entered between the Company and the Commission’s Staff.  The Electric Transportation Term Sheet (“ET Term Sheet”), Attachment 2 to the Settlement Agreement, set forth the terms and conditions of the Company’s Community Charger program.  
On March 7, 2025, the Company filed its 2025 Plan, identifying eleven (11) locations for the placement of EV charging stations.  All of the Company’s proposed charger locations are in Tier 1 and Tier 2 counties, and none of the proposed chargers are located within a mile of an Alternative Fuel Corridor (“AFC”), as required by the EV Term Sheet.  The locations proposed by the Company do not violate any provision of the EV Term Sheet governing the placement of EV chargers.
However, the EV Term Sheet (to which the parties agreed) provides:
Once the annual Plan is filed, private EV charging service providers (Private Providers) would have a one-time, 60-day right of first refusal (ROFR) to claim a location within 15 miles of a Company proposed location in the Plan.

A private provider can exercise a ROFR and place a charger within 15 miles of a Community Charger location proposed by the Company.  When the ROFR is exercised, a private provider’s charger is placed in lieu of the Company’s proposed Community Charger location.  The private provider’s charger satisfies the need for an EV charger at that location and advances the public’s interest in EV charging access.  The private charging provider incurs all costs for the charger, and ratepayer subsidy of the Company’s proposed EV charger is not required.
That said, the proposed locations for four (4) of the Company’s Community Chargers present an interesting issue because they are located within fifteen (15) miles of an existing fast charger.  The Company’s 2025 Plan acknowledges that three (3) of its proposed locations are located within fifteen (15) miles of an existing DCFC charger.  Based on AACE’s research, a fourth location also is located within that distance from an existing DCFC charger.  This begs the question: whether placement of a Community Charger within fifteen (15) miles of an already existing Company or privately-owned charger at ratepayer expense advances the goal of providing EV chargers in truly unserved areas?  
The four (4) Community Charger locations proposed by the Company are:
Porterdale (Hemlock & Main Streets): The Company’s proposed location is about three (3) miles from a parking lot (near a QuikTrip) in Covington that already hosts a bank of Tesla DCFC chargers.  The Company’s filing acknowledged that its proposed location is within fifteen (15) miles of existing chargers.

Rockmart (Water Street): The Company’s filing acknowledged that its proposed location is within fifteen (15) miles of an existing Company charger in Cedartown.

Warrenton (46 Norwood Street); The Company’s filing acknowledged that its proposed charger is within fifteen (15) miles of a DCFC in Thomson.  

Hazlehurst (154 E. Tallahassee Street):   A DCFC charger in Baxley is located just over fifteen (15) miles from Hazlehurst by road, but within fifteen (15) miles as the crow flies.  The Company’s filing did not acknowledge the presence of another charger located within fifteen (15) miles.  The EV Term Sheet does not provide guidance for calculating the distance between chargers.
Nothing about the location of these four (4) chargers violates any requirement of the EV Term Sheet.  But, the question remains as to whether ratepayer funding for Community Chargers was intended to support placement of Community Chargers within fifteen (15) miles of an existing charger.  
AACE appreciates the opportunity to continue working with the Staff, the Company and interested parties to address these issues regarding the implementation of the Community Charging Program as set forth in ET Term Sheet.
