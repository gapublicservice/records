===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-12 PD/STF-PIA-12-10.docx =====
STF-PIA-12-10
Question:
Please refer to the Company’s response to STF-PIA-3-45, noting that “the model demonstrated a need for higher operating reserves targets during overnight hours to force commitment of resources to ensure availability during daytime hours.”  
Does the advanced commitment of these resources incur a production cost in the model?
Please provide analysis quantifying the production cost impact of committing resources during the overnight hours.
Response:
The commitment of all dispatchable resources incurs a production cost.

It is not possible to isolate these additional production costs . First, the commitment of a resource incurs a startup cost captured by the model, but that cost does not vary by time of day. The startup cost would have been realized if the resource was committed during daylight hours. Second, at steady-state load conditions, the commitment of a resource would require one or more online resources to ramp down to account for the minimum output of the newly committed resource. This scenario likely happened when advanced commitment occurred, but the per-unit intra-hour dispatch cost details from the study are not available to calculate a change in production cost. If the data were available, hourly system load rarely remains the same across two consecutive hours so it would be difficult to conclusively determine the cost of committing a unit during overnight hours instead of daylight hours when there was a change in load served as well.

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-12 PD/STF-PIA-12-12.docx =====
STF-PIA-12-12
Question:
Please refer to the Company’s response to STF-PIA-3-42(a), stating that “If operating reserves drop below 1,250 MW during intra-hour, a flex violation is recorded.”  Does the use of operating reserves qualify as a violation of NERC Reliability Standard BAL-001-2?  If so, provide supporting documentation.  If not, please explain why this system condition is used to define a “flexibility violation.”
Response:
The operation of the Southern Company System requires the use of operating reserves on a sub-minute basis in order to ensure appropriate balancing behavior is achieved. Therefore, the use of operating reserves is not a violation of any North American Electric Reliability Corporation (“NERC”) standards. In the context of the Renewable Integration Study, the term "violation" indicates that the system's operating reserves fell below the required threshold of 1,250 MW at least once within a 5-minute interval during a single day. This does not imply that the Company would be out of compliance with NERC Reliability Standard BAL-001-2 if its real-time reserves were to drop below 1,250 MW.
The choice of the 1,250 MW threshold is simply a target used to determine if intra-hour violations are occurring and is consistent with the load shed point applied in the Reserve Margin Study. Another key consideration is that the 1,250 MW threshold plays a big part in establishing a 1-event-in-10-year loss of load expectation (“LOLE”) level of reliability, as described on page 10 of the study. For example, if the threshold was instead lowered to 0 MW, annual reliability would then be better than 1-in-10 years, so capacity would be removed from the model to return to the 1-in-10-year target. The baseline level of flexibility violations would again be established but with lower system capacity. The Study would still record a flexibility violation if reserves dropped below 0 MW, except the System would not have any additional reserves available to mitigate the system imbalance.

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-12 PD/STF-PIA-12-13.docx =====
STF-PIA-12-13
Question:
Please refer to the Company’s response to STF-PIA-3-31(b), stating that 3,800 MW of CT capacity was added to the Base Case to meet the LOLE standard of 0.1 days/year, and the Company’s response to STF-PIA-3-34, stating that for the 7.5 GW case, 2,875 MW was removed from the case to meet the 1-in-10 standard.  Is it a correct interpretation that the Base Case with 3,800 MW of CT capacity and the solar case with 7,500 MW of solar plus 925 MW of CT capacity both met the LOLE standard of 0.1 days/year?
Response:
Yes, 3,800 MW of CT-Gas resources were added to establish an annual 0.1 days/year no-solar base case. When 7.5 GW of solar were also added, 2,875 MW of the same CT-Gas resources were removed to re-establish the annual loss of load expectation (“LOLE”) of 0.1 days/year due to the capacity value that solar provided. The CT-Gas resources were also removed for the entire year when the solar was added. Therefore, the seasonal LOLE likely showed lower reliability in the winter and greater reliability in the summer even though the annual LOLE remained at 0.1 days/year. This is due to solar resources historically having a lower capacity contribution during the winter months as compared to summer months.

STF-PIA-12-17
Question:
Please refer to the Company’s response to “STF-PIA-3-8 Attachment B TRADE SECRET.xlsx”: 
Please explain why the 2-hr Battery receives 100% ELCC while the 4-hr Demand Response ELCC receives 85%. Provide any workpapers to support this if applicable.
Please explain why the Loss Factor is set at 5.24%. Please provide workpapers to support this calculation. 
Provide all analysis and workpapers to support the Retail Capacity Price Forecast found in cells C11 through C44. 
Did the Company consider energy benefits when calculating the incentive values? Explain why this was or was not included. 
Please explain whether the Company considered any additional RCB components when calculating the incentive values. If so, which components were considered.
Explain how the MUSH/LMI incentive of $1,000/kW was calculated for the Company-Directed option.
Explain how the $15/kW incentive was calculated for the Customer-Directed option. 
Response:
The 2-hour battery resource used to determine incentive levels in the Company-Directed pathway is assumed to be capable of providing operating reserves in the form of spinning and regulating reserves. To enable the System to respond to unforeseen events such as forced generator outages and maintain grid stability, a minimum level of operating reserves is maintained at all times. Because of the flexibility that batteries provide, they are well suited to perform this task provided they can respond in near real time. The Company anticipates this will be possible in the Company-Directed pathway. At this point in time, the System has not reached this operating reserve saturation point with committed or in-service battery resources, so incremental 2-hour batteries are receiving a 100% effective load carrying capability (“ELCC”) as they displace other resources from commitments to reserves. Once there are enough batteries on the System to cover the minimum operating reserves requirement, the high ELCC value of 2-hour batteries degrades. 
Battery resources in the Customer-Directed option will be dispatched in discrete events with advanced notice, similar to demand response programs and are not capable of providing operating reserves. Many of our modeled and historical reliability events are more than 4 hours in length, so a 4-hour demand response program is not capable of responding for the entire duration of those events. This duration restriction and the lead time for responses to materialize reduces the ELCC below 100%. 
The Loss Factor is set at 5.24% based on the assumption that all participating customers are taking service at the secondary distribution level. This percentage reflects the average losses that occur in delivering energy from a bulk system generator to the customer load and is derived from historical System data.  Please see STF-PIA-12-17 Attachment A.
Please see STF-PIA-12-17 Attachment B TRADE SECRET. 
No, the Company did not consider energy benefits when calculating the storage-based incentive values. The intent of the incentives is to promote the addition of storage, which is not an energy-generating resource. Participating customers will also benefit from reducing their own consumption and are able to participate in RNR which compensates customers for energy delivered to the grid. 
When calculating the incentive values for storage, the Company considered several Renewable Cost Benefit (“RCB”) components. Specifically, deferred generation capacity costs, which account for the reduced need to invest in new generation facilities due to the storage capacity. The Company also included avoided transmission losses and avoided distribution losses, recognizing that customer-sited resources reduce energy losses associated with delivery of energy to the load. 
The MUSH/LMI incentive of $1,000 per kW for the Company-Directed option was calculated based on the System value before applying the 75% shared savings factor. This approach reflects the full value of the avoided costs associated with the storage solutions in these segments. 
The combination of upfront incentive and pay-for-performance incentive in the Customer-Directed pathway was set to achieve 75% of the total System value. Having most of the incentive allocated through pay-for-performance protects non-participants. The $15 per kW incentive for the Customer-Directed option yields a 1.50 $/kWh pay for performance incentive over 50 hours. These round numbers should make it easier for stakeholders to determine the benefits of participating in the program and calculate anticipated incentives.

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-12 PD/STF-PIA-12-5.docx =====
STF-PIA-12-5
Question:
Please refer to “STF-PIA-3-22 Attachment A TRADE SECRET.xlsx” regarding the Locational Transmission Value.  Refer to the “Project estimate” tab. Please explain why several projects do not include a cost estimate in column H. Are the impacts of any accelerations/delays included in the analysis?
Response:
There are two categories of projects that do not include cost estimates in the ‘Project estimate’ tab of the referenced file. The first category is 500 kV Transmission Line Rebuilds. Four of these segments were identified in the study. 
Each constraint identified in the locational value study was assigned a potential project to raise the MVA rating of the constraint. These projects were intentionally kept simple in scope for consistency with the 2022 iteration of the locational value study.
The 500 kV transmission line rebuilds were excluded from cost estimation because rebuilds of that scope are not economically strategic solutions. The inclusion of these 500 kV rebuild costs would create ambiguity and obscure the locational value results. The total cost of the four projects alone would more than double the total transmission capital of distributed solar in each region. The Company determined that including these significant costs for unrealistic projects was not appropriate for the locational value study.
The second category of projects excluded from cost estimation were Generator Step-Up transformers (“GSUs”). GSUs are the responsibility of the generator and are not a system cost. This category represents the remaining constraints excluded from cost estimation in the 'Project estimate' tab of the file. 
The full project cost was included in the identified year of need. Projects that were accelerated or delayed by the presence of distributed energy resources were accounted for.

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-12 PD/STF-PIA-12-7.docx =====
STF-PIA-12-7
Question:
Please refer to Technical Appendix Vol. 2, the 2024 Renewable Integration Study, p. 12, stating: “To prepare this base case for the solar integration analysis, all solar and BESS resources were removed from the case.” Please explain why all existing and committed BESS resources were removed from the case.
Response:
One of the objectives of the Renewable Integration Study is to determine the flexibility benefit associated with battery energy storage system (“BESS”) resources at multiple solar penetrations. If BESS resources were embedded into the no-solar base case, it would not have been possible to determine the flexibility credit associated with the first tranche of BESS added to the System. As performed, the Renewable Integration Study accurately captures the integration costs of solar resources, excluding any impact from embedded flexible BESS resources. This also means that BESS resources are receiving the proper credit for helping to mitigate solar intermittence.
