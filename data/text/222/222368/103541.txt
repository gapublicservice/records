===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-13/STF-PIA-13-1.docx =====
STF-PIA-13-1
Question:
Please describe the Company’s process to determine eligible sites for installing solar, wind, and/or storage facilities.  Specifically:
Describe the Company’s criteria used to determine location exclusions. 
Describe the Company’s criteria used to determine potential siting locations near existing points of interconnection. 
Please provide all workpapers used by the Company to implement this process. Workpapers should be provided in electronic spreadsheet format with formulas intact.
Describe how the Company’s siting analysis informs the interconnection limits identified in the Company’s modeling. 
Response:
The Company did not determine eligible sites or propose any new installations in the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”). In instances where that work is done, it is performed as part of a Request for Proposal (“RFP”) process, which is separate from the IRP.

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-13/STF-PIA-13-2.docx =====
STF-PIA-13-2
Question:
Did the Company consider the reduced interconnection time and the lower interconnection and transmission costs of building resources that connect to existing points of interconnection? 
If yes, please provide the Company’s assumptions regarding how much reduced time and costs were assumed. If no, please explain why these factors were not included in the Company’s IRP modeling.
Response:
The Company is not proposing to build new generation resources in the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”).  The generic expansion modeling results presented in the Resource Mix Study do not represent resource commitments. Instead, they provide an informative roadmap for long-term planning and to support analyses. The Resource Mix Study offers insights into the optimal mix of technologies, which indicates the types of resources that may be competitive through a Request for Proposals (“RFP”) process. The procurement outcome, including associated transmission and interconnection impacts, will ultimately be determined based on market responses, the Company’s evaluation process, economic decision analysis, and established procurement protocols.

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-13/STF-PIA-13-6.docx =====
STF-PIA-13-6
Question:
Please refer to the Company’s response to STF-PIA-3-8(b):
Please explain how the limits encourage “right-sizing the ratio of solar and storage”.
Please explain how the maximum system sizes will be calculated.
Response:
The limits are intended to encourage an appropriate sizing of solar and battery capacity by achieving the following goals:
Encouraging solar system sizes that are consistent with existing behind the meter solar programs, which will enable more efficient and cost-effective program implementation and individual project management and processing.
Ensure that residential customers currently participating in existing solar programs can add an AC coupled battery, which could increase total export capacity, and still be eligible for participation. 
Ensure that a participating battery can be adequately charged by the solar resource. 
Limit potential distribution system upgrades and impacts, for which the costs are currently socialized across all retail customers and offset by the one-time Interconnection Fee. 

The maximum system size will be calculated based on the expectation that behind-the-meter assets should not be capable of pushing back more than the stated limit onto the grid. Currently, system size as determined for the purposes of the solar program qualification is based on the maximum continuous output of the solar inverter. If a system is paired with a battery, the total of both the solar and the battery export capabilities will be used to determine the system’s overall size with respect to qualification. AC and DC coupled configurations will differ in how nameplate ratings of individual components may translate into system size determination for program qualification. Regardless of the configuration, the total maximum capacity that could be delivered to the grid at any point, should not exceed the program defined limitations.   

===== DKT 56002 & 56003 STF-PIA-13/STF-PIA-13-7.docx =====
STF-PIA-13-7
Question:
Please describe how the Company considered the Inflation Reduction Act’s (IRA) additional tax credit bonus adder for locating zero-emission resources in energy communities in the development of the 2025 IRP?  
Response:
As discussed in Section 2.2.2 of the Resource Mix Study, bonuses for meeting the energy community provisions of the Inflation Reduction Act (“IRA”) were not included for generic expansion units due to uncertainty regarding the ability to meet the requirements.
As discussed in Technical Appendix Volume 1, Unit Upgrade Analyses, Plant Vogtle is in an energy community and is expected to qualify for the energy community tax credit bonus. 
As discussed in Technical Appendix Volume 1, Hydro Modernization, Plant Lloyd Shoals is in an energy community and is expected to qualify for the energy community tax credit bonus.

STF-PIA-13-8
Question:
Please describe how much federal funding or tax incentives Georgia Power plans to use during the next three years (2026-2028).
What is the Company’s plan for replacing these sources should the funding not materialize, or tax incentives are no longer available? 
Would Georgia Power anticipate canceling or delaying any related projects or investments should the funding not materialize, or the tax incentives no longer be available? Please explain what actions the Company will take.
Response:

This response is limited to the Company’s requests in the 2025 Integrated Resource Plan (“IRP”).
For hydro modernization, the amount of federal funding or tax incentives Georgia Power assumed in 2026 through 2028 as part of the hydro modernization cost-benefit analysis can be found in Technical Appendix Volume 1, Hydro Modernization workpapers provided to Commission Staff on January 31, 2025. A summary of all assumptions can be found in “2025IRP_AV_HydroMod_TRADE SECRET.xlsx” on the “Cost Inputs” tab. For detailed assumptions on how each of these tax incentives or federal fundings are calculated, please see the SAM files provided. 
For the nuclear upgrades, much of the federal funding and tax incentives take effect beyond the 2026-2028 timeframe. The amount of federal funding or tax incentives Georgia Power assumed in 2026 through 2028 as part of the nuclear upgrades cost-benefit analysis can be found in Technical Appendix Volume 1, Unit Upgrade Analyses, “2025 IRP Nuclear Upgrades TRADE SECRET.docx.” A summary of all assumptions can be found in the “2025IRP_AV_Nuclear TRADE SECRET.xlsm” workpaper, provided to Commission Staff on January 31, 2025, on the “Cost Inputs” tab. For detailed assumptions on how each of these tax incentives or federal fundings are calculated, please see the SAM files provided in STF-JKA-2-22 Attachment A TRADE SECRET & STF-JKA-2-22 Attachment B TRADE SECRET.
For transmission, please refer to the Company’s response to STF-DEA-4-7.
The Company will continue to monitor and seek available funding opportunities to minimize costs to customers as part of the commitment to provide clean, safe, reliable, and affordable electric service to customers. 

No, Georgia Power does not anticipate canceling or delaying any projects if the assumed federal funding or tax incentives are no longer available.
